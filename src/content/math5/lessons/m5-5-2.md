Kateri ate $\dfrac{2}{8}$ of a pizza. Her brother ate $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the same pizza. How much did they eat together? When the pieces are the same size, adding and subtracting fractions is just counting pieces.

## Building fractions from unit fractions

:::definition Unit fraction
A **unit fraction** has $1$ as its numerator, such as $\dfrac{1}{8}$. It is one equal piece of a whole.
:::

Every fraction is made of unit fractions. $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is five pieces of size $\dfrac{1}{8}$:
$$\dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{8}$$

You can also split $\dfrac{5}{8}$ into two parts. The bar shows $\dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{2}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8}$.

Find the missing numerator: $\dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{4}{10} + \dfrac{?}{10}$
@step $\dfrac{7}{10}$ is $7$ tenths. $\dfrac{4}{10}$ is $4$ of them.
@step $7 - 4 = 3$, so $3$ tenths are left.
@answer $\dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{4}{10} + \dfrac{3}{10}$. The missing numerator is $3$.

## Adding and subtracting with a common denominator

:::definition Common denominator
Fractions have a **common denominator** when their denominators are the same, like $\dfrac{2}{8}$ and $\dfrac{3}{8}$.
:::

With a common denominator, the pieces are the same size. Add or subtract the **numerators** to count the pieces. The **denominator stays the same**, because the size of the pieces does not change.

:::example Kateri's pizza
Find $\dfrac{2}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8}$.
@step Both fractions count eighths.
@step $2$ eighths plus $3$ eighths is $5$ eighths.
@answer $\dfrac{2}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{5}{8}$. They ate $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of the pizza.
:::

:::mistake Do not add the denominators
$\dfrac{2}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8}$ is not $\dfrac{5}{16}$. Sixteenths are smaller pieces than eighths. The pieces are still eighths, so the answer is $\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

Improper fractions work the same way.

:::example Improper fractions
Find $\dfrac{7}{5} + \dfrac{6}{5}$.
@step Add the numerators: $7 + 6 = 13$. Keep the denominator $5$.
@step $\dfrac{13}{5}$ is an improper fraction. $13 \div 5 = 2$ R $3$.
@answer $\dfrac{7}{5} + \dfrac{6}{5} = \dfrac{13}{5} = 2\dfrac{3}{5}$. Both forms are correct.
:::

:::tip Any order
You can add fractions in any order, just like natural numbers: $\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{2}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8}$.
:::

:::example Add with wholes and parts, or with improper fractions
Find $2\dfrac{3}{5} + 1\dfrac{4}{5}$.
@step **Way 1: wholes and parts.** Wholes: $2 + 1 = 3$. Parts: $\dfrac{3}{5} + \dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{7}{5} = 1\dfrac{2}{5}$. Together: $3 + 1\dfrac{2}{5} = 4\dfrac{2}{5}$.
@step **Way 2: improper fractions.** $2\dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{13}{5}$ and $1\dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{9}{5}$. Then $\dfrac{13}{5} + \dfrac{9}{5} = \dfrac{22}{5} = 4\dfrac{2}{5}$.
@answer Both ways give $4\dfrac{2}{5}$.
:::

:::example Subtract when the fraction part is too small
Find $3\dfrac{1}{5} - 1\dfrac{3}{5}$.
@step **Way 1: regroup.** You cannot take $\dfrac{3}{5}$ from $\dfrac{1}{5}$. Trade $1$ whole for $\dfrac{5}{5}$: $3\dfrac{1}{5} = 2 + \dfrac{6}{5}$. Then $2 - 1 = 1$ and $\dfrac{6}{5} - \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
@step **Way 2: improper fractions.** $\dfrac{16}{5} - \dfrac{8}{5} = \dfrac{8}{5} = 1\dfrac{3}{5}$.
@answer $3\dfrac{1}{5} - 1\dfrac{3}{5} = 1\dfrac{3}{5}$
:::

:::mistake Do not flip the fractions
In $3\dfrac{1}{5} - 1\dfrac{3}{5}$, do not subtract $\dfrac{1}{5}$ from $\dfrac{3}{5}$ to get $2\dfrac{2}{5}$. The first number's fraction part is the one being taken from. Regroup a whole instead.
:::

:::tip Which way is easier?
Wholes and parts is quick when the fraction parts are easy to add or subtract. Improper fractions are handy when you would need to regroup.
:::

## Solving fraction problems

1. Read the problem. Find the amounts.
2. Decide: put together (**add**), or find what is left or the difference (**subtract**)?
3. Write the number sentence and solve it.
4. Check that the answer makes sense.

:::example A hiking trail
A mountain trail is $4\dfrac{5}{8}$ km long. Jun has hiked $2\dfrac{2}{8}$ km. How far does Jun still have to hike?
@step "Still have to hike" means what is left, so subtract: $4\dfrac{5}{8} - 2\dfrac{2}{8}$.
@step Wholes: $4 - 2 = 2$. Parts: $\dfrac{5}{8} - \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{3}{8}$.
@answer Jun still has to hike $2\dfrac{3}{8}$ km.
:::

:::example Two steps in the kitchen
Ayesha has $5$ cups of flour. She uses $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ cups for pancakes and $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ cups for muffins. How much flour is left?
@step Add what she uses: wholes $1 + 2 = 3$, parts $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{4}{4} = 1$. She uses $4$ cups.
@step Subtract from what she has: $5 - 4 = 1$.
@answer Ayesha has $1$ cup of flour left.
:::

:::summary
- A unit fraction has numerator $1$. Every fraction is made of unit fractions.
- With a common denominator, add or subtract the numerators. Keep the denominator.
- Never add the denominators.
- Mixed numbers: add or subtract the wholes and the parts, or change to improper fractions first.
- Regroup $1$ whole as a fraction (like $\dfrac{5}{5}$) when the fraction part is too small to subtract from.
- In a problem, decide whether to add or subtract, then check that your answer makes sense.
:::
