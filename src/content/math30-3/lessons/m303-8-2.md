“Drive it home for \$469 a month!” Lease ads make a new vehicle look cheap. A lease can be a good deal, but only if you know what it really costs, what happens if you drive more than you planned, and what it would cost to keep the vehicle at the end.

## How a lease works

:::definition Lease
A **lease** is a long-term rental. You make monthly payments for a set **term** (often 24 to 60 months), then return the vehicle. You never own it unless you buy it at the end.
:::

At the start you may pay a down payment and an **acquisition fee**. In this course, the monthly lease payments include GST, and every question tells you the amounts.

$$\text{total lease cost} = \text{monthly payment} \times \text{number of months} + \text{down payment} + \text{fees}$$

:::example A 3-year lease
Ayesha leases a new compact SUV for \$468.75 a month for 36 months, payments including GST. She pays \$2,000 down and a \$495 acquisition fee. What is the total cost of the lease?
@step Lease payments: $468.75 \times 36 = 16{,}875.00$
@step Add the amounts paid at the start: $16{,}875.00 + 2{,}000 + 495 = 19{,}370.00$
@answer The lease costs \$19,370.00 in total, and Ayesha returns the SUV at the end.
:::

## Kilometre allowances and extra charges

:::definition Kilometre allowance
A lease includes a **kilometre allowance**, such as 20,000 km a year. Every kilometre over the allowance costs an **excess kilometre charge**, paid when the vehicle is returned.
:::

The dealer may also charge for damage beyond normal wear and tear, and a fee when you return the vehicle.

:::example Driving too much
Ayesha’s lease allows 20,000 km a year and charges \$0.15 for each kilometre over. When she returns the SUV after 3 years, the odometer shows 71,400 km. What is the excess kilometre charge, and the new total cost of the lease?
@step Allowance for the whole lease: $20{,}000 \times 3 = 60{,}000$ km
@step Kilometres over: $71{,}400 - 60{,}000 = 11{,}400$ km
@step Charge: $11{,}400 \times 0.15 = 1{,}710.00$
@step New total: $19{,}370.00 + 1{,}710.00 = 21{,}080.00$
@answer The excess kilometre charge is \$1,710.00, so the lease costs \$21,080.00 in total.
:::

:::mistake Using the yearly allowance
The allowance is per year. Multiply by the number of years in the lease before you compare it with the odometer. Comparing 71,400 km with 20,000 km would make the charge far too high.
:::

## Leasing to buy

:::definition Residual value
The **residual value** (or **buyout price**) is the price, set in the lease, at which you can buy the vehicle at the end of the lease. GST is charged on the buyout.
:::

$$\text{total cost to own} = \text{total lease cost} + \text{buyout} \times 1.05$$

:::example Keeping the SUV
At the end of her lease, Ayesha can buy the SUV for its residual value of \$19,800 plus 5% GST. The SUV’s price at the start was \$33,995 plus 5% GST. What is her total cost to own the SUV this way, and how does it compare with paying cash at the start? (Assume she stayed within the allowance.)
@step Buyout with GST: $19{,}800 \times 1.05 = 20{,}790.00$
@step Total cost to own: $19{,}370.00 + 20{,}790.00 = 40{,}160.00$
@step Cash price at the start: $33{,}995 \times 1.05 = 35{,}694.75$
@step Difference: $40{,}160.00 - 35{,}694.75 = 4{,}465.25$
@answer Leasing to buy costs \$40,160.00, which is \$4,465.25 more than paying cash at the start. The extra is the cost of spreading the payments out.
:::

## Buy or lease?

Neither choice is always better. Compare the **total** costs over the same time, and think about how you will use the vehicle.

| Leasing | Buying |
|---|---|
| Lower monthly payments and often little down | Higher monthly payments |
| A newer vehicle, usually under warranty | You own the vehicle and it keeps some value |
| Kilometre limits and wear-and-tear charges | No kilometre limits; drive and modify it as you like |
| Nothing to sell at the end | Cheaper over a long time if you keep it |

:::example Comparing over 3 years
Instead of leasing, Ayesha could buy the same SUV: \$5,000 down and \$940.62 a month for 36 months. Suppose it loses 16% of its value each year, and she could sell it after 3 years. Which costs less over the 3 years?
@step Total paid to buy: $5{,}000 + 940.62 \times 36 = 5{,}000 + 33{,}862.32 = 38{,}862.32$
@step Value after 3 years: $33{,}995 \times 0.84^3 = 20{,}148.97\ldots \approx 20{,}148.97$
@step Net cost of buying: $38{,}862.32 - 20{,}148.97 = 18{,}713.35$
@step Cost of leasing (within the allowance): $19{,}370.00$
@answer Buying has a net cost of \$18,713.35, which is \$656.65 less than leasing, because Ayesha would own a vehicle worth about \$20,149 at the end. If she drove 71,400 km on the lease, leasing would cost even more.
:::

:::mistake Comparing monthly payments
The lease payment (\$468.75) is much lower than the loan payment (\$940.62), but that does not make leasing cheaper. Compare total costs, and remember that a buyer still owns the vehicle at the end.
:::

:::summary
- Total lease cost = monthly payment × months + down payment + fees (+ any excess kilometre or end-of-lease charges).
- Excess kilometres = odometer − (yearly allowance × years); charge = excess km × rate per km.
- Lease to buy: total cost to own = total lease cost + buyout × 1.05.
- To compare buying and leasing, compare total costs over the same time. For buying, subtract the value the vehicle still has.
- Leasing suits low kilometres and wanting a new vehicle often; buying suits high kilometres, heavy use and keeping a vehicle a long time.
:::
