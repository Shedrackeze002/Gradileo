A dispatcher wants to know how many furnace calls to plan for, a tire shop wants to quote a typical wait, and a rancher wants to know the mass of a typical calf. Each one needs a single number that describes a whole set of data. That number is a **measure of central tendency**, and there are three to choose from.

## The mean

:::definition Mean
The **mean** is the total of the values divided by the number of values:
$$\text{mean} = \dfrac{\text{sum of the values}}{\text{number of values}}$$
It is what most people call the "average".
:::

The mean uses every value in the data, so it shares the total out equally. That makes it useful for estimating totals: a crew that uses a mean of 55 bags of concrete a day will need about $55 \times 15 = 825$ bags for a 15-day job.

:::example Furnace calls
Liam, a dispatcher at a furnace repair shop, recorded the service calls each day for 5 days: 8, 12, 9, 15, 11. What is the mean number of calls per day?
@step Add the values: $8 + 12 + 9 + 15 + 11 = 55$
@step Divide by the number of values: $55 \div 5 = 11$
@answer The mean is 11 calls a day.
:::

:::

## The median and the mode

:::definition Median
The **median** is the middle value when the data is put in order from least to greatest. With an even number of values, the median is halfway between the two middle values (their mean).
:::

:::definition Mode
The **mode** is the value that appears most often. A data set can have no mode (every value appears once) or more than one mode.
:::

:::example Waiting for tires
Mei timed how long, in minutes, 6 customers waited for a tire change at a shop in town: 22, 35, 28, 41, 30, 28. Find the mean, the median and the mode.
@step Mean: $22 + 35 + 28 + 41 + 30 + 28 = 184$, and $184 \div 6 \approx 30.7$ minutes.
@step Put the values in order: 22, 28, 28, 30, 35, 41
@step There are 6 values, so the median is halfway between the 3rd and 4th values: $(28 + 30) \div 2 = 29$
@step 28 appears twice, more than any other value, so the mode is 28.
@answer The mean is about 30.7 min, the median is 29 min and the mode is 28 min.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to sort
The median is the middle of the **ordered** list. The middle of the list as it was recorded (22, 35, **28, 41**, 30, 28) gives the wrong answer. Always put the values in order first.
:::

:::check m303-central-tendency 2
:::

## Outliers: choosing the best measure

:::definition Outlier
An **outlier** is a value that is far away from the rest of the data, such as a week when a worker was off sick, or a home that sold for far more than the others in town.
:::

Amara, an apprentice electrician, logged the hours she worked each week for 7 weeks: 40, 42, 38, 44, 12, 41, 43. She was off sick with the flu during the week she worked 12 hours.

:::example Which measure describes a typical week?
Find the mean and the median of Amara’s hours. Which one better describes a typical work week?
@step Mean: $40 + 42 + 38 + 44 + 12 + 41 + 43 = 260$, and $260 \div 7 \approx 37.1$ hours.
@step In order: 12, 38, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44. The median is the 4th value: 41 hours.
@step The outlier (12) pulls the mean down to 37.1, which is lower than six of the seven weeks. The median, 41, sits among the typical weeks.
@answer The median, 41 hours, better describes a typical work week.
:::

Use this guide to choose:

| Measure | Best when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | The data has no outliers, or you need to estimate a total | Bags of concrete a day for a long job |
| Median | The data has outliers that would pull the mean | Home prices, wages that include a manager |
| Mode | You want the most common value or category | The boot size to order the most of |

:::tip Removing the outlier
Without the sick week, the mean is $248 \div 6 \approx 41.3$ hours, very close to the median. That is a quick way to see how much one outlier moves the mean.
:::

:::check m303-central-tendency 2
:::

## Finding a missing value for a target mean

Sometimes you know the mean you need and want to find the last value. Work backwards from the total.

:::example The last module exam
Jaxon needs a mean of at least 75% on 5 module exams to pass his training period. His first four marks are 68%, 82%, 71% and 77%. What is the lowest mark he can get on the last exam?
@step Total needed: $75 \times 5 = 375$
@step Total so far: $68 + 82 + 71 + 77 = 298$
@step Last exam: $375 - 298 = 77$
@step Check: $(298 + 77) \div 5 = 375 \div 5 = 75$ ✓
@answer Jaxon needs at least 77% on the last exam.
:::

:::mistake Averaging the target and the marks
Do not just find the mean of the first four marks and compare it with 75. The total of all five values must be $75 \times 5$, so work with totals.
:::

:::check m303-central-tendency 3
:::

:::summary
- Mean = sum of the values ÷ number of values. It uses every value and is good for estimating totals.
- Median = the middle value of the **ordered** data; with an even number of values, the mean of the two middle values.
- Mode = the most common value.
- An outlier pulls the mean toward it but barely moves the median, so use the median when there are outliers.
- For a target mean: total needed = target × number of values; last value = total needed − total so far.
:::
