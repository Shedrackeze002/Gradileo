A score report says you are at the 85th percentile on a trades entrance test. That does not mean you got 85% of the questions right. **Percentiles** compare one value with everyone else in the group, and they show up on test results, growth charts for babies and wage surveys.

## What a percentile means

:::definition Percentile
The **$P$th percentile** is the value at or below which about $P\%$ of the data fall. A value’s **percentile rank** (PR) is the percent of the data at or below that value.
:::

If a welder’s hourly wage is at the 80th percentile of a wage survey, about 80% of the welders in the survey earn the same or less, and about 20% earn more. Percentile ranks go from 0 to 100, and the 50th percentile is the median.

:::example A growth chart
At a check-up at the clinic, a baby boy’s mass is at the 85th percentile for his age. What does this mean?
@step A percentile compares him with other babies his age.
@step About 85% of babies his age have a mass at or below his, and about 15% are heavier.
@answer He is heavier than about 85% of babies his age. It does **not** mean he is 85% of some ideal mass.
:::

:::mistake Percentile is not a percent score
A percentile rank of 85 on a test says nothing about how many questions you got right. If the test was very hard, you might have scored 60% and still be at the 85th percentile.
:::

## The percentile rank formula

To find a percentile rank, count the values below and the values equal to the score. In this course, use this formula:

$$PR = \dfrac{b + 0.5e}{n} \times 100$$

where $b$ is the number of values **below** the score, $e$ is the number of values **equal** to the score (including the score itself) and $n$ is the total number of values. Round the result to the nearest whole number.

:::example A safety test
Thirty apprentices wrote a fall-protection safety test. 21 of them scored lower than Kai, and 2 others got the same score as Kai. What is Kai’s percentile rank?
@step Below: $b = 21$. Equal, counting Kai: $e = 2 + 1 = 3$. Total: $n = 30$.
@step $PR = \dfrac{21 + 0.5(3)}{30} \times 100 = \dfrac{22.5}{30} \times 100 = 75$
@answer Kai’s percentile rank is 75.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to count the person
$e$ includes the person whose rank you are finding. If no one else has the same score, $e = 1$, not $0$.
:::

## Percentile ranks from a list or a table

With a frequency table, add the frequencies to find $n$, and add the frequencies of the scores below to find $b$.

:::

:::example A safety quiz
The table shows the scores of the workers at a meat-packing plant on a 10-question safety quiz. Hana scored 8. What is her percentile rank?
@step Total: $n = 2 + 3 + 5 + 6 + 3 + 1 = 20$
@step Below 8 (scores 5, 6 and 7): $b = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10$
@step Equal to 8: $e = 6$
@step $PR = \dfrac{10 + 0.5(6)}{20} \times 100 = \dfrac{13}{20} \times 100 = 65$
@answer Hana’s percentile rank is 65.
:::

## The value at a percentile

To go the other way and find the value at the $P$th percentile of $n$ ordered values, use this method:

1. Work out $R = \dfrac{P}{100} \times n$.
2. If $R$ is a whole number, the percentile is the mean of the values in positions $R$ and $R + 1$.
3. If $R$ is not a whole number, round $R$ **up** and use the value in that position.

:::example A pipeline crew
A pipeline crew recorded the number of joints welded each day for 16 days. In order: 12, 15, 15, 18, 20, 21, 21, 22, 24, 25, 25, 27, 28, 30, 31, 34. Find the 25th percentile and the 90th percentile.
@step 25th: $R = 0.25 \times 16 = 4$, a whole number, so use the mean of the 4th and 5th values: $(18 + 20) \div 2 = 19$
@step 90th: $R = 0.90 \times 16 = 14.4$, not a whole number, so round up to 15. The 15th value is 31.
@answer The 25th percentile is 19 joints and the 90th percentile is 31 joints.
:::

## Comparing percentile ranks

Percentile ranks let you compare people in different groups, even when the groups are different sizes.

:::example Two classes
Arjun and Talia wrote the same welding theory exam at different colleges. Arjun scored 81 in a class of 24: 17 classmates scored lower and no one else had his score. Talia scored 76 in a class of 32: 24 classmates scored lower and 1 other had her score. Who did better compared with their own class?
@step Arjun: $PR = \dfrac{17 + 0.5(1)}{24} \times 100 \approx 73$
@step Talia: $PR = \dfrac{24 + 0.5(2)}{32} \times 100 \approx 78$
@answer Talia did better compared with her class (PR 78 against 73), even though Arjun had the higher score.
:::

:::

:::summary
- The $P$th percentile is the value at or below which about $P\%$ of the data fall. A percentile rank is not a percent score.
- $PR = \dfrac{b + 0.5e}{n} \times 100$: $b$ below, $e$ equal (including the person), $n$ total.
- From a frequency table, add frequencies to find $n$ and $b$.
- Value at the $P$th percentile: $R = \dfrac{P}{100} \times n$; whole number → mean of positions $R$ and $R + 1$; otherwise round $R$ up.
- Compare people in different groups with percentile ranks, not raw scores.
:::
