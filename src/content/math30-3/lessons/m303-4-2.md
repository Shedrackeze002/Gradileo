A business logo is printed small on a business card and huge on a trade-show banner. A floor plan shrinks a house to fit on a page, and a hobby model shrinks a grain truck to fit on a shelf. Each is a **dilation**: the same shape at a different size.

## What a dilation does

:::definition Dilation and scale factor
A **dilation** enlarges or reduces a shape from a fixed point, the **centre of dilation**. Every distance from the centre is multiplied by the **scale factor** $k$.

- $k > 1$: an **enlargement** (the image is bigger).
- $0 < k < 1$: a **reduction** (the image is smaller).

The image has the same shape and the same angles as the original. Only the size changes.
:::

When the centre is the origin, multiply **both** coordinates by $k$:

$$(x, y) \to (kx,\ ky)$$

:::example Enlarging a logo
A sign maker enlarges the triangle $A(2, 1)$, $B(4, 1)$, $C(2, 3)$ in a logo by a scale factor of $2$ about the origin. Find the image.
@step Multiply both coordinates by $2$.
@step $A(2, 1) \to (4, 2)$, $B(4, 1) \to (8, 2)$, $C(2, 3) \to (4, 6)$
@answer The image is $A'(4, 2)$, $B'(8, 2)$, $C'(4, 6)$, an enlargement.
:::

A scale factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ makes a reduction: $(6, 4) \to (3, 2)$, halfway to the origin.

## Finding the scale factor

Compare any length in the image with the matching length in the original:

$$k = \dfrac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}$$

:::example A logo for a banner
A logo is $12\ \text{cm}$ wide on a business card. On a trade-show banner it is $30\ \text{cm}$ wide. Find the scale factor.
@step $k = \dfrac{30}{12} = 2.5$
@step $2.5 > 1$, so the banner logo is an enlargement.
@answer The scale factor is $2.5$.
:::

For a reduction the scale factor is less than $1$. A tile pattern $24\ \text{cm}$ wide copied at $6\ \text{cm}$ for a mosaic has $k = \frac{6}{24} = 0.25$.

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
Always put the **image** on top. Original over image gives $\frac{12}{30} = 0.4$, which would wrongly say the banner is smaller than the card.
:::

## Perimeter and area after a dilation

Every length is multiplied by $k$, so the **perimeter** is multiplied by $k$. Area uses two lengths (length × width), so the **area** is multiplied by $k \times k = k^2$.

:::example Enlarging a quilt block
A quilt block is a $10\ \text{in}$ by $10\ \text{in}$ square. It is enlarged by a scale factor of $1.5$. Find the new perimeter and area.
@step Original: perimeter $4 \times 10 = 40\ \text{in}$, area $10 \times 10 = 100\ \text{in}^2$.
@step Perimeter: $1.5 \times 40 = 60\ \text{in}$
@step Area: $1.5^2 \times 100 = 2.25 \times 100 = 225\ \text{in}^2$
@step Check: the new block is $15\ \text{in}$ by $15\ \text{in}$, and $15 \times 15 = 225$.
@answer The enlarged block has a perimeter of $60\ \text{in}$ and an area of $225\ \text{in}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying the area by k
$100 \times 1.5 = 150$ is wrong. Both the length and the width grow by $1.5$, so the area grows by $1.5^2 = 2.25$.
:::

To find $k$ from two areas, work backward: $k^2 = \dfrac{\text{image area}}{\text{original area}}$, then take the square root.

:::

## Scale drawings and scale models

A floor plan or a model is a dilation of the real object. A scale of $1 : n$ means a scale factor of $k = \frac{1}{n}$ from real life to the drawing, so real lengths are $n$ times the drawing lengths.

:::example Area from a floor plan
A garage plan is drawn at a scale of $1 : 50$. On the plan the room is $6.4\ \text{cm}$ by $9.2\ \text{cm}$. Find the real area.
@step Real length: $6.4 \times 50 = 320\ \text{cm} = 3.2\ \text{m}$
@step Real width: $9.2 \times 50 = 460\ \text{cm} = 4.6\ \text{m}$
@step Area: $3.2 \times 4.6 = 14.72$
@answer The room has an area of $14.72\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::tip Convert the lengths first
Change the real lengths to metres **before** multiplying. If you find the area in square centimetres first, divide by $10\,000$ (not $100$) to get square metres.
:::

A $1 : 24$ model of a pickup truck that is $23.5\ \text{cm}$ long stands for a truck $23.5 \times 24 = 564\ \text{cm}$, or $5.64\ \text{m}$, long.

:::check m303-dilation 3
:::

:::summary
- A dilation about the origin with scale factor $k$: $(x, y) \to (kx,\ ky)$.
- $k > 1$ is an enlargement; $0 < k < 1$ is a reduction. Shape and angles do not change.
- $k = \dfrac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}$.
- Perimeter is multiplied by $k$; area is multiplied by $k^2$.
- A scale of $1 : n$ has $k = \frac{1}{n}$: multiply drawing lengths by $n$ to get real lengths.
:::
