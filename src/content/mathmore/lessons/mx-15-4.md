A machine is set to fill bags with $500$ g of flour. One bag weighing $505$ g says little, but if $16$ bags average $505.4$ g, the machine may have drifted. The mean of a sample varies far less than single values, and that is what makes the test work.

## The distribution of a sample mean

:::definition Sample mean
If $X \sim \mathrm{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ and $\bar{X}$ is the mean of a random sample of $n$ values, then
$$\bar{X} \sim \mathrm{N}\!\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right).$$
The sample mean has the same mean as $X$, and standard deviation $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$, called the **standard error**.
:::

:::example The spread of a mean
Bags have masses $X \sim \mathrm{N}(500, 12^2)$ g. For a sample of $16$ bags, find the standard deviation of $\bar{X}$.
@step $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{16}}$
@answer $3$ g, a quarter of the spread of single bags.
:::

:::mistake Divide σ by √n, not n
The **variance** of $\bar{X}$ is $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}$, so its standard deviation is $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$. Dividing $\sigma$ by $n$ makes the spread far too small.

## Testing a mean

To test whether a mean has changed, assume $H_0$: $\mu = \mu_0$, so $\bar{X} \sim \mathrm{N}\!\left(\mu_0, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)$. Then either find the p-value of the sample mean, or find the critical region and see whether the sample mean lies in it.

:::example Has the mean increased?
Masses are $X \sim \mathrm{N}(500, 12^2)$ g. A sample of $16$ bags has mean $505.4$ g. Test at the $5\%$ level whether the mean has increased.
@step $H_0$: $\mu = 500$, $H_1$: $\mu > 500$. Under $H_0$, $\bar{X} \sim \mathrm{N}(500, 3^2)$.
@step $P(\bar{X} \ge 505.4) \approx 0.0359$.
@step $0.0359 < 0.05$, so the result is significant.
@answer Reject $H_0$: there is evidence that the mean mass has increased.
:::

For a **two-tailed** test at $5\%$, each tail holds $2.5\%$. Here the critical region would be $\bar{X} < 500 - 1.96 \times 3 \approx 494.1$ or $\bar{X} > 505.9$, and $505.4$ would **not** be in it: a two-tailed test needs stronger evidence in each direction.

:::tip Split α for two tails
In a two-tailed test, put half the significance level in each tail. At $5\%$, use $P(Z > z) = 0.025$, so $z \approx 1.96$.

:::summary
- The mean of a sample of $n$ from $\mathrm{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ is $\bar{X} \sim \mathrm{N}\!\left(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)$.
- Test with $H_0$: $\mu = \mu_0$; under $H_0$ use the distribution of $\bar{X}$.
- Reject $H_0$ if the p-value is below the significance level, or the sample mean is in the critical region.
- For a two-tailed test, half the significance level goes in each tail.
:::
