Adding $\dfrac{2}{x - 1} + \dfrac{3}{x + 2}$ gives $\dfrac{5x + 1}{(x - 1)(x + 2)}$. Partial fractions go the other way, splitting one fraction into simpler ones, which makes many fractions easier to expand and to integrate.

## Splitting a fraction

:::definition Partial fractions
A fraction whose denominator is a product of linear factors can be written as a sum of **partial fractions**, one for each factor:
$$\frac{px + q}{(x - a)(x - b)} \equiv \frac{A}{x - a} + \frac{B}{x - b}.$$
A **repeated** factor $(x - b)^2$ needs two fractions, $\dfrac{B}{x - b} + \dfrac{C}{(x - b)^2}$.
:::

To find the constants, multiply both sides by the denominator, then substitute values of $x$ that make a factor zero.

:::example Two linear factors
Express $\dfrac{5x + 1}{(x - 1)(x + 2)}$ in partial fractions.
@step $5x + 1 \equiv A(x + 2) + B(x - 1)$
@step $x = 1$: $6 = 3A$, so $A = 2$. $x = -2$: $-9 = -3B$, so $B = 3$.
@answer $\dfrac{5x + 1}{(x - 1)(x + 2)} \equiv \dfrac{2}{x - 1} + \dfrac{3}{x + 2}$
:::

## Improper fractions

When the degree of the numerator is at least the degree of the denominator, the fraction is **improper**. Divide first, or include a constant: for example
$$\frac{x^2 + 3}{(x - 1)(x + 2)} \equiv 1 + \frac{A}{x - 1} + \frac{B}{x + 2},$$
where the $1$ is the ratio of the $x^2$ coefficients. Then multiply through and substitute as before.

:::summary
- $\dfrac{px + q}{(x - a)(x - b)} \equiv \dfrac{A}{x - a} + \dfrac{B}{x - b}$; find $A$ and $B$ by substituting $x = a$ and $x = b$.
- A repeated factor $(x - b)^2$ gives $\dfrac{B}{x - b} + \dfrac{C}{(x - b)^2}$; compare coefficients for any constant left.
- An improper fraction needs a whole-number part as well, found by dividing.
:::
