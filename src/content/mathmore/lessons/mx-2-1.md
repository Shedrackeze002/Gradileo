A space probe that has travelled $6\,000\,000\,000$ km, and a dust grain with a mass of $0.000\,000\,4$ g: numbers like these are hard to read and easy to copy wrongly, because of all the zeros. Scientific notation writes them with a short number and a power of ten instead.

## Writing numbers in scientific notation

:::definition Scientific notation
A number is in **scientific notation** when it is written as
$$a \times 10^{n}$$
where $1 \le a < 10$ and $n$ is an integer. The number $a$ is the **coefficient**.
:::

$6\,000\,000\,000 = 6 \times 10^{9}$, because $10^{9}$ is $1$ followed by nine zeros. For a number that does not start with a single digit, move the decimal point to just after the first non-zero digit, and count the places it moved.

:::example A large number
Write $45\,300\,000$ in scientific notation.
@step Put the point after the first digit: $4.53$, which is from $1$ to $10$.
@step From $45\,300\,000$ to $4.53$ the point moves $7$ places to the left, so we multiply $4.53$ by $10^{7}$ to get back.
@answer $45\,300\,000 = 4.53 \times 10^{7}$
:::

## Small numbers and negative exponents

Recall that $10^{-1} = \tfrac{1}{10} = 0.1$, $10^{-2} = 0.01$ and $10^{-3} = 0.001$. Multiplying by a negative power of ten moves the point to the left, so numbers smaller than $1$ have negative exponents.

:::example A small number
Write $0.000\,072$ in scientific notation.
@step Put the point after the first non-zero digit: $7.2$.
@step From $0.000\,072$ to $7.2$ the point moves $5$ places to the right, so $0.000\,072 = 7.2 \div 10^{5}$.
@answer $0.000\,072 = 7.2 \times 10^{-5}$
:::

:::tip Which sign?
A number of $10$ or more has a positive exponent. A number between $0$ and $1$ has a negative exponent. A number from $1$ to $10$ has exponent $0$: $3.8 = 3.8 \times 10^{0}$.
:::

:::mistake A coefficient that is too big
$45.3 \times 10^{6}$ equals $45\,300\,000$, but it is not in scientific notation, because $45.3$ is not less than $10$. Write $45.3 = 4.53 \times 10^{1}$, so $45.3 \times 10^{6} = 4.53 \times 10^{7}$.
:::

## From scientific notation to ordinary numbers

To write $a \times 10^{n}$ in full, move the point in $a$ by $n$ places: to the right when $n$ is positive and to the left when $n$ is negative, filling empty places with zeros.

:::example Writing numbers in full
Write $3.06 \times 10^{5}$ and $8.1 \times 10^{-4}$ as ordinary numbers.
@step $3.06 \times 10^{5}$: move the point $5$ places right, $3.06 \to 306\,000$.
@step $8.1 \times 10^{-4}$: move the point $4$ places left, $8.1 \to 0.000\,81$.
@answer $3.06 \times 10^{5} = 306\,000$ and $8.1 \times 10^{-4} = 0.000\,81$
:::

:::summary
- Scientific notation writes a number as $a \times 10^{n}$, with $1 \le a < 10$ and $n$ an integer.
- Move the point to just after the first non-zero digit and count the places: moving left (a large number) gives a positive exponent, moving right (a small number) gives a negative one.
- To write the number in full, move the point $n$ places: right for positive $n$, left for negative $n$.
- $45.3 \times 10^{6}$ is not in scientific notation; the coefficient must be less than $10$.
:::
