Forces rarely line up neatly. A rope pulls at an angle, a slope pushes at an angle, two cables hold a sign from different sides. To work with forces like these, we split each one into two parts at right angles, and then add the parts.

## Resolving a force

:::definition Resolving a force
**Resolving** a force means splitting it into two **components** at right angles that together have the same effect. A force $F$ at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal has a horizontal component $F\cos\theta$ and a vertical component $F\sin\theta$.
:::

:::example Components of a force
Find the components of a force of $20$ N acting at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal.
@step Horizontal: $20\cos 30^\circ \approx 17.3$ N.
@step Vertical: $20\sin 30^\circ = 10$ N.
@answer The force has components of about $17.3$ N horizontally and $10$ N vertically.
:::

## The resultant of several forces

To add forces, resolve each one in the same two directions, add the components, and put the totals back together.

:::example A resultant force
Two forces act on a particle: $8$ N along the positive $x$-direction and $6$ N along the positive $y$-direction. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.
@step The components are already $X = 8$ and $Y = 6$.
@step $R = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$ N.
@answer The resultant is $10$ N at $\tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{6}{8}\right) \approx 36.9^\circ$ to the $x$-direction.
:::

## Forces in equilibrium

:::definition Equilibrium
A particle is in **equilibrium** when the resultant force on it is zero. Then the components balance in every direction: the forces up equal the forces down, and the forces left equal the forces right.
:::

:::example A lamp on two strings
A lamp of weight $50$ N hangs from two strings, one at $30^\circ$ and one at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal. Find the tensions.
@step Horizontally: $T_1\cos 30^\circ = T_2\cos 60^\circ$, so $T_2 = \sqrt{3}\,T_1$.
@step Vertically: $T_1\sin 30^\circ + T_2\sin 60^\circ = 50$, so $\tfrac{1}{2}T_1 + \tfrac{3}{2}T_1 = 50$.
@answer $T_1 = 25$ N (the string at $30^\circ$) and $T_2 = 25\sqrt{3} \approx 43.3$ N.
:::

:::mistake Sine or cosine?
The component **next to** the angle uses cosine and the one **opposite** it uses sine. If the angle is measured from the vertical, the vertical component is $F\cos\theta$, not $F\sin\theta$. Mark the angle on a diagram before you resolve.
:::

:::summary
- A force $F$ at $\theta$ to a direction has a component $F\cos\theta$ along it and $F\sin\theta$ at right angles to it.
- To find a resultant, resolve every force in two directions at right angles, add, and use $R = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2}$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{Y}{X}$.
- In equilibrium the resultant is zero, so the components balance in each direction.
:::
