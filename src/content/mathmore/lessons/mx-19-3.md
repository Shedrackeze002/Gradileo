Some integrals, such as $\displaystyle\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + x^2}\,dx$, are hard or impossible to find exactly. Splitting the area into thin trapezia gives a good estimate.

## The rule

:::definition The trapezium rule
Split $[a, b]$ into $n$ strips of width $h = \dfrac{b - a}{n}$, with $y$-values $y_0, y_1, \ldots, y_n$. Then
$$\int_a^b y\,dx \approx \frac{1}{2}h\big[y_0 + 2(y_1 + y_2 + \cdots + y_{n-1}) + y_n\big].$$
:::

:::example Four strips
Estimate $\displaystyle\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + x^2}\,dx$ with 4 strips.
@step $h = 0.5$, and the $y$-values at $x = 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2$ are $1,\ 1.1180,\ 1.4142,\ 1.8028,\ 2.2361$.
@step $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.5)\big[1 + 2(1.1180 + 1.4142 + 1.8028) + 2.2361\big] = 0.25 \times 11.9061$
@answer The estimate is $2.98$ to 3 significant figures.
:::

| $x$ | $0$ | $0.5$ | $1$ | $1.5$ | $2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $y$ | $1$ | $1.1180$ | $1.4142$ | $1.8028$ | $2.2361$ |

:::mistake Count the ordinates
$n$ strips have $n + 1$ $y$-values. Only the first and last are counted once; every value in between is doubled.

## Over or under?

When the curve bends upwards (it is **convex**), the straight top of each trapezium lies above the curve, so the rule gives an **overestimate**. When it bends downwards (**concave**), the rule gives an **underestimate**. $y = \sqrt{1 + x^2}$ is convex, and indeed the true value is $2.958$, a little less than $2.98$.

:::tip More strips, better estimate
Halving $h$ makes the trapezia hug the curve more closely, so the estimate improves. The percentage error is $\dfrac{|\text{estimate} - \text{exact}|}{\text{exact}} \times 100$.
:::

:::

:::summary
- $\displaystyle\int_a^b y\,dx \approx \tfrac{1}{2}h\big[y_0 + 2(y_1 + \cdots + y_{n-1}) + y_n\big]$, with $h = \dfrac{b - a}{n}$.
- A convex curve gives an overestimate; a concave curve gives an underestimate.
- More strips give a better estimate.
:::
