A coin lands on heads $15$ times in $20$ tosses. Is it biased, or was that just luck? A hypothesis test answers this by asking how unlikely such a result would be if the coin were fair.

## The language of testing

:::definition Hypothesis test
A **hypothesis test** uses a sample to decide between two statements about a population parameter, such as a proportion $p$:
- the **null hypothesis** $H_0$, which we assume is true, such as $p = 0.5$;
- the **alternative hypothesis** $H_1$, which we accept if there is enough evidence against $H_0$, such as $p > 0.5$.

The **significance level** is the probability of rejecting $H_0$ when it is in fact true; it is the threshold for “unlikely enough”, often $5\%$.
:::

A **one-tailed** test looks for a change in one direction ($H_1$: $p > \ldots$ or $p < \ldots$); a **two-tailed** test looks for a change in either direction ($H_1$: $p \ne \ldots$).

## Carrying out the test

Assume $H_0$ is true, so the count is $X \sim \mathrm{B}(n, p)$ with the value of $p$ from $H_0$. The **p-value** is the probability of a result at least as extreme as the one observed. If it is less than the significance level, the result is **significant**: reject $H_0$.

:::example Is the coin biased towards heads?
A coin lands on heads $15$ times in $20$ tosses. Test at the $5\%$ level whether it is biased towards heads.
@step $H_0$: $p = 0.5$, $H_1$: $p > 0.5$. Under $H_0$, $X \sim \mathrm{B}(20, 0.5)$.
@step $P(X \ge 15) = 1 - P(X \le 14) \approx 0.0207$.
@step $0.0207 < 0.05$, so the result is significant.
@answer Reject $H_0$: there is evidence that the coin is biased towards heads.
:::

The **critical region** is the set of results that would reject $H_0$. Here $P(X \ge 15) \approx 0.0207 \le 0.05$ but $P(X \ge 14) \approx 0.0577 > 0.05$, so the critical region is $X \ge 15$, with **critical value** $15$. The **actual significance level** is the probability of landing in it, $0.0207$.

:::summary
- $H_0$ states the assumed value of $p$; $H_1$ says how it might differ (one-tailed: $>$ or $<$).
- Under $H_0$, the count is $\mathrm{B}(n, p)$; the p-value is $P(\text{a result at least as extreme})$.
- Reject $H_0$ when the p-value is less than the significance level.
- The critical region holds the results that reject $H_0$; its probability is the actual significance level.
:::
