A boat crossing a river, a drone flying across a field, a puck sliding over ice: these move in two dimensions, so their positions and velocities are vectors. Everything we know about straight-line motion still works; we just apply it to the $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ components side by side.

## Vectors for motion

:::definition Position, velocity and acceleration vectors
The **position vector** $\mathbf{r}$ of a particle gives its position from a fixed origin $O$, as $x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}$, where $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are unit vectors along the $x$- and $y$-axes. Its **velocity** $\mathbf{v}$ is the rate of change of $\mathbf{r}$, and its **acceleration** $\mathbf{a}$ is the rate of change of $\mathbf{v}$. The **speed** is the magnitude of the velocity, $|\mathbf{v}|$.
:::

## Constant acceleration with vectors

When the acceleration vector is constant, the suvat formulas hold with vectors:
$$\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t \qquad \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{u}t + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^2$$
where $\mathbf{r}$ is measured from the starting point.

:::example Velocity and position
A particle starts at the origin with velocity $(2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j})$ m/s and has constant acceleration $(\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j})$ m/s². Find its velocity and position after $4$ s.
@step $\mathbf{v} = (2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}) + 4(\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}) = 6\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j}$ m/s.
@step $\mathbf{r} = 4(2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}) + \tfrac{1}{2}(16)(\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}) = 16\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j}$ m.
@answer After $4$ s the velocity is $6\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j}$ m/s (speed $\sqrt{61} \approx 7.81$ m/s) and the position is $16\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j}$ m.
:::

## Variable acceleration and calculus

When the position depends on $t$ in a more complicated way, the acceleration changes, and we use calculus on each component:
$$\mathbf{v} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \qquad \mathbf{a} = \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} \qquad \mathbf{r} = \int \mathbf{v}\,dt \qquad \mathbf{v} = \int \mathbf{a}\,dt$$

:::example Differentiating a position vector
A particle has position $\mathbf{r} = (t^3 - 3t)\mathbf{i} + 2t^2\mathbf{j}$ m at time $t$ s. Find its speed and its acceleration when $t = 2$.
@step $\mathbf{v} = (3t^2 - 3)\mathbf{i} + 4t\mathbf{j}$, so at $t = 2$, $\mathbf{v} = 9\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j}$ and the speed is $\sqrt{145} \approx 12.0$ m/s.
@step $\mathbf{a} = 6t\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$, so at $t = 2$, $\mathbf{a} = 12\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ m/s².
@answer Speed $\approx 12.0$ m/s and acceleration $12\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ m/s².
:::

:::summary
- Position, velocity and acceleration are vectors; work with the $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ components side by side.
- With constant acceleration: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t$ and $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{u}t + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^2$.
- Otherwise differentiate to go from $\mathbf{r}$ to $\mathbf{v}$ to $\mathbf{a}$, and integrate (with a constant vector) to go back.
- Speed is $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}$.
:::
