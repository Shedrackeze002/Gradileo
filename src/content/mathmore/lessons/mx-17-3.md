Writing out $5 + 8 + 11 + \cdots + 62$ is slow; sigma notation says the same thing in a few symbols. And many sequences are easiest to describe by how each term comes from the one before.

## Sigma notation

:::definition Sigma notation
$\displaystyle\sum_{r = 1}^{n} f(r)$ means the sum of $f(r)$ for $r = 1, 2, \ldots, n$. A **recurrence relation**, such as $u_{n + 1} = 2u_n - 1$, defines each term of a sequence from the one before; with a first term $u_1$, it fixes the whole sequence.
:::

Sums of linear terms are arithmetic series, and sums of powers are geometric series:
$$\sum_{r = 1}^{n} (ar + b) = \frac{n}{2}(\text{first} + \text{last}), \qquad \sum_{r = 1}^{n} ck^r = \frac{ck(k^n - 1)}{k - 1}.$$

:::example An arithmetic sum
Find $\displaystyle\sum_{r = 1}^{20} (3r + 2)$.
@step The first term is $5$ and the last is $62$, with $20$ terms.
@answer Sum $= \dfrac{20}{2}(5 + 62) = 670$
:::

## Recurrence relations

:::example Terms from a recurrence
$u_{n + 1} = 2u_n - 1$ and $u_1 = 3$. Find $u_4$.
@step $u_2 = 2 \times 3 - 1 = 5$, $u_3 = 2 \times 5 - 1 = 9$.
@answer $u_4 = 2 \times 9 - 1 = 17$
:::

A sequence is **increasing** if each term is larger than the one before, **decreasing** if each is smaller, and **periodic** if it repeats. For example, $u_{n + 1} = \dfrac{1}{1 - u_n}$ with $u_1 = 2$ gives $2, -1, \tfrac{1}{2}, 2, -1, \ldots$, with period $3$. To find a far-off term of a periodic sequence, divide its position by the period and use the remainder.

:::tip Unknown constants

:::summary
- $\displaystyle\sum_{r = 1}^{n} f(r)$ adds $f(1) + f(2) + \cdots + f(n)$.
- Linear terms give arithmetic series; powers give geometric series.
- A recurrence relation and a first term define a sequence term by term.
- Periodic sequences repeat; use the remainder on dividing by the period.
:::
