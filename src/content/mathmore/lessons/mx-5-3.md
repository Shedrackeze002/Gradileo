A system of two linear equations can be written as one matrix equation, $AX = B$. If we can "divide" by the matrix $A$, the solution follows in one step. The inverse of a matrix does exactly that, and the determinant tells us when it exists.

## The determinant

:::definition Determinant of a 2 × 2 matrix
For $A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$, the **determinant** is
$$\det A = ad - bc$$
:::

:::example A determinant
Find the determinant of $\begin{bmatrix} 4 & -3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$.
@step $ad - bc = 4 \times 5 - (-3) \times 2 = 20 + 6$.
@answer $\det A = 26$
:::

## The inverse matrix

:::definition Identity and inverse
The **identity matrix** $I = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ acts like the number $1$: $AI = IA = A$. The **inverse** $A^{-1}$ satisfies $AA^{-1} = A^{-1}A = I$. When $\det A \ne 0$,
$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc}\begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}$$
When $\det A = 0$, $A$ has no inverse.
:::

So to invert a $2 \times 2$ matrix: swap the two entries on the main diagonal, change the signs of the other two, and divide by the determinant.

## Solving a system with matrices

The system $ax + by = e$, $cx + dy = f$ is the matrix equation
$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} e \\ f \end{bmatrix}, \text{ that is, } AX = B,$$
and multiplying both sides by $A^{-1}$ gives $X = A^{-1}B$.

:::example Solving a system
Solve $2x + y = 7$ and $3x + 2y = 12$ with an inverse matrix.
@step $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, $\det A = 4 - 3 = 1$, so $A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.
@step $X = A^{-1}B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 14 - 12 \\ -21 + 24 \end{bmatrix}$.
@answer $x = 2$ and $y = 3$. (Check: $2(2) + 3 = 7$ and $3(2) + 2(3) = 12$.)
:::

:::mistake Multiplying on the wrong side
$X = A^{-1}B$, not $BA^{-1}$: the inverse goes on the left, because $A$ is on the left of $X$ in $AX = B$. (Here $BA^{-1}$ is not even defined.)
:::

:::summary
- The determinant of $\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ is $ad - bc$.
- If the determinant is not $0$, the inverse is $\dfrac{1}{ad - bc}\begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}$; if it is $0$, there is no inverse.
- A system of two equations is $AX = B$, and its solution is $X = A^{-1}B$.
:::
