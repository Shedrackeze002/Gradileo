Most equations, such as $x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0$ or $e^x + x = 5$, cannot be solved exactly. Numerical methods first trap a root in a small interval, then close in on it.

## Locating roots

:::definition Change of sign
If $f$ is **continuous** on the interval $[a, b]$, and $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have different signs, then $f(x) = 0$ has at least one root between $a$ and $b$.
:::

:::example Trapping a root
Show that $x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0$ has a root between $2$ and $3$.
@step $f(2) = 8 - 4 - 5 = -1$ and $f(3) = 27 - 6 - 5 = 16$.
@step $f$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous.
@answer There is a change of sign and $f$ is continuous, so there is a root between $2$ and $3$.
:::

:::mistake When the method fails
A change of sign only proves a root if $f$ is continuous: $\dfrac{1}{x - 2}$ changes sign between $1$ and $3$ because of an asymptote, not a root. And no change of sign does not mean no root: a repeated root, where the graph touches the axis, gives none.
:::

:::

## The Newton–Raphson method

:::definition Newton–Raphson
Starting from a value $x_0$ near a root of $f(x) = 0$,
$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}.$$
Each step follows the tangent at $x_n$ down to where it meets the $x$-axis.
:::

For $f(x) = x^3 - 2x - 5$, $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2$. With $x_0 = 2$: $x_1 = 2 - \dfrac{-1}{10} = 2.1$, and $x_2 = 2.1 - \dfrac{0.061}{11.23} \approx 2.0946$.

:::tip Use ANS
Type the formula once using your calculator’s ANS key, then press = again and again. The values stop changing when you have the root to the accuracy shown. The method fails if $f'(x_n) = 0$, since the tangent is then horizontal.
:::

:::

:::summary
- If $f$ is continuous and changes sign between $a$ and $b$, there is a root between them.
- Asymptotes and repeated roots can make the change of sign method mislead.
- Newton–Raphson: $x_{n+1} = x_n - \dfrac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$.
:::
