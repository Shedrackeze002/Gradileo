Two samples almost never have exactly the same mean, even when they come from the same population. The t-test tells us whether the difference between two sample means is big enough to show a real difference between the populations.

## The t-test for two means

:::definition Two-sample t-test
The **two-sample $t$-test** tests whether two populations have the same mean, using a sample from each. It assumes that both populations are normally distributed with equal variances. The hypotheses are about the population means $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$:
$$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 \qquad H_1: \mu_1 > \mu_2 \text{ (or } \mu_1 < \mu_2 \text{, or } \mu_1 \ne \mu_2\text{)}$$
:::

$H_1$ is **one-tailed** when the question asks whether one mean is greater (or less) than the other, and **two-tailed** when it asks whether they are different.

## The p-value

A calculator finds the test statistic and its **$p$-value**, the probability of a difference at least as large as the one observed if $H_0$ were true. We reject $H_0$ when the $p$-value is less than the significance level.

:::example Reading a p-value
A test of $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$ against $H_1: \mu_1 > \mu_2$ gives $p = 0.023$. What do we conclude at the $5\%$ level?
@step $0.023 < 0.05$, so the result is significant.
@answer Reject $H_0$: there is evidence that the first mean is greater.
:::

:::mistake What the p-value is not
The $p$-value is not the probability that $H_0$ is true, and not rejecting $H_0$ does not prove that the means are equal. It only means that the evidence is not strong enough.
:::

## The test statistic

With unbiased sample standard deviations $s_1$ and $s_2$, the two samples are combined into a **pooled variance**
$$s_p^2 = \frac{(n_1 - 1)s_1^2 + (n_2 - 1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2}$$
and the test statistic is
$$t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n_1} + \dfrac{1}{n_2}}}$$
with $n_1 + n_2 - 2$ degrees of freedom.

:::example Finding t
$n_1 = 10$, $\bar{x}_1 = 25.0$, $s_1 = 3$ and $n_2 = 10$, $\bar{x}_2 = 22.0$, $s_2 = 4$. Find $t$.
@step $s_p^2 = \dfrac{9 \times 9 + 9 \times 16}{18} = 12.5$, so $s_p \approx 3.536$.
@step $t = \dfrac{25.0 - 22.0}{3.536 \times \sqrt{0.2}} \approx 1.897$
@answer $t \approx 1.897$, with $18$ degrees of freedom
:::

For a one-tailed test with $H_1: \mu_1 > \mu_2$, we reject $H_0$ when $t$ is more than the critical value. In the example, the $5\%$ critical value with $18$ degrees of freedom is $1.734$, and $1.897 > 1.734$, so there is evidence that the first mean is greater.

:::check mx-t-test-statistic 3
:::

:::summary
- $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$; $H_1$ is one-tailed or two-tailed, depending on the question.
- Reject $H_0$ when the $p$-value is less than the significance level, or when $t$ is beyond the critical value.
- $t = \dfrac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}}$ with the pooled variance and $n_1 + n_2 - 2$ degrees of freedom.
:::
