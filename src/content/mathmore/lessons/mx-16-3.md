Scientists often suspect that data follow a power law, $y = ax^n$, or exponential growth, $y = kb^x$. Curves are hard to judge by eye, but taking logarithms turns both models into straight lines, whose gradient and intercept give the constants.

## Straight lines from logarithms

:::definition Logarithmic graphs
Taking logarithms (to base $10$) of the two models gives straight lines:
- $y = ax^n$ becomes $\log y = n\log x + \log a$: plot $\log y$ against $\log x$; the gradient is $n$ and the intercept is $\log a$.
- $y = kb^x$ becomes $\log y = x\log b + \log k$: plot $\log y$ against $x$; the gradient is $\log b$ and the intercept is $\log k$.
:::

If the points lie close to a straight line, the model fits.

:::example A power law
The graph of $\log y$ against $\log x$ is a straight line with gradient $1.5$ and intercept $0.6$. Find $a$ and $n$ in $y = ax^n$.
@step $\log y = n\log x + \log a$, so $n = 1.5$.
@step $\log a = 0.6$, so $a = 10^{0.6}$.
@answer $n = 1.5$ and $a \approx 3.98$, so $y \approx 3.98x^{1.5}$.
:::

:::mistake Undo the logarithm
The intercept is $\log a$, not $a$. If the intercept is $0.6$, then $a = 10^{0.6} \approx 3.98$, not $0.6$.
:::

## Exponential models

:::example From two data points
A quantity follows $y = kb^x$. When $x = 1$, $y = 6$, and when $x = 3$, $y = 54$. Find $k$ and $b$.
@step Dividing: $\dfrac{kb^3}{kb} = b^2 = \dfrac{54}{6} = 9$, so $b = 3$.
@step $k = \dfrac{6}{3} = 2$.
@answer $y = 2 \times 3^x$.
:::

:::check mx-log-exp 2
:::

:::tip Which axes?
A power law needs logs on **both** axes. An exponential model needs a log only on the $y$-axis, because $x$ is already an exponent.
:::

:::

:::summary
- $y = ax^n$: plot $\log y$ against $\log x$; gradient $n$, intercept $\log a$.
- $y = kb^x$: plot $\log y$ against $x$; gradient $\log b$, intercept $\log k$.
- Undo each log with $10^{\ldots}$ to find $a$, $k$ or $b$.
:::
