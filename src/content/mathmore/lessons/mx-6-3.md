Slice a double cone (two cones joined tip to tip) with a flat plane and four curves can appear: a circle, an ellipse, a parabola or a hyperbola. These are the **conic sections**. They describe the orbits of planets, the shape of satellite dishes and the paths of comets.

## Recognizing conic sections

:::definition The four conics (centred at the origin)
- **Circle:** $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$. Both squares, added, with the same coefficient.
- **Ellipse:** $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$. Both squares, added, with different denominators.
- **Hyperbola:** $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ or $\dfrac{y^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{x^2}{b^2} = 1$. Both squares, one subtracted.
- **Parabola:** $y = ax^2$ or $x = ay^2$. Only one variable squared.
:::

Replacing $x$ by $x - h$ and $y$ by $y - k$ moves the centre (or the vertex of a parabola) to $(h, k)$, without changing the shape.

:::example Naming the conic
Name the conic $\dfrac{(x - 2)^2}{9} - \dfrac{(y + 1)^2}{16} = 1$ and give its centre.
@step Both $x$ and $y$ are squared, and one square is subtracted: a hyperbola.
@step $x - 2$ and $y + 1 = y - (-1)$ give $h = 2$ and $k = -1$.
@answer A hyperbola with centre $(2, -1)$
:::

## Parabolas: focus and directrix

:::definition Focus and directrix
A **parabola** is every point the same distance from a fixed point, the **focus**, and a fixed line, the **directrix**. The parabola $x^2 = 4py$ has vertex $(0, 0)$, focus $(0, p)$ and directrix $y = -p$. The parabola $y^2 = 4px$ has focus $(p, 0)$ and directrix $x = -p$.
:::

:::example Focus and directrix
Find the focus and directrix of $x^2 = 12y$.
@step $4p = 12$, so $p = 3$.
@answer The focus is $(0, 3)$ and the directrix is $y = -3$. The parabola opens upward, towards the focus.
:::

## Ellipses: axes and foci

:::definition Axes and foci of an ellipse
In $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ with $a > b$, the **major axis** is horizontal with length $2a$, and the **minor axis** has length $2b$. The two **foci** lie on the major axis, $c$ from the centre, where $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$. (If the larger denominator is under $y^2$, the major axis is vertical.)
:::

:::example The foci of an ellipse
Find the axes and foci of $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.
@step $a^2 = 25$ and $b^2 = 9$, so $a = 5$ and $b = 3$. The major axis is horizontal, length $10$; the minor axis has length $6$.
@step $c^2 = 25 - 9 = 16$, so $c = 4$.
@answer The foci are $(-4, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$.
:::

:::mistake Adding in the ellipse
For an ellipse, $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ (subtract). For a hyperbola it is $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ (add), because a hyperbola's foci lie outside its vertices.
:::

:::summary
- Circle: same squares added. Ellipse: different squares added. Hyperbola: one square subtracted. Parabola: one variable squared.
- $x - h$ and $y - k$ move the centre or vertex to $(h, k)$.
- $x^2 = 4py$ has focus $(0, p)$ and directrix $y = -p$.
- An ellipse has major axis $2a$, minor axis $2b$ and foci $c$ from the centre, with $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$.
:::
