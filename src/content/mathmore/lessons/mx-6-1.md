A ship's radar does not say "3 km east and 4 km north"; it says "5 km away, on this bearing". Describing a point by its distance and angle from a fixed point gives **polar coordinates**, which suit anything that turns or spreads out from a centre.

## Polar coordinates

:::definition Polar coordinates
The **polar coordinates** $(r, \theta)$ of a point give its distance $r$ from the origin (the **pole**) and the angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis (the **polar axis**), measured anticlockwise.
:::

## Converting between the systems

:::definition Conversion formulas
From polar to rectangular: $x = r\cos\theta$ and $y = r\sin\theta$.
From rectangular to polar: $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{y}{x}$, with $\theta$ chosen in the quadrant of the point.
:::

:::example Polar to rectangular
Convert $(6, 150^\circ)$ to rectangular coordinates.
@step $x = 6\cos 150^\circ = 6 \times \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) \approx -5.2$
@step $y = 6\sin 150^\circ = 6 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 3$
@answer About $(-5.2, 3)$
:::

:::example Rectangular to polar
Convert $(-2, -5)$ to polar coordinates with $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
@step $r = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{29} \approx 5.4$
@step The point is in quadrant III. The reference angle is $\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right) \approx 68.2^\circ$.
@answer $\theta \approx 180^\circ + 68.2^\circ = 248.2^\circ$, so the point is about $(5.4, 248.2^\circ)$.
:::

:::mistake Taking the calculator's angle as it is
For $(-2, -5)$, $\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-5}{-2}\right) = \tan^{-1}(2.5) \approx 68.2^\circ$, which points into quadrant I. The point is in quadrant III, so add $180^\circ$.
:::

:::tip Many names for one point
In polar coordinates one point has many names: $(5, 30^\circ)$, $(5, 390^\circ)$ and $(5, -330^\circ)$ are the same point. That is why questions often ask for $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
:::
