A basketball player takes $10$ free throws, scoring each with probability $0.3$. The number she scores could be anything from $0$ to $10$, and the binomial distribution gives the probability of each.

## The binomial distribution

:::definition Binomial distribution
A random variable $X$ has a **binomial distribution**, $X \sim \mathrm{B}(n, p)$, when it counts the successes in $n$ trials where:
- there is a fixed number $n$ of trials;
- each trial has two outcomes, success or failure;
- the probability of success $p$ is the same for every trial;
- the trials are independent.

Then
$$P(X = r) = \binom{n}{r} p^r (1 - p)^{n - r}, \qquad r = 0, 1, \ldots, n.$$
:::

The $\binom{n}{r}$ counts the ways of placing $r$ successes among $n$ trials, and $p^r(1 - p)^{n - r}$ is the probability of any one of those ways.

:::example An exact probability
$X \sim \mathrm{B}(10, 0.3)$. Find $P(X = 3)$.
@step $P(X = 3) = \dbinom{10}{3}(0.3)^3(0.7)^7$
@step $= 120 \times 0.027 \times 0.08235\ldots$
@answer $P(X = 3) \approx 0.2668$
:::

:::mistake Check the conditions
The binomial model needs independent trials with a fixed probability. Drawing cards from a small pack **without replacement** changes the probability each time, so it is not binomial.

## Cumulative probabilities

**Cumulative** probabilities such as $P(X \le r)$ add the probabilities from $0$ up to $r$. Calculators find them directly (often called “Binomial CD”). Other forms are turned into $P(X \le \ldots)$:
- $P(X \ge r) = 1 - P(X \le r - 1)$;
- $P(X > r) = 1 - P(X \le r)$;
- $P(a \le X \le b) = P(X \le b) - P(X \le a - 1)$.

:::example At least three
$X \sim \mathrm{B}(10, 0.3)$. Find $P(X \ge 3)$.
@step $P(X \ge 3) = 1 - P(X \le 2)$.
@step $P(X \le 2) = 0.0282 + 0.1211 + 0.2335 \approx 0.3828$.
@answer $P(X \ge 3) \approx 0.6172$
:::

:::tip Write the whole numbers out
For a range such as $P(3 < X \le 7)$, first list which whole numbers are included ($4, 5, 6, 7$), then write it as $P(X \le 7) - P(X \le 3)$.
:::

:::summary
- $X \sim \mathrm{B}(n, p)$ counts successes in $n$ independent trials, each with success probability $p$.
- $P(X = r) = \dbinom{n}{r}p^r(1 - p)^{n - r}$.
- $P(X \ge r) = 1 - P(X \le r - 1)$ and $P(X > r) = 1 - P(X \le r)$.
- “At least one” is $1 - P(X = 0)$.
:::
