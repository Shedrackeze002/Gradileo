A ship's navigator and a phone's map both describe a place on the Earth with two angles. With those two angles and the radius of the Earth, we can find how far apart two places are.

## Latitude and longitude

:::definition Latitude and longitude
The **latitude** of a place is its angle north or south of the equator, measured at the centre of the Earth, from $0°$ at the equator to $90°$ at the poles. The **longitude** of a place is its angle east or west of the Greenwich meridian, from $0°$ to $180°$. A position is written with the latitude first, for example ($40°$N, $75°$W).
:::

All the places with the same latitude make a circle called a **parallel of latitude**. All the places with the same longitude make half a circle from pole to pole, called a **meridian**.

:::definition Great circle and small circle
A **great circle** is a circle on a sphere whose centre is the centre of the sphere, so its radius is the radius $R$ of the sphere. The equator and every meridian lie on great circles. Any other circle on the sphere is a **small circle**. Every parallel of latitude except the equator is a small circle.
:::

In this lesson we take the radius of the Earth as $R = 6400$ km.

## Distances along a meridian or the equator

Two places on the same meridian are separated by an angle at the centre of the Earth. If both are on the same side of the equator, the angle is the difference of their latitudes. If one is north and one is south, it is the sum. The distance is that fraction of the circumference of a great circle:

$$\text{distance} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi R$$

:::example Same side of the equator
Towns $A$ ($50°$N, $20°$E) and $B$ ($15°$N, $20°$E) lie on the same meridian. Find the distance between them along the meridian, to the nearest kilometre.
@step Angle $= 50° - 15° = 35°$
@step Distance $= \dfrac{35}{360} \times 2\pi \times 6400$
@answer $3910$ km
:::

:::example Opposite sides of the equator
Towns $C$ ($25°$N, $30°$W) and $D$ ($10°$S, $30°$W) lie on the same meridian. Find the distance between them.
@step Angle $= 25° + 10° = 35°$, since they are on opposite sides of the equator.
@step Distance $= \dfrac{35}{360} \times 2\pi \times 6400$
@answer $3910$ km
:::

The equator is also a great circle. For two points on the equator, the angle is the difference of their longitudes when both are east or both are west, and the sum when one is east and the other west.

:::check mx-great-circle 2
:::

Sailors measure distance in **nautical miles**. One nautical mile is the length of one minute of arc ($\frac{1}{60}$ of a degree) of a great circle, so $1°$ of a great circle is $60$ nautical miles.

## Distances along a parallel of latitude

A parallel of latitude is a smaller circle. In the figure, the radius $r$ of the parallel at latitude $\theta$ is one side of a right-angled triangle whose hypotenuse is $R$, so

$$r = R\cos\theta$$

:::example The radius of a parallel
Find the radius of the parallel of latitude $60°$N.
@step $r = R\cos\theta = 6400 \times \cos 60°$
@answer $3200$ km
:::

:::check mx-parallel-latitude 1
:::

For two places on the same parallel, the angle is the difference (or sum) of their longitudes, and the distance is that fraction of the circumference of the parallel:

$$\text{distance} = \frac{\alpha}{360} \times 2\pi R\cos\theta$$

:::example Along a parallel
Towns $P$ ($40°$N, $10°$W) and $Q$ ($40°$N, $50°$E) lie on the same parallel. Find the distance between them along the parallel, to the nearest kilometre.
@step Angle $= 10° + 50° = 60°$, since one is west and the other east.
@step Distance $= \dfrac{60}{360} \times 2\pi \times 6400 \times \cos 40°$
@answer $5134$ km
:::

:::mistake Do not forget the cosine
Along a parallel of latitude the circle is smaller than the equator, so the distance must be multiplied by $\cos\theta$. Leaving it out gives the distance along the equator instead, which is too long.
:::

:::

## Longitude and time

The Earth turns through $360°$ in $24$ hours, which is $15°$ each hour. So every $15°$ of longitude makes a difference of one hour in local time, and places to the east are ahead.

:::example A time difference
Town $X$ is at $30°$W and town $Y$ is at $45°$E. How many hours ahead is the local time at $Y$?
@step Angle $= 30° + 45° = 75°$
@step $75 \div 15 = 5$
@answer $5$ hours
:::

:::check mx-parallel-latitude 3
:::

:::summary
- Latitude is the angle north or south of the equator, and longitude is the angle east or west of Greenwich.
- Along a meridian or the equator (great circles): distance $= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi R$.
- A parallel of latitude $\theta$ has radius $R\cos\theta$, so distance along it $= \dfrac{\alpha}{360} \times 2\pi R\cos\theta$.
- $1°$ of a great circle is $60$ nautical miles, and $15°$ of longitude is $1$ hour of local time.
:::
