Dividing $1$ by sine, cosine and tangent gives three more functions, and undoing sine, cosine and tangent gives three more again. Each has its own graph, and the inverse functions only make sense on carefully chosen ranges.

## Sec, cosec and cot

:::definition Reciprocal trigonometric functions
$$\sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \operatorname{cosec}\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{1}{\tan\theta} = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}.$$
Each is undefined where the function underneath it is $0$; their graphs have asymptotes there. Dividing $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ by $\cos^2\theta$ gives $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$, and by $\sin^2\theta$ gives $1 + \cot^2\theta = \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$.
:::

:::example Solving with sec
Solve $\sec x = 2.5$ for $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$.
@step $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2.5} = 0.4$.
@step $\cos^{-1} 0.4 \approx 66.4^\circ$, and cosine is also positive in the fourth quadrant: $360^\circ - 66.4^\circ$.
@answer $x \approx 66.4^\circ$ or $x \approx 293.6^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake sec is not cos⁻¹
$\sec x = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$, but $\cos^{-1} x$ is the inverse function, $\arccos x$. They are different: $\sec 60^\circ = 2$, while $\cos^{-1} 0.5 = 60^\circ$.
:::

## Arcsin, arccos and arctan

The inverse functions undo sine, cosine and tangent. Since many angles share a sine, each inverse gives the one angle in a chosen **range**:
- $\arcsin x$: $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \le y \le \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, for $-1 \le x \le 1$;
- $\arccos x$: $0 \le y \le \pi$, for $-1 \le x \le 1$;
- $\arctan x$: $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < y < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, for every real $x$.

For example, $\arccos\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, since that is the angle between $0$ and $\pi$ whose cosine is $-\tfrac{1}{2}$.

:::summary
- $\sec\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta}$, $\operatorname{cosec}\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$, $\cot\theta = \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}$.
- $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$ and $1 + \cot^2\theta = \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$.
- To solve $\sec x = k$, solve $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{k}$.
- $\arcsin$ and $\arctan$ give angles from $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$; $\arccos$ gives angles from $0$ to $\pi$.
:::
