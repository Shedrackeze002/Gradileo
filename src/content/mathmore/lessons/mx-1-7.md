The mean and the median each squeeze a data set into one number. The **shape** of the data tells us more: where the values pile up, whether they trail off to one side, and whether one number can describe them fairly at all.

## Distributions and histograms

:::definition Distribution
The **distribution** of a data set is the way its values are spread out: which values occur, and how often each one occurs. We see a distribution by graphing it, with a dot plot for a small set of whole-number data or a histogram for a larger set of measurements.
:::

:::definition Histogram
A **histogram** groups numerical data into equal **intervals** along the horizontal axis and draws a bar over each interval. The height of a bar is the **frequency**: how many values fall in that interval. The bars touch, because the intervals cover the number line with no gaps.
:::

:::example Reading a histogram
A histogram of the masses of some parcels has bars over $0$ to $2$ kg, $2$ to $4$ kg, $4$ to $6$ kg and $6$ to $8$ kg, with heights $3$, $7$, $5$ and $1$. How many parcels are there, and how many have a mass of less than $4$ kg?
@step Altogether: $3 + 7 + 5 + 1 = 16$
@step Less than $4$ kg means the first two bars: $3 + 7 = 10$
@answer There are $16$ parcels, and $10$ of them have a mass of less than $4$ kg.
:::

:::check mx-histogram-frequencies 1
:::

:::tip Values on a boundary
A value that sits exactly on the edge of two bars has to go in one of them. Unless we are told otherwise, each bar includes its left end but not its right end, so a parcel of exactly $4$ kg is counted in the bar from $4$ to $6$.
:::

## The shape of a distribution

:::definition Symmetric and skewed
A distribution is **symmetric** when its left half and its right half are close to mirror images. It is **skewed** when one side has a long **tail** of values that stretch far from the peak. A tail on the right makes the distribution **skewed to the right**, and a tail on the left makes it **skewed to the left**. A distribution is **uniform** when every value or interval occurs about equally often, so there is no peak.
:::

Here we see a dot plot that is skewed to the right. Most of the values are $1$, $2$ or $3$, and a few larger values form a tail on the right.

:::mistake Naming the skew for the peak
A skewed distribution is named for the side its **tail** is on, not the side its peak is on. When the bars are tallest on the left and trail away to the right, the distribution is skewed to the right.
:::

:::definition Mode of a distribution
A **mode** of a distribution is a peak: a value or interval that is more common than its neighbours. A distribution with one peak is **unimodal**, and one with two separate peaks is **bimodal**. Two peaks often mean that two different groups have been mixed together, such as the heights of children and adults.
:::

## Shape, mean and median

The tail of a skewed distribution pulls the mean towards it, because the mean uses the size of every value. The median only counts positions, so it stays near the peak.

:::example Comparing the mean and the median
The data $2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 5, 20$ have a long tail to the right. Compare the mean and the median.
@step Median: the middle two values are $4$ and $4$, so the median is $4$.
@step Mean: $\dfrac{2 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 20}{8} = \dfrac{45}{8} \approx 5.6$
@answer The mean is greater than the median, because the value $20$ in the tail pulls the mean up.
:::

So in a symmetric distribution the mean and the median are about equal, in a distribution skewed to the right the mean is greater than the median, and in a distribution skewed to the left the mean is less than the median. This is why the median is the better measure of a typical value when data are skewed.

:::check mx-distribution-shape 3
:::

:::summary
- A distribution shows which values occur and how often. A histogram shows it with bars over equal intervals.
- Add bar heights to count the values in several intervals.
- Symmetric: the two halves mirror each other. Skewed: one long tail, and the skew is named for the tail's side. Uniform: no peak.
- Unimodal means one peak and bimodal means two.
- A tail pulls the mean towards it, so skewed right means mean $>$ median and skewed left means mean $<$ median.
:::
