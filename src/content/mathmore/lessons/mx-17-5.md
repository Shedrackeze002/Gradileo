A drone that flies $2$ m east, $3$ m south and $6$ m up has moved in three dimensions. Vectors handle this just as they do in two: a third unit vector, $\mathbf{k}$, points up out of the page.

## Vectors in 3D

:::definition Three-dimensional vectors
In three dimensions, $\mathbf{i}$, $\mathbf{j}$ and $\mathbf{k}$ are unit vectors along the $x$-, $y$- and $z$-axes. The vector $x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}$ has **magnitude**
$$|x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2},$$
and the point $(x, y, z)$ has **position vector** $x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}$.
:::

:::example Magnitude and distance
Find $|2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}|$, and the distance from $A(1, 2, -1)$ to $B(3, -1, 5)$.
@step $\sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{49} = 7$
@step $\overrightarrow{AB} = (3 - 1)\mathbf{i} + (-1 - 2)\mathbf{j} + (5 - (-1))\mathbf{k} = 2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}$
@answer The magnitude is $7$, and so $AB = 7$ too.
:::

The angle $\theta$ between a vector and the $x$-axis has $\cos\theta = \dfrac{x}{|\mathbf{v}|}$, and likewise for the other axes. For $2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}$, $\cos\theta = \tfrac{2}{7}$ gives $\theta \approx 73.4^\circ$.

## Geometry with 3D vectors

The rules from two dimensions carry over: $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$, the midpoint of $AB$ is $\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$, and in a parallelogram $ABCD$, $\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{BC}$.

:::tip Work one coordinate at a time
Every vector rule works separately on the $x$, $y$ and $z$ coordinates, so set your working out in three columns.
:::

:::

:::summary
- $|x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$.
- $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$, and the distance $AB$ is its magnitude.
- The angle with the $x$-axis has $\cos\theta = \dfrac{x}{|\mathbf{v}|}$.
- Midpoints and parallelograms work as in two dimensions, one coordinate at a time.
:::
