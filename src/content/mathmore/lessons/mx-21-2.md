Zooming in on a photo makes everything larger without changing its shape. In geometry this transformation is called a dilation, and on the Cartesian plane it comes down to a multiplication.

## What a dilation is

:::definition Dilation, centre and scale factor
A **dilation** is a transformation that enlarges or reduces a figure without changing its shape. Every point moves along the straight line that joins it to a fixed point, the **centre of dilation**. The **scale factor**, $k$, tells us how far: the image of each point is $k$ times as far from the centre as the original point was.
:::

A scale factor greater than $1$ gives an **enlargement**, a scale factor between $0$ and $1$ gives a **reduction**, and a scale factor of $1$ leaves the figure unchanged. We label the image of a point $A$ as $A'$, read "A prime".

## Dilations centred at the origin

When the centre is the origin, $(0, 0)$, the rule is short: multiply both coordinates by the scale factor.
$$(x, y) \rightarrow (kx, ky)$$

:::example Dilating a point
Point $A(3, -2)$ is dilated with centre $(0, 0)$ and scale factor $4$. Find $A'$.
@step Multiply each coordinate by $4$: $3 \times 4 = 12$ and $-2 \times 4 = -8$
@answer $A'(12, -8)$
:::

## A centre that is not the origin

For any other centre, we work with the movement from the centre to the point.

:::example Dilating about another centre
Point $P(5, 4)$ is dilated with centre $C(1, 2)$ and scale factor $3$. Find $P'$.
@step From $C$ to $P$: right $5 - 1 = 4$ and up $4 - 2 = 2$
@step Multiply the movement by $3$: right $12$ and up $6$
@step Start again from $C$: $(1 + 12,\ 2 + 6)$
@answer $P'(13, 8)$
:::

:::mistake Multiplying the coordinates when the centre is not the origin
The rule $(x, y) \rightarrow (kx, ky)$ only works for the centre $(0, 0)$. For another centre, multiply the distances from the centre, not the coordinates themselves.
:::

## Finding the scale factor, and what a dilation changes

To find the scale factor of a dilation centred at the origin, divide a coordinate of the image by the matching coordinate of the original point. If $P(2, 5)$ maps to $P'(6, 15)$, then $k = \dfrac{6}{2} = 3$.

:::

A dilation keeps every angle the same, so the image is **similar** to the original. Every length, including the perimeter, is multiplied by $k$. The area is multiplied by $k^2$, because an area is built from two lengths multiplied together.

:::example Lengths and areas
A rectangle is $6$ units by $4$ units. It is dilated with scale factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Find the perimeter and the area of the image.
@step Original perimeter: $2(6 + 4) = 20$. Image perimeter: $20 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 10$
@step Original area: $6 \times 4 = 24$. Image area: $24 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 24 \times \dfrac{1}{4} = 6$
@answer The image has a perimeter of $10$ units and an area of $6$ square units.
:::

:::check mx-dilation-scale-factor 2
:::

:::tip Working backwards
A dilation is undone by dividing. If the image of $P$ is $P'(8, -12)$ after a dilation with centre $(0, 0)$ and scale factor $4$, then $P$ is $(8 \div 4,\ -12 \div 4) = (2, -3)$.
:::

:::check mx-dilation-scale-factor 3
:::

:::summary
- A dilation enlarges ($k > 1$) or reduces ($0 < k < 1$) a figure from a centre, keeping its shape.
- With centre $(0, 0)$: $(x, y) \rightarrow (kx, ky)$.
- With another centre: multiply the movement from the centre by $k$, then add it to the centre.
- Scale factor $=$ image length $\div$ original length.
- Angles stay the same, lengths and perimeters are multiplied by $k$, and areas are multiplied by $k^2$.
:::
