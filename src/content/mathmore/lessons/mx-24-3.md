A carpenter has limited time and limited wood, and earns a different profit on each chair and each table. How many of each should the carpenter make to earn the most? Problems like this, with limits and something to make as large as possible, are solved by linear programming.

## Constraints and the feasible region

:::definition Linear programming
**Linear programming** is a method for finding the greatest or least value of a linear expression, the **objective**, when the variables must satisfy a set of linear inequalities, the **constraints**. The points that satisfy every constraint form the **feasible region**. We use it to make the best use of limited resources such as time, money and materials.
:::

We first write each limit as an inequality. The words tell us which sign to use.

| Words | Sign |
| --- | --- |
| at most, no more than, up to | $\le$ |
| at least, no fewer than | $\ge$ |
| less than, fewer than | $<$ |
| more than | $>$ |

:::example Writing a constraint
Amara buys $x$ pens at \$3 each and $y$ notebooks at \$5 each, and can spend at most \$60. Write this as an inequality.
@step The pens cost $3x$ dollars and the notebooks cost $5y$ dollars.
@step The total is at most $60$.
@answer $3x + 5y \le 60$
:::

Because we cannot buy a negative number of things, we also have $x \ge 0$ and $y \ge 0$.

:::

A point is in the feasible region only if it satisfies every constraint. For $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$, $x \le 6$, $y \le 5$ and $x + y \le 8$, the point $(4, 3)$ is feasible, because $4 \le 6$, $3 \le 5$ and $4 + 3 = 7 \le 8$. The point $(6, 5)$ is not, because $6 + 5 = 11$ is more than $8$.

:::

## The best value is at a corner

On a graph, each constraint is a half-plane with a straight boundary line, so the feasible region is a polygon. The objective, such as $P = 3x + 2y$, is the same along each line of a family of parallel lines. As we slide the line to increase $P$, the last point of the region it touches is a corner.

:::tip The corner rule
The greatest and least values of a linear objective over a feasible region are found at corners of the region. So we find the corners, work out the objective at each one, and compare.
:::

:::example Testing the corners
The corners of a feasible region are $(0, 0)$, $(6, 0)$, $(6, 2)$, $(3, 5)$ and $(0, 5)$. Find the greatest value of $P = 3x + 2y$.
@step $(0, 0)$: $P = 0$. $(6, 0)$: $P = 18$. $(6, 2)$: $P = 22$. $(3, 5)$: $P = 19$. $(0, 5)$: $P = 10$.
@answer The greatest value is $P = 22$, at $(6, 2)$.
:::

:::check mx-lp-optimize 1
:::

To find the corners ourselves, we find where the boundary lines meet. For the region above, $x + y = 8$ meets $x = 6$ at $(6, 2)$, and meets $y = 5$ at $(3, 5)$.

:::mistake Not every crossing is a corner
The lines $x = 6$ and $y = 5$ cross at $(6, 5)$, but that point breaks $x + y \le 8$, so it is outside the region. A corner must satisfy all the constraints.
:::

:::

## A full problem

For a problem in words we follow four steps: name the variables, write the constraints, write the objective, and test the corners.

:::check mx-lp-optimize 3
:::

:::summary
- Constraints are the inequalities that the variables must satisfy; together they give the feasible region.
- "At most" is $\le$ and "at least" is $\ge$.
- The objective is the linear expression we make as large or as small as possible.
- The best value is at a corner of the feasible region, so we test every corner.
:::
