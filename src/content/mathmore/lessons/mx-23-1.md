Two banks both advertise "12% per year". One adds interest once a year and the other adds it every month. They do not pay the same, and this lesson shows how to compare them fairly.

## Two kinds of rate

:::definition Nominal and effective interest rates
The **nominal rate** is the yearly rate that is quoted, before taking account of how often interest is added. The **effective rate** is the rate that would give the same growth if interest were added just once a year. When interest is added more than once a year, the effective rate is higher than the nominal rate.
:::

Suppose the nominal rate is $r$ (as a decimal) and interest is added $m$ times a year. Each time, the rate is $\dfrac{r}{m}$. After one year, 1 dollar has grown to $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^m$. If the effective rate is $i$, the same dollar grows to $1 + i$. So

$$1 + i = \left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^m$$

Interest added half-yearly has $m = 2$, quarterly has $m = 4$, monthly has $m = 12$ and daily has $m = 365$.

:::example Quarterly compounding
An account pays a nominal rate of $8\%$ per year, compounded quarterly. Find the effective rate.
@step $r = 0.08$ and $m = 4$, so the rate each quarter is $\dfrac{0.08}{4} = 0.02$.
@step $1 + i = 1.02^4 \approx 1.082432$
@step $i \approx 0.082432$
@answer $8.24\%$
:::

:::example Monthly compounding
A nominal rate of $12\%$ per year is compounded monthly. Find the effective rate.
@step The rate each month is $\dfrac{0.12}{12} = 0.01$.
@step $1 + i = 1.01^{12} \approx 1.126825$
@answer $12.68\%$
:::

So the bank that adds interest monthly really pays $12.68\%$, and the bank that adds it once a year pays exactly $12\%$.

:::mistake Compare effective rates, not nominal rates
A higher nominal rate is not always the better offer. A rate of $10\%$ compounded monthly has an effective rate of $10.47\%$, which beats $10.25\%$ compounded once a year. Always change both offers to effective rates before comparing them.
:::

## From effective back to nominal

Sometimes we know the effective rate and need the nominal rate. We use the same formula and solve for $r$: take the $m$th root, subtract 1, and multiply by $m$.

:::example Finding the nominal rate
An investment has an effective rate of $10\%$, with interest compounded quarterly. Find the nominal rate.
@step $1.1 = \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{4}\right)^4$
@step $1 + \dfrac{r}{4} = 1.1^{1/4} \approx 1.024114$
@step $r \approx 4 \times 0.024114 = 0.096455$
@answer $9.65\%$
:::

:::tip A quick check
The nominal rate must come out a little smaller than the effective rate. If it comes out larger, the root or the multiplication has gone wrong.
:::

## Nominal rates in compound interest

A nominal rate tells us the rate for each period. For an amount $P$ invested for $t$ years at a nominal rate $r$ compounded $m$ times a year,

$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{mt}$$

Here $\dfrac{r}{m}$ is the rate for one period and $mt$ is the number of periods.

:::example Monthly growth
\$2,000 is invested for 3 years at a nominal rate of $6\%$ per year, compounded monthly. Find the final amount.
@step The monthly rate is $\dfrac{0.06}{12} = 0.005$, and there are $12 \times 3 = 36$ months.
@step $A = 2000 \times 1.005^{36} \approx 2393.36$
@answer \$2,393.36
:::

:::summary
- The nominal rate is the quoted yearly rate. The effective rate is the yearly rate that gives the same growth with interest added once a year.
- $1 + i = \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^m$, where interest is added $m$ times a year.
- To compare offers, change each one to its effective rate.
- $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{m}\right)^{mt}$ for $t$ years at a nominal rate $r$.
:::
