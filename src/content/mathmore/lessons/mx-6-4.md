A graph of $y$ against $x$ shows the path of a ball but not when the ball was at each point. Writing $x$ and $y$ each in terms of time $t$ shows both: where the ball is, and when.

## Parametric equations

:::definition Parametric equations
**Parametric equations** give $x$ and $y$ each as a function of a third variable, the **parameter**, often $t$ for time: $x = f(t)$ and $y = g(t)$. As $t$ changes, the point $(x, y)$ traces a curve.
:::

:::example Finding a point
A point moves with $x = 2t + 1$ and $y = 3 - t$. Where is it when $t = 4$?
@step $x = 2(4) + 1 = 9$
@step $y = 3 - 4 = -1$
@answer At $t = 4$ the point is at $(9, -1)$.
:::

## Eliminating the parameter

To find the ordinary equation of the curve, **eliminate the parameter**: solve one equation for $t$ and substitute into the other.

:::example A line
Eliminate the parameter from $x = 2t + 1$, $y = 3 - t$.
@step From the first equation, $t = \dfrac{x - 1}{2}$.
@step Substitute: $y = 3 - \dfrac{x - 1}{2} = 3 - \dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}$.
@answer $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{7}{2}$, a line.
:::

## Circles

The equations $x = r\cos t$ and $y = r\sin t$ trace a circle of radius $r$, because $\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1$ gives $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$. Adding $h$ and $k$ moves the centre to $(h, k)$.

:::example A circle
Find the curve traced by $x = 3\cos t + 2$, $y = 3\sin t - 1$ for $0 \le t < 2\pi$.
@step $\cos t = \dfrac{x - 2}{3}$ and $\sin t = \dfrac{y + 1}{3}$.
@step $\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1$ gives $\dfrac{(x - 2)^2}{9} + \dfrac{(y + 1)^2}{9} = 1$.
@answer $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 9$: a circle with centre $(2, -1)$ and radius $3$.
:::

:::mistake Losing the direction and the timing
Eliminating the parameter gives the shape of the path but loses when and in which direction it is traced. The parametric equations keep both: as $t$ increases, $x = 3\cos t + 2$, $y = 3\sin t - 1$ goes round anticlockwise.
:::

:::check mx-parametric 3
:::

:::summary
- Parametric equations give $x$ and $y$ in terms of a parameter $t$; substitute a value of $t$ to find the point.
- Eliminate the parameter by solving one equation for $t$ and substituting into the other.
- $x = r\cos t + h$, $y = r\sin t + k$ is a circle with centre $(h, k)$ and radius $r$, by $\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1$.
:::
