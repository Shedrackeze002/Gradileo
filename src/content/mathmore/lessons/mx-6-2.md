A complex number $a + bi$ can be drawn as the point $(a, b)$. Describing that point by its distance and angle from the origin, as in polar coordinates, gives the polar form of the number, and multiplying complex numbers becomes as simple as multiplying lengths and adding angles.

## The complex plane

:::definition The complex plane
In the **complex plane**, the number $a + bi$ is the point $(a, b)$: the horizontal axis is the **real axis** and the vertical axis is the **imaginary axis**.
:::

:::definition Modulus, argument and polar form
The **modulus** of $z = a + bi$ is its distance from $0$, $r = |z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. Its **argument** $\theta$ is the angle from the positive real axis. Then $a = r\cos\theta$ and $b = r\sin\theta$, so
$$z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$$
This is the **polar form** (or trigonometric form) of $z$.
:::

:::example Writing a number in polar form
Write $z = -1 + i$ in polar form.
@step $r = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4$
@step The point $(-1, 1)$ is in quadrant II, with reference angle $45^\circ$, so $\theta = 135^\circ$.
@answer $z = \sqrt{2}(\cos 135^\circ + i\sin 135^\circ)$
:::

## Multiplying and dividing in polar form

:::definition Products and quotients
For $z = r_1(\cos\theta_1 + i\sin\theta_1)$ and $w = r_2(\cos\theta_2 + i\sin\theta_2)$:
$$zw = r_1 r_2\big(\cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2) + i\sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)\big)$$
$$\frac{z}{w} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\big(\cos(\theta_1 - \theta_2) + i\sin(\theta_1 - \theta_2)\big)$$
Multiply the moduli and add the arguments; divide the moduli and subtract the arguments.
:::

:::example Multiplying in polar form
Let $z = 3(\cos 40^\circ + i\sin 40^\circ)$ and $w = 2(\cos 110^\circ + i\sin 110^\circ)$. Find $zw$.
@step Multiply the moduli: $3 \times 2 = 6$.
@step Add the arguments: $40^\circ + 110^\circ = 150^\circ$.
@answer $zw = 6(\cos 150^\circ + i\sin 150^\circ)$
:::

:::mistake Multiplying the arguments
The arguments are **added**, not multiplied: multiplying by $w$ turns $z$ through the angle of $w$. (It is the same law as $10^{m} \times 10^{n} = 10^{m+n}$.)
:::

:::summary
- The complex number $a + bi$ is the point $(a, b)$ in the complex plane.
- Its modulus is $r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ and its argument $\theta$ is its angle from the positive real axis; $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$.
- To multiply, multiply the moduli and add the arguments; to divide, divide the moduli and subtract the arguments.
:::
