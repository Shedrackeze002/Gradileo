Are the players on the volleyball team taller than the players on the soccer team? Measuring a sample from each team gives two sets of data. Comparing them takes two things: their centres, and how spread out they are.

## Comparing the centres

:::definition Population and sample
A **population** is the whole group we want to know about. A **sample** is the part of it we actually measure. We use the sample to draw conclusions about the population.
:::

The simplest comparison is of the means (or medians) of the two samples.

:::example Comparing the means
Heights, in centimetres, of six players from each team: volleyball $172,\ 178,\ 181,\ 175,\ 184,\ 176$; soccer $168,\ 171,\ 166,\ 174,\ 170,\ 167$. Compare the means.
@step Volleyball: $1066 \div 6 \approx 177.7$ cm.
@step Soccer: $1016 \div 6 \approx 169.3$ cm.
@answer The volleyball players' mean is about $8.3$ cm greater.
:::

:::

## Taking the spread into account

A difference of $8$ cm means a lot if most players are within $2$ or $3$ cm of their team's mean, and very little if heights within each team vary by $15$ cm. So we compare the difference between the means with the spread, using the mean absolute deviation.

:::definition Comparing with the MAD
Divide the difference between the means by the MAD (when the two MADs are about the same). The result says how many MADs apart the means are.
- About $2$ MADs or more: the data overlap little, and the populations are likely to be different.
- Less than $1$ MAD: the data overlap a lot, and the difference could be chance.
:::

In the dot plot above, the two samples have means $5$ and $12$ and each has a MAD of about $0.9$, so the means are $7$ apart, about $8$ MADs ($7 \div 0.9$), and the groups do not overlap at all.

:::example Is the difference real?
Two orchards' apples have mean masses of $180$ g and $186$ g, and each sample has a MAD of $12$ g. Is there a clear difference?
@step Difference of the means: $186 - 180 = 6$ g.
@step In MADs: $6 \div 12 = 0.5$.
@answer The means are only half a MAD apart, so the masses overlap a lot. The samples do not show a clear difference.
:::

:::mistake Comparing only the means
A difference in the means alone does not show that two populations differ. Always look at the spread too: the same difference can be large or small compared with it.
:::

:::summary
- To compare two populations, compare samples from each by their centres and their spreads.
- Divide the difference between the means by the MAD to see how far apart they are compared with the spread.
- About $2$ MADs or more suggests a real difference; less than $1$ MAD means the data overlap a lot.
:::
