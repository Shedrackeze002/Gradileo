The graph of $y = x^2 + 2x + 5$ never touches the $x$-axis, so the equation $x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0$ has no real solutions. With complex numbers it has two, and every quadratic equation has solutions.

## Square roots of negative numbers

:::definition The square root of a negative number
For a positive number $n$, $\sqrt{-n} = i\sqrt{n}$. So $x^2 = -n$ has the two solutions $x = \pm i\sqrt{n}$.
:::

:::example Solving $x^2 + 16 = 0$
Solve $x^2 + 16 = 0$.
@step $x^2 = -16$.
@step $x = \pm\sqrt{-16} = \pm 4i$.
@answer $x = 4i$ or $x = -4i$
:::

:::

## The quadratic formula with a negative discriminant

The solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$. The **discriminant** $b^2 - 4ac$ decides what kind of solutions there are.

:::definition The discriminant
- $b^2 - 4ac > 0$: two real solutions.
- $b^2 - 4ac = 0$: one real solution (a repeated root).
- $b^2 - 4ac < 0$: two complex solutions, $p + qi$ and $p - qi$, which are conjugates.
:::

:::example Two complex solutions
Solve $x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0$.
@step $a = 1$, $b = 2$, $c = 5$. Discriminant: $4 - 20 = -16$, which is negative.
@step $\sqrt{-16} = 4i$, so $x = \dfrac{-2 \pm 4i}{2}$.
@answer $x = -1 + 2i$ or $x = -1 - 2i$
:::

:::mistake Dividing only one term by $2a$
In $x = \dfrac{-2 \pm 4i}{2}$, both $-2$ and $4i$ are divided by $2$: $-1 \pm 2i$. Writing $-2 \pm 2i$ divides only the imaginary part.
:::

:::summary
- $\sqrt{-n} = i\sqrt{n}$, so $x^2 = -n$ has solutions $\pm i\sqrt{n}$.
- When the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ is negative, the quadratic formula gives two complex solutions.
- The two complex solutions are conjugates, $p + qi$ and $p - qi$.
- The graph of such a quadratic does not cross the $x$-axis.
:::
