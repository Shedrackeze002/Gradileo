Students who revise for longer tend to score higher, but how strong is that link, and could a sample show it by chance? The product moment correlation coefficient measures the strength of a straight-line link, and a hypothesis test checks whether it is real.

## Measuring correlation

:::definition Product moment correlation coefficient
The **product moment correlation coefficient** $r$ measures how close a set of pairs $(x, y)$ is to a straight line:
$$r = \frac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}}.$$
It always lies between $-1$ and $1$. Values near $1$ show strong positive correlation, values near $-1$ strong negative correlation, and values near $0$ little or no linear correlation.
:::

:::example Finding r
For a sample, $S_{xx} = 40$, $S_{yy} = 90$ and $S_{xy} = 48$. Find $r$.
@step $r = \dfrac{48}{\sqrt{40 \times 90}} = \dfrac{48}{60}$
@answer $r = 0.8$, strong positive correlation.
:::

:::mistake Correlation is not causation
A strong correlation shows that two quantities move together, not that one causes the other. Ice cream sales and sunburn cases are correlated because both rise in hot weather.
:::

## Testing for correlation

A sample can show some correlation by chance even when the population has none. The test uses the **population** correlation coefficient $\rho$:
- $H_0$: $\rho = 0$ (no correlation in the population);
- $H_1$: $\rho > 0$ or $\rho < 0$ (one-tailed), or $\rho \ne 0$ (two-tailed).

Compare $r$ with the **critical value** for the sample size and significance level, which tables or the question give. If $r$ is beyond it, reject $H_0$.

:::example Is the correlation significant?
For a random sample of $10$ students, $r = 0.8$ between revision time and test mark. Test at the $5\%$ level for positive correlation. The critical value is $0.5494$.
@step $H_0$: $\rho = 0$, $H_1$: $\rho > 0$.
@step $0.8 > 0.5494$, so the result is significant.
@answer Reject $H_0$: there is evidence of positive correlation between revision time and test mark.
:::

:::summary
- $r = \dfrac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}}$ lies between $-1$ and $1$; its sign gives the direction and its size the strength.
- Correlation does not show that one variable causes the other.
- Test with $H_0$: $\rho = 0$ against $H_1$: $\rho > 0$, $\rho < 0$ or $\rho \ne 0$.
- Reject $H_0$ when $r$ is beyond the critical value.
:::
