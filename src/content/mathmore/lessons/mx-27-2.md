The gradient tells us how steep a line is as a number. The same steepness can be given as an angle, and trigonometry links the two.

## The angle of inclination

:::definition Angle of inclination
The **angle of inclination** of a line is the angle $\theta$ that the line makes with the positive direction of the $x$-axis, measured anticlockwise. It lies between $0°$ and $180°$, and the gradient of the line is $m = \tan\theta$.
:::

In the figure, the right-angled triangle under the line has a horizontal side called the run and a vertical side called the rise. The gradient is $\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$, and this is also $\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$ for the angle $\theta$, so $m = \tan\theta$.

To go from a gradient to an angle we use the inverse tangent.

:::example A positive gradient
A line has gradient $2$. Find its angle of inclination, to 1 decimal place.
@step $\tan\theta = 2$
@step $\theta = \tan^{-1}(2) \approx 63.4°$
@answer $63.4°$
:::

A line that slopes downwards has a negative gradient, and its inclination is **obtuse**. The calculator gives a negative angle for $\tan^{-1}$ of a negative number, so we add $180°$.

:::example A negative gradient
A line has gradient $-1.5$. Find its angle of inclination, to 1 decimal place.
@step $\tan^{-1}(-1.5) \approx -56.3°$
@step $\theta = 180° - 56.3° = 123.7°$
@answer $123.7°$
:::

:::mistake Leaving a negative angle
An angle of inclination is never negative. When the gradient is negative, add $180°$ to the calculator's answer so that the angle lies between $90°$ and $180°$.
:::

When two points are given, we find the gradient first.

:::example Through two points
Find the angle of inclination of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 9)$, to 1 decimal place.
@step $m = \dfrac{9 - 2}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{7}{4}$
@step $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{7}{4}\right) \approx 60.3°$
@answer $60.3°$
:::

## The angle between two lines

Two lines that cross make an angle equal to the difference between their inclinations. If the difference is more than $90°$, we subtract it from $180°$ to get the acute angle.

:::example The acute angle between two lines
Find the acute angle between $y = 3x - 1$ and $y = -x + 4$, to 1 decimal place.
@step First line: $\theta_1 = \tan^{-1}(3) \approx 71.57°$
@step Second line: $\theta_2 = 180° + \tan^{-1}(-1) = 135°$
@step Difference $= 135° - 71.57° = 63.43°$, which is already acute.
@answer $63.4°$
:::

## From the angle back to the line

Going the other way, $m = \tan\theta$ gives the gradient straight from the inclination.

:::example Gradient from the inclination
A line has an angle of inclination of $130°$. Find its gradient, to 2 decimal places.
@step $m = \tan 130° \approx -1.19$
@answer $-1.19$
:::

:::check mx-inclination-line 1
:::

With the gradient and one point, we can find the whole equation $y = mx + c$.

:::example The equation of the line
A line has an angle of inclination of $45°$ and passes through $(2, 7)$. Find its equation.
@step $m = \tan 45° = 1$
@step $7 = 1 \times 2 + c$, so $c = 5$.
@answer $y = x + 5$
:::

:::tip Two angles worth remembering
$\tan 45° = 1$ and $\tan 135° = -1$. A line at $45°$ rises one unit for each unit across, and a line at $135°$ falls one unit for each unit across.
:::

:::summary
- The angle of inclination $\theta$ is measured anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis, with $0° \le \theta < 180°$.
- $m = \tan\theta$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}(m)$, plus $180°$ when $m$ is negative.
- The angle between two lines is the difference between their inclinations.
:::
