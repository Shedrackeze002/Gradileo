Finding $P(X \le 45)$ for $X \sim \mathrm{B}(100, 0.4)$ means adding $46$ binomial probabilities. When $n$ is large, the bar chart of a binomial distribution is shaped like a normal curve, so a normal distribution gives a good, quick approximation.

## Setting up the approximation

:::definition Normal approximation
When $n$ is large and $p$ is close to $0.5$, $X \sim \mathrm{B}(n, p)$ can be approximated by
$$Y \sim \mathrm{N}(np,\ np(1 - p)),$$
the normal distribution with the same mean and variance.
:::

The binomial is **discrete** and the normal is **continuous**, so each whole number $k$ is treated as the interval from $k - 0.5$ to $k + 0.5$. This is the **continuity correction**:
- $P(X \le k) \approx P(Y < k + 0.5)$ and $P(X < k) \approx P(Y < k - 0.5)$;
- $P(X \ge k) \approx P(Y > k - 0.5)$ and $P(X > k) \approx P(Y > k + 0.5)$.

$X \le 45$ includes $45$, so its interval runs up to $45.5$; $X < 45$ leaves $45$ out, so it stops at $44.5$. Write out the whole numbers included before choosing the half.

## Using the approximation

:::example At most 45
$X \sim \mathrm{B}(100, 0.4)$. Use a normal approximation to find $P(X \le 45)$.
@step $np = 40$ and $np(1 - p) = 24$, so $Y \sim \mathrm{N}(40, 24)$.
@step With the continuity correction, $P(X \le 45) \approx P(Y < 45.5)$.
@answer $P(Y < 45.5) \approx 0.8692$
:::

The exact binomial answer is $0.8689$, so the approximation is very close.

:::tip Variance, not standard deviation
$\mathrm{N}(40, 24)$ has variance $24$. When a calculator asks for the standard deviation, enter $\sqrt{24} \approx 4.899$.

:::summary
- For large $n$ and $p$ near $0.5$, $\mathrm{B}(n, p)$ is approximately $\mathrm{N}(np, np(1 - p))$.
- Use a continuity correction: each whole number $k$ covers $k - 0.5$ to $k + 0.5$.
- $P(X \le k) \approx P(Y < k + 0.5)$ and $P(X \ge k) \approx P(Y > k - 0.5)$.
:::
