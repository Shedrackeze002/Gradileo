When a group of people is sorted twice, first one way and then another, a frequency tree keeps track of every count at once. It also makes probabilities easy to read off.

## Completing a frequency tree

:::definition Frequency tree
A **frequency tree** shows how a total splits into groups, and how each group splits again. The number at each **branch** is a frequency (a count), and the numbers on the branches coming out of a point always add up to the number at that point.
:::

Here we see $80$ museum visitors, split into $50$ adults and $30$ children, and then split again by whether they bought a ticket for a special exhibition. The two adult branches add up to $20 + 30 = 50$, and the two children's branches to $18 + 12 = 30$.

:::example Filling in the missing numbers
$80$ people visited a museum. $30$ of them were children. $20$ of the adults and $18$ of the children bought a ticket. Complete the frequency tree.
@step Adults: $80 - 30 = 50$.
@step Adults who did not buy: $50 - 20 = 30$. Children who did not buy: $30 - 18 = 12$.
@answer The tree is the one shown above.
:::

:::tip Fractions and percentages
When the information is a fraction or a percentage, work out the count first: $\tfrac{3}{5}$ of $80$ is $\tfrac{80}{5} \times 3 = 48$, and $25\%$ of $48$ is $0.25 \times 48 = 12$. Every number on the tree is a count, so it must be a whole number.
:::

## Probabilities from a frequency tree

The probability of choosing someone from a group is the count for that group divided by the total we are choosing from.

:::example A probability
A visitor is chosen at random. Find the probability that they are an adult who bought a ticket.
@step There are $20$ adults who bought a ticket, out of $80$ visitors.
@answer $\dfrac{20}{80} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
:::

When we are told something about the person first, we choose only from that group, so the total changes.

:::example Given that they bought a ticket
One of the visitors who bought a ticket is chosen at random. Find the probability that it is a child.
@step $20 + 18 = 38$ visitors bought a ticket, so we choose from $38$, not $80$.
@step $18$ of them are children.
@answer $\dfrac{18}{38} = \dfrac{9}{19}$
:::

:::mistake Dividing by the wrong total
"One of the visitors who bought a ticket" means the total is everyone who bought a ticket, not everyone in the tree. Read the question to see which group we choose from.
:::

:::check mx-frequency-tree-probability 3
:::

:::summary
- A frequency tree splits a total into groups, then splits each group again.
- The branches from each point add up to the number at that point.
- Probability $= \dfrac{\text{count in the group we want}}{\text{count we are choosing from}}$.
- If we are told something first, choose only from that group.
:::
