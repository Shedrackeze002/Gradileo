A bucket, a lampshade and a paper cup all have the same shape: a cone with its point sliced off. We can measure this shape by thinking about the cone it came from.

## What a frustum is

:::definition Frustum
A **frustum** is the solid left when the top of a cone or pyramid is cut off by a plane parallel to its base. A frustum of a cone has two circular ends of different radii, $R$ and $r$, joined by a curved surface.
:::

## Volume: whole cone minus small cone

The volume of a cone is $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$. So the volume of a frustum is

$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi R^2 H - \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 x$$

where $H$ is the height of the whole cone and $x$ is the height of the cone that was removed.

:::example Both cones are given
A cone has a base radius of $6$ cm and a height of $12$ cm. A small cone of radius $2$ cm and height $4$ cm is cut from its top. Find the volume of the frustum, to 2 decimal places.
@step Whole cone: $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi \times 6^2 \times 12 \approx 452.3893$ cm³
@step Small cone: $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi \times 2^2 \times 4 \approx 16.7552$ cm³
@step Frustum $= 452.3893 - 16.7552$
@answer $435.63$ cm³
:::

## Completing the cone

Usually we are given only the frustum: its two radii and its height $h$. Then we find the height $x$ of the missing cone with similar triangles. The small cone has radius $r$ and height $x$, and the whole cone has radius $R$ and height $x + h$, so

$$\frac{x}{x + h} = \frac{r}{R}$$

:::example Only the frustum is given
A frustum has a base radius of $9$ cm, a top radius of $6$ cm and a height of $5$ cm. Find its volume, to 2 decimal places.
@step Similar triangles: $\dfrac{x}{x + 5} = \dfrac{6}{9}$, so $9x = 6x + 30$ and $x = 10$.
@step The whole cone has height $10 + 5 = 15$ cm.
@step Whole cone: $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi \times 9^2 \times 15 \approx 1272.3450$ cm³
@step Small cone: $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi \times 6^2 \times 10 \approx 376.9911$ cm³
@answer $895.35$ cm³
:::

:::mistake A frustum is not a cylinder with an average radius
Using $\pi \times 7.5^2 \times 5$ for the frustum above gives about $883.57$ cm³, which is too small. The volume must come from the two cones.
:::

## Surface area

The curved surface of a cone is $\pi r l$, where $l$ is the slant height. Subtracting the small cone's curved surface from the whole cone's gives a neat result for the frustum:

$$\text{curved surface area} = \pi (R + r) l$$

where $l$ is the slant height of the frustum itself, measured along its sloping side.

:::example Curved surface
A frustum has end radii of $7$ cm and $4$ cm and a slant height of $5$ cm. Find its curved surface area, to 2 decimal places.
@step $\pi(R + r)l = \pi \times (7 + 4) \times 5 = 55\pi$
@answer $172.79$ cm²
:::

:::check mx-frustum-surface 1
:::

When we are given the vertical height $h$ instead, the slant height is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose other sides are $h$ and the difference of the radii: $l = \sqrt{h^2 + (R - r)^2}$.

:::example From the vertical height
A solid frustum has end radii of $10$ cm and $4$ cm and a vertical height of $8$ cm. Find its total surface area, to 2 decimal places.
@step Slant height: $l = \sqrt{8^2 + (10 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$ cm
@step Curved surface: $\pi \times 14 \times 10 = 140\pi$
@step Two ends: $\pi \times 10^2 + \pi \times 4^2 = 116\pi$
@step Total $= 256\pi$
@answer $804.25$ cm²
:::

:::

:::summary
- A frustum is a cone or pyramid with its top cut off parallel to the base.
- Volume $=$ whole cone $-$ cone removed. Find the missing height with similar triangles: $\dfrac{x}{x + h} = \dfrac{r}{R}$.
- Curved surface area $= \pi(R + r)l$, with slant height $l = \sqrt{h^2 + (R - r)^2}$.
- For the total surface area of a solid frustum, add the two circular ends.
:::
