Saving \$200 at the end of every month does not just add up to \$200 times the number of months. Each payment also earns interest, and the earlier payments earn it for longer. An annuity formula adds all of that growth in one step.

## Annuities

:::definition Annuity
An **annuity** is a series of equal payments made at equal intervals, such as every month or every year. In this lesson each payment is made at the end of its interval. The **future value** of an annuity is the total of all the payments together with the interest they have earned, just after the last payment.
:::

Suppose a payment $x$ is made at the end of each period for $n$ periods, and the interest rate for one period is $i$. The last payment earns no interest, the one before it earns interest for one period, and so on. The total is a geometric series, and its sum is

$$F = \frac{x\left[(1 + i)^n - 1\right]}{i}$$

:::example Yearly payments
\$1,000 is paid into an account at the end of every year for 5 years. The account pays $6\%$ per year, compounded annually. Find the value just after the last payment.
@step $x = 1000$, $i = 0.06$ and $n = 5$.
@step $F = \dfrac{1000\left[1.06^5 - 1\right]}{0.06} = \dfrac{1000 \times 0.338226}{0.06}$
@answer \$5,637.09
:::

The payments alone total \$5,000, so \$637.09 of the final value is interest.

:::

## Monthly payments

When payments are monthly and interest is compounded monthly, we work in months: $i$ is the yearly rate divided by 12, and $n$ is the number of months.

:::example Saving every month
\$200 is paid into a savings plan at the end of every month for 5 years. The plan pays $6\%$ per year, compounded monthly. Find its value just after the last payment.
@step $i = \dfrac{0.06}{12} = 0.005$ and $n = 12 \times 5 = 60$.
@step $F = \dfrac{200\left[1.005^{60} - 1\right]}{0.005}$
@answer \$13,954.01
:::

:::mistake Match the rate to the payments
With monthly payments, using $i = 0.06$ and $n = 5$ is wrong, and so is using $i = 0.005$ with $n = 5$. The rate and the number of payments must both be counted in the same period.
:::

:::tip Keep the full value on the calculator
Do not round $1.005^{60}$ before finishing. Rounding it to four places changes the answer by several dollars, because it is then divided by a very small number.
:::

:::check mx-annuity-future 2
:::

The interest earned is the future value minus the total paid in. For the plan above, the payments total $60 \times 200$, which is \$12,000, so the interest is \$1,954.01.

:::check mx-annuity-future 3
:::

## Sinking funds

:::definition Sinking fund
A **sinking fund** is a fund built up by regular equal payments so that it reaches a known amount at a known time, for example to replace a machine or to repay a debt in one sum.
:::

Now the future value $F$ is known and the payment $x$ is wanted. We rearrange the annuity formula:

$$x = \frac{F \times i}{(1 + i)^n - 1}$$

:::example Saving towards a target
A fund must hold \$20,000 in 4 years. Payments are made at the end of every month, and the fund pays $6\%$ per year, compounded monthly. Find the monthly payment.
@step $i = 0.005$ and $n = 12 \times 4 = 48$.
@step $x = \dfrac{20000 \times 0.005}{1.005^{48} - 1} = \dfrac{100}{0.270489}$
@answer \$369.70
:::

Without interest the payment would be \$20,000 divided by 48, which is \$416.67. The interest earned by the fund makes each payment smaller.

:::

:::check mx-sinking-fund 2
:::

When something will be sold at the end, such as an old machine, the fund only has to reach the cost minus the sale price.

:::check mx-sinking-fund 3
:::

:::summary
- An annuity is a series of equal payments at equal intervals.
- Future value: $F = \dfrac{x\left[(1 + i)^n - 1\right]}{i}$, with $i$ the rate for one period and $n$ the number of payments.
- For monthly payments, divide the yearly rate by 12 and count the payments in months.
- Sinking fund payment: $x = \dfrac{F \times i}{(1 + i)^n - 1}$.
:::
