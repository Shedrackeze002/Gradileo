Some equations are true only for particular values: $2x + 1 = 7$ only when $x = 3$. Others are true for every value at all, and these identities are tools for factoring, mental arithmetic and proof. This lesson also answers a big question: how many roots does a polynomial have?

## Polynomial identities

:::definition Identity
An **identity** is an equation that is true for every value of its variables. To show that an equation is an identity, expand or simplify one side until it matches the other. To show that it is not, one counterexample is enough.
:::

:::note Identities to know
- $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ and $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
- $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$
- $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$ and $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$
- $(x^2 + y^2)^2 = (x^2 - y^2)^2 + (2xy)^2$
:::

:::example Mental arithmetic with an identity
Work out $53^2 - 47^2$ without a calculator.
@step Use $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$ with $a = 53$ and $b = 47$.
@step $(53 + 47)(53 - 47) = 100 \times 6$.
@answer $53^2 - 47^2 = 600$
:::

:::mistake $(a + b)^2$ is not $a^2 + b^2$
Try $a = 2$, $b = 1$: $(2 + 1)^2 = 9$, but $2^2 + 1^2 = 5$. The middle term $2ab$ is missing.
:::

:::example Pythagorean triples
Use $(x^2 + y^2)^2 = (x^2 - y^2)^2 + (2xy)^2$ with $x = 3$, $y = 2$.
@step $x^2 - y^2 = 5$, $2xy = 12$ and $x^2 + y^2 = 13$.
@answer $5^2 + 12^2 = 13^2$: the triangle with sides $5$, $12$ and $13$ is right-angled.

## The fundamental theorem of algebra

:::definition The fundamental theorem of algebra
A polynomial of degree $n \ge 1$ has exactly $n$ complex roots, when each root is counted as many times as it is repeated (its **multiplicity**).
:::

So a cubic has $3$ roots and a quartic $4$. Some of them may be real and some not. When the coefficients are real, the non-real roots come in **conjugate pairs**: if $a + bi$ is a root, so is $a - bi$. That is why a cubic with real coefficients always has at least one real root.

:::example Finding the other roots
$p(x) = x^3 - 5x^2 + 17x - 13$ has real coefficients and $2 + 3i$ is a root. Find the other roots.
@step The conjugate $2 - 3i$ is also a root.
@step The two give the factor $(x - (2 + 3i))(x - (2 - 3i)) = (x - 2)^2 + 9 = x^2 - 4x + 13$.
@step $x^3 - 5x^2 + 17x - 13 = (x^2 - 4x + 13)(x - 1)$: compare the constant terms, $13 \times (-1) = -13$.
@answer The roots are $2 + 3i$, $2 - 3i$ and $1$.
:::

:::summary
- An identity is true for every value; prove it by expanding, and disprove it with one counterexample.
- $(a \pm b)^2$, $a^2 - b^2$, the sum and difference of cubes, and $(x^2 + y^2)^2 = (x^2 - y^2)^2 + (2xy)^2$ are worth knowing.
- A polynomial of degree $n$ has exactly $n$ complex roots, counted with multiplicity.
- With real coefficients, non-real roots come in conjugate pairs $a \pm bi$.
:::
