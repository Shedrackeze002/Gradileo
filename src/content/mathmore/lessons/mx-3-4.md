A circle is every point at the same distance from its centre. On a coordinate grid, that distance can be written with the Pythagorean theorem, and the result is an equation that every point of the circle satisfies.

## The equation of a circle

Take a circle with centre $(h, k)$ and radius $r$. A point $(x, y)$ is on the circle when its distance from the centre is $r$. The horizontal distance is $x - h$, the vertical distance is $y - k$, and by the Pythagorean theorem their squares add up to $r^2$.

:::definition The equation of a circle
The circle with centre $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ has equation
$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$$
A circle centred at the origin has equation $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$.
:::

:::example Centre and radius from the equation
Find the centre and radius of $(x + 4)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 49$.
@step Write $x + 4$ as $x - (-4)$, so $h = -4$. And $k = 1$.
@step $r^2 = 49$, so $r = 7$.
@answer The centre is $(-4, 1)$ and the radius is $7$.
:::

:::mistake The signs in the brackets
The equation has **minus** signs: $(x - h)^2$. So $(x + 4)^2$ means $h = -4$, not $4$, and the centre of $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 9$ is $(2, -5)$.
:::

A circle has centre $(3, 2)$ and passes through $(6, 6)$. Write its equation.

## Completing the square

Expanding the brackets gives the **general form** $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$, which hides the centre and radius. To find them, complete the square for $x$ and for $y$.

:::example From general form to centre and radius
Find the centre and radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$.
@step Group the terms and move the constant: $(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 12$.
@step Half of $-6$ is $-3$, and $(-3)^2 = 9$. Half of $4$ is $2$, and $2^2 = 4$. Add $9$ and $4$ to both sides.
@step $(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 4y + 4) = 12 + 9 + 4$, so $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$.
@answer The centre is $(3, -2)$ and the radius is $5$.
:::

:::tip Coefficients other than 1
If the equation starts $2x^2 + 2y^2$, divide every term by $2$ first, so that $x^2$ and $y^2$ each have coefficient $1$.
:::

:::summary
- The circle with centre $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ is $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$; it comes from the Pythagorean theorem.
- Watch the signs: $(x + 4)^2$ means the centre's $x$-coordinate is $-4$.
- For the radius from the centre and a point, use $r^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2$.
- From general form, complete the square in $x$ and in $y$ to find the centre and radius.
:::
