Sailors, pilots and walkers need a way to give a direction that cannot be misread. "North-east" is only accurate to the nearest $45^\circ$, so instead we give an angle measured from north, called a bearing.

## Three-figure bearings

:::definition Bearing
A **bearing** is a direction given as an angle measured **clockwise from north**. It is always written with **three figures**, so an angle of $60^\circ$ is the bearing $060^\circ$, and due west is $270^\circ$.
:::

## Bearings back

The bearing of $A$ from $B$ is the direction back. The two north lines are parallel, so the direction back differs from the direction there by exactly $180^\circ$.

:::example Finding the bearing back
The bearing of $B$ from $A$ is $060^\circ$. Find the bearing of $A$ from $B$.
@step The bearing is less than $180^\circ$, so add $180^\circ$.
@step $060^\circ + 180^\circ = 240^\circ$
@answer The bearing of $A$ from $B$ is $240^\circ$.
:::

:::tip Add or subtract 180°
If the bearing is less than $180^\circ$, add $180^\circ$. If it is $180^\circ$ or more, subtract $180^\circ$. The answer must always be between $000^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.
:::

## Bearings with trigonometry

A journey on a bearing makes a right-angled triangle with the north line, so we can find how far north and east it goes with sine and cosine, and find a bearing from two distances with tangent.

:::example How far east?
A ship sails $12$ km from a harbour on a bearing of $050^\circ$. How far east of the harbour is it?
@step The $50^\circ$ angle is at the harbour, between north and the ship's path. The distance east is opposite that angle, and $12$ km is the hypotenuse.
@step East $= 12 \sin 50^\circ = 9.192\ldots$
@answer The ship is about $9.2$ km east of the harbour.
:::

:::example A distance and a bearing
Sam walks $5$ km due north and then $3$ km due east. Find Sam's distance and bearing from the start.
@step Distance $= \sqrt{5^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{34} = 5.830\ldots$ km
@step The angle at the start, clockwise from north, has $\tan \theta = \dfrac{3}{5}$, so $\theta = 30.96\ldots^\circ$.
@answer Sam is about $5.8$ km from the start, on a bearing of about $031.0^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- A bearing is measured clockwise from north and written with three figures.
- The bearing back differs by $180^\circ$.
- Draw a north line at each point, mark the right-angled triangle, and use Pythagoras' theorem or trigonometry.
:::
