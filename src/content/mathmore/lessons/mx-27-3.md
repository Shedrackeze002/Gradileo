A tangent just touches a circle, and at the point where it touches it stands at right angles to the radius. That one fact is enough to find the tangent's equation.

## The gradient of a tangent

:::definition Tangent to a circle
A **tangent to a circle** is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point, called the **point of contact**. The tangent is **perpendicular to the radius** drawn to the point of contact.
:::

Perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to $-1$. So if the radius has gradient $m_r$, the tangent has gradient
$$m_t = -\frac{1}{m_r}$$
which is the negative reciprocal: turn the fraction upside down and change its sign.

For a circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ the centre is the origin, so the radius to the point $(x_1, y_1)$ has gradient $\dfrac{y_1}{x_1}$.

:::example A circle centred at the origin
The point $(3, 4)$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$. Find the gradient of the tangent at this point.
@step Gradient of the radius $= \dfrac{4 - 0}{3 - 0} = \dfrac{4}{3}$
@step Gradient of the tangent $= -\dfrac{3}{4}$ (negative reciprocal)
@answer $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
:::

A circle $(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$ has its centre at $(a, b)$, so the radius runs from $(a, b)$ to the point of contact.

:::example A circle with another centre
The point $(5, 1)$ lies on the circle $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25$. Find the gradient of the radius and of the tangent at this point.
@step The centre is $(2, -3)$.
@step Gradient of the radius $= \dfrac{1 - (-3)}{5 - 2} = \dfrac{4}{3}$
@step Gradient of the tangent $= -\dfrac{3}{4}$
@answer Radius $\dfrac{4}{3}$, tangent $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
:::

:::mistake Reading the centre with the wrong signs
In $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25$ the centre is $(2, -3)$ and not $(-2, 3)$. The centre has the values of $x$ and $y$ that make each bracket zero.
:::

:::check mx-circle-tangent-gradient 2
:::

When the circle is written as $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$, we complete the square first to find the centre.

:::example Completing the square first
The point $(4, 6)$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y - 20 = 0$. Find the gradient of the tangent at this point.
@step $(x - 1)^2 - 1 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 - 20 = 0$, so $(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 25$.
@step The centre is $(1, 2)$, and the gradient of the radius is $\dfrac{6 - 2}{4 - 1} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
@step Gradient of the tangent $= -\dfrac{3}{4}$
@answer $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
:::

## The equation of the tangent

The tangent passes through the point of contact, so once we have its gradient we put that point into $y = mx + c$ to find $c$.

:::example The tangent at a point
Find the equation of the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ at the point $(3, 4)$.
@step Gradient of the radius $= \dfrac{4}{3}$, so the gradient of the tangent is $m = -\dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step $c = y - mx = 4 - \left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right) \times 3 = 4 + \dfrac{9}{4} = \dfrac{25}{4}$
@answer $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + \dfrac{25}{4}$
:::

:::tip Check the answer
The point of contact must satisfy the tangent's equation. Here $-\dfrac{3}{4} \times 3 + \dfrac{25}{4} = \dfrac{16}{4} = 4$, which matches the $y$-coordinate.
:::

:::check mx-circle-tangent-equation 1
:::

:::example A circle with another centre
Find the equation of the tangent to $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25$ at the point $(5, 1)$.
@step The centre is $(2, -3)$, so the gradient of the radius is $\dfrac{4}{3}$ and $m = -\dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step $c = 1 - \left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right) \times 5 = 1 + \dfrac{15}{4} = \dfrac{19}{4}$
@answer $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + \dfrac{19}{4}$
:::

:::summary
- A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
- Find the centre, then the gradient of the radius, then take the negative reciprocal for the tangent.
- Put the point of contact into $y = mx + c$ to find $c$.
:::
