Join the middles of two sides of any triangle and something neat happens: the new line runs parallel to the third side and is exactly half as long. This lesson starts with that fact and then widens it to any line drawn parallel to a side.

## The midpoint theorem

:::definition Midpoint theorem
The **midpoint theorem** says that the line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is **parallel to the third side** and **half its length**.
:::

In the figure, $D$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$, so $DE \parallel BC$ and $DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC$.

The reason is similarity. Triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ share the angle at $A$, and $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the triangles are similar with scale factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$. This means that $DE$ is half of $BC$, and the equal corresponding angles make $DE$ parallel to $BC$.

:::example Using the midpoint theorem
In triangle $ABC$, $D$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$. $BC = 17$ cm. Find $DE$.
@step $DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC$ (midpoint theorem)
@step $DE = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 17$
@answer $8.5$ cm
:::

:::check mx-midpoint-theorem 1
:::

The theorem also works backwards. A line through the midpoint of one side, drawn parallel to a second side, **bisects the third side**.

:::example Lengths given as expressions
$D$ and $E$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$. $DE = (x + 4)$ cm and $BC = (3x + 2)$ cm. Find $x$ and $BC$.
@step $BC = 2 \times DE$, so $3x + 2 = 2(x + 4)$.
@step $3x + 2 = 2x + 8$, so $x = 6$.
@step $BC = 3 \times 6 + 2 = 20$ cm
@answer $x = 6$ and $BC = 20$ cm
:::

:::check mx-midpoint-theorem 2
:::

When all three midpoints are joined, the small triangle in the middle has every side half as long as the side it is parallel to. So its perimeter is half the perimeter of the big triangle, and its area is $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$ of the area.

:::check mx-midpoint-theorem 3
:::

## The proportionality theorem

The midpoint theorem is a special case. The line does not need to pass through midpoints, as long as it is parallel to a side.

:::definition Proportionality theorem
The **proportionality theorem** says that a line drawn parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides **in the same ratio**. If $DE \parallel BC$, with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$, then
$$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$
:::

:::example Finding a part of a side
In triangle $ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$. $AD = 4$ cm, $DB = 6$ cm and $AE = 6$ cm. Find $EC$.
@step $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$ (line parallel to one side of a triangle)
@step $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{6}{EC}$
@step $EC = \dfrac{6 \times 6}{4} = 9$
@answer $9$ cm
:::

:::check mx-proportionality-theorem 1
:::

The parallel line also cuts off the same fraction of each whole side, so $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}$. This form is the quicker one when the whole sides are given.

:::example Using the whole sides
$DE \parallel BC$, $AD = 3$ cm, $AB = 12$ cm and $AC = 20$ cm. Find $AE$ and $EC$.
@step $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}$, so $\dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{AE}{20}$.
@step $AE = \dfrac{3 \times 20}{12} = 5$ cm
@step $EC = 20 - 5 = 15$ cm
@answer $AE = 5$ cm and $EC = 15$ cm
:::

:::mistake Parts and wholes
Keep each ratio consistent. $\dfrac{AD}{DB}$ compares part with part, so it must be matched with $\dfrac{AE}{EC}$. Matching it with $\dfrac{AE}{AC}$ mixes a part with a whole and gives a wrong answer.
:::

:::check mx-proportionality-theorem 2
:::

:::example Solving for x
$DE \parallel BC$, $AD = x$ cm, $DB = (x + 3)$ cm, $AE = 4$ cm and $EC = 6$ cm. Find $x$.
@step $\dfrac{x}{x + 3} = \dfrac{4}{6}$
@step $6x = 4(x + 3) = 4x + 12$
@step $2x = 12$
@answer $x = 6$
:::

:::check mx-proportionality-theorem 3
:::

:::summary
- Midpoint theorem: the line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half its length.
- Joining all three midpoints gives a triangle with half the perimeter and a quarter of the area.
- Proportionality theorem: if $DE \parallel BC$, then $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$, and also $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}$.
:::
