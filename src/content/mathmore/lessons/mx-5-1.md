A wind of $30$ km/h from the west and a wind of $30$ km/h from the north push a plane in very different ways. Some quantities need a direction as well as a size: velocity, force, displacement. These are vectors.

## Vectors and their components

:::definition Vector
A **vector** is a quantity with a **magnitude** (a size) and a **direction**. It is drawn as an arrow. The vector from $P$ to $Q$ is written $\overrightarrow{PQ}$, and a vector can be named with a bold letter, $\mathbf{v}$.
:::

:::definition Component form
A vector that moves $a$ across and $b$ up is written in **component form** as $\langle a, b \rangle$. The vector from $P(x_1, y_1)$ to $Q(x_2, y_2)$ is $\langle x_2 - x_1,\ y_2 - y_1 \rangle$. Its magnitude is
$$|\langle a, b \rangle| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$$
:::

:::example Components and magnitude
Find $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ for $P(1, 1)$ and $Q(5, 4)$, and its magnitude.
@step $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \langle 5 - 1,\ 4 - 1 \rangle = \langle 4, 3 \rangle$.
@step $|\overrightarrow{PQ}| = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
@answer $\langle 4, 3 \rangle$, with magnitude $5$
:::

## Direction

The **direction** of a vector is the angle $\theta$ it makes with the positive $x$-axis, measured anticlockwise. The calculator's $\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)$ gives an angle in quadrant I or IV, so check which quadrant the vector points into and adjust.

:::example Direction of a vector
Find the direction of $\langle -3, 4 \rangle$.
@step The vector points left and up: quadrant II.
@step The reference angle is $\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right) \approx 53.1^\circ$.
@answer In quadrant II, $\theta = 180^\circ - 53.1^\circ \approx 126.9^\circ$.
:::

Going the other way, a vector with magnitude $|v|$ and direction $\theta$ has components $\langle |v|\cos\theta,\ |v|\sin\theta \rangle$.

## Adding vectors and multiplying by a scalar

:::definition Vector operations
- **Adding:** $\langle a, b \rangle + \langle c, d \rangle = \langle a + c,\ b + d \rangle$. In a drawing, put the vectors tip to tail; the sum runs from the start of the first to the end of the second.
- **Multiplying by a scalar** $k$: $k\langle a, b \rangle = \langle ka, kb \rangle$. This stretches the vector by $k$, and reverses it when $k$ is negative.
:::

:::example Combining vectors
Let $\mathbf{u} = \langle 2, -1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle -3, 4 \rangle$. Find $3\mathbf{u} - 2\mathbf{v}$.
@step $3\mathbf{u} = \langle 6, -3 \rangle$ and $2\mathbf{v} = \langle -6, 8 \rangle$.
@step $3\mathbf{u} - 2\mathbf{v} = \langle 6 - (-6),\ -3 - 8 \rangle$.
@answer $\langle 12, -11 \rangle$
:::

:::mistake Adding the magnitudes
The magnitude of a sum is not the sum of the magnitudes. $\langle 3, 0 \rangle$ and $\langle 0, 4 \rangle$ have magnitudes $3$ and $4$, but their sum $\langle 3, 4 \rangle$ has magnitude $5$.
:::

:::summary
- A vector has a magnitude and a direction; in component form it is $\langle a, b \rangle$.
- The vector from $P$ to $Q$ is $Q$ minus $P$, and its magnitude is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
- The direction is the angle from the positive $x$-axis, adjusted to the vector's quadrant; the components are $\langle |v|\cos\theta,\ |v|\sin\theta \rangle$.
- Add vectors component by component, and multiply by a scalar by multiplying each component.
:::
