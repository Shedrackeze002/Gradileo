A surveyor can measure the three sides of a triangular field with a tape, but measuring its height or an angle is much harder. Heron's formula gives the area from the three sides alone.

## The formula

:::definition Heron's formula
**Heron's formula** gives the area of a triangle with sides $a$, $b$ and $c$. First find the **semi-perimeter** $s$, which is half the perimeter: $s = \dfrac{a + b + c}{2}$. Then
$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$$
:::

:::example Whole-number answer
Find the area of a triangle with sides $13$ cm, $14$ cm and $15$ cm.
@step $s = \dfrac{13 + 14 + 15}{2} = 21$
@step Area $= \sqrt{21(21 - 13)(21 - 14)(21 - 15)} = \sqrt{21 \times 8 \times 7 \times 6}$
@step $= \sqrt{7056}$
@answer $84$ cm²
:::

We can check the formula on a triangle we know. The $3$, $4$, $5$ triangle is right-angled, so its area is $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 4 = 6$. Heron's formula gives $s = 6$ and $\sqrt{6 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \sqrt{36} = 6$, the same.

:::mistake Use half the perimeter
$s$ is half the perimeter, not the perimeter. For sides $13$, $14$ and $15$ the perimeter is $42$, and $s = 21$. Using $42$ in the formula gives a much larger, wrong answer.
:::

Most triangles do not give a whole number under the root, so we use a calculator and round.

:::example A triangular field
A field has sides of $8$ m, $11$ m and $13$ m. Find its area, to 2 decimal places.
@step $s = \dfrac{8 + 11 + 13}{2} = 16$
@step Area $= \sqrt{16 \times 8 \times 5 \times 3} = \sqrt{1920}$
@answer $43.82$ m²
:::

## Heights from Heron's formula

The area of a triangle is also $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$. So once Heron's formula has given the area, we can find the height measured from any side: $h = \dfrac{2 \times \text{Area}}{\text{base}}$.

:::example The height from the longest side
The triangle with sides $13$ cm, $14$ cm and $15$ cm has an area of $84$ cm². Find its height measured from the $15$ cm side.
@step $84 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 15 \times h$
@step $h = \dfrac{2 \times 84}{15}$
@answer $11.2$ cm
:::

## Quadrilaterals

A diagonal splits a quadrilateral into two triangles. If we know the four sides and that diagonal, we use Heron's formula on each triangle and add.

:::example A plot of land
A plot $ABCD$ has $AB = 5$ m, $BC = 12$ m, $CD = 10$ m and $DA = 13$ m, and the diagonal $AC$ is $13$ m. Find its area.
@step Triangle $ABC$ has sides $5$, $12$ and $13$: $s = 15$, area $= \sqrt{15 \times 10 \times 3 \times 2} = 30$ m².
@step Triangle $ACD$ has sides $10$, $13$ and $13$: $s = 18$, area $= \sqrt{18 \times 8 \times 5 \times 5} = 60$ m².
@answer $90$ m²
:::

:::check mx-heron-apply 3
:::

:::summary
- The semi-perimeter is $s = \dfrac{a + b + c}{2}$.
- Heron's formula: Area $= \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$.
- The height from a side is $\dfrac{2 \times \text{Area}}{\text{base}}$.
- Split a quadrilateral into two triangles along a diagonal and add their areas.
:::
