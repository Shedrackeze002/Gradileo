One club gains $50$ new members every year. Another doubles its membership every year. At first the steady club may be far ahead, but the doubling club catches up and then races past. These are the two basic kinds of growth: linear and exponential.

## Two kinds of growth

:::definition Linear and exponential growth
- In **linear growth**, the amount **increases by the same number** in each equal step of time: there is a **common difference**. Its graph is a straight line, and its rule is $y = b + kx$.
- In **exponential growth**, the amount is **multiplied by the same factor** in each equal step of time: there is a **common ratio** greater than $1$. Its graph curves upward more and more steeply, and its rule is $y = a \times r^{x}$.
:::

:::example Which kind of growth?
Decide whether each table shows linear or exponential growth.
Table A: $x = 0, 1, 2, 3$ gives $y = 5, 15, 45, 135$. Table B: $x = 0, 1, 2, 3$ gives $y = 5, 15, 25, 35$.
@step Table A: the differences are $10, 30, 90$, not equal; the ratios are $15 \div 5 = 3$, $45 \div 15 = 3$, $135 \div 45 = 3$, all equal. Exponential, with common ratio $3$.
@step Table B: the differences are $10, 10, 10$, all equal. Linear, with common difference $10$.
@answer Table A is exponential ($y = 5 \times 3^{x}$); Table B is linear ($y = 5 + 10x$).
:::

## Exponential growth always wins

A quantity that grows exponentially eventually exceeds any quantity that grows linearly, however large the linear one starts and however slowly the exponential one starts. Each step adds a fixed amount to the linear quantity, but adds more and more to the exponential one, because it adds a fraction of an ever larger amount.

:::example When does it overtake?
Club A has $200$ members and gains $50$ a year. Club B has $10$ members and doubles each year. After how many years does Club B first have more members?
@step Club A: $A = 200 + 50n$. Club B: $B = 10 \times 2^{n}$.
@step Year $4$: $A = 400$, $B = 160$. Year $5$: $A = 450$, $B = 320$.
@step Year $6$: $A = 500$, $B = 640$.
@answer Club B first has more members after $6$ years.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Linear growth adds the same amount in each step (a common difference); exponential growth multiplies by the same factor (a common ratio).
- Check the differences and the ratios of a table to tell which it is.
- Exponential growth eventually overtakes linear growth, however far behind it starts.
:::
