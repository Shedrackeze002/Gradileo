Spin a coin on a table and, for a moment, it looks like a ball. A turning flat shape seems to sweep out a solid. Turning a shape all the way around a line is a way to describe many solids exactly.

## Solids of revolution

:::definition Solid of revolution
A **solid of revolution** is the solid swept out when a flat shape turns a full turn about a line, the **axis of rotation**.
:::

:::definition Three solids of revolution
- A **rectangle** turned about one of its sides sweeps out a **cylinder**. The side on the axis is the height, and the other side is the radius.
- A **right triangle** turned about one of its legs sweeps out a **cone**. The leg on the axis is the height, and the other leg is the radius of the base.
- A **semicircle** turned about its diameter sweeps out a **sphere** with the same radius.
:::

:::example Naming the solid
A right triangle with legs $3$ cm and $7$ cm is turned about the $7$ cm leg. Describe the solid.
@step The $7$ cm leg lies on the axis, so it is the height.
@step The $3$ cm leg sweeps out a circle of radius $3$ cm: the base.
@step The hypotenuse sweeps out the slanted surface.
@answer A cone with radius $3$ cm and height $7$ cm.
:::

:::mistake Which side is the radius?
The side on the axis does not move, so it is the **height**. The side that sweeps around is the **radius**. A $4$ cm by $9$ cm rectangle turned about its $9$ cm side gives a cylinder of radius $4$ cm and height $9$ cm; turned about its $4$ cm side, it gives a wide, flat cylinder of radius $9$ cm and height $4$ cm.
:::

## The volume of a solid of revolution

:::definition Volume formulas
Cylinder: $V = \pi r^2 h$. Cone: $V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$. Sphere: $V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.
:::

:::example Finding the volume
A rectangle $4$ cm by $9$ cm is turned about its $9$ cm side. Find the volume of the solid to the nearest tenth.
@step It sweeps out a cylinder with radius $4$ cm and height $9$ cm.
@step $V = \pi \times 4^2 \times 9 = 144\pi \approx 452.4\ \text{cm}^3$
@answer About $452.4\ \text{cm}^3$
:::

:::example The volume of a cone of revolution
The triangle from the first example sweeps out a cone with radius $3$ cm and height $7$ cm. Find its volume to the nearest tenth.
@step $V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \tfrac{1}{3} \times \pi \times 3^2 \times 7 = 21\pi$
@answer $V \approx 66.0\ \text{cm}^3$
:::

:::summary
- A solid of revolution is swept out by turning a flat shape a full turn about an axis.
- A rectangle about a side gives a cylinder, a right triangle about a leg gives a cone, and a semicircle about its diameter gives a sphere.
- The side on the axis is the height; the side that sweeps around is the radius.
- Find the volume with the formula for the solid.
:::
