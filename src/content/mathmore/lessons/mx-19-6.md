A population that grows in proportion to its size, or a cup of tea that cools faster the hotter it is, is described by a differential equation: an equation with a derivative in it. Many can be solved by separating the variables.

## Separating the variables

:::definition Separable differential equation
A first-order differential equation is **separable** if it can be written $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y)$. Then
$$\int \frac{1}{g(y)}\,dy = \int f(x)\,dx.$$
Integrating both sides gives the **general solution**, with one constant; a known point gives the **particular solution**.
:::

:::example Solving dy/dx = 2xy
Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2xy$, given that $y = 3$ when $x = 0$.
@step $\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{y}\,dy = \int 2x\,dx$, so $\ln|y| = x^2 + c$.
@step $y = Ae^{x^2}$, and $y = 3$ at $x = 0$ gives $A = 3$.
@answer $y = 3e^{x^2}$
:::

:::mistake One constant is enough
Integrating both sides gives a constant on each side, but they combine into one. Add it straight away, on the side with $x$, and find it before you rearrange.

## Modelling

If a quantity $N$ grows or decays at a rate proportional to its size, $\dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN$, so $N = N_0e^{kt}$. For example, if $N = 500$ at $t = 0$ and $800$ at $t = 3$, then $k = \tfrac{1}{3}\ln 1.6 \approx 0.157$, and the model predicts $N \approx 2395$ at $t = 10$. A cooling object in a room at $20^\circ$C follows $\dfrac{d\theta}{dt} = -k(\theta - 20)$, so $\theta = 20 + Ae^{-kt}$.

:::summary
- For $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y)$: $\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{g(y)}\,dy = \int f(x)\,dx$.
- Add one constant, and use a known point to find it.
- $\dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN$ gives $N = N_0e^{kt}$; cooling to a room at $T$ gives $\theta = T + Ae^{-kt}$.
:::
