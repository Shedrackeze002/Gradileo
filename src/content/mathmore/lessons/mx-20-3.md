When a tap fills a tank at a steady rate, doubling the time doubles the volume of water. Relationships like this one are proportional, and each of them is controlled by a single number.

## Proportional relationships

:::definition Proportional relationship
Two quantities are in a **proportional relationship** when one is always the same multiple of the other. If the quantities are $x$ and $y$, then
$$y = kx$$
for a fixed number $k$. When $x$ is multiplied by any number, $y$ is multiplied by the same number, and when $x = 0$, $y = 0$.
:::

:::definition Constant of proportionality
The **constant of proportionality** is the number $k$ in $y = kx$. It is the value of $y \div x$ for every pair of values (apart from $0$ and $0$), so it tells us how much $y$ there is for each $1$ of $x$. It is the unit rate of the relationship.
$$k = \frac{y}{x}$$
:::

:::example Finding the constant of proportionality
The cost $C$ dollars of some rope is proportional to its length $L$ metres. A length of $8$ m costs \$20. Find the constant of proportionality and write the equation.
@step $k = \dfrac{C}{L} = \dfrac{20}{8} = 2.5$
@step So $C = 2.5L$.
@answer $k = 2.5$: the rope costs \$2.50 for every metre.
:::

## Tables and graphs

In a table of a proportional relationship, dividing $y$ by $x$ gives the same answer in every column. That answer is $k$, and once we know it we can fill in any missing value.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Time, t (h)","2","5","8"],"rows":[["Distance, d (km)","90","225","?"]]}
:::

:::example Using a table
The table above shows a proportional relationship. Find $k$ and the missing distance.
@step $k = \dfrac{90}{2} = 45$. Check with the second column: $\dfrac{225}{5} = 45$.
@step So $d = 45t$. When $t = 8$: $d = 45 \times 8 = 360$
@answer $k = 45$ km for every hour, and the missing distance is $360$ km.
:::

:::check mx-constant-of-proportionality 2
:::

The graph of a proportional relationship is a straight line that passes through the origin, $(0, 0)$. The constant of proportionality is the **slope** of that line: how far the line rises for each $1$ unit it moves to the right. So if the line passes through $(4, 10)$, then $k = \dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$.

## Proportional or not?

A relationship is proportional only when $\dfrac{y}{x}$ is the same for every pair. A straight-line graph is not enough: the line must also pass through the origin.

:::example Testing a table
Is $y$ proportional to $x$ for the pairs $(2, 7)$, $(4, 13)$ and $(6, 19)$?
@step $\dfrac{7}{2} = 3.5$ and $\dfrac{13}{4} = 3.25$
@step The quotients are different.
@answer The relationship is not proportional. (The rule is $y = 3x + 1$, a straight line that misses the origin.)
:::

:::mistake Checking the differences instead of the quotients
In the example above, $y$ goes up by $6$ every time $x$ goes up by $2$. A steady increase makes the graph a straight line, but it does not make the relationship proportional. Always test $y \div x$.
:::

:::check mx-proportional-or-not 1
:::

:::tip Spotting it in an equation
A proportional relationship has an equation of the form $y = kx$ and nothing else. $y = 4x$ is proportional. $y = 4x + 3$, $y = \dfrac{4}{x}$ and $y = x^2$ are not.
:::

:::summary
- In a proportional relationship, $y = kx$: one quantity is always the same multiple of the other.
- The constant of proportionality is $k = \dfrac{y}{x}$, the amount of $y$ for each $1$ of $x$.
- In a table, $y \div x$ is the same for every pair. On a graph, the relationship is a straight line through $(0, 0)$ with slope $k$.
- A relationship with an added constant, such as $y = 3x + 1$, is not proportional.
:::
