Is a dice fair? Does the way students travel to school depend on their age? Both questions compare the counts we observe with the counts we would expect if a hypothesis were true, and the chi-squared statistic measures how far apart they are.

## The chi-squared statistic

:::definition Chi-squared statistic
The **chi-squared statistic** compares observed frequencies $O$ with expected frequencies $E$:
$$\chi^2_{calc} = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$$
A large value means that the observed counts are far from what $H_0$ predicts.
:::

We reject $H_0$ when $\chi^2_{calc}$ is more than the critical value for the significance level and the number of **degrees of freedom**, $\nu$. (A calculator's $p$-value gives the same decision: reject $H_0$ when it is less than the significance level.)

## The goodness of fit test

A goodness of fit test checks whether data fit a given distribution. For $k$ categories there are $\nu = k - 1$ degrees of freedom.

:::example A spinner
A spinner with four equal sections lands on them $18$, $30$, $27$ and $25$ times. Test at the $5\%$ level whether it is fair, given the critical value $7.815$.
@step $H_0$: the spinner is fair. Each expected frequency is $\dfrac{100}{4} = 25$.
@step $\chi^2_{calc} = \dfrac{49}{25} + \dfrac{25}{25} + \dfrac{4}{25} + \dfrac{0}{25} = 3.12$
@step $\nu = 4 - 1 = 3$, and $3.12 < 7.815$.
@answer Do not reject $H_0$: there is not enough evidence that the spinner is unfair.
:::

## The test for independence

A test for independence uses a **contingency table**, which counts the data under two variables at once. $H_0$ says the two variables are independent. If they are, the expected frequency in each cell is
$$E = \frac{\text{row total} \times \text{column total}}{\text{grand total}}$$
and there are $\nu = (\text{rows} - 1)(\text{columns} - 1)$ degrees of freedom.

:::example Expected frequencies
In a survey of $200$ people, $80$ are under $30$ and $90$ prefer tea. Find the expected number of under-30s who prefer tea if age and choice of drink are independent.
@step $E = \dfrac{80 \times 90}{200}$
@answer $36$
:::

:::summary
- $\chi^2_{calc} = \sum \dfrac{(O - E)^2}{E}$, and $H_0$ is rejected when it is more than the critical value.
- Goodness of fit: expected frequencies from the distribution in $H_0$, with $\nu = k - 1$.
- Independence: $E = \dfrac{\text{row total} \times \text{column total}}{\text{grand total}}$, with $\nu = (r - 1)(c - 1)$.
:::
