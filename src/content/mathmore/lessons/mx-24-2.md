The cost of a taxi ride is not simply proportional to the distance, because there is a fixed charge before the taxi moves at all. And the volume of a cylinder depends on two things at once, its radius and its height. This lesson covers both kinds of variation.

## Joint variation

:::definition Joint variation
A quantity **varies jointly** as two others when it is directly proportional to their product: "$z$ varies jointly as $x$ and $y$" means $z = kxy$ for a constant $k$. A quantity can also vary directly as one quantity and inversely as another: "$z$ varies directly as $x$ and inversely as $y$" means $z = \dfrac{kx}{y}$. In each case one constant $k$ links all the quantities.
:::

We use the same three steps as for direct and inverse variation: write the rule with $k$, use the given values to find $k$, and then use the rule.

:::example Jointly as two quantities
$z$ varies jointly as $x$ and $y$. When $x = 2$ and $y = 5$, $z = 30$. Find $z$ when $x = 4$ and $y = 3$.
@step $z = kxy$
@step $30 = k \times 2 \times 5 = 10k$, so $k = 3$.
@step $z = 3 \times 4 \times 3$
@answer $z = 36$
:::

:::check mx-joint-variation 1
:::

:::example Directly and inversely
$z$ varies directly as $x$ and inversely as the square of $y$. When $x = 3$ and $y = 2$, $z = 9$. Find $z$ when $x = 5$ and $y = 3$.
@step $z = \dfrac{kx}{y^2}$
@step $9 = \dfrac{3k}{4}$, so $k = \dfrac{9 \times 4}{3} = 12$.
@step $z = \dfrac{12 \times 5}{9} = \dfrac{60}{9}$
@answer $z \approx 6.67$
:::

:::mistake One constant, not two
"Directly as $x$ and inversely as $y$" has a single constant: $z = \dfrac{kx}{y}$. We do not write one constant for $x$ and another for $y$.
:::

## Partial variation

:::definition Partial variation
A quantity is in **partial variation** when it is the sum of two parts: one part is constant, and the other varies directly as another quantity. "$C$ is partly constant and partly varies as $d$" means $C = a + bd$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants.
:::

A taxi fare is a good example: a fixed charge $a$, plus a charge $b$ for each kilometre.

:::check mx-partial-variation 1
:::

There are two constants to find, so we need two pairs of values. Each pair gives an equation, and we solve the two equations together.

:::example A taxi fare
The cost $C$ dollars of a taxi ride is partly constant and partly varies as the distance $d$ km. A ride of $4$ km costs \$11 and a ride of $10$ km costs \$20. Find the cost of a ride of $16$ km.
@step $C = a + bd$, so $11 = a + 4b$ and $20 = a + 10b$.
@step Subtract the first equation from the second: $9 = 6b$, so $b = 1.5$.
@step $a = 11 - 4 \times 1.5 = 5$, so $C = 5 + 1.5d$.
@step $C = 5 + 1.5 \times 16 = 29$
@answer The ride costs \$29.
:::

The fixed charge is \$5 and each kilometre costs \$1.50. On a graph, $C = a + bd$ is a straight line that crosses the vertical axis at $a$ and has gradient $b$. Direct variation is the special case $a = 0$, a line through the origin.

:::check mx-partial-variation 3
:::

:::summary
- $z$ varies jointly as $x$ and $y$: $z = kxy$.
- $z$ varies directly as $x$ and inversely as $y$: $z = \dfrac{kx}{y}$.
- Partial variation: $y = a + bx$, a constant part plus a part that varies.
- Joint variation needs one set of values to find $k$; partial variation needs two pairs to find $a$ and $b$.
:::
