Push a heavy box gently and nothing happens; push harder and suddenly it slides. Friction explains both: it grows to match a small push, but only up to a limit, and once the push is past that limit the box moves.

## The friction law

:::definition Friction
**Friction** is a force between two surfaces in contact that acts along the surfaces and opposes motion, or the tendency to move. Its size $F$ obeys
$$F \le \mu R$$
where $R$ is the normal reaction and $\mu$ is the **coefficient of friction**, a number for the two surfaces. When the object is moving, or on the point of moving, friction is **limiting**: $F = \mu R$.
:::

An object that is on the point of moving is in **limiting equilibrium**: still at rest, with friction at its greatest possible value.

:::example Does the box move?
A box of mass $10$ kg rests on rough ground with $\mu = 0.3$. A horizontal force of $20$ N pushes it. Does it move?
@step $R = 10 \times 9.8 = 98$ N, so the greatest friction is $0.3 \times 98 = 29.4$ N.
@step The push, $20$ N, is less than $29.4$ N.
@answer The box stays at rest, and friction is just $20$ N, enough to balance the push.
:::

:::mistake Friction is not always μR
$\mu R$ is the **greatest** friction can be. If the object is at rest and not about to move, friction is only as large as it needs to be. Use $F = \mu R$ only when the object is moving or on the point of moving.
:::

## Motion with friction

Once the object slides, friction is $\mu R$ against the motion, and $F = ma$ gives the acceleration. When a pull acts at an angle above the horizontal, its upward part lifts some of the weight, so the normal reaction, and the friction with it, is smaller.

:::example A pull at an angle
A sledge of mass $20$ kg is pulled across rough ground ($\mu = 0.25$) by a rope at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal with a tension of $80$ N. Find its acceleration.
@step Vertically: $R + 80\sin 30^\circ = 20 \times 9.8$, so $R = 196 - 40 = 156$ N.
@step Friction: $F = 0.25 \times 156 = 39$ N.
@step Horizontally: $80\cos 30^\circ - 39 = 20a$, so $69.3 - 39 = 20a$.
@answer $a \approx 1.51$ m/s².
:::

:::summary
- Friction acts along the surface, against motion or the tendency to move.
- $F \le \mu R$, with $F = \mu R$ when the object is moving or on the point of moving (limiting).
- Find the normal reaction by resolving at right angles to the surface; a pull at an angle changes it.
- Then use $F = ma$ along the surface to find the acceleration.
:::
