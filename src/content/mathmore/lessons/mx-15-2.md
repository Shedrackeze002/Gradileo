The normal distribution usually answers questions like “what fraction of women are shorter than $160$ cm?”. The inverse normal distribution answers the reverse: “how tall must a woman be to be in the tallest $10\%$?”.

## From a probability to a value

:::definition Inverse normal
For $X \sim \mathrm{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the **inverse normal** function finds the value $a$ with a given probability to its left: $P(X < a) = p$. Through the standard normal $Z = \dfrac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$,
$$a = \mu + z\sigma, \qquad \text{where } P(Z < z) = p.$$
:::

:::example The tallest 10%
Heights are modelled by $X \sim \mathrm{N}(170, 8^2)$, in cm. Find the height $a$ that only $10\%$ are taller than.
@step $P(X > a) = 0.1$, so $P(X < a) = 0.9$.
@step $P(Z < z) = 0.9$ gives $z \approx 1.2816$.
@answer $a = 170 + 1.2816 \times 8 \approx 180$ cm.
:::

## Finding μ or σ

If the mean or standard deviation is unknown, a known probability gives an equation. Turn the probability into $z$, then use $\dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma} = z$. With both unknown, two probabilities give two simultaneous equations.

:::example An unknown standard deviation
$X \sim \mathrm{N}(50, \sigma^2)$ and $P(X < 56) = 0.8$. Find $\sigma$.
@step $P(Z < z) = 0.8$ gives $z \approx 0.8416$.
@step $\dfrac{56 - 50}{\sigma} = 0.8416$, so $\sigma = \dfrac{6}{0.8416}$.
@answer $\sigma \approx 7.13$
:::

:::tip Check the sign of z
A value below the mean has a negative $z$. If $P(X < a)$ is less than $0.5$, $a$ is below the mean and $z < 0$.
:::

:::check mx-normal-parameters 3
:::

:::summary
- For $P(X < a) = p$: find $z$ with $P(Z < z) = p$, then $a = \mu + z\sigma$.
- For $P(X > a) = p$, use $P(X < a) = 1 - p$.
- An unknown $\mu$ or $\sigma$ comes from $\dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma} = z$; two unknowns need two equations.
:::
