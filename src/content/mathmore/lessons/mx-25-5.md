When two chords of a circle cross, they cut each other into four pieces. The lengths of those pieces are linked by a rule that uses only multiplication.

## Chords that cross inside the circle

:::definition Intersecting chords theorem
The **intersecting chords theorem** says that when two chords $AB$ and $CD$ of a circle cross at a point $P$, the products of the two parts of each chord are equal: $AP \times PB = CP \times PD$.
:::

The rule comes from similar triangles. Triangles $APC$ and $DPB$ have equal angles at $P$ (vertically opposite), and angle $CAB$ equals angle $CDB$ (angles in the same segment). So the triangles are similar, and $\dfrac{AP}{DP} = \dfrac{CP}{BP}$, which gives $AP \times PB = CP \times PD$.

:::example Finding a part of a chord
Chords $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $P$. $AP = 6$ cm, $PB = 4$ cm and $CP = 3$ cm. Find $PD$.
@step $AP \times PB = CP \times PD$
@step $6 \times 4 = 3 \times PD$
@answer $PD = 8$ cm
:::

## Secants from a point outside

A **secant** is a straight line that cuts a circle at two points. When two secants are drawn from a point $P$ outside the circle, one through $A$ and then $B$, the other through $C$ and then $D$, the same kind of rule holds:

$$PA \times PB = PC \times PD$$

Each product uses the distance from $P$ to the near point and the whole distance from $P$ to the far point.

:::example Two secants
From $P$, one secant meets a circle at $A$ and $B$ with $PA = 4$ cm and $AB = 8$ cm. Another meets it at $C$ and $D$ with $PC = 6$ cm. Find the chord $CD$.
@step $PB = 4 + 8 = 12$ cm
@step $PA \times PB = PC \times PD$, so $4 \times 12 = 6 \times PD$ and $PD = 8$ cm.
@step $CD = PD - PC = 8 - 6$
@answer $CD = 2$ cm
:::

:::mistake Use the whole distance to the far point
The product is $PA \times PB$, not $PA \times AB$. In the example, $PB = 12$ cm, not $8$ cm.
:::

:::check mx-intersecting-chords 2
:::

If one of the lines swings round until it just touches the circle at $T$, its two crossing points come together, and the rule becomes $PT^2 = PA \times PB$.

:::example A tangent and a secant
From $P$, a tangent touches a circle at $T$ with $PT = 6$ cm, and a secant meets the circle at $A$ and $B$ with $PA = 3$ cm. Find the chord $AB$.
@step $PT^2 = PA \times PB$, so $36 = 3 \times PB$ and $PB = 12$ cm.
@step $AB = PB - PA = 12 - 3$
@answer $AB = 9$ cm
:::

:::

## Common tangents to two circles

:::definition Common tangent
A **common tangent** is a straight line that touches two circles. A **direct** common tangent has both circles on the same side of it. A **transverse** common tangent crosses between the circles, so that they are on opposite sides of it.
:::

A radius meets a tangent at a right angle, and this gives a right-angled triangle each time. Let the circles have radii $R$ and $r$, with their centres a distance $d$ apart.

For a direct common tangent, we draw a line from the centre of the smaller circle parallel to the tangent. It makes a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $d$ and one side $R - r$, so

$$\text{direct common tangent} = \sqrt{d^2 - (R - r)^2}$$

For a transverse common tangent, the same construction gives a side of $R + r$, so

$$\text{transverse common tangent} = \sqrt{d^2 - (R + r)^2}$$

:::example Both kinds of tangent
Two circles have radii $7$ cm and $2$ cm, and their centres are $15$ cm apart. Find the length of a direct common tangent and of a transverse common tangent, to 2 decimal places where needed.
@step Direct: $\sqrt{15^2 - (7 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{200} \approx 14.14$ cm
@step Transverse: $\sqrt{15^2 - (7 + 2)^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ cm
@answer $14.14$ cm and $12$ cm
:::

:::tip Difference for direct, sum for transverse
The direct tangent stays outside both circles and uses the difference of the radii. The transverse tangent crosses between them and uses the sum.
:::

The same triangle gives the distance between the centres when the tangent is known: $d^2 = \text{tangent}^2 + (R + r)^2$ for a transverse tangent.

:::summary
- Chords crossing at $P$ inside a circle: $AP \times PB = CP \times PD$.
- Two secants from $P$ outside: $PA \times PB = PC \times PD$. A tangent and a secant: $PT^2 = PA \times PB$.
- Direct common tangent $= \sqrt{d^2 - (R - r)^2}$.
- Transverse common tangent $= \sqrt{d^2 - (R + r)^2}$.
:::
