A door opens easily when you push the handle and hardly at all when you push near the hinge. The same force has a bigger turning effect the further it acts from the pivot. That turning effect is the moment of the force, and balancing moments tells us when a beam, a see-saw or a bridge stays level.

## The moment of a force

:::definition Moment
The **moment** of a force about a point is its turning effect about that point:
$$\text{moment} = F \times d$$
where $d$ is the **perpendicular** distance from the point to the line of action of the force. Moments are measured in newton metres (N m), and each one turns either **clockwise** or **anticlockwise**.
:::

:::example A spanner
A force of $40$ N acts at right angles to a spanner, $0.25$ m from the centre of a nut. Find its moment about the centre of the nut.
@answer Moment $= 40 \times 0.25 = 10$ N m.
:::

When a force acts at an angle $\theta$ to a rod, only its component at right angles to the rod, $F\sin\theta$, turns it. So the moment about the end of a rod of length $L$ is $FL\sin\theta$.

## Beams in equilibrium

:::definition Rigid body in equilibrium
A **rigid body**, such as a beam, is in equilibrium when the resultant force on it is zero **and** the resultant moment about every point is zero. For a horizontal beam this gives two equations: the upward forces equal the downward forces, and the clockwise moments about any point equal the anticlockwise moments.
:::

A **uniform** beam has its weight at its midpoint. Taking moments about a point where an unknown force acts removes that force from the equation, so choose that point.

:::example Reactions at two supports
A uniform beam $AB$, $4$ m long with mass $20$ kg, rests on supports at $A$ and $B$. A load of $30$ kg sits $1$ m from $A$. Find the reactions.
@step Moments about $A$: $R_B \times 4 = 20g \times 2 + 30g \times 1 = 70g$, so $R_B = 17.5g = 171.5$ N.
@step Vertically: $R_A + R_B = 50g = 490$, so $R_A = 318.5$ N.
@answer $R_A = 318.5$ N and $R_B = 171.5$ N. The support nearer the load carries more.

## On the point of tilting

As a load moves towards the end of a beam beyond a support, the reaction at the other support shrinks. When it reaches zero, the beam is **on the point of tilting** about the nearer support.

:::mistake Taking moments about the wrong point
On the point of tilting about $D$, the reaction at the other support is zero, so take moments about $D$: the reaction at $D$ drops out, and the only moments left are the weights on either side.
:::

:::check mx-beams 3
:::

:::summary
- Moment $=$ force $\times$ perpendicular distance from the point to the line of the force, in N m, clockwise or anticlockwise.
- For a force at angle $\theta$ to a rod of length $L$, the moment about the end is $FL\sin\theta$.
- A beam in equilibrium has no resultant force and no resultant moment: resolve vertically and take moments.
- Take moments about the point where an unknown force acts; on the point of tilting, the reaction at the other support is zero.
:::
