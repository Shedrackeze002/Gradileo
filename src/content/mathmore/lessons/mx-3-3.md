Measuring the angles of one triangle shows that they add up to about $180^\circ$; it does not show that every triangle's angles do. A **proof** shows that a statement is true in every case, by a chain of steps that each follow from facts already known.

## The parts of a proof

:::definition Postulate and theorem
A **postulate** is a basic fact accepted without proof, such as "a linear pair of angles adds up to $180^\circ$". A **theorem** is a statement that has been proved, such as "vertical angles are congruent". Once a theorem is proved, it can be used as a reason in later proofs.
:::

:::definition Two-column proof
A **two-column proof** lists the **statements** in order in one column and the **reason** for each in the other. A reason is the given information, a definition, a property of equality or congruence, a postulate or a theorem already proved. The last statement is what we set out to prove.
:::

:::note Reasons used often
- **Given:** stated in the problem.
- **Reflexive property:** a segment or angle is congruent to itself, $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AB}$ (useful when two triangles share a side).
- **Substitution:** equal quantities can replace each other.
- **Addition or subtraction property of equality:** adding or subtracting the same amount on both sides.
- **Definitions:** of a midpoint (it splits a segment into two congruent parts), of an angle bisector (it splits an angle into two congruent angles), of congruent segments and angles.
- **Congruence tests:** SSS, SAS, ASA, and **CPCTC**, "corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent".
:::

## A first proof

:::example Vertical angles are congruent
**Given:** lines $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $E$. **Prove:** $\angle AEC \cong \angle BED$.
@step $m\angle AEC + m\angle CEB = 180^\circ$. Reason: linear pair postulate.
@step $m\angle CEB + m\angle BED = 180^\circ$. Reason: linear pair postulate.
@step $m\angle AEC + m\angle CEB = m\angle CEB + m\angle BED$. Reason: substitution (both equal $180^\circ$).
@step $m\angle AEC = m\angle BED$. Reason: subtraction property of equality (subtract $m\angle CEB$).
@answer $\angle AEC \cong \angle BED$. Reason: definition of congruent angles.
:::

## Proving triangles congruent

Many proofs show two triangles are congruent, then use CPCTC to conclude that some of their parts match. The plan: find three pairs of congruent parts, name the test (SSS, SAS or ASA), then use CPCTC.

The proof above shows that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are congruent. **Given:** $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$, and $\overline{AD}$ bisects $\angle BAC$ with $D$ on $\overline{BC}$. **Prove:** $\angle B \cong \angle C$.

:::mistake The angle must be between the sides
SAS needs the angle **between** the two sides. Two sides and an angle that is not between them (SSA) do not prove two triangles congruent.
:::

:::summary
- A proof shows that a statement is always true, through steps that each follow from known facts.
- A two-column proof lists the statements in one column and the reason for each in the other.
- Reasons are the given facts, definitions, properties of equality and congruence, postulates and theorems already proved.
- To prove parts of triangles congruent, prove the triangles congruent (SSS, SAS, ASA) and then use CPCTC.
:::
