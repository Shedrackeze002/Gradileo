The mean says where data sit; the standard deviation says how spread out they are. With a large data set we rarely have every value to hand, but two totals, $\sum x$ and $\sum x^2$, are enough to find both.

## Variance and standard deviation

:::definition Variance and standard deviation
The **variance** of $n$ values is the mean of the squares minus the square of the mean:
$$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2, \qquad \text{where } \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}.$$
The **standard deviation** $\sigma$ is the square root of the variance. It measures the typical distance of the values from the mean, in the same units as the data.
:::

For a frequency table, use $\sum f$ for $n$, $\sum fx$ for $\sum x$ and $\sum fx^2$ for $\sum x^2$.

:::example From the totals
For $10$ values, $\sum x = 120$ and $\sum x^2 = 1580$. Find the mean and the standard deviation.
@step $\bar{x} = \dfrac{120}{10} = 12$
@step $\sigma^2 = \dfrac{1580}{10} - 12^2 = 158 - 144 = 14$
@answer $\bar{x} = 12$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{14} \approx 3.74$.
:::

:::mistake Square the mean, not the sum
The formula subtracts $\bar{x}^2$, the square of the **mean**, after dividing $\sum x^2$ by $n$. Writing $\dfrac{\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2}{n}$ forgets to divide $(\sum x)^2$ by $n$ a second time.
:::

## Coding

Large or awkward values are easier to work with after **coding**: a change such as $y = \dfrac{x - a}{b}$. Adding or subtracting a constant moves the mean but not the spread; dividing by $b$ divides both the mean and the standard deviation by $b$. To undo a code, write $x = a + by$, so
$$\bar{x} = a + b\bar{y}, \qquad \sigma_x = b\,\sigma_y.$$

:::example Undoing a code
Data are coded with $y = \dfrac{x - 100}{5}$. The coded values have mean $2.4$ and standard deviation $1.5$. Find the mean and standard deviation of $x$.
@step $x = 100 + 5y$, so $\bar{x} = 100 + 5 \times 2.4 = 112$.
@step $\sigma_x = 5 \times 1.5 = 7.5$ (the $100$ does not change the spread).
@answer $\bar{x} = 112$ and $\sigma_x = 7.5$.
:::

:::summary
- $\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum x}{n}$ and $\sigma^2 = \dfrac{\sum x^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2$; $\sigma$ is the square root.
- For a frequency table, use $\sum f$, $\sum fx$ and $\sum fx^2$.
- For coding $y = \dfrac{x - a}{b}$: $\bar{x} = a + b\bar{y}$ and $\sigma_x = b\,\sigma_y$.
- Adding a constant never changes the standard deviation.
:::
