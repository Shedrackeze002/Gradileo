“Half the base times the height” needs the height of a triangle, which is often not given. When two sides and the angle between them are known, trigonometry gives the height, and with it the area.

## Area = ½ab sin C

:::definition Area of a triangle
For a triangle with sides $a$ and $b$ and the angle $C$ between them,
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C.$$
The angle must be the **included** angle: the one between the two sides used.
:::

The height from one end of $a$ to side $b$ is $a\sin C$, so the area is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times a\sin C$.

:::example Two sides and the angle between
Find the area of a triangle with $a = 7$ cm, $b = 9$ cm and $C = 40^\circ$.
@step Area $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 9 \times \sin 40^\circ$
@answer Area $\approx 20.2$ cm²
:::

## Problems with areas

Since $\sin C = \sin(180^\circ - C)$, an area and two sides give two possible angles, one acute and one obtuse.

:::example Two possible angles
A triangle has $a = 5$ cm, $b = 8$ cm and area $12$ cm². Find the two possible values of $C$.
@step $12 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 8 \times \sin C$, so $\sin C = 0.6$.
@step $\sin^{-1} 0.6 \approx 36.9^\circ$, and $180^\circ - 36.9^\circ = 143.1^\circ$.
@answer $C \approx 36.9^\circ$ or $C \approx 143.1^\circ$.
:::

With three sides, use the cosine rule to find an angle, then $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. A parallelogram with sides $a$ and $b$ and angle $\theta$ is two such triangles, so its area is $ab\sin\theta$.

:::summary
- Area of a triangle $= \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, with $C$ the angle between $a$ and $b$.
- From an area and two sides, $\sin C$ gives an acute angle and an obtuse one, $180^\circ$ minus it.
- Three sides: the cosine rule first, then the area formula.
- A parallelogram has area $ab\sin\theta$.
:::
