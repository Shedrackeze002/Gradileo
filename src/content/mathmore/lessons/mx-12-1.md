Mechanics is the part of mathematics that describes how things move and why. Before we can use any formula, we need two things: every quantity in the right units, and a clear model of the situation that says what we will ignore.

## Quantities and SI units

:::definition SI units
**SI units** are the standard units scientists use everywhere. The three base units we need are the **metre** (m) for length, the **kilogram** (kg) for mass and the **second** (s) for time. Other units are **derived** from them: speed in metres per second (m/s), acceleration in metres per second per second (m/s²), and force in **newtons** (N), where $1$ N is the force that gives a mass of $1$ kg an acceleration of $1$ m/s².
:::

A quantity with only a size, such as a mass of $5$ kg or a speed of $12$ m/s, is a **scalar**. A quantity with a size and a direction, such as a velocity of $12$ m/s due east or a force of $30$ N downwards, is a **vector**.

:::example Changing a speed into m/s
A car travels at $72$ km/h. Write this speed in metres per second.
@step $72$ km $= 72\,000$ m and $1$ hour $= 3600$ s.
@step $72\,000 \div 3600 = 20$
@answer $72$ km/h $= 20$ m/s. (Dividing by $3.6$ gives the same answer.)
:::

## Mass and weight

Mass is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms. **Weight** is the force of gravity on it, so it is measured in newtons. Near the Earth's surface every object falls with the same acceleration $g$, so its weight is
$$W = mg$$
where $m$ is the mass in kilograms. A level questions use $g = 9.8$ m/s² unless they say otherwise.

:::example Finding a weight
A bag of flour has a mass of $1500$ g. Find its weight, taking $g = 9.8$ m/s².
@step $1500$ g $= 1.5$ kg.
@step $W = mg = 1.5 \times 9.8 = 14.7$
@answer The weight is $14.7$ N.
:::

:::mistake Mass in grams
The formula $W = mg$ needs the mass in kilograms. Using $1500$ instead of $1.5$ makes the weight a thousand times too big.
:::

## Modelling assumptions

:::definition Modelling assumption
A **modelling assumption** is a simplification we choose to make so that a real situation can be described with mathematics. Each assumption ignores something small, and it tells us which forces and rules to use.
:::

These are the words that appear again and again.

| Word | What it means | What it lets us assume |
|---|---|---|
| particle | its size can be ignored | all its mass is at one point, and it does not turn |
| light | its mass can be ignored | the tension is the same all along a string |
| inextensible | it does not stretch | joined objects move with the same speed and acceleration |
| smooth | there is no friction | the only contact force is at right angles to the surface |
| rough | friction acts | a friction force acts along the surface |
| rigid | it does not bend | a rod can push as well as pull |
| uniform | its mass is spread evenly | its weight acts at its centre |

:::summary
- Use SI units: metres, kilograms and seconds, with speeds in m/s, accelerations in m/s² and forces in newtons.
- To change km/h into m/s, divide by $3.6$; to change m/s into km/h, multiply by $3.6$.
- Weight is a force: $W = mg$, with $m$ in kilograms and $g = 9.8$ m/s².
- Modelling words such as particle, light, inextensible, smooth and uniform each remove one complication, and each tells us something we can use in the working.
:::
