A list of adult heights that includes $17.4$ m has an error in it, and a weather station that records $-60$ °C in July has a faulty sensor. Before data are summarized they are checked for values like these, which can pull the mean and standard deviation a long way.

## Finding outliers

:::definition Outlier
An **outlier** is a value that is very different from the rest of the data. Two common rules decide what “very different” means:
- more than $1.5 \times$ the interquartile range below $Q_1$ or above $Q_3$;
- more than $2$ (or $3$) standard deviations from the mean.

The question will say which rule to use.
:::

:::example The 1.5 × IQR rule
For a data set, $Q_1 = 30$ and $Q_3 = 42$. Is $63$ an outlier?
@step IQR $= 42 - 30 = 12$, and $1.5 \times 12 = 18$.
@step The limits are $30 - 18 = 12$ and $42 + 18 = 60$.
@answer $63 > 60$, so $63$ is an outlier.
:::

## Cleaning data

**Cleaning data** means removing values that are errors, such as impossible readings, and deciding what to do with outliers. An outlier that is a real value may be kept; one caused by an error should be removed. Removing a value changes the totals: take it out of $\sum x$ and $\sum x^2$, reduce $n$ by one, and work the statistics out again.

:::example Removing an outlier
$20$ values have mean $15$. The value $35$ is found to be a recording error and is removed. Find the new mean.
@step Total $= 20 \times 15 = 300$.
@step Without the error: $300 - 35 = 265$, from $19$ values.
@answer New mean $= \dfrac{265}{19} \approx 13.9$.
:::

:::tip The median barely moves
Removing an outlier changes the mean and the standard deviation a lot, but the median and the quartiles hardly at all. That is why the median and IQR are preferred for data with outliers.

:::summary
- An outlier lies beyond $Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ or $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$, or more than $2$ standard deviations from the mean.
- Cleaning data removes errors; real outliers may be kept, depending on the context.
- To remove a value, take it out of $\sum x$ and $\sum x^2$ and reduce $n$ by one.
:::
