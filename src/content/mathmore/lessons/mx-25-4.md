Any three points that are not in a line lie on a circle, but a fourth point usually misses it. When all four corners of a quadrilateral do lie on one circle, its angles obey a simple rule.

## Cyclic quadrilaterals

:::definition Cyclic quadrilateral
A **cyclic quadrilateral** is a quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on one circle. In a cyclic quadrilateral, **opposite angles add up to $180°$**.
:::

In the figure, $A$ and $C$ are opposite, and so are $B$ and $D$, so $A + C = 180°$ and $B + D = 180°$.

The reason is the angle at the centre. The angle at $A$ stands on the arc $BCD$, and the angle at $C$ stands on the other arc $BAD$. Each is half the angle at the centre on its arc, and the two angles at the centre make a full turn of $360°$. So $A + C = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 360° = 180°$.

:::example Opposite angles
$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with $A = 74°$ and $B = 112°$. Find $C$ and $D$.
@step $C = 180° - 74° = 106°$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral)
@step $D = 180° - 112° = 68°$
@answer $C = 106°$ and $D = 68°$
:::

## The exterior angle

If the side $BC$ is extended beyond $C$ to $E$, then angle $DCE$ and angle $BCD$ lie on a straight line and add up to $180°$. But $A$ and angle $BCD$ also add up to $180°$. So the **exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle**: $\angle DCE = A$.

:::mistake Opposite, not adjacent
The rule is about opposite angles. Angles next to each other, such as $A$ and $B$, do not usually add up to $180°$.
:::

:::check mx-cyclic-quadrilateral 2
:::

When the opposite angles are given as expressions, we add them and put the sum equal to $180$.

:::example Solving for x
In a cyclic quadrilateral $PQRS$, $P = (3x + 10)°$ and $R = (2x + 20)°$. Find $x$ and angle $P$.
@step $(3x + 10) + (2x + 20) = 180$
@step $5x + 30 = 180$, so $x = 30$.
@answer $x = 30$ and $P = 100°$
:::

:::check mx-cyclic-quadrilateral 3
:::

## The alternate segment theorem

A tangent touches a circle at one point. A chord drawn from that point makes an angle with the tangent, and it also divides the circle into two segments.

:::definition Alternate segment theorem
The **alternate segment theorem** says that the angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact equals the angle in the alternate segment, which is the angle that the chord subtends at the circumference on the other side of the chord.
:::

In the figure, $PTQ$ is the tangent at $T$. The angle between the tangent $TQ$ and the chord $TB$ equals angle $TAB$, and both are marked $x$. In the same way, the angle between $TP$ and the chord $TA$ equals angle $TBA$.

:::example One angle
In the figure, angle $BTQ = 58°$. Find angle $TAB$.
@step $\angle TAB = \angle BTQ$ (alternate segment theorem)
@answer $58°$
:::

:::example All three angles of the triangle
In the figure, angle $BTQ = 58°$ and angle $ATP = 47°$. Find the angles of triangle $TAB$.
@step $\angle TAB = \angle BTQ = 58°$ (alternate segment theorem)
@step $\angle TBA = \angle ATP = 47°$ (alternate segment theorem)
@step $\angle ATB = 180° - 58° - 47°$ (angles on the straight line $PTQ$)
@answer $58°$, $47°$ and $75°$
:::

Put a finger on the chord. The angle between the tangent and the chord is on one side of it, and the equal angle is at the circumference on the other side, facing the chord.

:::summary
- A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on one circle.
- Its opposite angles add up to $180°$, and an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.
- Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
:::
