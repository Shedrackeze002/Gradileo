No real number squares to give $-1$: every real number squared is zero or positive. So the equation $x^2 = -1$ has no real solution. Mathematicians gave it one anyway, by inventing a new number, and the numbers built from it turned out to be useful everywhere from electrical engineering to the study of waves.

## The imaginary unit

:::definition The imaginary unit and complex numbers
The **imaginary unit** $i$ is a number with $i^2 = -1$. A **complex number** is a number $a + bi$, where $a$ and $b$ are real. $a$ is its **real part** and $b$ its **imaginary part**.
:::

Every real number is a complex number with imaginary part $0$, and numbers such as $3i$ (with real part $0$) are called **imaginary**. Square roots of negative numbers can now be written: $\sqrt{-9} = \sqrt{9}\,\sqrt{-1} = 3i$.

## Adding, subtracting and multiplying

Complex numbers follow the usual rules of algebra, with $i$ treated like a variable. The only new rule is $i^2 = -1$.

:::example Adding and subtracting
Simplify $(3 + 5i) + (2 - 7i)$ and $(3 + 5i) - (2 - 7i)$.
@step Add the real parts and the imaginary parts: $(3 + 2) + (5 - 7)i = 5 - 2i$.
@step Subtract both parts: $(3 - 2) + (5 - (-7))i = 1 + 12i$.
@answer $5 - 2i$ and $1 + 12i$
:::

:::

:::example Multiplying
Multiply $(2 + 3i)(4 - i)$.
@step Expand: $8 - 2i + 12i - 3i^2$.
@step Replace $i^2$ with $-1$: $-3i^2 = 3$.
@step Collect: $(8 + 3) + (-2 + 12)i = 11 + 10i$.
@answer $11 + 10i$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting that $i^2 = -1$
$(2i)(3i) = 6i^2 = -6$, not $6i^2$ left as it is, and not $6$. Always replace $i^2$ with $-1$.
:::

:::check mx-complex-arith 2
:::

:::tip Powers of i
The powers of $i$ repeat every four: $i^1 = i$, $i^2 = -1$, $i^3 = -i$, $i^4 = 1$, then $i^5 = i$ again. So $i^{23} = i^{20} \times i^{3} = -i$, using the remainder when $23$ is divided by $4$.
:::

## Conjugates and division

:::definition Complex conjugate
The **conjugate** of $a + bi$ is $a - bi$. A complex number times its conjugate is real: $(a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2$.
:::

To divide by a complex number, multiply the top and the bottom by the conjugate of the bottom. The bottom becomes real, and the top can be divided part by part.

:::example Dividing
Divide $\dfrac{7 + i}{1 + 2i}$.
@step Multiply top and bottom by $1 - 2i$. Bottom: $(1 + 2i)(1 - 2i) = 1 + 4 = 5$.
@step Top: $(7 + i)(1 - 2i) = 7 - 14i + i - 2i^2 = 9 - 13i$.
@answer $\dfrac{9 - 13i}{5} = \dfrac{9}{5} - \dfrac{13}{5}i$
:::

:::summary
- $i$ is a number with $i^2 = -1$; a complex number is $a + bi$ with real part $a$ and imaginary part $b$.
- Add and subtract the real and imaginary parts separately; multiply by expanding and replacing $i^2$ with $-1$.
- The powers of $i$ repeat every four: $i, -1, -i, 1$.
- To divide, multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom, $a - bi$.
:::
