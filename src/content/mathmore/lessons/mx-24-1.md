The formula $v = u + at$ gives the final speed $v$ straight away. But what if we know the final speed and want the acceleration $a$? We can rearrange the formula once, so that it gives $a$ directly.

## The subject of a formula

:::definition Subject of a formula
The **subject** of a formula is the letter that stands alone on one side of the equals sign. In $v = u + at$ the subject is $v$. **Changing the subject** means rearranging the formula so that a different letter stands alone, for example $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$. We do it so that we can calculate that quantity directly.
:::

The method is the same as solving an equation: we undo what has been done to the new subject, one step at a time, and do the same to both sides.

:::example One step, then two
Make $u$ the subject of $v = u + at$, and then make $a$ the subject.
@step For $u$: subtract $at$ from both sides. $u = v - at$.
@step For $a$: subtract $u$ from both sides. $v - u = at$.
@step Divide both sides by $t$.
@answer $u = v - at$, and $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$
:::

:::

## Substituting after rearranging

Once the letter we want is the subject, we substitute the numbers.

:::example Finding the acceleration
In $v = u + at$, $v = 26$, $u = 8$ and $t = 6$. Find $a$.
@step $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$
@step $a = \dfrac{26 - 8}{6} = \dfrac{18}{6}$
@answer $a = 3$
:::

:::check mx-subject-evaluate 1
:::

## Fractions and brackets

When the formula has a fraction, we multiply both sides by the denominator first. When it has a bracket, we divide by the number outside it, or expand it.

:::example A fraction and a bracket
Make $a$ the subject of $A = \dfrac{h(a + b)}{2}$.
@step Multiply both sides by $2$: $2A = h(a + b)$.
@step Divide both sides by $h$: $\dfrac{2A}{h} = a + b$.
@step Subtract $b$ from both sides.
@answer $a = \dfrac{2A}{h} - b$
:::

:::mistake Do the same to the whole side
To undo "$+\,b$" in $\dfrac{2A}{h} = a + b$ we subtract $b$ from the whole left side, giving $\dfrac{2A}{h} - b$. It is not $\dfrac{2A - b}{h}$, where $b$ has wrongly been divided by $h$ as well.
:::

:::

:::check mx-subject-evaluate 2
:::

## Squares, roots and a subject that appears twice

We undo a square with a square root, and a square root by squaring both sides.

:::example A square
Make $r$ the subject of $A = \pi r^2$.
@step Divide both sides by $\pi$: $\dfrac{A}{\pi} = r^2$.
@step Take the square root of both sides. A radius is positive, so we take the positive root.
@answer $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}$
:::

When the new subject appears twice, we collect its terms on one side and factorize.

:::example The subject appears twice
Make $x$ the subject of $ax + b = cx + d$.
@step Collect the terms in $x$: $ax - cx = d - b$.
@step Factorize: $x(a - c) = d - b$.
@answer $x = \dfrac{d - b}{a - c}$
:::

:::check mx-change-subject 3
:::

:::check mx-subject-evaluate 3
:::

:::summary
- The subject of a formula is the letter alone on one side.
- To change the subject, undo each operation in turn and do the same to both sides.
- Clear fractions by multiplying, undo squares with square roots, and undo roots by squaring.
- If the new subject appears twice, collect its terms and factorize.
:::
