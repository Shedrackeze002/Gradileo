The median splits a data set into two halves. Splitting each half again gives four groups with about the same number of values in each. The points where the data split are the **quartiles**, and a box plot draws them.

## Quartiles and the five-number summary

:::definition Quartiles
Put the values in order. The **median** splits them into a lower half and an upper half. The **lower quartile** $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half, and the **upper quartile** $Q_3$ is the median of the upper half. When the number of values is odd, the median itself is left out of both halves.
:::

:::definition Five-number summary
The **five-number summary** of a data set is its minimum, lower quartile, median, upper quartile and maximum.
:::

:::example Finding the quartiles (an odd number of values)
Find the five-number summary of $12,\ 15,\ 9,\ 20,\ 14,\ 18,\ 11$.
@step In order: $9,\ 11,\ 12,\ 14,\ 15,\ 18,\ 20$. There are $7$ values, so the median is the $4$th: $14$.
@step The lower half is $9,\ 11,\ 12$ (leaving out the median). Its median is $Q_1 = 11$.
@step The upper half is $15,\ 18,\ 20$. Its median is $Q_3 = 18$.
@answer Minimum $9$, $Q_1 = 11$, median $14$, $Q_3 = 18$, maximum $20$.
:::

:::example Finding the quartiles (an even number of values)
Find the quartiles of $3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 8,\ 9,\ 12,\ 13,\ 15$.
@step There are $8$ values. The median is halfway between the $4$th and $5$th: $(8 + 9) \div 2 = 8.5$.
@step The lower half is $3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 8$. Its median is $(5 + 6) \div 2 = 5.5$, so $Q_1 = 5.5$.
@step The upper half is $9,\ 12,\ 13,\ 15$. Its median is $(12 + 13) \div 2 = 12.5$, so $Q_3 = 12.5$.
@answer $Q_1 = 5.5$, median $8.5$, $Q_3 = 12.5$.
:::

## The interquartile range

:::definition Interquartile range
The **interquartile range** (IQR) is $Q_3 - Q_1$: the spread of the middle half of the data.
:::

The range uses only the two most extreme values, so a single unusual value changes it a lot. The IQR ignores the top and bottom quarters, so it describes the spread of the typical values better. In the first example, the IQR is $18 - 11 = 7$ and the range is $20 - 9 = 11$.

## Box plots

A **box plot** (also called a box-and-whisker plot) draws the five-number summary above a number line. The box runs from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$ with a line at the median, and the whiskers reach out to the minimum and the maximum.

:::

Each of the four parts (left whisker, left of the box, right of the box, right whisker) holds about a quarter of the values. A long part does not hold more values; it means those values are more spread out.

:::example Reading a box plot
In the box plot above, find the median and the IQR. About what percent of the values are less than $11$?
@step The line in the box is at $14$, so the median is $14$.
@step The box runs from $11$ to $18$, so the IQR is $18 - 11 = 7$.
@step Values less than $Q_1 = 11$ are on the left whisker: about a quarter of them.
@answer Median $14$, IQR $7$, and about $25\%$ of the values are less than $11$.
:::

:::mistake A longer part holds more values
The right whisker above is short and the right part of the box is long, but each holds about a quarter of the data. Length shows spread, not how many.
:::

## Comparing box plots

Box plots drawn over the same number line make two groups easy to compare: the medians show which group is typically higher, and the lengths of the boxes show which middle half is more spread out.

:::summary
- The quartiles split ordered data into four parts with about the same number of values: $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half and $Q_3$ the median of the upper half.
- The five-number summary is the minimum, $Q_1$, the median, $Q_3$ and the maximum; a box plot draws it.
- The interquartile range, $Q_3 - Q_1$, is the spread of the middle half and is not thrown off by extreme values.
- Each part of a box plot holds about a quarter of the data; a long part means spread-out values, not more values.
:::
