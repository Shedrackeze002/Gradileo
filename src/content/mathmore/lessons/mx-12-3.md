Drop a stone and it speeds up as it falls; throw a ball upwards and it slows, stops for an instant, then falls back. If we ignore air resistance, both move with the same constant acceleration, so the suvat formulas describe them exactly.

## Acceleration due to gravity

:::definition Free fall
An object moving under gravity alone, with no air resistance, is in **free fall**. It has a constant acceleration of $g$ **downwards**, the **acceleration due to gravity**. Near the Earth's surface $g \approx 9.8$ m/s², whether the object is going up, coming down or at its highest point.
:::

Choose a positive direction before writing anything down. If upwards is positive, then $a = -9.8$ for every object in free fall, and a point below the start has a negative displacement.

:::example The greatest height
A ball is projected vertically upwards at $14.7$ m/s. Find its greatest height above the point of projection and the time it takes to get there.
@step Upwards positive: $u = 14.7$, $a = -9.8$. At the top the ball is momentarily at rest, so $v = 0$.
@step $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$: $0 = 14.7^2 - 19.6s$, so $s = \dfrac{216.09}{19.6} = 11.025$.
@step $v = u + at$: $0 = 14.7 - 9.8t$, so $t = 1.5$.
@answer The greatest height is about $11.0$ m, reached after $1.5$ s.
:::

## Falling to the ground

When an object lands below its starting point, the displacement is negative if upwards is positive. The equation for the time is then usually a quadratic, and we keep the positive root.

:::example Thrown upwards from a cliff
A stone is thrown vertically upwards at $7$ m/s from the edge of a cliff $20$ m above the sea. Find the time it takes to reach the sea and its speed as it lands.
@step Upwards positive: $u = 7$, $a = -9.8$, $s = -20$.
@step $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$: $-20 = 7t - 4.9t^2$, so $4.9t^2 - 7t - 20 = 0$.
@step $t = \dfrac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 + 392}}{9.8} = \dfrac{7 \pm 21}{9.8}$, and time is positive, so $t = \dfrac{28}{9.8} \approx 2.86$.
@step $v^2 = u^2 + 2as = 49 + 2(-9.8)(-20) = 441$, so the speed is $21$ m/s.
@answer It reaches the sea after about $2.86$ s, at $21$ m/s.
:::

:::tip Symmetry
With no air resistance the motion up and the motion down mirror each other: a ball takes as long to rise to its highest point as to fall back to where it started, and it passes each height at the same speed on the way up and on the way down.
:::

:::summary
- In free fall the acceleration is $g = 9.8$ m/s² downwards, all the time.
- Choose a positive direction; with upwards positive, $a = -9.8$ and points below the start have negative displacement.
- At the greatest height $v = 0$.
- A quadratic in $t$ often has two roots: keep the one that fits the question (the positive one, or the later one for the way down).
:::
