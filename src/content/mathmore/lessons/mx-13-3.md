On a slope, gravity still pulls straight down, but the object can only move along the slope. The trick is to resolve forces in two new directions: parallel to the slope, where the motion happens, and perpendicular to it, where the forces balance.

## Resolving on a slope

:::definition Inclined plane
An **inclined plane** is a flat surface at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal. For an object of mass $m$ on it, the weight $mg$ resolves into $mg\sin\alpha$ **down the slope** and $mg\cos\alpha$ **into the slope**. The normal reaction is perpendicular to the slope, and any friction acts along it.
:::

:::example Sliding down a smooth slope
A particle is released on a smooth slope at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. Find its acceleration.
@step Down the slope, the only force is $mg\sin 30^\circ$.
@step $mg\sin 30^\circ = ma$, so $a = g\sin 30^\circ = 9.8 \times 0.5$.
@answer $a = 4.9$ m/s², whatever the mass.
:::

## Rough slopes

On a rough slope, $R = mg\cos\alpha$ (if no other force acts at an angle to the slope), and friction $\mu R$ acts up the slope when the object slides down, or down the slope when it moves up.

:::example Sliding down a rough slope
A particle of mass $5$ kg slides down a slope at $30^\circ$, with $\mu = 0.2$. Find its acceleration.
@step Perpendicular: $R = 5 \times 9.8\cos 30^\circ \approx 42.4$ N, so friction $= 0.2R \approx 8.49$ N up the slope.
@step Parallel: $5 \times 9.8\sin 30^\circ - 8.49 = 5a$, so $24.5 - 8.49 = 5a$.
@answer $a \approx 3.20$ m/s².
:::

:::check mx-inclined-plane 2
:::

:::mistake Friction always against the motion
Friction changes direction with the motion. A box pushed up a slope has friction acting down the slope, adding to $mg\sin\alpha$; once it slides back down, friction acts up the slope.
:::

:::check mx-inclined-plane 3
:::

## Connected particles on a slope

A particle on a slope may be joined by a string over a pulley at the top to a particle hanging freely. Write $F = ma$ for each particle in its own direction of motion, then add the equations.

:::example A slope and a pulley
Particle $A$ (mass $4$ kg) is on a smooth slope with $\tan\alpha = \tfrac{3}{4}$, joined over a smooth pulley at the top to $B$ (mass $6$ kg) hanging freely. Find the acceleration and the tension.
@step $\tan\alpha = \tfrac{3}{4}$ gives $\sin\alpha = \tfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos\alpha = \tfrac{4}{5}$.
@step $A$ (up the slope): $T - 4g \times \tfrac{3}{5} = 4a$. $B$ (down): $6g - T = 6a$.
@step Adding: $6g - 2.4g = 10a$, so $a = 0.36 \times 9.8 \approx 3.53$ m/s².
@answer $a \approx 3.53$ m/s², and from $B$, $T = 6(9.8 - 3.528) \approx 37.6$ N.
:::

:::tip Exact sines and cosines
When a question gives $\tan\alpha = \tfrac{3}{4}$ or $\tfrac{5}{12}$, sketch a right-angled triangle with those sides. The hypotenuse is $5$ or $13$, so $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$ are exact fractions.
:::

:::summary
- Resolve parallel and perpendicular to the slope: the weight gives $mg\sin\alpha$ down the slope and $mg\cos\alpha$ into it.
- With no other force at an angle, $R = mg\cos\alpha$, and friction is at most $\mu R$, against the motion.
- An object on a rough slope stays put once it stops only if $\mu \ge \tan\alpha$.
- For connected particles, write $F = ma$ for each in its own direction, and add to remove the tension.
:::
