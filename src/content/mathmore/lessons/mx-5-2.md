A shop records the sales of three items over two days in a grid of numbers. A grid like this is a matrix, and matrices can be added, scaled and multiplied, which makes them a tool for organizing data, transforming shapes and solving systems of equations.

## Matrices

:::definition Matrix
A **matrix** is a rectangular array of numbers in rows and columns. A matrix with $m$ rows and $n$ columns has **dimensions** $m \times n$. The number in row $i$ and column $j$ is the **entry** $a_{ij}$.
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \text{ is } 2 \times 3, \text{ and } a_{13} = 5.$$
:::

## Adding and multiplying by a scalar

:::definition Adding and scalar multiplication
Matrices with the same dimensions are added entry by entry. To multiply a matrix by a number $k$ (a **scalar**), multiply every entry by $k$.
:::

:::example Combining matrices
Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 1 & -6 \end{bmatrix}$. Find $2A + B$.
@step $2A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 8 \\ -4 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$.
@step Add $B$ entry by entry.
@answer $2A + B = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 8 \\ -3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
:::

## Multiplying matrices

:::definition Matrix multiplication
The entry in row $i$, column $j$ of $AB$ is row $i$ of $A$ times column $j$ of $B$: multiply the matching entries and add. The product exists only when $A$ has as many columns as $B$ has rows.
$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ae + bg & af + bh \\ ce + dg & cf + dh \end{bmatrix}$$
:::

:::example Multiplying two matrices
Find $AB$ for the matrices $A$ and $B$ above.
@step Row 1, column 1: $1 \times 5 + 4 \times 1 = 9$. Row 1, column 2: $1 \times 0 + 4 \times (-6) = -24$.
@step Row 2, column 1: $-2 \times 5 + 3 \times 1 = -7$. Row 2, column 2: $-2 \times 0 + 3 \times (-6) = -18$.
@answer $AB = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -24 \\ -7 & -18 \end{bmatrix}$
:::

:::mistake Multiplying entry by entry
Matrices are **not** multiplied entry by entry: each entry of the product combines a whole row with a whole column. Also, the order matters: in general $AB \ne BA$.
:::

:::summary
- A matrix is a rectangular array; an $m \times n$ matrix has $m$ rows and $n$ columns.
- Add matrices of the same size entry by entry; a scalar multiplies every entry.
- Each entry of $AB$ is a row of $A$ times a column of $B$; the order matters, and $AB$ is usually not $BA$.
:::
