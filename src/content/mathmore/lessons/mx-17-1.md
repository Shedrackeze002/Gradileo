Some statements are hard to prove directly: how could we show that no fraction at all is equal to $\sqrt{2}$? Instead, we suppose there is one, and show that this leads to something impossible.

## How a proof by contradiction works

:::definition Proof by contradiction
A **proof by contradiction** begins by assuming that the statement is **false** (its **negation**). It then reasons from that assumption until it reaches a **contradiction**: something impossible, or something that clashes with the assumption. So the assumption was wrong, and the statement is true.
:::

The negation of “every … is …” is “there is one that is not”; the negation of “if $A$, then $B$” is “$A$ is true and $B$ is false”.

## √2 is irrational

:::example The irrationality of √2
Prove that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.
@step Assume $\sqrt{2}$ is rational: $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a}{b}$, with $a$ and $b$ integers that have no common factor.
@step Squaring: $a^2 = 2b^2$, so $a^2$ is even, and therefore $a$ is even: $a = 2c$.
@step Then $4c^2 = 2b^2$, so $b^2 = 2c^2$ is even, and $b$ is even too.
@answer $a$ and $b$ are both even, so they have a common factor $2$: a contradiction. So $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.
:::

:::check mx-contradiction-logic 2
:::

:::mistake Assume the opposite, not something else
The first line must be the exact negation of the statement. To prove “$\sqrt{2}$ is irrational”, assume “$\sqrt{2}$ is rational”, not “$\sqrt{2}$ is an integer”.
:::

## There are infinitely many primes

Assume there are only finitely many primes, $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n$. Let $N = p_1p_2 \cdots p_n + 1$. Each $p_i$ divides the product, so $N$ leaves a remainder of $1$ when divided by every prime on the list. But $N > 1$ has a prime factor, and it is not on the list, which was meant to hold every prime: a contradiction.

:::example The first few primes
For the primes $2, 3, 5, 7$: $N = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 + 1$.
@step $N = 210 + 1 = 211$.
@answer Dividing $211$ by $2$, $3$, $5$ or $7$ leaves a remainder of $1$, so its prime factors (here $211$ itself) are new primes.
:::

:::tip N need not be prime
$N = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11 \times 13 + 1 = 30031 = 59 \times 509$ is not prime. The proof does not need $N$ to be prime: only that its prime factors are missing from the list.

:::summary
- A proof by contradiction assumes the negation of the statement and reaches something impossible.
- $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational: assuming $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a}{b}$ in lowest terms makes $a$ and $b$ both even.
- There are infinitely many primes: $p_1p_2 \cdots p_n + 1$ has a prime factor not on any finite list.
:::
