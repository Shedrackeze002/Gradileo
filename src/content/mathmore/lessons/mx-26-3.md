A loan is repaid with equal payments, but the payments add up to more than the amount borrowed. To find a fair repayment we ask what the future payments are worth today.

## Present value

:::definition Present value of an annuity
The **present value** of an annuity is the single amount that, invested today at the given interest rate, would pay for all of its payments. Each payment is made at the end of its period, so the first one is made one period from now.
:::

A payment $x$ made $k$ periods from now is worth $\dfrac{x}{(1 + i)^k}$ today. Adding these for $k = 1$ to $n$ gives a geometric series, and its sum is

$$P = \frac{x\left[1 - (1 + i)^{-n}\right]}{i}$$

Here $x$ is the payment, $i$ is the interest rate for one period and $n$ is the number of payments.

:::example A fund that pays out
A fund must pay \$5,000 at the end of every year for 4 years. It earns $8\%$ per year, compounded annually. How much must it hold now?
@step $x = 5000$, $i = 0.08$ and $n = 4$.
@step $P = \dfrac{5000\left[1 - 1.08^{-4}\right]}{0.08} = \dfrac{5000 \times 0.264970}{0.08}$
@answer \$16,560.63
:::

The four payments total \$20,000, but the fund needs less than that now because it keeps earning interest while it pays out.

## A loan is a present value

When a bank lends money, the amount it lends is the present value of the repayments it will receive. So the same formula tells us how large a loan a given repayment can support.

:::example How much can be borrowed
A borrower can repay \$400 at the end of every month for 3 years. The bank charges $6\%$ per year, compounded monthly. Find the largest loan.
@step $i = \dfrac{0.06}{12} = 0.005$ and $n = 12 \times 3 = 36$.
@step $P = \dfrac{400\left[1 - 1.005^{-36}\right]}{0.005}$
@answer \$13,148.41
:::

:::mistake The power is negative
The present value formula uses $(1 + i)^{-n}$, with a negative power. Using $(1 + i)^n$ instead gives the future value formula's bracket and a wrong, much larger answer.
:::

## Finding the repayment

More often the loan $P$ is known and the repayment $x$ is wanted. Rearranging the formula gives

$$x = \frac{P \times i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}$$

:::example A loan repaid monthly
\$15,000 is borrowed and repaid with equal payments at the end of every month for 5 years. Interest is $9\%$ per year, compounded monthly. Find the repayment, and the total interest paid.
@step $i = \dfrac{0.09}{12} = 0.0075$ and $n = 12 \times 5 = 60$.
@step $x = \dfrac{15000 \times 0.0075}{1 - 1.0075^{-60}} = \dfrac{112.5}{0.361300}$, which is \$311.38.
@step Total repaid: $60 \times 311.38 = 18682.80$.
@step Interest: $18682.80 - 15000 = 3682.80$.
@answer The repayment is \$311.38 a month, and about \$3,683 of interest is paid.
:::

:::tip Paying over a longer time
A longer loan has smaller repayments but more of them, and the total interest is larger. The same \$15,000 over 10 years costs about \$190 a month, but the interest rises to about \$7,800.
:::

:::summary
- The present value of an annuity is what its future payments are worth today: $P = \dfrac{x\left[1 - (1 + i)^{-n}\right]}{i}$.
- A loan is the present value of its repayments.
- Repayment on a loan: $x = \dfrac{P \times i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}$.
- Total interest is the total repaid minus the amount borrowed.
:::
