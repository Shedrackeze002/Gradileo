When an object speeds up or slows down at a steady rate, five quantities describe its motion along a straight line, and five formulas link them. Choosing the right formula is the whole skill: we pick the one that leaves out the quantity we neither know nor want.

## The five quantities

:::definition Constant acceleration
An object has **constant acceleration** (or uniform acceleration) when its velocity changes by the same amount every second. Its motion over a time interval is described by five quantities, often called **suvat**:
- $s$, the **displacement** (in m), how far it is from where it started, with a direction;
- $u$, the initial velocity (in m/s);
- $v$, the final velocity (in m/s);
- $a$, the acceleration (in m/s²), negative when the object slows down (a **deceleration**);
- $t$, the time taken (in s).
:::

The velocity–time graph of constant acceleration is a straight line from $u$ to $v$, so its gradient is the acceleration and the area under it is the displacement.

From the gradient, $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$, so $v = u + at$. From the area of the trapezium, $s = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)t$. Eliminating one letter at a time from these two gives the other three:

$$v = u + at \qquad s = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)t \qquad s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \qquad s = vt - \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \qquad v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

## Choosing the formula

Write down the five letters, fill in the three you know and the one you want. The fifth letter is missing from exactly one formula, and that is the formula to use.

:::example Finding a displacement
A car moving at $4$ m/s accelerates at $2$ m/s² for $5$ s. How far does it travel?
@step $u = 4$, $a = 2$, $t = 5$, and we want $s$. We do not know or want $v$.
@step The formula without $v$ is $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$.
@step $s = 4 \times 5 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 5^2 = 20 + 25 = 45$
@answer The car travels $45$ m.
:::

:::example Finding a deceleration
A cyclist moving at $20$ m/s brakes steadily and stops in $50$ m. Find the acceleration.
@step $u = 20$, $v = 0$, $s = 50$, and we want $a$. The time is not needed.
@step $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ gives $0 = 400 + 100a$.
@answer $a = -4$ m/s²: a deceleration of $4$ m/s².
:::

:::check mx-suvat 2
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the direction
Velocity, acceleration and displacement have directions. Choose a positive direction first. An object that is slowing down has an acceleration in the opposite direction to its velocity, so $a$ is negative.
:::

## Problems with more than one stage

When the acceleration changes, each stage needs its own suvat list, and the final velocity of one stage is the initial velocity of the next. A velocity–time sketch helps: the distance is still the area under the graph.

:::example Two vehicles
A lorry passes a point $O$ at a steady $15$ m/s. At that moment a car starts from rest at $O$ and accelerates at $2$ m/s² in the same direction. When does the car catch the lorry?
@step After $t$ seconds the lorry has gone $15t$ m and the car $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times t^2 = t^2$ m.
@step They meet when $t^2 = 15t$, so $t(t - 15) = 0$.
@answer $t = 15$ s (the other solution, $t = 0$, is the start). They are then $225$ m from $O$.
:::

:::summary
- The suvat formulas hold only when the acceleration is constant.
- List $s, u, v, a, t$, mark the three you know and the one you want, and use the formula without the fifth.
- Choose a positive direction; a deceleration is a negative acceleration.
- In problems with stages, the end of one stage is the start of the next, and the area under the velocity–time graph is the distance.
:::
