A grouped frequency table tells us how many values are in each class, but not where the middle value is. Adding the frequencies up as we go, and plotting those running totals, lets us estimate the median and the quartiles.

## Cumulative frequency

:::definition Cumulative frequency
The **cumulative frequency** at a value is the number of values up to and including it: a running total of the frequencies. The last cumulative frequency is the total.
:::

:::example Running totals
The table shows the times $60$ people took to finish a puzzle. Find the cumulative frequencies.

| Time $t$ (minutes) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| $0 < t \le 10$ | $5$ |
| $10 < t \le 20$ | $10$ |
| $20 < t \le 30$ | $20$ |
| $30 < t \le 40$ | $20$ |
| $40 < t \le 50$ | $5$ |

@step $5$, then $5 + 10 = 15$, then $15 + 20 = 35$, then $35 + 20 = 55$, then $55 + 5 = 60$.
@answer The cumulative frequencies are $5,\ 15,\ 35,\ 55$ and $60$.
:::

## Cumulative frequency graphs

:::definition Median, quartiles and interquartile range
The **median** is the middle value, at a cumulative frequency of $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of the total. The **lower quartile** is a quarter of the way through, at $\tfrac{1}{4}$ of the total, and the **upper quartile** is three quarters of the way, at $\tfrac{3}{4}$. The **interquartile range** is upper quartile $-$ lower quartile, and it measures how spread out the middle half of the data is.
:::

:::example The median and quartiles
Use the graph to estimate the median and the interquartile range of the puzzle times.
@step Median: half of $60$ is $30$. Across from $30$ to the graph and down gives $27.5$ minutes.
@step Lower quartile: across from $15$ gives $20$ minutes. Upper quartile: across from $45$ gives $35$ minutes.
@answer The median is about $27.5$ minutes, and the interquartile range is about $35 - 20 = 15$ minutes.
:::

:::mistake Plotting at the middle of the class
The cumulative frequency counts everything **up to the end** of the class, so the point goes at the upper class boundary, not the midpoint.
:::
