The suvat formulas describe motion, but they do not say what causes it. Newton’s laws connect motion to its cause: a resultant force changes how an object moves, and the bigger the mass, the smaller the change.

## Newton’s three laws

:::definition Force and Newton’s laws
A **force** is a push or a pull, measured in newtons (N). The **resultant force** on an object is the single force that has the same effect as all the forces on it together.
- **Newton’s first law:** an object stays at rest, or keeps moving at a constant velocity, unless a resultant force acts on it.
- **Newton’s second law:** the resultant force equals the mass times the acceleration, $F = ma$, and the acceleration is in the direction of the resultant force.
- **Newton’s third law:** when one object exerts a force on another, the second exerts an equal and opposite force on the first.
:::

The forces that appear most often are **weight** ($mg$, downwards), the **normal reaction** $R$ from a surface (at right angles to it), **tension** in a string or a rope (pulling along it), a **driving force** from an engine, and **resistance** to motion.

:::example A car on a level road
A car of mass $1200$ kg has a driving force of $3000$ N and moves against a resistance of $600$ N. Find its acceleration.
@step The resultant force forwards is $3000 - 600 = 2400$ N.
@step $F = ma$: $2400 = 1200a$.
@answer $a = 2$ m/s².
:::

## Forces as vectors

Newton’s second law works with vectors too, $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$: add the forces component by component, then divide by the mass.

:::example Two forces on a particle
Forces $(3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ N and $(5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j})$ N act on a particle of mass $2$ kg. Find its acceleration and the magnitude of the acceleration.
@step Resultant force $= 8\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}$ N.
@step $\mathbf{a} = \tfrac{1}{2}(8\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}) = 4\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}$ m/s².
@answer $|\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{4^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{17} \approx 4.12$ m/s².
:::

## Lifts

A person standing in a lift moves with the lift, so they have the same acceleration. The floor pushes up on them with a normal reaction $R$, and by Newton’s third law they push down on the floor with the same force. That is what a set of bathroom scales in a lift would read.

:::example Going up
A man of mass $70$ kg stands in a lift accelerating upwards at $1.2$ m/s². Find the reaction of the floor on him.
@step Forces on the man: $R$ upwards and $70g$ downwards. The acceleration is upwards.
@step $R - 70 \times 9.8 = 70 \times 1.2$
@answer $R = 70 \times 11 = 770$ N, more than his weight of $686$ N, so he feels heavier.
:::

:::mistake Using the weight as the reaction
The reaction equals the weight only when the acceleration is zero. When the lift accelerates upwards the reaction is bigger than the weight; when it accelerates downwards it is smaller.
:::

:::summary
- A resultant force causes an acceleration: $F = ma$, in the direction of the resultant.
- With no resultant force, an object stays at rest or moves at a constant velocity.
- Forces come in equal and opposite pairs between two objects.
- With vectors, $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$: add the forces component by component.
- In a lift, the reaction on a passenger is $m(g + a)$ when accelerating upwards and $m(g - a)$ when accelerating downwards.
:::
