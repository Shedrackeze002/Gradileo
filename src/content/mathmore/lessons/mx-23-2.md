If it is 10 o’clock now, then in 5 hours it will be 3 o’clock, not 15 o’clock. On a clock, $10 + 5 = 3$. This is arithmetic that goes round in a circle, and it is called modular arithmetic.

## Remainders

:::definition Modular arithmetic
In **arithmetic modulo $m$**, we only keep the remainder after dividing by $m$. The number $m$ is the **modulus**. We write $a \equiv r \pmod m$, read "$a$ is congruent to $r$ modulo $m$", when $a$ and $r$ leave the same remainder on division by $m$. For example, $17 \equiv 2 \pmod 5$, because $17 = 3 \times 5 + 2$.
:::

Modulo $m$, every whole number is congruent to exactly one of $0, 1, 2, \ldots, m - 1$. Modulo $5$, the numbers $2, 7, 12, 17, \ldots$ are all congruent to $2$: each is $5$ more than the one before.

:::example Reducing a number
Find $47 \pmod 6$.
@step The largest multiple of $6$ that is not more than $47$ is $42 = 7 \times 6$.
@step $47 - 42 = 5$
@answer $47 \equiv 5 \pmod 6$
:::

:::

Adding or subtracting the modulus does not change a number’s value modulo $m$. This is how we reduce a negative number: we keep adding $m$ until we reach a number from $0$ to $m - 1$.

:::example A negative number
Find $-8 \pmod 5$.
@step $-8 + 5 = -3$, and $-3 + 5 = 2$.
@answer $-8 \equiv 2 \pmod 5$
:::

:::mistake The remainder is never negative
$-8 = -1 \times 5 - 3$ might suggest a remainder of $-3$, but the values modulo $5$ are $0$ to $4$. We write $-8 = -2 \times 5 + 2$, so the remainder is $2$.
:::

:::check mx-mod-reduce 2
:::

## Days and clocks

The days of the week repeat every $7$ days, so they follow arithmetic modulo $7$. The hours on a clock face follow arithmetic modulo $12$.

:::example Days of the week
Today is Tuesday. Which day will it be in $100$ days?
@step $100 = 14 \times 7 + 2$, so $100 \equiv 2 \pmod 7$.
@step Fourteen whole weeks bring us back to Tuesday. Then we count $2$ more days.
@answer It will be Thursday.
:::

:::check mx-mod-reduce 3
:::

## Adding and multiplying

We write $\oplus$ for addition and $\otimes$ for multiplication modulo $m$: we add or multiply as usual, then reduce the answer.

:::example Modulo 7
Work out $23 \oplus 19$ and $23 \otimes 19$ modulo $7$.
@step $23 \equiv 2$ and $19 \equiv 5 \pmod 7$.
@step $2 + 5 = 7 \equiv 0$, so $23 \oplus 19 = 0$.
@step $2 \times 5 = 10 \equiv 3$, so $23 \otimes 19 = 3$.
@answer $23 \oplus 19 = 0$ and $23 \otimes 19 = 3$, modulo $7$.
:::

:::tip Reduce first
We may reduce each number before we add or multiply, and the answer is the same. It keeps the numbers small: $2 \times 5$ is easier than $23 \times 19 = 437$.
:::

## Solving a congruence

To solve $3x \equiv 2 \pmod 5$ we only need to try $x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$, because every whole number is congruent to one of these.

| $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $3x \pmod 5$ | $0$ | $3$ | $1$ | $4$ | $2$ |

So $x = 4$.

:::

:::summary
- $a \equiv r \pmod m$ means $a$ and $r$ leave the same remainder when divided by $m$.
- The values modulo $m$ are $0, 1, \ldots, m - 1$; add $m$ to a negative number until it is in this range.
- Days of the week work modulo $7$, and clock hours modulo $12$.
- To add or multiply modulo $m$, reduce the numbers, calculate, and reduce again.
:::
