Two archers each score an average of $8$ points an arrow. One scores $8$ almost every time; the other's arrows land anywhere from $4$ to $10$. The mean is the same, but the archers are not. To tell them apart we need a measure of how far the values are from the mean.

## Distance from the mean

For each value, the **deviation** is how far it is from the mean. We care about the distance, not the direction, so we take the absolute value: a value $3$ below the mean and a value $3$ above are both $3$ away.

:::definition Mean absolute deviation
The **mean absolute deviation** (MAD) of a data set is the mean of the distances of the values from the mean.
1. Find the mean.
2. Find the distance of each value from the mean.
3. Find the mean of those distances.
:::

:::example Finding the MAD
Find the mean absolute deviation of $4,\ 7,\ 9,\ 10,\ 15$.
@step Mean: $(4 + 7 + 9 + 10 + 15) \div 5 = 45 \div 5 = 9$.
@step Distances from $9$: $|4 - 9| = 5$, $|7 - 9| = 2$, $|9 - 9| = 0$, $|10 - 9| = 1$, $|15 - 9| = 6$.
@step Mean of the distances: $(5 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 6) \div 5 = 14 \div 5 = 2.8$.
@answer The MAD is $2.8$: on average, a value is $2.8$ away from the mean.
:::

:::mistake Adding the signed deviations
The deviations $-5,\ -2,\ 0,\ 1,\ 6$ add up to $0$: they always do, because the mean is the balance point of the data. That is why the MAD uses distances (absolute values), which are never negative.
:::

## What the MAD tells us

A small MAD means the values sit close to the mean, so the mean describes the data well. A large MAD means the values are spread out. For the archers, the first might have a MAD of $0.4$ points and the second a MAD of $1.8$ points.

:::example Comparing spread
Group A's test scores have mean $70$ and MAD $3$. Group B's have mean $70$ and MAD $11$. Describe the difference.
@step The means are the same, so both groups score $70$ on average.
@step Group A's scores are typically only $3$ points from $70$; Group B's are typically $11$ points away.
@answer Both groups have the same centre, but Group B's scores are much more spread out.
:::

:::summary
- The mean absolute deviation is the mean distance of the values from the mean.
- Find the mean, then each value's distance from it (always positive), then the mean of those distances.
- The signed deviations always add to $0$, so distances are used instead.
- A small MAD means the values are close together; a large MAD means they are spread out.
:::
