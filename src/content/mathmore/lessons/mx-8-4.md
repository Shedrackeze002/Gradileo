The sine, cosine and tangent of an angle are defined for any angle, not just those in a right-angled triangle. Plotting them from $0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$ gives three graphs with repeating patterns, and those patterns let us find every angle that has a given sine, cosine or tangent.

## The three graphs

:::definition Period
A graph that repeats is **periodic**, and its **period** is the length of one repeat. The graphs of $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ have a period of $360^\circ$ and always lie between $-1$ and $1$. The graph of $y = \tan x$ has a period of $180^\circ$, and has **asymptotes** (lines it gets closer and closer to but never meets) at $x = 90^\circ$ and $x = 270^\circ$.
:::

## Stretches and translations

Changing the equation changes the graph in the same ways as for any function:

| Equation | What happens to $y = \sin x$ |
|---|---|
| $y = a\sin x$ | stretched vertically: it goes from $-a$ to $a$ |
| $y = \sin x + c$ | moved up by $c$ |
| $y = \sin(x - c)$ | moved $c$ to the right (for $y = \sin(x + c)$, $c$ to the left) |
| $y = \sin bx$ | squashed horizontally: the period becomes $360^\circ \div b$ |

:::example Maximum and minimum
Find the maximum and minimum values of $y = 3\sin x + 1$, and where the maximum is.
@step $\sin x$ goes from $-1$ to $1$, so $3\sin x$ goes from $-3$ to $3$.
@step Adding $1$ moves the graph up: from $-2$ to $4$.
@answer The maximum is $4$, at $x = 90^\circ$, and the minimum is $-2$.
:::

## Solving equations with the graphs

A calculator's $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$ keys give only one solution. The graphs show where the others are.

:::example Every solution of sin x = 0.5
Solve $\sin x = 0.5$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$.
@step $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$.
@step The line $y = 0.5$ crosses the sine graph twice. The graph is symmetrical about $x = 90^\circ$, so the other solution is $180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$.
@answer $x = 30^\circ$ or $x = 150^\circ$
:::

:::tip Using the symmetry
For $\sin x = k$ the second solution is $180^\circ - x$. For $\cos x = k$ it is $360^\circ - x$. For $\tan x = k$ add $180^\circ$. If the calculator gives a negative angle, add $360^\circ$ (or $180^\circ$ for tan) to bring it into the range first.
:::

:::mistake Stopping at the calculator answer
For $\cos x = -0.5$ the calculator gives $120^\circ$, but the line $y = -0.5$ also crosses the cosine graph at $360^\circ - 120^\circ = 240^\circ$. Always check the graph for a second solution.

:::summary
- $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ have period $360^\circ$ and lie between $-1$ and $1$; $y = \tan x$ has period $180^\circ$ and asymptotes at $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$.
- $y = a\sin x + c$ goes from $c - a$ to $c + a$; $y = \sin(x - c)$ is a translation $c$ to the right; $y = \sin bx$ has period $360^\circ \div b$.
- To solve $\sin x$, $\cos x$ or $\tan x = k$, find one solution with the inverse key, then use the symmetry of the graph for the others.
:::
