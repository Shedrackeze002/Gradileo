Near $\theta = 0$, the graph of $y = \sin\theta$ (in radians) is almost the same as the line $y = \theta$. Engineers and physicists use this every day: for small angles, trigonometric functions can be replaced by simple polynomials.

## The approximations

:::definition Small-angle approximations
When $\theta$ is small and measured in **radians**,
$$\sin\theta \approx \theta, \qquad \cos\theta \approx 1 - \frac{\theta^2}{2}, \qquad \tan\theta \approx \theta.$$
:::

For example, $\cos 0.1 \approx 1 - \dfrac{0.01}{2} = 0.995$, while the true value is $0.995004\ldots$. The approximations get better as $\theta$ gets smaller.

:::mistake Radians only
The approximations are only true in radians. To estimate $\sin 3^\circ$, first convert: $3^\circ = \dfrac{3\pi}{180} \approx 0.0524$ radians.
:::

## Using them in expressions

For $\cos k\theta$, replace $\theta$ by $k\theta$: $\cos k\theta \approx 1 - \dfrac{k^2\theta^2}{2}$.

:::example A ratio for small θ
Find the approximate value of $\dfrac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{\theta\tan\theta}$ when $\theta$ is small.
@step $1 - \cos 2\theta \approx 1 - \left(1 - \dfrac{4\theta^2}{2}\right) = 2\theta^2$
@step $\theta\tan\theta \approx \theta^2$
@answer The expression is approximately $\dfrac{2\theta^2}{\theta^2} = 2$.
:::

:::summary
- For small $\theta$ in radians: $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, $\cos\theta \approx 1 - \dfrac{\theta^2}{2}$, $\tan\theta \approx \theta$.
- For $\cos k\theta$, use $1 - \dfrac{k^2\theta^2}{2}$.
- Convert degrees to radians first, and drop powers above $\theta^2$.
:::
