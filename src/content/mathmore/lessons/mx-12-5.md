A car pulling a trailer and two weights hanging over a pulley have something in common: two objects joined together, moving with the same acceleration. We can look at the whole system or at each part, and using both views together finds the acceleration and the force in the connection.

## Connected particles

:::definition Connected particles
**Connected particles** are objects joined by a string, a rope or a rod so that they move together. If the connection is **inextensible** (it does not stretch), the objects have the same speed and the same acceleration. The force in the connection is a **tension** when it pulls, and a **thrust** (a compression) when a rigid rod pushes.
:::

The force in the connection acts on both objects, equally and in opposite directions (Newton’s third law), so when we treat the system as one object it cancels out.

:::example A car and a trailer
A car of mass $1000$ kg tows a trailer of mass $500$ kg with a light rigid towbar. The driving force is $2400$ N, and the resistances are $300$ N on the car and $150$ N on the trailer. Find the acceleration and the tension in the towbar.
@step Whole system: $2400 - 300 - 150 = 1500a$, so $a = 1.3$ m/s².
@step Trailer alone: the tension $T$ pulls it forwards, the resistance holds it back. $T - 150 = 500 \times 1.3$.
@answer $a = 1.3$ m/s² and $T = 800$ N.
:::

:::mistake Including the tension in the whole system
Looking at the car and trailer together, the towbar pulls the trailer forwards and the car backwards by the same amount. Those two forces cancel, so the tension never appears in the whole-system equation.
:::

## Pulleys

When a light inextensible string passes over a **smooth pulley**, the tension is the same on both sides. The two particles move in different directions, so write $F = ma$ for each in its own direction of motion.

:::example Two particles over a pulley
Particles $A$ of mass $5$ kg and $B$ of mass $3$ kg hang over a smooth pulley on a light inextensible string, and are released from rest. Find the acceleration and the tension.
@step $A$ moves down: $5g - T = 5a$. $B$ moves up: $T - 3g = 3a$.
@step Adding removes $T$: $2g = 8a$, so $a = \dfrac{2 \times 9.8}{8} = 2.45$ m/s².
@step From $B$: $T = 3(9.8 + 2.45) = 36.75$ N.
@answer $a = 2.45$ m/s² and $T \approx 36.8$ N.
:::

:::tip When a string goes slack
If the falling particle hits the floor, the string goes slack and the tension becomes zero. The other particle then moves freely under gravity, starting with the speed it had at that moment.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Inextensible connections give both objects the same acceleration.
- Treat the system as one object to find the acceleration: the tension cancels.
- Then look at one object alone to find the tension (or thrust).
- Over a smooth pulley the tension is the same on both sides; write $F = ma$ for each particle in its own direction of motion and add the equations.
:::
