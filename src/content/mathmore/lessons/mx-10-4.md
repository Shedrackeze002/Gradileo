Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry work in any right-angled triangle, including the ones hidden inside a box or a pyramid. The skill in three dimensions is finding the right triangle and drawing it flat.

## Pythagoras' theorem in 3D

:::definition Space diagonal
The **space diagonal** of a cuboid joins one corner to the opposite corner, passing through the inside. For a cuboid of length $l$, width $w$ and height $h$:
$$d = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2 + h^2}$$
:::

This comes from using Pythagoras' theorem twice. First the diagonal of the base is $\sqrt{l^2 + w^2}$. That diagonal, the height and the space diagonal then make a right-angled triangle standing upright inside the box.

:::example The longest rod in a box
A box is $8$ cm long, $6$ cm wide and $5$ cm high. Find the longest straight rod that fits inside it.
@step Base diagonal $= \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$ cm.
@step Space diagonal $= \sqrt{10^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{125} = 11.18\ldots$
@answer The longest rod is about $11.2$ cm.
:::

## The angle between a line and a plane

:::definition Angle between a line and a plane
The **angle between a line and a plane** (a flat surface) is the angle between the line and its shadow on the plane: the line drawn on the plane directly below it. The line, its shadow and the vertical drop make a right-angled triangle.
:::

:::example The angle of a space diagonal
Find the angle between the space diagonal of the $8$ by $6$ by $5$ cm box and its base.
@step The shadow of the diagonal on the base is the base diagonal, $10$ cm. The height is $5$ cm.
@step $\tan \theta = \dfrac{5}{10}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.5) = 26.56\ldots^\circ$
@answer The angle is about $26.6^\circ$.
:::

:::tip Draw the triangle flat
Sketch the right-angled triangle you need on its own, label the lengths you know, and only then choose Pythagoras' theorem, sine, cosine or tangent.
