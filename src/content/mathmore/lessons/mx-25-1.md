A straight cut across a pizza, away from the centre, leaves two pieces with one straight edge and one curved edge. Each piece is a segment of a circle, and we can find its area from pieces we already know: a sector and a triangle.

## What a segment is

:::definition Segment of a circle
A **segment** of a circle is the region between a chord and the arc that the chord cuts off. A chord divides a circle into two segments: the smaller one is the **minor segment** and the larger one is the **major segment**. We measure a segment by the angle $\theta$ that its chord subtends at the centre.
:::

The two radii $OA$ and $OB$ and the arc $AB$ make the sector $AOB$. The two radii and the chord make the triangle $AOB$. So the minor segment is what is left of the sector when the triangle is taken away.

$$\text{minor segment} = \text{sector } AOB - \text{triangle } AOB = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2} r^2 \sin\theta$$

Here $r$ is the radius and $\theta$ is the angle at the centre in degrees. The triangle's area comes from $\dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ with $a = b = r$.

:::example A right angle at the centre
A chord subtends $90°$ at the centre of a circle of radius $8$ cm. Find the area of the minor segment, to 2 decimal places.
@step Sector: $\dfrac{90}{360} \times \pi \times 8^2 \approx 50.2655$ cm²
@step Triangle: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 8^2 \times \sin 90° = 32$ cm²
@step Segment $= 50.2655 - 32$
@answer $18.27$ cm²
:::

:::example Any angle
A chord subtends $110°$ at the centre of a circle of radius $6$ cm. Find the area of the minor segment, to 2 decimal places.
@step Sector: $\dfrac{110}{360} \times \pi \times 6^2 \approx 34.5575$ cm²
@step Triangle: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 6^2 \times \sin 110° \approx 16.9145$ cm²
@step Segment $= 34.5575 - 16.9145$
@answer $17.64$ cm²
:::

Keep at least four decimal places in the sector and the triangle, and round only at the end. Rounding both to two places first can change the last digit of the answer.

## The major segment

The major segment is the rest of the circle, so we subtract the minor segment from the area of the whole circle.

:::example The larger piece
For the circle of radius $6$ cm above, find the area of the major segment.
@step Circle: $\pi \times 6^2 \approx 113.0973$ cm²
@step Major segment $= 113.0973 - 17.6430$
@answer $95.45$ cm²
:::

:::

## The chord and the perimeter

The line from $O$ to the midpoint of the chord cuts triangle $AOB$ into two equal right-angled triangles, each with an angle of $\dfrac{\theta}{2}$ at $O$ and a hypotenuse of $r$. So half the chord is $r\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, and

$$\text{chord } AB = 2r\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$$

The perimeter of a segment is its arc plus its chord, where the arc is $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$.

:::example Chord and perimeter
A chord subtends $120°$ at the centre of a circle of radius $9$ cm. Find the chord, and the perimeter of the minor segment, to 2 decimal places.
@step Chord: $2 \times 9 \times \sin 60° \approx 15.5885$ cm
@step Arc: $\dfrac{120}{360} \times 2\pi \times 9 \approx 18.8496$ cm
@step Perimeter $= 18.8496 + 15.5885$
@answer The chord is $15.59$ cm and the perimeter is $34.44$ cm.
:::

:::mistake Half the angle, not the whole angle
The chord uses $\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, because the right-angled triangle has half the angle at the centre. The area of the triangle uses $\sin\theta$, the whole angle. Mixing the two is the most common slip in this topic.
:::

When the chord and the angle are known, the same formula rearranged gives the radius: $r = \dfrac{\text{chord}}{2\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}$.

:::summary
- A segment is the region between a chord and its arc.
- Minor segment $=$ sector $-$ triangle $= \dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta$.
- Major segment $=$ circle $-$ minor segment.
- Chord $= 2r\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, and the perimeter of a segment is the arc plus the chord.
:::
