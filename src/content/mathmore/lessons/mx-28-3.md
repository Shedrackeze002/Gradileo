Two judges score the same dancers. They may use the scale differently, but do they put the dancers in the same order? Spearman's coefficient answers that by comparing the ranks instead of the scores.

## Spearman's rank correlation coefficient

:::definition Spearman's rank correlation coefficient
**Spearman's rank correlation coefficient** $r_s$ measures how closely two rankings agree. Each set of data is ranked, $d$ is the difference between the two ranks of each item, and with no tied ranks
$$r_s = 1 - \frac{6\sum d^2}{n(n^2 - 1)}$$
where $n$ is the number of items. It lies between $-1$ and $1$.
:::

A value of $r_s = 1$ means that the two rankings are identical, $r_s = -1$ means that one is the reverse of the other, and a value near $0$ means that there is little association between them. This is also the product moment correlation coefficient of the ranks, so a calculator can find $r_s$ by finding $r$ for the two columns of ranks.

:::example Finding r_s
Two judges score five dancers. Judge A: $7, 9, 4, 6, 8$. Judge B: $6, 8, 5, 4, 9$.
@step Ranks for A (largest is $1$): $3, 1, 5, 4, 2$
@step Ranks for B: $3, 2, 4, 5, 1$
@step $d$: $0, -1, 1, -1, 1$, so $\sum d^2 = 4$.
@step $r_s = 1 - \dfrac{6 \times 4}{5(25 - 1)} = 1 - \dfrac{24}{120}$
@answer $r_s = 0.8$
:::

## Interpreting and testing

The sign of $r_s$ gives the direction of the association, and its size gives the strength. Spearman's coefficient only needs the order of the values, so it suits data that are ranks or judgements, and a relationship that is **monotonic** (always rising, or always falling) without being a straight line. The product moment coefficient $r$ measures how close the points are to a straight line, so it suits measurements with a linear pattern.

To test for association, $H_0$ says there is no association in the population ($\rho_s = 0$), and $H_1$ says there is positive association ($\rho_s > 0$), negative association ($\rho_s < 0$) or some association ($\rho_s \ne 0$). We reject $H_0$ when $r_s$ is beyond the critical value for the sample size and significance level.

:::example A test
For $8$ pairs, $r_s = 0.69$. Test at the $5\%$ level for positive association, given the critical value $0.643$.
@step $H_0: \rho_s = 0$ and $H_1: \rho_s > 0$
@step $0.69 > 0.643$, so the result is significant.
@answer Reject $H_0$: there is evidence of positive association.
:::

:::summary
- Rank both sets, find $\sum d^2$, and use $r_s = 1 - \dfrac{6\sum d^2}{n(n^2 - 1)}$.
- $r_s$ measures how well two rankings agree, from $-1$ to $1$, and suits ranks and monotonic relationships.
- Reject $H_0: \rho_s = 0$ when $r_s$ is beyond the critical value.
:::
