Tides in the Bay of Fundy, hours of daylight in a northern city and the height of a Ferris wheel seat all repeat on a regular cycle. A sinusoidal function turns each cycle into an equation you can evaluate and solve.

## Building a model

To model a periodic situation with $y = a\sin b(t - c) + d$ or $y = a\cos b(t - c) + d$:

- $d$ is the middle value, halfway between the maximum and minimum.
- $|a|$ is the amplitude, half the distance between the maximum and minimum.
- $b = \dfrac{2\pi}{\text{period}}$, with the period in the units of $t$.
- Choose the function and $c$ from the starting point: $\sin$ starts on the midline going up, $\cos$ starts at a maximum, and $-\cos$ starts at a minimum.

:::example A Ferris wheel
A Ferris wheel has a radius of $20$ m, its centre is $22$ m above the ground, and it makes one rotation every $60$ s. A rider boards at the lowest point at $t = 0$. Write an equation for the rider's height and find the height after $25$ s.
@step $|a| = 20$ (the radius) and $d = 22$ (the centre). The rider starts at a minimum, so use $-\cos$: $a = -20$.
@step $b = \dfrac{2\pi}{60} = \dfrac{\pi}{30}$, so $h(t) = -20\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{30}t\right) + 22$.
@step $h(25) = -20\cos\left(\dfrac{25\pi}{30}\right) + 22 = -20\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 22 = 10\sqrt{3} + 22 \approx 39.32$
@answer $h(t) = -20\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{30}t\right) + 22$; after $25$ s the rider is about $39.3$ m above the ground.
:::

:::mistake Degree mode
The angle $\frac{\pi}{30}t$ is in radians. In degree mode, $h(25)$ comes out as about $2.0$ m, which cannot be right: after $25$ s the rider is near the top of the wheel.
:::

:::example Solving the model
For $h(t) = -20\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{30}t\right) + 22$, when during the first rotation is the rider $30$ m above the ground, and for how long is the rider higher than that?
@step $-20\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{30}t\right) + 22 = 30$, so $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{30}t\right) = \dfrac{30 - 22}{-20} = -0.4$.
@step Let $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{30}t$. In one cycle, $\theta = \cos^{-1}(-0.4) \approx 1.9823$ or $\theta = 2\pi - 1.9823 \approx 4.3009$.
@step $t = \dfrac{30\theta}{\pi}$: $t \approx 18.93$ s or $t \approx 41.07$ s.
@step The graph is above $30$ m between these times: $41.07 - 18.93 = 22.14$.
@answer At about $18.9$ s and $41.1$ s; the rider is above $30$ m for about $22.1$ s of each rotation.
:::

:::mistake Only one time
The inverse cosine gives a single angle. The second angle in the same cycle, $2\pi - \theta$ for cosine or $\pi - \theta$ for sine, gives the second time.
:::

## Daylight in Riverton

A model for the hours of daylight in Riverton, a northern city, on day $t$ of the year is
$$L(t) = 4.9\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{365}(t - 80)\right) + 12.2$$
The period is $365$ days, and $c = 80$ shifts the start of the cycle to about March 21, when day and night are nearly equal. The model gives a maximum of $12.2 + 4.9 = 17.1$ hours, a quarter year later (day $171$), and a minimum of $7.3$ hours in December.

:::example A winter day
Use the model to estimate the hours of daylight on February 14 (day $45$).
@step $L(45) = 4.9\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{365}(45 - 80)\right) + 12.2 = 4.9\sin(-0.6025) + 12.2$
@step $\approx 4.9(-0.5668) + 12.2 \approx 9.42$
@answer About $9.4$ hours of daylight
:::

:::example Long days
For how many days of the year does the model give more than $15$ hours of daylight?
@step $\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{365}(t - 80)\right) = \dfrac{15 - 12.2}{4.9} \approx 0.5714$
@step The angle is $\sin^{-1}(0.5714) \approx 0.6082$ or $\pi - 0.6082 \approx 2.5333$.
@step $t = 80 + \dfrac{365\theta}{2\pi}$: $t \approx 115.3$ or $t \approx 227.2$. Daylight is above $15$ hours between these days.
@answer $227.2 - 115.3 \approx 112$ days
:::

:::tip Check with a graph
Graph the model and the line $y = k$ on a graphing calculator. The intersection points confirm your algebra and show whether the function is above or below $k$ between them.
:::

:::summary
- Amplitude: half of max − min. Midline: $d = \frac{\max + \min}{2}$. $b = \frac{2\pi}{\text{period}}$.
- Pick sine, cosine or negative cosine from where the cycle starts.
- Evaluate models in radian mode.
- To find when a value is reached, solve for the angle, use both angles in the cycle, then solve for $t$.
:::
