About three quarters of the elements are metals. A zigzag line, like a staircase, runs down the right side of the periodic table: the metals are to its left and the non-metals to its right. Metals and non-metals have very different properties, which is why we use them for very different jobs.

## Properties of metals and non-metals

:::definition Metals and non-metals
**Metals** are usually shiny (**lustrous**), good **conductors** of heat and electricity, **malleable** (can be hammered into shape) and **ductile** (can be drawn into wires). Most are strong, dense solids with high melting points.

**Non-metals** are usually dull, **brittle** when solid, and poor conductors (**insulators**). Many are gases at room temperature, and most have low melting points.
:::

| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | shiny | usually dull |
| Conduct heat and electricity? | yes, well | no (except graphite, a form of carbon) |
| When hit | bend or flatten | shatter, if solid |
| State at room temperature | solid (except mercury, a liquid) | many are gases; bromine is a liquid |

:::mistake Exceptions
Mercury is a metal but a liquid at room temperature. Graphite is a non-metal but conducts electricity. The table describes most metals and non-metals, not every one.
:::

:::example A non-metal's properties
Sulfur is a yellow solid that shatters when hit and does not conduct electricity. Is it a metal or a non-metal?
@step It is dull, brittle and an insulator.
@answer A non-metal.
:::

## Metal or non-metal?

You can decide in two ways: from an element's **properties**, or from its **position** in the table.

- Left of the zigzag line, and in the middle: **metals** (sodium, magnesium, iron, copper, zinc).
- Right of the line: **non-metals** (carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, sulfur, chlorine, the noble gases).
- Next to the line: **metalloids**, such as silicon, which have some properties of each. Silicon is shiny but brittle, and conducts a little, which makes it useful in computer chips.

Hydrogen is the odd one out: it sits above group 1, but it is a non-metal gas.

:::example From a description
An element is a grey solid that rings when struck and conducts heat well. Is it more likely a metal or a non-metal?
@step Ringing (being sonorous) and conducting heat are metal properties.
@answer A metal.
:::

:::summary
- Metals are shiny, conduct heat and electricity, and are malleable and ductile.
- Non-metals are mostly dull, brittle insulators, and many are gases.
- Metals are left of the zigzag line in the periodic table; non-metals are to its right; metalloids sit next to it.
:::
