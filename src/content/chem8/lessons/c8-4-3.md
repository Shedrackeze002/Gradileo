Copper pipes carry water, aluminium cans hold drinks, and steel beams hold up skyscrapers. Each metal is chosen for its properties. And very often, the metal in an object is not pure at all, but an alloy: a mixture designed to be better than the pure metal.

## Choosing a metal

:::definition Matching properties to uses
Engineers choose a metal by asking which **properties** a job needs: strength, low density, conducting heat or electricity, being easy to shape, or not corroding.
:::

| Metal | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| copper | wiring, water pipes | conducts electricity; easy to bend; does not react with water |
| aluminium | drink cans, aircraft, power cables | low density; malleable; resists corrosion |
| steel | bridges, building frames, cars | very strong and cheap |
| titanium | hip joints, aircraft engines | strong, light, does not react with the body |
| gold | jewellery, electrical contacts | shiny, easy to shape, never tarnishes |

:::example Power cables
Copper conducts electricity better than aluminium. Why are overhead power cables made of aluminium?
@step Aluminium still conducts well.
@step It has about a third of the density of copper, so a long cable is much lighter.
@answer Lighter cables need fewer, smaller pylons, which saves money.
:::

## Alloys

:::definition Alloy
An **alloy** is a mixture of a metal with one or more other elements. Most metal objects are alloys, because alloys can be made harder, stronger or more resistant to corrosion than pure metals.
:::

| Alloy | Made of | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| steel | iron and a little carbon | bridges, tools |
| stainless steel | iron, chromium and nickel | cutlery, sinks |
| brass | copper and zinc | door handles, musical instruments |
| bronze | copper and tin | statues, medals |

In a pure metal, the atoms are all the same size and sit in neat layers that slide over one another easily, so the metal is soft. In an alloy, atoms of a different size disrupt the layers, so they cannot slide as easily, and the alloy is **harder**.

Pure gold is too soft for everyday jewellery, so it is alloyed with copper or silver. Gold's purity is measured in **carats**: pure gold is 24 carat, and an alloy of a given carat number has that many parts of gold in every 24 parts.

:::example 18-carat gold
What percentage of an 18-carat gold ring is gold?
@step 18 parts in every 24 are gold.
@step $\dfrac{18}{24} \times 100 = 75$.
@answer 75% gold.
:::

:::summary
- Metals are chosen for their properties: copper conducts, aluminium is light, steel is strong, titanium and gold do not react.
- An **alloy** is a metal mixed with other elements: steel, stainless steel, brass, bronze.
- Different-sized atoms stop the layers sliding, so alloys are usually harder than pure metals.
- Gold purity is in **carats**: 24 carat is pure; 18 carat is 18 ÷ 24 = 75% gold.
:::
