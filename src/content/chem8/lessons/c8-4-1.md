Cooking on a gas stove, a campfire, the engine of a car: all of these depend on burning. Burning gives us heat and light, but it also makes waste gases, and out of control it is dangerous. In this lesson you will find out what burning is, what it makes, and how fires are put out.

## Combustion

:::definition Combustion
**Combustion** is the chemical name for burning. It is a reaction in which a **fuel** reacts with **oxygen**, giving out **heat** and usually **light**.
:::

Many fuels, such as natural gas (methane), petrol and candle wax, are made of **carbon and hydrogen**. When they burn completely, in plenty of oxygen:

- the carbon becomes **carbon dioxide**;
- the hydrogen becomes **water**.

$$\text{methane} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water}$$

A fuel with no carbon, such as hydrogen, makes only water. Charcoal, which is almost pure carbon, makes only carbon dioxide.

:::example A candle under a jar
A burning candle is covered with a glass jar. Droplets form on the inside of the jar, and soon the flame goes out. Explain both.
@step Candle wax contains hydrogen, which burns to make water: the droplets.
@step The flame uses up the oxygen in the jar. Without oxygen, combustion stops.
@answer The droplets are water made by the burning wax, and the flame goes out when the oxygen is used up.
:::

:::tip Testing for carbon dioxide
Bubble a gas through **limewater**. If it is carbon dioxide, the limewater turns **cloudy** (milky).
:::

:::mistake Too little oxygen
With too little oxygen, fuels burn incompletely and make **carbon monoxide**, a poisonous gas with no colour or smell, and black soot. This is why gas heaters and boilers need good air flow and regular checks.
:::

## The fire triangle

:::definition Fire triangle
A fire needs three things at once: **fuel**, **heat** and **oxygen**. These are the three sides of the **fire triangle**. Take away any one side, and the fire goes out.
:::

| Method | Side removed |
|---|---|
| spraying water on burning wood | heat (the water cools it) |
| a fire blanket over a burning pan | oxygen |
| sand or a carbon dioxide extinguisher | oxygen |
| turning off the gas supply | fuel |
| a firebreak cut through a forest | fuel |

:::example A pan of oil on fire
Why should you never throw water on a burning pan of cooking oil?
@step The water sinks below the hot oil and boils instantly.
@step The steam throws burning oil out of the pan, spreading the fire.
@answer It makes the fire spread. Turn off the heat and cover the pan with a fire blanket or a damp tea towel to cut off the oxygen.
:::

:::summary
- **Combustion** is a fuel reacting with oxygen, giving out heat and light.
- Fuels of carbon and hydrogen burn to carbon dioxide and water; carbon dioxide turns limewater cloudy.
- Too little oxygen gives poisonous carbon monoxide.
- The **fire triangle**: fuel, heat and oxygen. Remove one to put out a fire. Never use water on burning oil or electrical fires.
:::
