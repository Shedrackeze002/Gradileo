Look around a kitchen. The kettle has a plastic case, the plates are china, the window is glass, and the worktop might be a mix of stone chips and resin. Metals are only one family of materials. In this lesson you will meet the other three, and learn to choose the right material for a job.

## Polymers, ceramics and composites

:::definition Kinds of material
- **Polymers** are made of very long chain molecules. Most **plastics** are polymers: polythene, PVC, nylon. They are usually light, flexible or tough, waterproof, and good **insulators**.
- **Ceramics** are made by heating clay or minerals to high temperatures: pottery, china, brick and **glass**. They are hard, resist heat, and are good insulators, but they are **brittle**.
- **Composites** combine two or more materials, to get the best properties of each: reinforced concrete, fibreglass, carbon fibre, plywood.
:::

| Material | Kind | Useful properties |
|---|---|---|
| polythene bag | polymer | light, flexible, waterproof |
| china plate | ceramic | hard, heat-resistant, easy to clean |
| window glass | ceramic | transparent, weatherproof |
| reinforced concrete | composite | strong when squeezed (concrete) and when stretched (steel rods) |
| carbon fibre | composite | very strong and very light |

:::example Why reinforce concrete?
Concrete is strong when it is squeezed but cracks when it is stretched. Why are steel rods set inside concrete beams?
@step Steel is strong when it is stretched.
@answer The composite has the strengths of both: it resists squeezing and stretching.
:::

:::mistake Long life has a cost
Most plastics do not rot, which makes them useful, but it also means plastic litter stays in the environment for hundreds of years. Reusing and recycling plastics reduces the problem.
:::

## Choosing a material

To choose a material, decide which **property matters most** for the job, then pick the material that has it, and check that it does not have a property that would make it fail.

:::example A saucepan
Which materials should be used for the body and the handle of a saucepan?
@step The body must conduct heat to the food: a metal, such as stainless steel or aluminium.
@step The handle must not conduct heat to the cook's hand: a hard plastic or wood.
@answer A metal body and a plastic or wooden handle.
:::

| Job | Property that matters most | Good choice |
|---|---|---|
| covering for electrical wires | flexible insulator | PVC plastic |
| oven tiles | heat-resistant, easy to clean | ceramic |
| racing bike frame | strong and light | carbon fibre |
| crash helmet shell | strong, light, does not shatter | fibreglass |

:::summary
- **Polymers** (plastics) are long-chain molecules: light, flexible, insulating, and slow to rot.
- **Ceramics** (pottery, brick, glass) are hard and heat-resistant but brittle.
- **Composites** combine materials to get the best of each: reinforced concrete, fibreglass, carbon fibre.
- Choose a material by the property the job needs most.
:::
