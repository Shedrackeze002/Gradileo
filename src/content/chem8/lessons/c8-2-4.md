Mendeleev predicted elements nobody had ever seen. You can make predictions too: because elements in a group are alike, their properties change in a steady pattern down the group. Follow the pattern one step further, and you can predict an element you have never studied.

## Patterns down a group

:::definition Trend
A **trend** is a steady change in a property from one element to the next down a group (or along a period). If you know the trend, you can **predict** the property of the next element.
:::

| Alkali metal | Melting point (°C) |
|---|---|
| lithium | 181 |
| sodium | 98 |
| potassium | 63 |
| rubidium | 39 |
| caesium | ? |

:::example Predicting a melting point
Predict the melting point of caesium, the next alkali metal.
@step The melting point falls down the group: 181, 98, 63, 39.
@step It falls by less each time (83, then 35, then 24), so the next fall should be smaller still, around 10 °C.
@answer About 29 °C. (The real value is 28.5 °C: caesium melts on a hot day.)
:::

Reactivity follows trends too. Alkali metals get **more** reactive down group 1, so caesium, below rubidium, explodes in water. Halogens get **less** reactive down group 17, so fluorine at the top is the most reactive of all.

:::mistake Not every trend goes the same way
Reactivity **rises** down group 1 but **falls** down group 17. Always look at the data for the group you are working with.
:::

## Elements at work

The way an element is used depends on its properties.

| Element | Use | Property it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| copper | electrical wires | conducts electricity, ductile |
| aluminium | aircraft bodies | strong, low density, resists corrosion |
| gold | jewellery, phone contacts | shiny, conducts, never tarnishes |
| carbon (graphite) | pencil "lead" | soft, leaves a mark |
| silicon | computer chips | conducts a little (a metalloid) |
| chlorine | water treatment | kills bacteria |
| helium | balloons | low density, unreactive |
| argon | light bulbs | unreactive |

:::example Choosing an element
Why are the contacts inside phones often coated with a thin layer of gold?
@step The contacts must conduct electricity for many years.
@answer Gold conducts well and does not react with air, so it stays clean and conducting.
:::

:::summary
- Properties change in a steady **trend** down a group, so you can predict the next element.
- Alkali metals get more reactive down the group; halogens get less reactive.
- Each element's uses depend on its properties: copper conducts, aluminium is light, gold and the noble gases are unreactive.
:::
