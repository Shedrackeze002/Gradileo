Chemists do not write "two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom" every time they mean water. They write H₂O. A chemical formula packs the whole recipe of a compound into a few letters and numbers.

## What a formula tells you

:::definition Chemical formula
A **chemical formula** shows the elements in a substance, using their symbols, and how many atoms of each there are, using small numbers called **subscripts** written after each symbol. No number means **one** atom.
:::

$$\ce{H2O}: \quad 2 \text{ hydrogen atoms}, \ 1 \text{ oxygen atom}$$

To find the elements, read the symbols: each **capital letter** starts a new one.

:::example Reading a formula
Which elements are in calcium carbonate, $\ce{CaCO3}$?
@step Each capital letter starts a new symbol, so the symbols are Ca, C and O.
@answer Calcium, carbon and oxygen: three elements.
:::

| Formula | Name | Elements |
|---|---|---|
| $\ce{CO2}$ | carbon dioxide | carbon, oxygen |
| $\ce{NaCl}$ | sodium chloride | sodium, chlorine |
| $\ce{CH4}$ | methane | carbon, hydrogen |
| $\ce{NH3}$ | ammonia | nitrogen, hydrogen |
| $\ce{H2SO4}$ | sulfuric acid | hydrogen, sulfur, oxygen |

## Counting atoms

To count the atoms of an element, look at the subscript straight after its symbol.

:::example Counting in glucose
How many atoms of each element does the formula of glucose, $\ce{C6H12O6}$, show, and how many altogether?
@step Carbon: 6. Hydrogen: 12. Oxygen: 6.
@answer $6 + 12 + 6 = 24$ atoms altogether.
:::

Two more rules:

- A subscript after a **bracket** multiplies everything inside it. In $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$ there is 1 calcium atom, $1 \times 2 = 2$ oxygen atoms and $1 \times 2 = 2$ hydrogen atoms.
- A big number **in front** counts whole units. $\ce{3H2O}$ means three water molecules: $3 \times 2 = 6$ hydrogen atoms and $3 \times 1 = 3$ oxygen atoms.

:::example Brackets
How many oxygen atoms does the formula $\ce{Mg(NO3)2}$ show?
@step Inside the bracket there are 3 oxygen atoms.
@step The 2 after the bracket doubles them: $3 \times 2 = 6$.
@answer 6 oxygen atoms.
:::

:::mistake The subscript belongs to the symbol before it
In $\ce{CO2}$ the 2 belongs to O, not to C: there is one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.
:::

:::summary
- A **formula** uses symbols for the elements and subscripts for the number of atoms; no number means one.
- Each capital letter starts a new symbol.
- A subscript after a bracket multiplies everything inside the bracket.
- A number in front of a formula counts whole units.
:::
