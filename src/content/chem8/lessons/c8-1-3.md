Iron filings are grey and magnetic, and sulfur is a yellow powder. Stir them together and you can still pull the iron out with a magnet. Heat them, and they glow and turn into a black solid that no magnet can sort. The first is a mixture and the second is a compound, and the difference matters.

## Elements, compounds and mixtures

:::definition Three kinds of matter
- An **element** contains only one kind of atom: oxygen, iron, gold.
- A **compound** contains two or more elements **chemically joined**: water, carbon dioxide, sodium chloride.
- A **mixture** contains two or more substances **not chemically joined**: air, sea water, steel.

Elements and compounds are **pure substances**; mixtures are not.
:::

:::example Sorting substances
Is brass, which is copper and zinc melted and stirred together, an element, a compound or a mixture?
@step Brass contains two substances, copper and zinc.
@step They are mixed, not chemically joined, and the amounts can vary.
@answer A mixture (an alloy).
:::

A formula can tell you whether a substance is an element or a compound. Count the different symbols, remembering that each **capital letter** starts a new one: O₂ and N₂ are elements, while H₂O, NaCl and CO are compounds.

## Compounds and mixtures compared

| | Mixture of iron and sulfur | Compound: iron sulfide |
|---|---|---|
| How it is made | stirring together | heating, a chemical reaction |
| Properties | iron is still grey and magnetic, sulfur still yellow | a new black solid, not magnetic |
| Proportions | any amounts of each | always fixed proportions |
| Separating | easy: a magnet pulls out the iron | needs another chemical reaction |

:::definition Fixed proportions
In a compound the elements are always joined in the **same proportions**. Every water molecule is two hydrogen atoms to one oxygen atom. A mixture, such as salty water, can have any amount of each part.
:::

:::example The magnet test
A grey-and-yellow powder is tested with a magnet, and grey specks are pulled out. Is the powder a mixture or a compound?
@step The iron still has its own property, magnetism.
@answer A mixture: in a compound the iron would no longer be magnetic.
:::

:::mistake Making a compound is a reaction
Stirring things together makes a mixture. Making a compound needs a **chemical reaction**, and there are usually signs: the iron and sulfur glow as they react.
:::

:::summary
- **Element**: one kind of atom. **Compound**: elements chemically joined. **Mixture**: substances not joined.
- In a mixture each part keeps its properties and can be in any amount; it can be separated by physical methods.
- A compound has new properties and fixed proportions, and only a chemical reaction can split it.
:::
