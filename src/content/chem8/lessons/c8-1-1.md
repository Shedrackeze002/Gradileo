Everything around you, from the air you breathe to the screen you are reading, is built from tiny particles called atoms. There are only about 118 different kinds of atom, but they combine to make millions of different substances. In this lesson you will meet atoms and elements, and the short symbols chemists use to write them.

## Atoms and elements

:::definition Atom and element
An **atom** is the smallest particle of an element. Atoms are far too small to see, even with an ordinary microscope: about ten million of them side by side would stretch one millimetre.

An **element** is a substance made of only **one kind of atom**. Gold contains only gold atoms, and oxygen contains only oxygen atoms. An element cannot be broken down into anything simpler by a chemical reaction.
:::

There are about 118 known elements. Most are solids at room temperature, such as iron, carbon and sulfur; a few are gases, such as oxygen, nitrogen and helium; and just two, mercury and bromine, are liquids.

:::example The smallest piece of gold
A gold ring is cut in half, then one half is cut in half again, over and over. What is the smallest piece that is still gold?
@step Each piece is still made only of gold atoms.
@step A single atom of gold is the smallest particle that is still gold.
@answer One atom of gold.
:::

:::mistake Not every substance is an element
Water, salt and air are not elements. Water and salt contain more than one kind of atom joined together, and air is a mixture of several gases. Only a substance with **one kind of atom** is an element.
:::

## Chemical symbols

Every element has a **chemical symbol** of one or two letters, used by scientists all over the world.

:::definition Chemical symbol
A **chemical symbol** is a short code for an element. The first letter is always a **capital**; a second letter, if there is one, is always **small**.
:::

Most symbols come from the element's English name:

| Element | Symbol | Element | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| hydrogen | H | magnesium | Mg |
| carbon | C | aluminium | Al |
| nitrogen | N | chlorine | Cl |
| oxygen | O | calcium | Ca |
| helium | He | zinc | Zn |

A few come from the element's old Latin name:

| Element | Symbol | Latin name |
|---|---|---|
| sodium | Na | natrium |
| potassium | K | kalium |
| iron | Fe | ferrum |
| copper | Cu | cuprum |
| silver | Ag | argentum |
| gold | Au | aurum |
| lead | Pb | plumbum |

:::example Capital letters matter
What is the difference between Co and CO?
@step Co is one capital and one small letter, so it is one element: cobalt.
@step CO has two capitals, so it is two elements: carbon (C) and oxygen (O), joined together.
@answer Co is the element cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide, a compound of carbon and oxygen.
:::

:::summary
- An **atom** is the smallest particle of an element; an **element** contains only one kind of atom.
- There are about 118 elements; they cannot be broken down by chemical reactions.
- Each element has a **symbol**: a capital letter, sometimes followed by a small letter.
- A few symbols come from Latin names: Na (sodium), K (potassium), Fe (iron), Cu (copper), Ag (silver), Au (gold), Pb (lead).
:::
