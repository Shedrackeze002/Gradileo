Every reaction can be told as a short story: these substances went in, and these came out. A word equation is that story written in one line, so that anyone can see at a glance what reacted and what was made.

## Word equations

:::definition Word equation
A **word equation** names the reactants on the left and the products on the right, with an arrow between them:

reactants → products

The arrow means "react to make". A plus sign (+) separates the substances on each side.
:::

$$\text{magnesium} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{magnesium oxide}$$

:::example Writing a word equation
Methane, the gas in many cookers, burns in oxygen to make carbon dioxide and water. Write the word equation.
@step Reactants: methane and oxygen. Products: carbon dioxide and water.
@answer methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
:::

:::mistake Products never go on the left
An equation always runs from what you start with to what is made. Writing carbon dioxide + water → methane + oxygen describes a different reaction.
:::

:::tip Oxygen from the air
When something burns in air, it reacts with oxygen. Oxygen is a reactant even though nobody added it on purpose.
:::

## Naming simple compounds

When a **metal** reacts with a **non-metal**, the compound is named with:

1. the **metal** first, unchanged;
2. the **non-metal** second, with its name changed to end in **-ide**.

| Non-metal | Becomes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| oxygen | oxide | magnesium oxide |
| chlorine | chloride | sodium chloride |
| sulfur | sulfide | iron sulfide |
| bromine | bromide | potassium bromide |

:::example Naming a product
What is formed when calcium reacts with chlorine? Write the word equation.
@step The metal, calcium, comes first. Chlorine becomes chloride.
@answer calcium + chlorine → calcium chloride
:::

Working backwards, a name ending in "-ide" tells you the elements: copper oxide contains copper and oxygen, and zinc sulfide contains zinc and sulfur.

:::summary
- A **word equation** reads reactants → products, with + between the substances on each side.
- When something burns, oxygen is a reactant.
- A metal and a non-metal make a compound named metal first, then the non-metal ending in **-ide**: oxide, chloride, sulfide.
:::
