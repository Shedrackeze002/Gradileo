Every year, rust eats away a huge amount of iron and steel: car bodies, bridges, ships and railings. Replacing and protecting them costs a great deal of money. If we understand exactly what makes iron rust, we can stop it.

## What makes iron rust

:::definition Rusting
**Rusting** is the corrosion of iron and steel. Iron reacts slowly with **oxygen** and **water** to make **rust**, a flaky, reddish-brown form of iron oxide.

$$\text{iron} + \text{oxygen} + \text{water} \rightarrow \text{rust (hydrated iron oxide)}$$
:::

Rusting is a chemical change, and a slow **oxidation**: a reaction with oxygen. Rust is weak and flakes off, exposing fresh iron underneath, so rusting carries on until the iron is eaten through.

A classic experiment shows that iron needs **both** water and oxygen:

| Tube | Conditions | After a week |
|---|---|---|
| A | nail in tap water, open to the air | rusted |
| B | nail in dry air, with a drying agent | not rusted: no water |
| C | nail in boiled water under a layer of oil | not rusted: no oxygen |
| D | nail in salty water, open to the air | rusted even more |

:::example Reading the experiment
Why does the nail in tube C not rust, even though it is under water?
@step Boiling removes the dissolved air from the water, and the oil stops air getting back in.
@answer There is no oxygen, and iron needs both water and oxygen to rust.
:::

:::mistake Only iron rusts
Other metals corrode, but only iron and steel **rust**. Aluminium, copper and gold do not rust.

## Stopping rust

Most ways of stopping rust put a **barrier** between the iron and the water and oxygen.

| Method | Good for |
|---|---|
| painting | gates, cars, bridges |
| oiling or greasing | chains, hinges and other moving parts |
| coating with plastic | dish racks, garden wire |
| coating with tin | food cans |
| **galvanizing**: coating with zinc | buckets, road barriers, roofing |
| using **stainless steel**, an alloy with chromium | cutlery, sinks |

Galvanizing does more than make a barrier. Zinc is **more reactive** than iron, so even if the coating is scratched, the zinc reacts instead of the iron. This is called **sacrificial protection**.

:::example A bicycle chain
Why is a bicycle chain oiled rather than painted?
@step Paint would crack and flake off the moving links.
@answer Oil keeps out water and oxygen and still lets the chain move freely.

:::summary
- Iron rusts only when **water and oxygen** are both present; salt makes it faster.
- Rusting is a slow oxidation, a chemical change; only iron and steel rust.
- Paint, oil, plastic and tin are barriers; galvanizing with zinc also protects when scratched; stainless steel does not rust.
:::
