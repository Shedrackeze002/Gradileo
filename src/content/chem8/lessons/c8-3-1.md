Melt an ice cube and you still have water. Burn a piece of paper and you are left with ash and smoke that will never be paper again. Both are changes, but only one makes something new. Telling the two kinds apart is one of the first skills of chemistry.

## Physical and chemical changes

:::definition Physical change and chemical change
In a **physical change**, no new substance is made. The substance may change its state, shape or size, or dissolve, but it is still the same substance.

In a **chemical change** (a **chemical reaction**), one or more **new substances** are made.
:::

| Physical changes | Chemical changes |
|---|---|
| ice melting | paper burning |
| water boiling | an iron nail rusting |
| sugar dissolving in tea | a cake baking |
| a glass breaking | milk going sour |
| chopping vegetables | baking soda fizzing in vinegar |

The key question is always: **has a new substance been made?**

:::example A candle
A lit candle's wax melts near the wick, and some of it burns in the flame. Which change is physical and which is chemical?
@step Melted wax is still wax, so melting is a physical change.
@step Burning wax reacts with oxygen to make carbon dioxide and water, new substances.
@answer Melting is physical; burning is chemical.
:::

:::mistake Dissolving is physical
When sugar dissolves, it seems to vanish, but no new substance is made: the water tastes sweet, and evaporating the water gives the sugar back.
:::

## Can it be reversed?

:::definition Reversible change
A **reversible** change can be undone easily, for example by cooling, heating or evaporating. An **irreversible** change cannot be undone easily.
:::

Most **physical** changes are reversible: ice melts and water freezes again; salt dissolves and evaporating the water brings it back. Most **chemical** changes are irreversible: a boiled egg cannot be made runny again, and ash cannot be turned back into wood, because new substances have been made.

:::example Getting the salt back
Salt is dissolved in water. Can the change be reversed, and how?
@step Dissolving is a physical change, so it should be reversible.
@answer Yes: evaporate the water, and the salt is left behind.
:::

:::summary
- A **physical change** makes no new substance: changes of state, dissolving, breaking, cutting.
- A **chemical change** makes one or more new substances: burning, rusting, cooking, rotting.
- Most physical changes are easily reversed; most chemical changes are not.
:::
