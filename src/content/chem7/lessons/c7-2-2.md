An ice lolly drips on a hot day. A puddle disappears in the sun. A cold can of juice gets wet on the outside. Each of these is a **change of state**: a substance turning from one state into another without becoming a new substance.

## The changes of state

:::definition Changes of state
A **change of state** happens when a substance is heated or cooled enough for its particles to change how they are arranged. The substance stays the same substance: ice, water and steam are all water.

- **Melting**: solid → liquid.
- **Freezing**: liquid → solid.
- **Evaporation**: liquid → gas. When it happens all through the liquid, with bubbles, it is called **boiling**.
- **Condensation**: gas → liquid.
- **Sublimation**: solid → gas, without becoming a liquid first.
:::

To melt, evaporate or sublime, the particles must move apart and move more freely. That needs energy, so the substance must **take in heat**. Freezing and condensation go the other way: the particles come closer together and the substance **gives out heat**.

:::example Water on a cold can
Drops of water form on the outside of a cold can of juice. Where do they come from, and which change of state is this?
@step The water was a gas: water vapour in the air.
@step The vapour touches the cold can, gives out heat, and turns into a liquid.
@answer Condensation of water vapour from the air.
:::

:::mistake The water does not come through the can
The drops are not juice leaking out. They come from the air around the can, which always holds some invisible water vapour.
:::

## Melting points and boiling points

:::definition Melting point and boiling point
The **melting point** of a substance is the temperature at which it melts (and at which the liquid freezes). Its **boiling point** is the temperature at which it boils. Each pure substance has its own fixed melting point and boiling point, which we can use to identify it.
:::

So the state of a substance at any temperature depends on where that temperature sits:

- **below** the melting point, it is a **solid**;
- **between** the melting point and the boiling point, it is a **liquid**;
- **above** the boiling point, it is a **gas**.

| Substance | Melting point (°C) | Boiling point (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| oxygen | −218 | −183 |
| ethanol | −114 | 78 |
| mercury | −39 | 357 |
| water | 0 | 100 |
| sulfur | 115 | 445 |
| iron | 1538 | 2862 |

:::example The state of ethanol
What is the state of ethanol at 20 °C, and at 90 °C?
@step 20 °C is between −114 °C and 78 °C, so ethanol is a liquid.
@step 90 °C is above 78 °C, so ethanol is a gas.
@answer A liquid at 20 °C and a gas at 90 °C.
:::

:::mistake Negative temperatures
−120 °C is colder than −114 °C, so ethanol is a solid at −120 °C. With negative numbers, the number further from zero is the colder temperature.
:::

:::summary
- Melting (s → l), evaporation or boiling (l → g) and sublimation (s → g) need the substance to **take in heat**.
- Freezing (l → s) and condensation (g → l) **give out heat**.
- A change of state does not make a new substance.
- Below its **melting point** a substance is solid; between its melting and **boiling points** it is liquid; above its boiling point it is gas.
:::
