Chemistry is the study of matter: what things are made of, and how they change. But what exactly counts as matter? A stone obviously does. What about the air in a balloon, the light from a lamp, or the sound of a bell? In this lesson you will learn the test for matter, and how scientists describe a substance by its properties.

## What counts as matter

:::definition Matter
**Matter** is anything that has **mass** and takes up space (has **volume**). We use the word to separate the "stuff" that things are made of from things such as light and sound, which are forms of energy.

- **Mass** is the amount of matter in an object, measured in grams (g) or kilograms (kg).
- **Volume** is the amount of space an object takes up, measured in millilitres (mL), litres (L) or cubic centimetres (cm³).

Solids, liquids and gases are all matter: a brick, a glass of water and the air in a tyre.
:::

Gases are easy to forget, because most are invisible. But air is matter. A blown-up balloon has a little more mass than an empty one, because the air inside has mass. And an upturned empty glass pushed into water stays partly dry inside, because it is full of air that takes up space.

Light, sound, heat, electricity and radio signals are **not** matter. They are forms of energy: they have no mass, and they do not take up space.

:::example Is it matter?
Is the steam coming from a kettle matter? Is the sound the kettle makes when it boils matter?
@step Steam is water as a gas. It has mass and takes up space, so it is matter.
@step Sound is energy carried by vibrations. It has no mass of its own, so it is not matter.
@answer Steam is matter; the sound is not.
:::

:::mistake "Light" is not the same as "not matter"
Helium is so light that it makes a balloon float, but it is still matter: a balloon full of helium has more mass than an empty one. Being light (having a small mass) is not the same as having no mass.
:::

## Properties

Every **substance**, a single kind of matter such as water, iron or salt, has its own set of properties.

:::definition Property
A **property** is something about a substance that you can observe or measure. Properties let us describe a substance, tell it apart from others and choose it for a job.

- A **qualitative** property is described in words: *it is a shiny grey metal*, *it smells of vinegar*.
- A **quantitative** property is measured as a number: *it melts at 327 °C*, *its mass is 25 g*.
:::

Here are some properties you will use often:

| Property | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| colour and lustre | its colour, and whether it is shiny | gold is yellow and lustrous |
| hardness | how hard it is to scratch or dent | diamond is very hard |
| malleable | can be hammered into a new shape without breaking | aluminium foil |
| brittle | breaks or shatters easily | glass, chalk |
| magnetic | pulled towards a magnet | iron, nickel |
| conductor | lets heat or electricity pass through | copper |
| soluble | dissolves in water | sugar, salt |
| viscosity | how thick and slow-pouring a liquid is | honey is viscous |

:::example Choosing by property
Why are electrical wires made of copper covered in plastic?
@step Copper is a good conductor of electricity, so current flows easily along the wire.
@step Plastic does not conduct electricity, so it stops the current reaching your hand.
@answer Copper conducts the electricity; the plastic insulates it to keep people safe.
:::

Properties also work like clues. To identify an unknown substance, test one property at a time and rule out every substance that does not fit.

:::example Identifying a substance
A shiny grey solid conducts electricity and is pulled towards a magnet. Is it copper, iron or sulfur?
@step Sulfur is a yellow non-metal that does not conduct electricity, so it is ruled out.
@step Copper conducts but is reddish-brown and not magnetic, so it is ruled out.
@answer Iron: it is shiny grey, conducts and is magnetic.
:::

:::summary
- **Matter** is anything that has mass and takes up space. Solids, liquids and gases, including air, are matter.
- Light, sound, heat and electricity are forms of energy, not matter.
- **Mass** is the amount of matter in an object; **volume** is the space it takes up.
- A **property** is something we can observe or measure. Qualitative properties are described in words; quantitative properties are numbers.
- Properties help us describe, identify and choose substances.
:::
