Which is heavier, a kilogram of feathers or a kilogram of iron? Neither: they have the same mass. But the feathers fill a big sack and the iron fits in your hand. The difference is **density**, and it explains why a huge ship floats while a tiny pebble sinks.

## What density is

:::definition Density
**Density** is the mass of a substance in each unit of its volume: how tightly its matter is packed. We use it to compare substances fairly, whatever the size of the pieces.

$$\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$$

With the mass in grams and the volume in cm³, the density is in **g/cm³**. For a liquid measured in mL, it is in **g/mL** (the same size of unit, since 1 mL = 1 cm³).
:::

| Substance | Density (g/cm³) |
|---|---|
| polystyrene foam | 0.05 |
| cork | 0.24 |
| oak wood | 0.75 |
| ice | 0.92 |
| water | 1.00 |
| aluminium | 2.7 |
| iron | 7.9 |
| lead | 11.3 |
| gold | 19.3 |

:::example Calculating a density
A metal block has a mass of 54 g and a volume of 20 cm³. What is its density, and which metal in the table could it be?
@step $\text{density} = \dfrac{54}{20} = 2.7\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
@step Aluminium has a density of 2.7 g/cm³.
@answer $2.7\ \text{g/cm}^3$: it could be aluminium.
:::

:::mistake Density does not depend on size
Cut a block of wax in half and each half has half the mass and half the volume, so mass ÷ volume is unchanged. A small piece and a large piece of the same substance have the same density.
:::

The formula can be turned round. If you know the density, you can find a mass or a volume:

- mass = density × volume
- volume = mass ÷ density

:::example Finding a mass
Iron has a density of 7.9 g/cm³. What is the mass of 10 cm³ of iron?
@step mass = density × volume $= 7.9 \times 10$.
@answer 79 g.
:::

## Floating and sinking

:::definition Floating and sinking
A solid **floats** on a liquid if it is **less dense** than the liquid, and **sinks** if it is **denser**. Water has a density of 1 g/cm³, so anything less dense than 1 g/cm³ floats on water.
:::

Wood (0.75 g/cm³) floats and iron (7.9 g/cm³) sinks. A huge log floats while a small pebble sinks, because floating depends on density, not on size or mass. Ice (0.92 g/cm³) is slightly less dense than water, which is why icebergs float with most of their bulk under the surface.

Liquids that do not mix stack up in layers in the same way: the densest at the bottom, the least dense on top. Poured carefully into a tall glass, honey (1.42 g/mL) sinks below water (1.00 g/mL), and vegetable oil (0.92 g/mL) floats on top.

:::example Will it float?
A toy has a mass of 30 g and a volume of 40 cm³. Will it float in water?
@step density $= \dfrac{30}{40} = 0.75\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
@step $0.75$ is less than 1, the density of water.
@answer It floats.
:::

:::summary
- **Density = mass ÷ volume**, in g/cm³ (or g/mL). It tells how tightly matter is packed.
- Every piece of the same substance has the same density, whatever its size.
- mass = density × volume, and volume = mass ÷ density.
- A solid **less dense** than a liquid **floats** on it; a **denser** one sinks. Water's density is 1 g/cm³.
- Liquids that do not mix form layers with the densest at the bottom.
:::
