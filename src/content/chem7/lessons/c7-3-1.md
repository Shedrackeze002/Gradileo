A carton of juice says "100% pure". A bottle of water says "pure spring water". To a chemist, neither is pure. In science, **pure** has a precise meaning, and most of the things around us, from air to milk to soil, are mixtures.

## Pure substances and mixtures

:::definition Pure substance and mixture
A **pure substance** contains only **one** substance, made of only one kind of particle. Distilled water, oxygen gas, table salt and copper are pure substances.

A **mixture** contains **two or more** substances that are mixed together but not chemically joined. Each substance in a mixture keeps its own properties, and the substances can be separated again. Air, sea water, orange juice and soil are mixtures.
:::

In everyday talk, "pure" usually means "nothing added". Pure orange juice has nothing added, but it is still a mixture of water, sugars, acids and much more.

:::example Pure or mixture?
Is tap water a pure substance or a mixture?
@step Tap water contains small amounts of minerals and other substances dissolved in it.
@answer It is a mixture. Only distilled water is pure water.
:::

Pure substances can be recognized by their **melting and boiling points**. A pure solid melts sharply at one exact temperature. A mixture melts gradually over a range of temperatures, and anything dissolved in water changes the temperature at which it boils and freezes.

:::example A purity test
Two samples of a white powder are heated. Sample A melts sharply at 122 °C. Sample B starts to melt at 110 °C and is not fully melted until 118 °C. Which is pure?
@step A pure substance melts at one fixed temperature.
@answer Sample A is pure; sample B, melting over a range, is a mixture.
:::

## Kinds of mixture

Mixtures are sorted by how well their parts are mixed.

:::definition Kinds of mixture
- A **mechanical mixture** has parts you can see and pick out: gravel and sand, a salad, mixed nuts. It is **heterogeneous** (not the same all through).
- A **suspension** is cloudy, and its parts **settle out** when left to stand: muddy water, chalk stirred into water, shaken salad dressing.
- A **colloid** is cloudy, but its tiny bits are spread so finely that they **never settle**: milk, fog, whipped cream.
- A **solution** is one substance dissolved completely in another. It looks the same all through (it is **homogeneous**), is usually clear, and never separates by standing: salt water, sugar in tea, air.
:::

| Kind | Can you see the parts? | Clear or cloudy? | Settles if left? |
|---|---|---|---|
| mechanical mixture | yes | (separate pieces) | already separate |
| suspension | not at first | cloudy | yes |
| colloid | no | cloudy | no |
| solution | no | clear | no |

:::example Naming the kind
A mixture looks cloudy, but even after a week nothing has settled at the bottom. What kind of mixture is it?
@step It is cloudy, so it is not a solution.
@step Nothing settles, so it is not a suspension.
@answer It is a colloid, like milk.
:::

:::summary
- A **pure substance** contains only one substance; a **mixture** contains two or more substances not chemically joined.
- A pure substance melts and boils at fixed temperatures; a mixture melts over a range.
- **Mechanical mixtures** have parts you can see; **suspensions** are cloudy and settle; **colloids** are cloudy and never settle; **solutions** are clear and the same all through.
:::
