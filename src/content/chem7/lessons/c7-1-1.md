A science laboratory is full of useful things that can also hurt you: flames, hot glass, sharp edges and chemicals that burn, poison or catch fire. Scientists stay safe by following a few simple rules every time, and by reading the warnings printed on every bottle. In this lesson you will learn to read those warnings and to work safely.

## Hazard symbols

:::definition Hazard symbol
A **hazard symbol** (or hazard pictogram) is a small picture, in a red-bordered diamond, printed on the label of a dangerous chemical. Each picture warns of one kind of danger, so you can see the risk at a glance, whatever language you speak. We use them to decide how to handle a chemical safely before we open it.
:::

The same symbols are used in many countries. These are the ones you will meet most often:

| The picture shows | It warns | Take care to |
|---|---|---|
| a flame | **Flammable**: it catches fire easily | keep it away from flames, sparks and heat |
| a flame above a circle | **Oxidizer**: it makes other things burn more fiercely | keep it away from anything that burns |
| liquid dripping onto a hand and a surface | **Corrosive**: it attacks skin, eyes and metals | wear goggles and gloves; wash splashes off at once |
| a skull and crossbones | **Acutely toxic**: a small amount can poison | never let it touch skin, be breathed in or swallowed |
| an exclamation mark | **Harmful or irritant**: it can irritate skin, eyes or lungs | avoid touching or breathing it; wash your hands |
| a person with a star-shaped burst on the chest | **Serious health hazard**: it can cause long-term harm | use small amounts with good air flow |
| a gas cylinder | **Gas under pressure**: the container may burst if heated | keep the cylinder upright and away from heat |
| a dead tree and a dead fish | **Hazardous to the environment** | never pour it down the sink |
| an exploding bomb | **Explosive** | do not heat, strike or drop it |

:::example Reading a label
A bottle of ethanol (alcohol) has a hazard symbol showing a flame. What does the symbol warn, and what should you do?
@step A plain flame means the substance is flammable: it catches fire easily.
@step So it must be kept well away from any flame, spark or hot surface.
@answer Ethanol is flammable; keep it away from flames and heat.
:::

:::mistake A flame is not the same as a flame over a circle
A plain flame means the substance itself burns. A flame sitting on top of a circle means the substance is an **oxidizer**: it may not burn itself, but it feeds a fire and makes other things burn much more fiercely.
:::

## Working safely

Most accidents in a school lab come from a few careless moments. These rules prevent nearly all of them.

- **Goggles on** whenever chemicals are out or anything is heated, until everything is cleared away.
- **Read the label** before you use any chemical, and use only the amount you need.
- **Tie back long hair**, tuck in ties and loose clothing, and stand up when you use a burner.
- **Never eat, drink or taste** anything in a lab, and never sniff a chemical directly: **waft** the air towards your nose with your hand.
- When heating a test tube, point the open end **away from everyone**.
- Turn a lit burner that is not in use to the **yellow safety flame**, which is easy to see.
- **Tell your teacher at once** about any spill, breakage, cut, burn or smell of gas.
- Put waste where your teacher says, and **wash your hands** before you leave.

:::example A splash on the skin
During an experiment, a drop of dilute acid splashes onto your hand. What should you do?
@step Acid can damage skin, so it must be washed away quickly.
@step Running water washes the acid off and dilutes what is left.
@answer Rinse your hand under running water for several minutes, and tell your teacher.
:::

:::tip Walk, do not run
Even when something goes wrong, walk. Running in a lab knocks over equipment and people, and turns one accident into two.
:::

:::summary
- **Hazard symbols** in red diamonds warn of a chemical's dangers: flammable, oxidizer, corrosive, toxic, harmful, health hazard, gas under pressure, environmental hazard and explosive.
- Read every label before you use a chemical, and follow the care it calls for.
- Wear goggles, tie back hair, never eat, drink or taste in the lab, and waft to smell.
- Point heated test tubes away from people, and use the yellow safety flame when a burner is not in use.
- Report every spill, breakage and injury to your teacher straight away.
:::
