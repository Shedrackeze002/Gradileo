The black ink in a felt-tip pen looks like one colour, but it is often a mixture of several dyes. Forensic scientists use this to match the ink on a note to the pen that wrote it. The method they use is called chromatography, and it finishes our set of ways to separate mixtures.

## Paper chromatography

:::definition Chromatography
**Paper chromatography** separates the **dyes** in a mixture such as ink.

1. A **pencil** line, the start line, is drawn near the bottom of a strip of paper, and a small dot of ink is put on it.
2. The bottom edge of the paper is dipped in a **solvent**, such as water, with the dot **above** the solvent.
3. The solvent soaks up the paper and carries the dyes with it. Different dyes travel at **different speeds**, so they spread out into separate spots.

The finished paper is a **chromatogram**. The highest point the solvent reached is the **solvent front**.
:::

Each spot is a different dye. A pure dye gives only **one** spot; an ink that gives three spots is a mixture of three dyes. Two inks are the same if they give exactly the same spots at the same heights.

:::example Reading a chromatogram
In the chromatogram above, how many dyes does ink X contain, and which are they?
@step Ink X gives two spots, so it contains two dyes.
@step Its lower spot is level with dye A, and its upper spot is level with dye B.
@answer Two dyes: A and B.
:::

:::mistake Pencil, not pen
The start line is drawn in pencil because pencil does not dissolve in the solvent. A line drawn in pen would run up the paper and add extra spots. The ink dot must also sit above the solvent, or it would simply wash off into it.
:::

## Choosing a method

To choose a method, ask two questions. Is the solid **dissolved** or not? And which part do we want to **keep**: the solid, the liquid, or both?

| To separate… | Use | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| pieces of different sizes | sieving | small pieces fall through the holes |
| a magnetic metal from other solids | a magnet | only iron, steel, nickel and cobalt are pulled |
| an insoluble solid from a liquid | filtration | the solid is caught by the filter paper |
| a dissolved solid from a solution, keeping the solid | evaporation | the solvent escapes as a gas |
| a pure liquid from a solution | distillation | the vapour is condensed and collected |
| the dyes in an ink | chromatography | the dyes travel at different speeds |

Some mixtures need several steps, one after the other.

:::example Salt and sand
How can you get dry salt from a mixture of salt and sand?
@step Add water and stir. The salt dissolves; the sand does not.
@step Filter. The sand is the residue; the salt water is the filtrate.
@step Evaporate the filtrate. The water leaves and the salt is left.
@answer Dissolve in water, filter, then evaporate the filtrate.
:::

:::tip Magnetic parts first
If a mixture has iron in it, take the iron out with a magnet first, while everything is still dry.
:::

- **Chromatography** separates dyes, which travel up the paper at different speeds. Each spot is one dye; a pure dye gives one spot.
- Draw the start line in pencil and keep the ink dot above the solvent.
- Choose a method by asking whether the solid is dissolved and which part you want to keep.
- Some mixtures need several steps, such as dissolving, filtering and then evaporating.
