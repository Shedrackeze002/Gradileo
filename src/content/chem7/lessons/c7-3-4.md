Why do we stir sugar into tea, and why does sugar vanish so much faster in hot tea than in iced tea? How quickly a solute dissolves is called the **rate of dissolving**. In this lesson you will find what changes it, and how scientists design a fair test to find out.

## The rate of dissolving

:::definition Rate of dissolving
The **rate of dissolving** is how quickly a solute dissolves in a solvent. We usually measure it by timing how long a fixed mass of solute takes to dissolve: the shorter the time, the faster the rate.
:::

Three things make a solid dissolve faster:

- **Stirring** (or shaking) brings fresh solvent to the solid and carries dissolved particles away.
- **Heating** the solvent makes its particles move faster, so they hit the solid more often and harder and pull its particles away sooner.
- **Smaller pieces** have more surface touching the solvent, so more particles can be pulled away at once. Granulated sugar dissolves faster than a sugar cube of the same mass.

:::example The fastest cup
In which will a spoonful of granulated sugar dissolve fastest: cold water left still, cold water stirred, or hot water stirred?
@step Stirring helps, and so does heat.
@answer Hot water, stirred.
:::

:::mistake Faster is not more
Stirring makes sugar dissolve **faster**, but it does not let **more** sugar dissolve. How much can dissolve in total is the solubility, which depends on the temperature, not on stirring.
:::

## Fair tests

To find out whether one thing really changes the rate of dissolving, we must change that one thing and keep everything else the same. This is a **fair test**.

:::definition Variables in a fair test
- The **independent variable** is the one thing you change on purpose.
- The **dependent variable** is what you measure, to see the effect.
- The **controlled variables** are everything else that could affect the result, which you keep the same.
:::

:::example Designing a fair test
Fatima wants to find out whether the temperature of water changes how fast salt dissolves. Name her variables.
@step She changes the temperature of the water: that is the independent variable.
@step She times how long the salt takes to dissolve: that is the dependent variable.
@step She must keep the mass of salt, the volume of water, the size of the grains and the amount of stirring the same: these are controlled variables.
@answer Independent: temperature. Dependent: the time to dissolve. Controlled: mass of salt, volume of water, grain size and stirring.
:::

:::tip Repeat and average
Repeating each test and taking the average of the times makes a result more reliable, because one odd reading has less effect.

:::summary
- Stirring, heating the solvent and using smaller pieces all make a solid **dissolve faster**.
- They change the rate, not how much can dissolve in total.
- In a **fair test**, change only the **independent variable**, measure the **dependent variable**, and keep the **controlled variables** the same.
:::
