A recycling plant gets lorries full of mixed rubbish, and a gold prospector shakes a pan of river gravel. Both are separating mixtures. Because the parts of a mixture are not chemically joined, we can pull them apart again, as long as we find a property that is different for each part.

## Sieving, magnets and picking out

:::definition Separating a mixture
To **separate** a mixture, we use a property that one part has and the other part does not.

- **Sieving** uses the **size** of the pieces: small pieces fall through the holes and large pieces are caught.
- **Magnetic separation** uses whether a substance is **magnetic**: a magnet pulls out iron, steel and nickel, and leaves everything else.
- **Picking out by hand** works when the pieces are large and easy to tell apart.
:::

Only a few metals are magnetic: iron, steel (which is mostly iron), nickel and cobalt. Copper, aluminium, gold and all non-metals are not, so a magnet cannot separate copper from sand.

:::example Iron and sand
How can you separate iron filings from sand?
@step Look for a property that is different. Iron is magnetic and sand is not.
@answer Pass a magnet over the mixture: the iron filings are pulled out and the sand is left.
:::

:::tip Wrap the magnet
Wrap the magnet in cling film or paper first. Then you can pull the iron off easily by peeling the wrapping away.
:::

## Filtration

Sand and water cannot be sieved apart, because the water and the sand grains both slip through the holes of a kitchen sieve. Filter paper has holes far too small to see.

:::definition Filtration
**Filtration** separates an **insoluble** solid from a liquid. The mixture is poured through filter paper in a funnel.

- The **filtrate** is the liquid that passes through the paper.
- The **residue** is the solid left on the paper.

Water particles are tiny and pass through the paper, while the much larger grains of the solid are caught.
:::

If the solid settles quickly, much of the clear liquid can be poured off the top first. This is called **decanting**, and it makes filtering faster.

:::mistake A filter cannot catch dissolved solids
Salt water poured through filter paper comes out just as salty. Dissolved particles are spread out between the water particles and are as small as them, so they pass straight through. Filtration only removes solids that have **not** dissolved.
:::

:::example Salt and sand
A 50 g mixture of salt and sand is stirred into water and filtered. The dried residue has a mass of 32 g. What mass of salt was in the mixture?
@step The salt dissolves and passes through the filter. The sand is the residue.
@answer $50 - 32 = 18$ g of salt.
:::

:::summary
- We separate a mixture using a property that is different for its parts.
- **Sieving** uses size; a **magnet** pulls out iron, steel, nickel and cobalt.
- **Filtration** separates an insoluble solid from a liquid: the **filtrate** passes through, the **residue** stays on the paper.
- Dissolved solids pass through filter paper, so filtration cannot separate a solution.
:::
