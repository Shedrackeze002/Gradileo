Long bridges are built with small gaps between their sections. Power lines sag lower on a hot summer day. A smell spreads across a room in a few minutes. The particle model explains all of these, through two ideas: **expansion** and **diffusion**.

## Expansion and contraction

:::definition Expansion and contraction
**Expansion** is the increase in size of a substance when it is heated. **Contraction** is the decrease in size when it is cooled. Most solids, liquids and gases expand when heated and contract when cooled.
:::

When a solid is heated, its particles vibrate more strongly and push one another slightly further apart, so the solid takes up more room. The particles themselves do **not** grow. Liquids and gases expand in the same way, and gases expand the most.

Expansion is put to use, and guarded against, all around us:

- **Bridges and railways** have small gaps, or expansion joints, so the sections can expand on hot days without buckling.
- **Power lines** are hung loosely, so they can contract on cold days without snapping.
- The liquid in a **thermometer** expands as it warms and rises up the thin tube.
- A stuck metal lid on a glass jar loosens under hot water, because the metal expands more than the glass.

:::example A ball and ring
A metal ball just passes through a metal ring. The ball is heated. Will it still pass through? Explain.
@step Heating makes the particles of the ball vibrate more and move slightly apart.
@step So the ball expands and becomes slightly bigger.
@answer No: the heated ball has expanded, so it no longer fits through the ring.
:::

:::mistake The particles do not get bigger
In expansion, the number of particles and their size stay the same. Only the space between them grows. The mass does not change either.
:::

## Diffusion

:::definition Diffusion
**Diffusion** is the spreading out of particles, by their own random movement, from where there are many of them to where there are few, until they are evenly mixed.
:::

Open a bottle of perfume at the front of a room, and soon it can be smelled at the back. Drop food colouring into still water, and the colour slowly spreads until all the water is coloured. Nobody stirs; the particles spread themselves.

Diffusion is:

- **fastest in gases**, where the particles move quickly and have lots of space;
- **slower in liquids**, where the particles keep bumping into neighbours;
- **almost nothing in solids**, where the particles are held in place;
- **faster when it is warmer**, because the particles move faster.

:::example Tea in hot and cold water
A tea bag is put into a cup of hot water and another into a cup of cold water. In which cup does the colour spread faster, and why?
@step The colour spreads by diffusion.
@step In hot water the particles move faster, so they spread out faster.
@answer The hot water, because its particles move faster.
:::

:::summary
- Most substances **expand** when heated and **contract** when cooled, because their particles move more and take up more room. The particles do not grow.
- Expansion joints in bridges, sagging power lines and thermometers are everyday examples.
- **Diffusion** is particles spreading out by their own movement until they are evenly mixed.
- Diffusion is fastest in gases, slower in liquids and almost nothing in solids, and it is faster when warmer.
:::
