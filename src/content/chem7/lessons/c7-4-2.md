Filtering salt water does not get the salt out, because the salt is dissolved. To separate a solution we need heat: we turn the solvent into a gas. What we do with that gas decides whether we keep the solid, or the liquid as well.

## Evaporation and crystallization

:::definition Evaporation
**Evaporation** separates a dissolved solid from its solution. The solution is heated in an evaporating dish until the solvent turns into a gas and escapes into the air. The solid, which has a much higher boiling point, is left behind.
:::

Water boils at 100 °C, but salt would need over 1400 °C, so only the water leaves. In hot, sunny countries, sea water is let into shallow ponds called salt pans, and the Sun evaporates the water to leave sea salt.

:::definition Crystallization
**Crystallization** is the forming of crystals of a solute as the solvent evaporates or the solution cools. If the solvent leaves **slowly**, the crystals have time to grow large.
:::

To get good crystals of copper sulfate, we heat the solution only until about half the water has gone, then leave it to cool and evaporate slowly. Boiling it dry makes the hot solid spit out of the dish and can break it down.

:::example Salt from a solution
A solution has 30 g of salt in every litre. 250 mL of it is evaporated to dryness. What mass of salt is left?
@step $250\ \text{mL} = 0.25\ \text{L}$
@step $30 \times 0.25 = 7.5$
@answer $7.5$ g of salt.
:::

## Distillation

Evaporation loses the water to the air. To keep the water as well, we must catch the vapour and cool it.

:::definition Distillation
**Distillation** gets a pure liquid from a solution. It is two changes of state, one after the other:

1. The solution is heated in a flask until the solvent **boils** and becomes a vapour.
2. The vapour passes into a **condenser**, a tube cooled by cold water flowing around it, where it **condenses** back into a liquid.

The pure liquid collected at the end is the **distillate**. The dissolved solid stays behind in the flask.
:::

| Part of the apparatus | What happens there when salt water is distilled |
|---|---|
| flask, heated from below | the water boils and becomes steam; the salt stays behind |
| condenser, cooled by cold water | the steam cools and condenses into liquid water |
| beaker at the end | pure water, the distillate, drips in |

Distillation is used to make drinking water from sea water on ships and in dry countries. It can also separate two liquids that boil at different temperatures, such as alcohol (78 °C) and water (100 °C).

:::example What goes where?
Salt water is distilled. What is in the flask at the end, and what is in the beaker?
@step The water boils at 100 °C and goes into the condenser. The salt does not boil.
@answer The salt stays in the flask; the beaker collects pure water.
:::

:::mistake The distillate is not salty
The salt never leaves the flask, so the distillate is pure water. Only the substance that boils travels through the condenser.
:::

:::summary
- **Evaporation** removes the solvent as a gas and leaves the dissolved solid behind.
- **Crystallization**: slow evaporation or cooling lets large crystals form.
- **Distillation** is boiling then condensing: the vapour is cooled in a **condenser** and collected as the pure **distillate**.
- Use evaporation to keep the solid; use distillation to keep the liquid too.
:::
