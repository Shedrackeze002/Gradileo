Two skaters stand face to face on smooth ice and push each other away. Each one moves off backwards. Neither had any momentum before the push, and afterwards both do. Has momentum been made from nothing? It has not. Their momenta point in opposite directions, and when you add them with their signs the total is still zero. This lesson is about that total, which a collision or an explosion cannot change.

## Collisions

When two objects collide, each pushes on the other. By Newton's third law the two forces are equal in size and opposite in direction, and they act for the same time. So the two impulses are equal and opposite, and the two changes in momentum are equal and opposite as well. Whatever momentum one object loses, the other gains.

:::definition The law of conservation of momentum
The total momentum of an **isolated system** stays constant. For two objects that collide along one line:

$$m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$

Here $u_1$ and $u_2$ are the velocities just before the collision, and $v_1$ and $v_2$ are the velocities just after it. A system is isolated when no net force acts on it from outside.
:::

Forces from outside, such as friction, do act on real cars and carts. But a collision is over so quickly that these forces have no time to change the total momentum much. That is why we always compare the moments **just before** and **just after** the collision.

Every collision problem is solved in the same way.

1. Choose a positive direction, and give every velocity its sign.
2. Add up the momentum of everything just before the collision.
3. Write the total momentum just after the collision, with a letter for what you do not know.
4. Set the two totals equal, and solve.

:::example Two carts that stick together
A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s runs into a 4 kg cart that is at rest. They stick together. How fast do they move just after the collision?
@step Before: $2 \times 3 + 4 \times 0 = 6$ kg·m/s.
@step After, the two move as one object of mass $2 + 4 = 6$ kg: the momentum is $6v$.
@step $6v = 6$.
@answer $v = 1$ m/s, in the direction the first cart was moving.
:::

:::example A head-on tackle
An 80 kg rugby player running east at 7 m/s tackles a 100 kg player running west at 3 m/s, and they hold on to each other. What is their velocity just after the tackle?
@step Take east as positive. Before: $80 \times 7 + 100 \times (-3) = 560 - 300 = 260$ kg·m/s.
@step After: $(80 + 100)v = 180v$.
@step $v = \dfrac{260}{180}$.
@answer $v = +1.44$ m/s. The two players move east.
:::

The lighter player wins this tackle, because 560 kg·m/s of momentum to the east is more than 300 kg·m/s to the west.

Objects do not have to stick together. If they bounce apart, you need to know one of the velocities after the collision to find the other.

:::example Two carts that bounce apart
Cart A, of mass 2 kg, moves east at 4 m/s and collides with cart B, of mass 1 kg, which moves west at 1 m/s. Just after the collision, cart A moves east at 1.5 m/s. What is the velocity of cart B?
@step Take east as positive. Before: $2 \times 4 + 1 \times (-1) = 7$ kg·m/s.
@step After: cart A has $2 \times 1.5 = 3$ kg·m/s, so cart B has $7 - 3 = 4$ kg·m/s.
@step $v_B = \dfrac{4}{1}$.
@answer $v_B = +4$ m/s. Cart B has turned around and moves east.
:::

:::mistake Leaving out the sign of a velocity
In the tackle above, adding $560 + 300$ gives 860 kg·m/s and a speed of 4.8 m/s, which is far too fast. The two players run in opposite directions, so one momentum is negative. Choose the positive direction before you write any numbers.
:::

## Recoil and explosions

In an explosion, objects that were together fly apart. The forces act between the parts of the system, so the law of conservation of momentum holds exactly as it does for a collision. If everything was at rest at the start, the total momentum is zero, and it is still zero afterwards:

$$0 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$

The two momenta are equal in size and opposite in direction.

:::example Two skaters push apart
Two skaters stand at rest on smooth ice and push each other apart. The 60 kg skater moves east at 2 m/s. How fast does the 80 kg skater move?
@step The 60 kg skater has $60 \times 2 = 120$ kg·m/s of momentum, directed east.
@step The 80 kg skater must have 120 kg·m/s directed west, so that the total stays zero.
@step $v = \dfrac{120}{80}$.
@answer 1.5 m/s, directed west.
:::

The heavier skater moves more slowly. Equal momentum does not mean equal speed. This is why a gun moves back, or **recoils**, far more slowly than its bullet moves forward.

:::example The recoil of a rifle
A 4 kg rifle at rest fires a 10 g bullet at 800 m/s. How fast does the rifle recoil?
@step $m = 10\ \text{g} = 0.010$ kg, so the bullet has $0.010 \times 800 = 8$ kg·m/s of momentum.
@step The rifle has 8 kg·m/s in the opposite direction: $v = \dfrac{8}{4}$.
@answer The rifle recoils at 2 m/s.
:::

A rocket works in the same way. It throws exhaust gas backwards at high speed, and gains an equal amount of momentum forwards. It does not need air or ground to push against, so it works in empty space.

When the object is already moving before it breaks up, the total momentum is not zero. It is the momentum the whole object had.

:::example An explosion in flight
A 5 kg firework moving east at 4 m/s explodes into two pieces. Just after the explosion, a 2 kg piece moves east at 13 m/s. What is the velocity of the other piece, which has a mass of 3 kg?
@step Take east as positive. Before: $5 \times 4 = 20$ kg·m/s.
@step After: the 2 kg piece has $2 \times 13 = 26$ kg·m/s, so the 3 kg piece has $20 - 26 = -6$ kg·m/s.
@step $v = \dfrac{-6}{3}$.
@answer $v = -2$ m/s. The 3 kg piece moves west.
:::

In an explosion the total momentum stays the same, but the total kinetic energy increases. The extra energy comes from the chemical energy of the explosive, or from a spring, or from the muscles of the skaters.

:::mistake Giving both objects the same speed
After an explosion from rest, the two objects have the same size of **momentum**, not the same speed. The object with less mass moves faster. Find the momentum of one object first, and then divide by the mass of the other.
:::

:::summary
- The total momentum of an isolated system stays constant: $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$.
- Compare the moments just before and just after a collision, and give every velocity its sign.
- Objects that stick together move as one object with the two masses added.
- In an explosion from rest the total momentum stays zero, so the momenta are equal in size and opposite in direction.
- The lighter object moves faster. A rifle recoils slowly because it has much more mass than its bullet.
:::
