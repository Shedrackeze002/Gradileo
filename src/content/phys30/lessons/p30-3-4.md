A camera, a projector, a microscope, a pair of glasses and your own eye all depend on a lens. A lens is a piece of glass or clear plastic with curved faces. Light refracts as it enters and again as it leaves, and the curve of the faces is chosen so that the rays come together or spread apart in a controlled way. The ray diagrams and the equation of the last lesson work for lenses with only small changes.

## Rays and images in lenses

A **converging** lens is thicker in the middle than at the edges. It is also called a convex lens. It brings parallel rays together at a point. A **diverging** lens is thinner in the middle than at the edges, and is also called a concave lens. It spreads parallel rays apart.

Light can pass through a lens from either side, so a lens has a focus on each side, at the same distance from the lens.

- The **principal axis** is the line through the centre of the lens, at right angles to it.
- The **focus**, $F$, of a converging lens is the point where rays parallel to the axis meet after they pass through the lens. For a diverging lens, the rays spread out as if they came from a focus on the side the light came from.
- The **focal length**, $f$, is the distance from the centre of the lens to the focus. A strongly curved lens bends light more, so it has a shorter focal length.

### Three rays that find the image

We treat the lens as thin, and draw all of the bending at a single line through its centre. Three rays from the top of the object are easy to draw.

1. A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through the focus on the far side of the lens.
2. A ray through the centre of the lens carries straight on.
3. A ray through the focus on the near side leaves the lens parallel to the principal axis.

For a diverging lens, ray 1 leaves the lens as if it came from the focus on the near side, and ray 2 still carries straight on. The rays spread apart after the lens, so they are extended back with dashed lines to find a virtual image.

### The images of a converging lens

| Where the object is | The image |
|---|---|
| Beyond $2f$ | Real, upside down, smaller than the object |
| At $2f$ | Real, upside down, the same size |
| Between $2f$ and $f$ | Real, upside down, larger than the object |
| At $f$ | No image: the rays leave parallel |
| Between $f$ and the lens | Virtual, upright, larger than the object |

The pattern is the same as that of a concave mirror, with one difference. Light passes through a lens, so a **real** image is on the **far** side of the lens from the object, and a **virtual** image is on the **same** side as the object.

A diverging lens always forms a virtual, upright image that is smaller than the object.

### Lenses at work

- A **camera** and the **eye** have the object far beyond $2f$, so a small, real, upside-down image falls on the sensor or on the retina. The brain turns the picture the right way up.
- A **projector** has the slide or the display between $f$ and $2f$, so a large, real image falls on a distant screen. The picture is upside down, so the slide is put in upside down.
- A **magnifying glass** is held closer to the object than its focus, so the eye sees a large, upright, virtual image.

A **short-sighted** eye bends light too strongly. The light from a distant object comes to a focus in front of the retina, and a diverging lens corrects it. A **long-sighted** eye does not bend the light from a near object enough, and a converging lens corrects it.

Every part of a lens sends light from every point of the object to the matching point of the image. If half of a lens is covered, the whole image is still there, but it is dimmer.

A ray through the centre of a thin lens is not bent. Only the other two rays change direction.
:::

## The thin lens equation

The equation for a thin lens is the same as the mirror equation.

:::definition The thin lens equation and magnification
$$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i} \qquad\qquad m = \dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$$

- $f$ is the focal length;
- $d_o$ is the distance from the lens to the object, and $d_i$ is the distance from the lens to the image;
- $h_o$ and $h_i$ are the heights of the object and the image;
- $m$ is the magnification.
:::

| Quantity | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Focal length $f$ | Converging lens | Diverging lens |
| Image distance $d_i$ | Real image, on the far side of the lens | Virtual image, on the same side as the object |
| Image height $h_i$ and magnification $m$ | Upright image | Upside-down image |

:::example A real image
An object 4.0 cm tall is 30 cm from a converging lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the position and the height of the image.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{d_o} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{2}{30}$, so $d_i = 15$ cm.
@step $m = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = -\dfrac{15}{30} = -0.50$, so $h_i = -0.50 \times 4.0 = -2.0$ cm.
@answer The image is 15 cm from the lens, on the far side. It is real, upside down and 2.0 cm tall.
:::

:::example A magnifying glass
A stamp is 6.0 cm from a magnifying glass of focal length 10 cm. Find the position of the image and the magnification.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{6.0} = \dfrac{3}{30} - \dfrac{5}{30} = -\dfrac{2}{30}$, so $d_i = -15$ cm.
@step $m = -\dfrac{-15}{6.0} = +2.5$.
@answer The image is 15 cm from the lens, on the same side as the stamp. It is virtual, upright and 2.5 times as large.
:::

:::example A diverging lens
An object is 30 cm from a diverging lens of focal length 20 cm. Find the position of the image and the magnification.
@step The lens is diverging, so $f = -20$ cm.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{-20} - \dfrac{1}{30} = -\dfrac{3}{60} - \dfrac{2}{60} = -\dfrac{5}{60}$, so $d_i = -12$ cm.
@step $m = -\dfrac{-12}{30} = +0.40$.
@answer The image is 12 cm from the lens, on the same side as the object. It is virtual, upright and 0.40 times the size of the object.
:::

:::example A projector
A projector forms a sharp picture on a screen 200 cm from its lens. The picture is 4.0 times as large as the slide. Find the distance from the slide to the lens, and the focal length of the lens.
@step The image is real, so it is upside down and $m = -4.0$. Then $d_o = -\dfrac{d_i}{m} = \dfrac{200}{4.0} = 50$ cm.
@step $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{50} + \dfrac{1}{200} = \dfrac{5}{200}$.
@answer The slide is 50 cm from the lens, and the focal length is 40 cm. The slide is between $f$ and $2f$, as it must be for a large real image.
:::

:::example A camera lens
A camera lens has a focal length of 50.0 mm. How far from the lens must the sensor be to photograph a person 2.00 m away?
@step Work in millimetres: $d_o = 2000$ mm.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{50.0} - \dfrac{1}{2000} = 0.02000 - 0.00050 = 0.01950$.
@answer $d_i = 51.3$ mm. For a distant object the image is only a little further from the lens than the focus.
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
The distances may be in centimetres or millimetres, but all three must be in the same unit. Change them before you take any reciprocals.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the negative focal length of a diverging lens
A diverging lens has a negative focal length, like a convex mirror. Its image distance always comes out negative too, since its image is always virtual.
:::

:::summary
- A converging lens is thicker in the middle and brings parallel rays to a focus. A diverging lens is thinner in the middle and spreads them apart.
- Rays for a diagram: parallel to the axis passes through the far focus; through the centre carries straight on; through the near focus leaves parallel.
- A converging lens forms a real, upside-down image of an object beyond its focus, and a virtual, upright, larger image of an object inside its focus.
- A diverging lens always forms a virtual, upright, smaller image.
- $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i}$ and $m = \dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$.
- Negative $f$: diverging lens. Negative $d_i$: virtual image on the object's side. Negative $m$: upside-down image.
:::
