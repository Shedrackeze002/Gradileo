Oersted showed that a current makes a magnetic field. Michael Faraday was sure that the reverse must also be possible, and in 1831, after years of trying, he found how: a magnetic field makes a current only while it is **changing**. This is **electromagnetic induction**. Almost all of the electrical energy you use is made this way, in generators that turn coils near magnets.

## Electromagnetic induction

Connect a coil to a sensitive meter and push a bar magnet into the coil. The meter shows a current, but only while the magnet moves. Hold the magnet still inside the coil, and the current stops. Pull the magnet out, and the current flows the other way. The moving magnet **induces** an emf in the coil, and the emf drives the current.

The emf is larger when the magnet moves faster, when the magnet is stronger and when the coil has more turns.

### Magnetic flux and Faraday's law

To say exactly what must change, we need the idea of **magnetic flux**. It measures how much magnetic field passes through a loop.

:::definition Magnetic flux
The magnetic flux through a loop of area $A$, with a field $B$ at right angles to the plane of the loop, is

$$\Phi = BA$$

It is measured in **webers** (Wb). One weber is one tesla times one square metre.
:::

:::definition Faraday's law
The emf induced in a coil is proportional to the rate at which the magnetic flux through it changes. For a coil of $N$ turns,

$$\varepsilon = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$$

where $\Delta\Phi$ is the change in the flux through each turn, in webers, and $\Delta t$ is the time it takes, in seconds.
:::

The flux through a coil changes when the field changes, when the area changes, or when the coil turns. A steady flux, however large, induces nothing.

:::example A coil in a changing field
A coil of 200 turns has an area of 0.0050 m². A magnetic field passes through the coil at right angles to its plane, and changes steadily from 0.10 T to 0.60 T in 0.25 s. Find the size of the emf induced in the coil.
@step The change in the flux through each turn: $\Delta\Phi = A\Delta B = 0.0050 \times 0.50 = 0.0025$ Wb.
@step $\varepsilon = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = 200 \times \dfrac{0.0025}{0.25}$.
@answer $\varepsilon = 2.0$ V.
:::

:::example A change in flux given in milliwebers
The magnetic flux through each turn of a 500-turn coil falls steadily from 8.0 mWb to 2.0 mWb in 0.60 s. Find the size of the induced emf.
@step $\Delta\Phi = 6.0\ \text{mWb} = 0.0060$ Wb.
@step $\varepsilon = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = 500 \times \dfrac{0.0060}{0.60}$.
@answer $\varepsilon = 5.0$ V.
:::

A straight conductor that moves across a magnetic field also has an emf induced in it. The field pushes the free charges in the conductor towards one end, as it pushes any moving charge.

For a conductor of length $L$ that moves at speed $v$, with the conductor, its velocity and the field all at right angles to one another,

$$\varepsilon = BLv$$

:::example A rod on rails
A metal rod slides at 6.0 m/s along two parallel rails that are 0.50 m apart, at right angles to a magnetic field of 0.40 T. The rails are joined by a 2.0 Ω resistor, and the rod and the rails have no resistance. Find the emf induced in the rod, and the current in the resistor.
@step $\varepsilon = BLv = 0.40 \times 0.50 \times 6.0 = 1.2$ V.
@step $I = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{R} = \dfrac{1.2}{2.0}$.
@answer The emf is 1.2 V, and the current is 0.60 A.
:::

:::example The wings of an aircraft
An aircraft with a wingspan of 40 m flies level at 250 m/s where the vertical part of the Earth's magnetic field is $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$ T. What emf is induced between its wingtips?
@step The wings are a conductor that cuts across the vertical field.
@step $\varepsilon = BLv = 5.0 \times 10^{-5} \times 40 \times 250$.
@answer $\varepsilon = 0.50$ V.
:::

### Lenz's law

Faraday's law gives the size of the induced emf. Its direction comes from a law found by Heinrich Lenz.

:::definition Lenz's law
An induced current flows in the direction that **opposes the change** that causes it.
:::

Push the north pole of a magnet towards a coil, and the induced current makes that end of the coil a north pole, which repels the magnet. Pull the north pole away, and that end of the coil becomes a south pole, which attracts the magnet and tries to hold it back. Either way, you must do work to move the magnet, and that work is the source of the electrical energy.

Lenz's law is the conservation of energy at work. If the induced current helped the change instead, the magnet would be pulled in faster and faster, and energy would be created from nothing.

The same thing happens to the rod on the rails. The induced current in the rod is a current in a magnetic field, so the rod feels a force, and by Lenz's law that force is backwards, against the motion. A strong magnet dropped down a copper pipe falls slowly for the same reason: it induces currents in the pipe, and their fields oppose its fall.

:::mistake Expecting a current from a strong but steady field
A magnet at rest inside a coil induces nothing, however strong it is. Only a **change** in the flux induces an emf, and a faster change induces a larger one.
:::

## Generators and transformers

A **generator** is a motor run backwards. Something turns a coil in a magnetic field: falling water, steam, the wind or a diesel engine. The flux through the coil changes all the time, and an emf is induced. A generator changes kinetic energy to electrical energy.

Each side of the coil cuts across the field one way for half a turn and the other way for the next half. So the emf reverses every half turn, and the generator gives an **alternating current**.

- The emf is greatest when the plane of the coil is parallel to the field. The sides of the coil are then cutting straight across the field, and the flux is changing fastest.
- The emf is zero when the plane of the coil is at right angles to the field. The flux through the coil is at its greatest, but for a moment it is not changing.
- Turn the coil twice as fast, and the flux changes twice as fast. Both the greatest emf and the frequency double.

The coil of an alternating current generator is joined to its circuit through two **slip rings**. Each ring is joined to one end of the coil and always touches the same brush, so the alternating emf reaches the circuit unchanged. If the slip rings are replaced by the split-ring commutator of a motor, the connections swap every half turn, just as the emf reverses. The output then rises and falls, but it always flows the same way: a direct current generator.

### The transformer

A **transformer** changes the voltage of an alternating supply. Two coils are wound on the same iron core. An alternating current in the **primary** coil makes a magnetic field in the core that changes all the time. The changing flux passes through the **secondary** coil and induces an alternating emf in it.

The same flux passes through every turn of both coils, so the voltages are in the same ratio as the numbers of turns:

$$\dfrac{V_s}{V_p} = \dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$$

More turns on the secondary than on the primary gives a **step-up** transformer. Fewer gives a **step-down** transformer. A transformer does not work with a steady direct current, because a steady current gives a steady field, and a steady field induces nothing.

:::example A step-down transformer
A transformer has 1000 turns on its primary coil and 50 turns on its secondary coil. The primary is connected to a 240 V alternating supply. Find the secondary voltage.
@step $\dfrac{V_s}{V_p} = \dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$, so $V_s = V_p \times \dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$.
@step $V_s = 240 \times \dfrac{50}{1000}$.
@answer $V_s = 12$ V.
:::

A transformer cannot create energy. An **ideal** transformer wastes none, so the power that goes in equals the power that comes out:

$$V_pI_p = V_sI_s$$

If the voltage is stepped down, the current is stepped up by the same factor, and the other way round.

:::example The current in the primary coil
The 12 V secondary of the transformer above supplies a current of 3.0 A to a lamp. Find the current in the primary coil. Take the transformer to be ideal.
@step Power out: $P = V_sI_s = 12 \times 3.0 = 36$ W.
@step Power in equals power out: $240 \times I_p = 36$.
@answer $I_p = 0.15$ A.
:::

### Sending energy across the country

The cables that carry electrical energy from a power station have resistance, so they are heated by the current, and the power wasted is $I^2R$. For a given power $P = VI$, a higher voltage needs a smaller current. So the voltage is stepped up before the energy is sent and stepped down again where it is used.

:::example Why the voltage is stepped up
A power station sends 200 kW through cables with a total resistance of 5.0 Ω. Find the power wasted in the cables when the energy is sent at 4000 V, and when it is sent at 40 000 V.
@step At 4000 V: $I = \dfrac{P}{V} = \dfrac{200\,000}{4000} = 50$ A.
@step Wasted: $I^2R = 50^2 \times 5.0 = 12\,500$ W.
@step At 40 000 V: $I = \dfrac{200\,000}{40\,000} = 5.0$ A.
@step Wasted: $I^2R = 5.0^2 \times 5.0 = 125$ W.
@answer 12 500 W at 4000 V, but only 125 W at 40 000 V. Ten times the voltage wastes a hundredth of the power.
:::

:::mistake Thinking that a step-up transformer gives more power
A step-up transformer raises the voltage, but it lowers the current by the same factor. The power stays the same, or a little less in a real transformer.
:::

:::summary
- A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an emf. Flux is $\Phi = BA$, in webers.
- Faraday's law: $\varepsilon = N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$. For a conductor that moves across a field, $\varepsilon = BLv$.
- Lenz's law: an induced current opposes the change that causes it, as the conservation of energy requires.
- A generator turns a coil in a field and gives an alternating current through slip rings. A commutator makes the output direct.
- An ideal transformer: $\dfrac{V_s}{V_p} = \dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$ and $V_pI_p = V_sI_s$. It needs an alternating current.
- Energy is sent at a high voltage because a small current wastes less power, $I^2R$, in the cables.
:::
