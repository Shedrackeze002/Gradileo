A flat bathroom mirror shows you a copy of yourself that is the right way up and the right size. The inside of a shiny spoon shows you upside down, and the back of the spoon shows a small face in a wide room. All three obey one simple law. This lesson shows how that law, with a ruler and three special rays, finds the image in any mirror, and how one equation does the same job with numbers.

## Rays and images in mirrors

Light travels in straight lines, so we draw its path as a **ray**. When a ray meets a mirror, we measure its direction from the **normal**, the line at right angles to the surface at the point where the ray strikes.

:::definition The law of reflection
The angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence:

$$\theta_r = \theta_i$$

Both angles are measured from the normal, and the two rays and the normal lie in one plane.
:::

A ray that strikes a mirror at 30° to its surface makes an angle of 60° with the normal, so it reflects at 60° to the normal.

### The image in a flat mirror

Rays from a point on an object spread out, reflect from the mirror, and keep spreading. To an eye, they seem to come from a point behind the mirror. No light is really there, so the image is called **virtual**.

- A **real image** is formed where rays of light actually meet. It can be caught on a screen.
- A **virtual image** is formed where rays only appear to come from. It cannot be caught on a screen.

The image in a flat mirror is virtual, upright, the same size as the object, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

### Curved mirrors

A **concave** mirror curves inwards, like the inside of a spoon. It brings parallel rays together, so it is a converging mirror. A **convex** mirror bulges outwards, like the back of a spoon. It spreads parallel rays apart, so it is a diverging mirror.

Four terms describe a curved mirror.

- The **centre of curvature**, $C$, is the centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part.
- The **principal axis** is the line through $C$ and the middle of the mirror.
- The **focus**, $F$, is the point on the axis where rays parallel to the axis meet after they reflect from a concave mirror. For a convex mirror, the reflected rays spread out as if they came from a focus behind the mirror.
- The **focal length**, $f$, is the distance from the mirror to the focus. It is half of the radius of curvature: $f = \dfrac{R}{2}$.

### Three rays that find the image

Every ray obeys the law of reflection, but three rays from the top of the object are easy to draw without measuring an angle.

1. A ray parallel to the principal axis reflects through the focus.
2. A ray through the focus reflects parallel to the principal axis.
3. A ray through the centre of curvature strikes the mirror along a normal, so it reflects back along its own path.

The top of the image is where the reflected rays meet. Two rays are enough, and the third is a check.

### The images of a concave mirror

The image changes as the object moves towards a concave mirror.

| Where the object is | The image |
|---|---|
| Beyond $C$ | Real, upside down, smaller than the object |
| At $C$ | Real, upside down, the same size |
| Between $C$ and $F$ | Real, upside down, larger than the object |
| At $F$ | No image: the reflected rays are parallel |
| Between $F$ and the mirror | Virtual, upright, larger than the object |

A makeup or shaving mirror is concave, and the face is inside the focus, so the image is upright and enlarged. The reflector of a torch or a car headlamp works the other way round: the lamp sits at the focus, and the light leaves as a parallel beam. A satellite dish and a solar cooker gather parallel rays to the focus.

A convex mirror always forms a virtual, upright image that is smaller than the object. Since the image is small, the mirror shows a wide view, which is why convex mirrors are used in shops, at road bends and on the passenger side of cars.

With a single mirror, a real image is always upside down, and a virtual image is always upright.

:::mistake Measuring the angles from the mirror
The angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the normal, not from the surface. If a question gives the angle between the ray and the mirror, subtract it from 90° first.
:::

## The mirror equation

A ray diagram drawn to scale gives the position and the size of the image. The mirror equation gives both without a drawing.

:::definition The mirror equation and magnification
$$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i} \qquad\qquad m = \dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$$

- $f$ is the focal length;
- $d_o$ is the distance from the mirror to the object, and $d_i$ is the distance from the mirror to the image;
- $h_o$ is the height of the object, and $h_i$ is the height of the image;
- $m$ is the magnification.
:::

All of the distances must be in the same unit, but the unit need not be the metre. The signs carry the description of the image.

| Quantity | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Focal length $f$ | Concave (converging) mirror | Convex (diverging) mirror |
| Image distance $d_i$ | Real image, in front of the mirror | Virtual image, behind the mirror |
| Image height $h_i$ and magnification $m$ | Upright image | Upside-down image |

If the size of $m$ is more than 1, the image is larger than the object. If it is less than 1, the image is smaller.

:::example A real image in a concave mirror
A candle 4.0 cm tall stands 30 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Find the position and the height of its image.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{d_o} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{2}{30}$, so $d_i = 15$ cm.
@step $m = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = -\dfrac{15}{30} = -0.50$.
@step $h_i = m h_o = -0.50 \times 4.0 = -2.0$ cm.
@answer The image is 15 cm in front of the mirror. It is real, upside down and 2.0 cm tall.
:::

The object in this example is beyond $C$, which is 20 cm from the mirror, and the image is real, upside down and smaller, just as the table says.

:::example A magnifying mirror
A makeup mirror is concave, with a focal length of 20 cm. A face is 12 cm from the mirror. Find the position of the image and the magnification.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{20} - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{3}{60} - \dfrac{5}{60} = -\dfrac{2}{60}$, so $d_i = -30$ cm.
@step $m = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = -\dfrac{-30}{12} = +2.5$.
@answer The image is 30 cm behind the mirror. It is virtual, upright and 2.5 times as large as the face.
:::

:::example A convex mirror
A security mirror is convex, with a focal length of 15 cm. A shopper stands 30 cm from it. Find the position of the image and the magnification.
@step The mirror is convex, so $f = -15$ cm.
@step $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{-15} - \dfrac{1}{30} = -\dfrac{2}{30} - \dfrac{1}{30} = -\dfrac{3}{30}$, so $d_i = -10$ cm.
@step $m = -\dfrac{-10}{30} = +0.33$.
@answer The image is 10 cm behind the mirror. It is virtual, upright and one third of the size of the shopper.
:::

:::mistake Adding the distances instead of their reciprocals
The equation adds $\dfrac{1}{d_o}$ and $\dfrac{1}{d_i}$, not $d_o$ and $d_i$. After you find $\dfrac{1}{d_i}$, remember to turn it over to get $d_i$.
:::

:::mistake Leaving out the negative sign of a convex mirror
A convex mirror has a negative focal length. If you put in a positive value, the equation describes a concave mirror and gives the wrong image.
:::

:::summary
- The angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, both measured from the normal.
- A real image is where rays meet and can be caught on a screen. A virtual image is where rays only appear to come from.
- A flat mirror gives a virtual, upright image of the same size, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
- A concave mirror converges light, and a convex mirror diverges it. The focal length is half of the radius of curvature.
- Rays for a diagram: parallel to the axis reflects through $F$; through $F$ reflects parallel; through $C$ reflects back on itself.
- $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i}$ and $m = \dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$.
- Negative $f$: convex mirror. Negative $d_i$: virtual image. Negative $m$: upside-down image.
:::
