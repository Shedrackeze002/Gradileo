A magnet held near the screen of an old television pulled the picture out of shape. The picture was painted by a beam of electrons, and the magnet pushed the beam sideways. A magnetic field pushes on a moving charge in a way that no other force does: the push is always at right angles to the motion. This one fact steers the beams of particle accelerators, sorts atoms by mass and bends the particles of the solar wind round the Earth.

## The force on a moving charge

A charge at rest in a magnetic field feels no magnetic force. A charge that moves along the field lines feels none either. The force appears only when the charge moves **across** the field.

:::definition The magnetic force on a moving charge
A charge $q$ that moves with speed $v$ at right angles to a magnetic field $B$ feels a force

$$F = qvB$$

If its velocity makes an angle $\theta$ with the field, only the part of the velocity that is across the field counts:

$$F = qvB\sin\theta$$

The charge is in coulombs, the speed in metres per second and the field in teslas.
:::

The formula shows the three things the force needs. With no charge ($q = 0$, a neutron), no speed ($v = 0$) or motion along the field ($\theta = 0°$), the force is zero.

:::example The force on a proton
A proton, with a charge of $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ C, moves at $3.0 \times 10^{6}$ m/s at right angles to a magnetic field of 0.50 T. Find the magnetic force on it.
@step $F = qvB = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 3.0 \times 10^{6} \times 0.50$.
@answer $F = 2.4 \times 10^{-13}$ N.
:::

:::example Motion at an angle to the field
The same proton now moves at 30° to the field. Find the force.
@step $F = qvB\sin\theta = 2.4 \times 10^{-13} \times \sin 30°$.
@answer $F = 1.2 \times 10^{-13}$ N, half as much as before.
:::

The force is tiny, but so is the mass of the proton. By Newton's second law the first proton has an acceleration of $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{2.4 \times 10^{-13}}{1.67 \times 10^{-27}} = 1.4 \times 10^{14}$ m/s².

### The direction of the force

The force is at right angles to both the velocity and the field. A second rule for the right hand gives its direction.

:::definition The right-hand rule for the force on a charge
For a **positive** charge, hold the right hand flat. Point the thumb along the velocity and the fingers along the magnetic field. The palm pushes in the direction of the force.

For a **negative** charge, the force is in the opposite direction.
:::

:::example Which way is the force?
A proton moves east through a magnetic field that points north. What is the direction of the magnetic force on it? What would it be for an electron?
@step Thumb to the east, along the velocity. Fingers to the north, along the field.
@step The palm faces upward.
@answer The force on the proton is straight up. An electron is negative, so the force on it is straight down.
:::

Because the force is always at right angles to the velocity, it never speeds the charge up or slows it down. A magnetic force does no work. It changes the **direction** of the motion, and nothing else.

### The velocity selector

Put an electric field and a magnetic field in the same space, at right angles to each other, and send a beam of charged particles through at right angles to both. The fields can be arranged so that the electric force $qE$ pushes one way and the magnetic force $qvB$ pushes the other. A particle goes straight through only if the two forces balance:

$$qE = qvB \qquad \text{so} \qquad v = \dfrac{E}{B}$$

The charge has cancelled. Particles of every other speed are pushed to one side, so the device picks out one speed. It is called a **velocity selector**.

:::example A velocity selector
A velocity selector has an electric field of $6.0 \times 10^{4}$ N/C and a magnetic field of 0.20 T. What is the speed of the particles that pass straight through?
@step $v = \dfrac{E}{B} = \dfrac{6.0 \times 10^{4}}{0.20}$.
@answer $v = 3.0 \times 10^{5}$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Using the right hand, unchanged, for an electron
The rule gives the force on a positive charge. For an electron or any other negative charge, find the direction with the right hand and then reverse it.
:::

## Charges moving in circles

A force of constant size that is always at right angles to the velocity is exactly what circular motion needs. You met it in Grade 11 as the centripetal force. So a charge that enters a uniform magnetic field at right angles to the field moves in a circle, with the magnetic force pointing towards the centre all the way round.

Set the magnetic force equal to the centripetal force:

$$qvB = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \qquad \text{so} \qquad r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$$

A faster or heavier particle follows a larger circle. A stronger field or a larger charge gives a tighter one.

:::example The radius of a proton's circle
A proton ($m = 1.67 \times 10^{-27}$ kg, $q = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ C) moves at $3.0 \times 10^{6}$ m/s at right angles to a magnetic field of 0.50 T. Find the radius of its path.
@step $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB} = \dfrac{1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 3.0 \times 10^{6}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 0.50}$.
@step $r = \dfrac{5.01 \times 10^{-21}}{8.0 \times 10^{-20}}$.
@answer $r = 0.0626$ m, about 6.3 cm.
:::

:::example An electron in a weaker field
An electron ($m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}$ kg) moves at $2.0 \times 10^{7}$ m/s at right angles to a magnetic field of 2.0 mT. Find the radius of its path.
@step Change to teslas: $B = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ T.
@step $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB} = \dfrac{9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 2.0 \times 10^{7}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-3}}$.
@answer $r = 0.0569$ m. The electron curves the opposite way to a proton, because the force on a negative charge is reversed.
:::

### The time for one circle

The distance round the circle is $2\pi r$, so the period is $T = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v}$. Put in $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$ and the speed cancels:

$$T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB}$$

A faster particle follows a larger circle, but it takes the same time to go round.

:::example The period of a proton
How long does the proton in the 0.50 T field take to complete one circle?
@step $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 1.67 \times 10^{-27}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 0.50}$.
@answer $T = 1.31 \times 10^{-7}$ s. The proton circles nearly eight million times each second.
:::

### The mass spectrometer

A **mass spectrometer** uses these circles to measure the masses of atoms. The atoms are first ionized, so that each carries a charge. A velocity selector lets through only ions of one speed. The ions then enter a magnetic field and follow a semicircle to a detector. Since $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$, heavier ions land further away, and the mass follows from the radius:

$$m = \dfrac{qBr}{v}$$

:::example Finding the mass of an ion
Ions with a single elementary charge leave a velocity selector at $3.0 \times 10^{5}$ m/s and enter a magnetic field of 0.40 T, where they follow a path of radius 0.156 m. Find the mass of one ion.
@step $m = \dfrac{qBr}{v} = \dfrac{1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 0.40 \times 0.156}{3.0 \times 10^{5}}$.
@step $m = \dfrac{9.98 \times 10^{-21}}{3.0 \times 10^{5}}$.
@answer $m = 3.33 \times 10^{-26}$ kg. One atomic mass unit is $1.66 \times 10^{-27}$ kg, so this is 20 atomic mass units: the ions are neon-20.
:::

:::mistake Thinking that the magnetic field speeds the particle up
The magnetic force is at right angles to the velocity, so it does no work. The particle goes round its circle at a constant speed. Only an electric field can change the speed of a charge.
:::

:::summary
- A magnetic field exerts a force on a charge that moves across it: $F = qvB\sin\theta$, which is $F = qvB$ at right angles.
- The force is at right angles to the velocity and to the field. Right hand: thumb along the velocity, fingers along the field, and the palm pushes a positive charge. Reverse it for a negative charge.
- A magnetic force changes the direction of a charge, never its speed.
- In a velocity selector the electric and magnetic forces balance: $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$.
- A charge that moves at right angles to a uniform field follows a circle: $qvB = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$, so $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$.
- The period, $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB}$, does not depend on the speed.
:::
