:::note Spoiler
This lesson follows *Macbeth* to its ending, including the deaths of Lady Macbeth and Macbeth.
:::

King Duncan is a guest in Macbeth’s castle. While the King dines, Macbeth slips away alone and argues with himself about murdering him. He finds many reasons not to, and only one reason to go ahead:

> I have no spur\
> To prick the sides of my intent, but only\
> Vaulting ambition, which o’erleaps itself\
> And falls on th’ other—
>
> — Macbeth, *Macbeth*, Act 1, Scene 7

A spur pricks a horse to make it run. Macbeth’s only spur is **ambition**, which he pictures as a rider who jumps so hard into the saddle that he falls off the other side. In this lesson you will meet the characters of the play, follow its events act by act, and explain why the characters act as they do.

## The people of the play

| Character | Who they are |
|---|---|
| **Macbeth** | Thane of Glamis, a brave general who murders King Duncan and takes the crown |
| **Lady Macbeth** | his wife, who urges him on and later walks in her sleep, trying to wash blood from her hands |
| **Duncan** | the King of Scotland, murdered in his sleep while a guest in Macbeth’s castle |
| **Malcolm** and **Donalbain** | Duncan’s sons; Malcolm, the elder, flees to England and Donalbain to Ireland |
| **Banquo** | a general who fights beside Macbeth; the Witches tell him his descendants will be kings |
| **Fleance** | Banquo’s son, who escapes when his father is murdered |
| **Macduff** | the Thane of Fife, who discovers Duncan’s body and at last kills Macbeth |
| **Lady Macduff** | Macduff’s wife, murdered with her children while he is in England |
| **The Witches** | three “Weird Sisters” whose prophecies set the tragedy in motion; Hecate is their goddess |
| **Ross** and **Lennox** | Scottish noblemen; Ross brings Macduff the news of his family’s deaths |
| **Siward** | the English general who leads an army against Macbeth; Macbeth kills his son |
| **The Porter** | the drunken gatekeeper of Macbeth’s castle |

## The story, act by act

| Act | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | After a victory in battle, Macbeth and Banquo meet three witches, who hail Macbeth as Thane of Glamis, Thane of Cawdor and future king. Duncan makes Macbeth Thane of Cawdor, then names Malcolm his heir. Lady Macbeth persuades her hesitating husband to kill Duncan while the King is their guest. |
| 2 | Macbeth sees a dagger floating before him, then murders Duncan in his sleep. Macduff discovers the body, Macbeth kills the King’s grooms, and Duncan’s sons flee, so suspicion falls on them. Macbeth goes to Scone to be crowned. |
| 3 | Fearing the Witches’ promise to Banquo, Macbeth has him murdered, but Fleance escapes. At a banquet, Banquo’s ghost appears to Macbeth alone. Macduff refuses to come to court and goes to England. |
| 4 | The Witches show Macbeth apparitions that seem to make him safe. He has Macduff’s wife and children murdered. In England, Malcolm tests Macduff’s loyalty, and the news from home turns Macduff’s grief to resolve. |
| 5 | Lady Macbeth walks in her sleep and later dies. Malcolm’s army, carrying branches cut from Birnam Wood, attacks Macbeth’s castle at Dunsinane. Macduff kills Macbeth, and Malcolm becomes king. |

## Why do they do it?

:::definition Ambition, motive, regicide and tyrant
- **Ambition** is a strong desire for power, success or rank.
- A **motive** is the reason behind a character’s action.
- **Regicide** is the killing of a king.
- A **tyrant** is a ruler who uses power cruelly and unjustly. In Acts 4 and 5, his enemies call Macbeth “tyrant” again and again.
:::

Lady Macbeth knows her husband well. Reading his letter about the Witches, she worries that he wants the crown but is too gentle to take it by the quickest way:

> Yet do I fear thy nature;\
> It is too full o’ th’ milk of human kindness\
> To catch the nearest way. Thou wouldst be great;\
> Art not without ambition, but without\
> The illness should attend it.
>
> — Lady Macbeth, *Macbeth*, Act 1, Scene 5

When he tries to back out, she attacks his pride as a man, and her taunt works:

> When you durst do it, then you were a man;\
> And, to be more than what you were, you would\
> Be so much more the man.
>
> — Lady Macbeth, *Macbeth*, Act 1, Scene 7

Once he is king, Macbeth’s motive changes. Ambition gives way to fear, and every murder is meant to make him safe:

> To be thus is nothing,\
> But to be safely thus. Our fears in Banquo\
> Stick deep, and in his royalty of nature\
> Reigns that which would be fear’d:
>
> — Macbeth, *Macbeth*, Act 3, Scene 1

:::example Choosing evidence for a motive
Claim: “Once he is king, Macbeth acts out of fear more than ambition.” Which quotation supports it better: “I have no spur / To prick the sides of my intent, but only / Vaulting ambition” or “To be thus is nothing, / But to be safely thus”?
@step Check when each line is spoken. The first comes in Act 1, before the murder; the second in Act 3, after Macbeth is crowned.
@step Check what each line says. The first names ambition as his reason; the second says that being king means nothing unless he is safe.
@answer The second quotation. It is spoken after he becomes king and shows that his need for safety now drives him. The first is true of Macbeth in Act 1, but it supports a different claim.
:::

:::mistake A motive is not a result
A motive is why a character acts, not what happens afterwards. Macbeth does not kill Banquo “because Fleance escapes”: Fleance escapes during the murder. Check that the reason you give comes before the action and is something the character knows.
:::

## Foils: Banquo and Macduff

:::definition Foil
A **foil** is a character whose contrast with another character makes that character’s qualities stand out.
:::

Banquo hears the Witches too, and their promise tempts him. On the night of the murder he is heavy with tiredness yet afraid to sleep, and he prays against the dreams that tempt him:

> A heavy summons lies like lead upon me,\
> And yet I would not sleep. Merciful powers,\
> Restrain in me the cursed thoughts that nature\
> Gives way to in repose!
>
> — Banquo, *Macbeth*, Act 2, Scene 1

Macbeth acts on his thoughts; Banquo resists his. Macduff is a foil of another kind. Lady Macbeth told her husband that a man dares to kill; when Macduff hears that his wife and children have been murdered, Malcolm urges him to take revenge, and he answers that a man also grieves:

> **Malcolm.** Dispute it like a man.
>
> **Macduff.** I shall do so;\
> But I must also feel it as a man:\
> I cannot but remember such things were,\
> That were most precious to me.
>
> — *Macbeth*, Act 4, Scene 3

:::summary
- Macbeth, urged on by Lady Macbeth, murders Duncan, then Banquo, then Macduff’s family; Macduff kills him and Malcolm becomes king.
- Ambition drives Macbeth before the murder; once he is king, fear drives him to murder again.
- A motive is the reason for an action; support it with a line spoken at the right moment by someone who knows.
- Banquo, who resists temptation, and Macduff, who feels grief as a man, are foils to Macbeth.
:::
