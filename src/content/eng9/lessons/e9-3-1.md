*The Tempest* is one of the last plays William Shakespeare wrote, probably in 1610 or 1611. It is set on a remote island ruled by a magician, Prospero, who was once the Duke of Milan. When his enemies sail close by, he raises a storm to bring them into his power. In this lesson you will follow the plot and meet the characters.

## The plot

**Act 1.** A ship is caught in a violent storm and seems to sink. On the island, Prospero tells Miranda their story. Twelve years ago his brother Antonio, helped by Alonso, King of Naples, seized his dukedom and set them adrift at sea. Now those same enemies were on the ship, and Prospero, with Ariel’s help, has raised the storm to bring them to the island. Everyone has survived, scattered across the island. Ferdinand, Alonso’s son, believes his father has drowned. He meets Miranda, and they fall in love at first sight.

**Act 2.** Alonso searches for his son. While the King sleeps, Antonio persuades Sebastian to kill him and seize the crown of Naples; Ariel wakes Gonzalo just in time, and Gonzalo rouses the King. Meanwhile, Caliban meets Stephano and Trinculo, takes Stephano for a god because of his wine, and swears to serve him.

**Act 3.** Prospero makes Ferdinand carry heavy logs, to test whether his love for Miranda is real. Caliban persuades Stephano to kill Prospero and rule the island. Ariel, invisible, makes mischief among them, and then appears to the royal party as a fearsome harpy to accuse Alonso, Antonio and Sebastian of their crime against Prospero.

**Act 4.** Prospero blesses Ferdinand and Miranda’s engagement with a magical **masque** of spirits. He breaks it off suddenly when he remembers Caliban’s plot, which ends with the three plotters chased away by spirits in the shape of hounds.

**Act 5.** With his enemies at his mercy, Prospero chooses to forgive them. He gives up his magic, regains his dukedom and frees Ariel. Alonso finds Ferdinand alive and playing chess with Miranda, and everyone prepares to sail home to Italy.

:::example Seeing the pattern
@step Twelve years ago, Antonio betrayed his brother to seize power.
@step In Act 2, Antonio persuades Sebastian to betray his brother, Alonso.
@step In Act 3, Caliban persuades Stephano to kill Prospero and take the island.
@answer Both plots echo Antonio’s original crime of seizing power by betrayal. The repetition makes Prospero’s final choice to forgive stand out all the more.
:::

## What the characters want

- **Prospero** wants justice for the wrong done to him. He controls everyone on the island, but in the end he chooses mercy over revenge.
- **Miranda** has seen almost no one but her father and Caliban, so she meets the lords with amazement and sees only goodness in them.
- **Ariel** serves loyally but keeps reminding Prospero of his promise of freedom.
- **Caliban** sees himself as a native whose island was taken: “This island’s mine, by Sycorax my mother, / Which thou tak’st from me” (1.2).
- **Ferdinand** is a loyal, good-hearted prince. Prospero tests him because “too light winning / Make the prize light”: what is won too easily is not valued.

:::mistake Prospero is not simply a hero
Prospero has been wronged, but he is also harsh: he threatens Ariel, enslaves Caliban and controls Miranda’s life. Strong answers treat him as a complex character, neither a pure hero nor a pure villain.
:::

:::summary
- Prospero, the deposed Duke of Milan, raises a storm to bring his enemies to his island.
- Ferdinand and Miranda fall in love; two plots against rulers fail.
- Prospero forgives his enemies, gives up his magic and frees Ariel.
- The characters’ motives include justice, freedom, love and power.
:::
