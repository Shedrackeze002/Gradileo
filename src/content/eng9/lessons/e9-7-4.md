Good writing is often rewritten writing. When you revise for style, you cut what adds nothing and sharpen what is left. In this lesson you will make your writing more **concise** and choose **precise words**.

## Concision

:::definition Concise
Writing is **concise** when it says what it means in as few words as clarity allows. Concision cuts padding, not meaning.
:::

| Wordy | Concise |
|---|---|
| at this point in time | now |
| due to the fact that | because |
| The two twins were identical in appearance and looked the same. | The twins were identical. |
| It is my opinion that the ending is something that is weak. | The ending is weak. |

Look out for **redundancy** (saying the same thing twice, as in “two twins”), **empty openers** (“It is my opinion that”, “There is a fact that”) and **padded phrases** (“in order to” for “to”).

:::example Trimming a sentence
@step “In my personal opinion, I think that the main character is a person who is very brave.”
@step “In my personal opinion” and “I think” say the same thing; cut both. The thesis is already your opinion.
@step “a person who is very brave” can shrink to one adjective.
@answer “The main character is brave.” Or, more precisely: “The main character is fearless.”
:::

## Precise word choice

Vague words such as *nice*, *good*, *went* and *got* leave the reader to guess. Precise words show exactly what you mean.

- **Strong verbs** beat a weak verb plus an adverb: “The old man **shuffled**“ says more than “The old man walked slowly”.
- **Specific adjectives** beat vague ones: “The soup was **rich and peppery**“ says more than “The soup was nice”.
- **Avoid clichés**, phrases used so often they have lost their force, such as “as busy as a bee”.

Writers sometimes break the rules on purpose. “Deafeningly silent” is an **oxymoron**: it joins opposites, so the reader notices how intense the silence feels.

:::mistake A thesaurus is not a shortcut
Swapping a plain word for a long one does not make writing better. “Utilize” is not more precise than “use”. Choose the word that fits the meaning exactly, whether it is short or long.

:::summary
- **Concise** writing cuts redundancy, empty openers and padded phrases.
- Prefer **strong verbs** and **specific adjectives** to vague words.
- Avoid **clichés**; choose the word that fits the meaning exactly.
:::
