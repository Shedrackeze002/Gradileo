A personal essay starts with something that really happened to you, and then asks what it meant. In this lesson you will shape a **personal essay** around a vivid moment and write **reflection** that reaches a real insight.

## The personal essay

:::definition Personal essay
A **personal essay** is non-fiction that explores the writer’s own experience and what it means. It combines storytelling with reflection.
:::

Three things make a personal essay work.

- **A focused moment.** One afternoon can be shown in vivid detail; a whole year usually cannot. Depth beats breadth.
- **An anecdote.** A short, real story used to make a point. (Don’t confuse it with an *antidote*, which cures a poison.)
- **Your own voice.** Honest and personal, not stiff like a science report or borrowed from a famous author.

:::example Two openings
@step Opening A: “This essay is about the time I moved to a new country with my family.”
@step Opening B: “The first word I learned in English was ‘sorry’, and I said it a hundred times that week.”
@step Opening A announces the topic. Opening B gives a specific, surprising detail.
@answer Opening B is stronger: it drops the reader into the experience and hints at what the essay will explore, the feeling of not belonging.
:::

## Reflection

:::definition Reflection
**Reflection** is thinking about what an experience meant and how it changed you. Narration says what happened; reflection says what you understand about it now.
:::

| Narration | Reflection |
|---|---|
| My brother slammed the door and ran off into the rain. | I realize now that I was not angry with my brother, but afraid of losing him. |

Reflection is usually woven through the essay, as the writer moves between *then* and *now*, and it is strongest near the end.

:::mistake Avoid the ready-made moral
“I learned to never give up” could end a thousand essays, so it tells the reader nothing about yours. Reach for the specific, surprising thing you discovered: “Losing the race taught me that I had been running for my father’s pride, not my own joy.”
:::

:::summary
- A **personal essay** explores a real experience, often through an **anecdote**, in your own voice.
- Focus on one vivid moment.
- **Reflection** explains what the experience meant; avoid clichés and aim for specific insight.
:::
