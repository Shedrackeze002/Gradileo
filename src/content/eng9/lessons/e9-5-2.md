False stories can travel around the world in minutes, while the truth is still checking its facts. In this lesson you will learn to recognize **misinformation** and to check a claim before you trust it or share it.

## Recognizing misinformation

:::definition Misinformation
**Misinformation** is false or misleading information, shared whether or not people know it is false. **Disinformation** is false information spread on purpose to deceive.
:::

| Warning sign | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Clickbait** | a headline designed to make you click, often by exaggerating: “Scientists HATE this one weird trick!” |
| **Manipulated or reused images** | a dramatic photo that has been edited, or taken from a different event |
| **Strong emotion** | a post that makes you furious or frightened, so you share it before you think |
| **No source** | a claim or quotation with no link to where it came from |

Misinformation often spreads faster than corrections because it is surprising or emotional. An **echo chamber**, an online space where people mainly see views that match their own, can let false claims go unchallenged.

:::example A viral photo
@step After a storm, a photo of a shark swimming down a flooded street is shared thousands of times.
@step The image is dramatic and surprising, which is a reason to slow down, not to share.
@step A reverse image search can show where the photo first appeared.
@answer Many such photos turn out to be edited or reused from somewhere else, so the number of shares proves nothing about whether it is real.
:::

## Checking a claim

Start with one question: **who is behind this, and what do they want?**

Professional fact-checkers use **lateral reading**: instead of judging a site by how it looks, they open new tabs and check what other sources say about the site or the claim.

1. **Find the source.** Who made the claim first? Is there a link?
2. **Read laterally.** What do reliable, independent sources say?
3. **Check the quote or image.** Search for the exact words, or do a reverse image search.
4. **Look for fact-checks.** Fact-checking organisations investigate viral claims and show their evidence.

:::mistake Two copies are not two sources
If two websites report the same claim, one may simply have copied the other. Look for **independent** confirmation from trustworthy sources.

:::summary
- **Misinformation** is false or misleading; **disinformation** is false on purpose.
- Watch for clickbait, manipulated images, strong emotion and missing sources.
- Check claims with **lateral reading** and **independent** sources before you share.
:::
