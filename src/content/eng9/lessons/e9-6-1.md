Some words look like verbs but do a different job in the sentence. These **verbals** let you pack more action into fewer words. In this lesson you will identify the three kinds of verbal and use **verbal phrases** to combine sentences.

## Three kinds of verbal

:::definition Verbal
A **verbal** is a verb form used as a noun, an adjective or an adverb, rather than as the main verb.
:::

| Verbal | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Gerund** | an -ing form used as a **noun** | **Swimming** is my favourite sport. |
| **Participle** | a verb form used as an **adjective** (-ing, or a past form such as *broken*) | The **crying** baby fell asleep. The **broken** window let in the cold. |
| **Infinitive** | *to* + a verb, used as a noun, adjective or adverb | Amara wants **to travel** the world. |

:::example Gerund or participle?
@step “Leo is good at solving puzzles.” Here *solving* comes after the preposition *at*, and it names an activity.
@step “The solving team won a prize.” Here *solving* describes the noun *team*.
@answer In the first sentence *solving* is a **gerund** (a noun); in the second it is a **participle** (an adjective). The same -ing word can do either job, so look at what it does.
:::

## Verbal phrases

A verbal can lead a whole phrase, such as *trembling with fear*, *having won the cup* or *to win the final*. These phrases are useful for combining short sentences.

| Two sentences | Combined |
|---|---|
| Amara opened the letter. She was trembling. | **Trembling**, Amara opened the letter. |
| The team won the cup. The team celebrated all night. | **Having won the cup**, the team celebrated all night. |

*Having won* shows that one action came before the other. An infinitive phrase often explains purpose: “**To win the final**, we must defend well.”

:::mistake Don’t let it dangle
A participial phrase at the start of a sentence must describe the word straight after the comma. “Running for the bus, my bag fell open” says the bag was running. Write “Running for the bus, I dropped my bag” instead.
:::

:::summary
- A **gerund** is an -ing noun; a **participle** works as an adjective; an **infinitive** is *to* + verb.
- Verbal phrases can combine sentences smoothly.
- A participial phrase must describe the word after the comma, or it **dangles**.
:::
