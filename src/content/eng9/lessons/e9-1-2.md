The characters we remember are usually the ones who change. In this lesson you will trace a **character arc**, find what drives the change, and see how writers use a **foil**, a contrasting character, to make another character’s qualities stand out.

## Character arcs

:::definition Character arc
A **character arc** is the way a character changes over the course of a story. A **dynamic** character changes; a **static** character stays essentially the same.
:::

To trace an arc, ask three questions:

1. **Who is the character at the start?** What do they want, believe or fear?
2. **What changes them?** The event that pushes a character to change is the **catalyst**: a loss, a meeting, a mistake or a challenge.
3. **Who are they at the end?** Often the change comes in an **epiphany**, a sudden moment of understanding.

:::example Tracing an arc
@step In Charles Dickens’s *A Christmas Carol* (1843), Ebenezer Scrooge begins as a cold, greedy miser who calls Christmas “humbug”.
@step Three ghosts visit him. They show him his lonely past, the struggling families around him, and a future in which he dies unloved.
@step He wakes on Christmas morning transformed, and becomes generous and kind.
@answer Scrooge is a **dynamic** character. The ghosts’ visits are the **catalyst**, and his change of heart on Christmas morning completes the arc.
:::

Arcs can go the other way, too. A character who starts out hopeful and ends bitter, or who loses their innocence, also has an arc.

:::mistake Quote the change
When you write about an arc, give evidence from both ends: what the character says or does at the start, and what they say or do at the end. The contrast proves the change.
:::

## Foils

:::definition Foil
A **foil** is a character whose contrast with another character highlights their traits. Foils usually share a situation but differ in a key quality.
:::

In *A Christmas Carol*, Scrooge’s clerk Bob Cratchit is poor, with a large family and little money, yet he is warm and cheerful. His kindness in poverty shows how cold Scrooge is despite his wealth.

| Character | Foil | What the contrast shows |
|---|---|---|
| a brave knight | his cowardly squire | the knight’s courage |
| a reckless swimmer who leaps without looking | a friend who tests the water first | the swimmer’s recklessness |
| a hero who forgives an enemy | a rival who takes revenge in the same situation | what the hero’s choice means |

Sometimes a foil makes the opposite choice in the same situation, so readers can see the road not taken.

:::summary
- A **character arc** traces how a character changes; **dynamic** characters change, **static** ones do not.
- The **catalyst** pushes the change; an **epiphany** is a sudden moment of understanding.
- A **foil** contrasts with another character to highlight their qualities.
:::
