Every advert is built for someone. It chooses its words, images and channels to make a particular group of people want something. In this lesson you will identify common **advertising techniques** and explain how an advert is designed for its **target audience**.

## Advertising techniques

| Technique | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Testimonial** | a famous or trusted person vouches for the product | “I only drink Volt water,” says a champion sprinter. |
| **Scarcity** | suggests the product will soon be gone, so you must act now | Only 50 left! Offer ends at midnight! |
| **Bandwagon** | suggests you should do what everyone else is doing | Join the two million families who have already switched. |
| **Glittering generalities** | vague, positive words that sound good but promise nothing specific | The natural choice for a better, brighter life. |

A **slogan** is a short, catchy phrase that makes a brand memorable, often through rhythm, rhyme or wordplay.

:::example Unpicking an advert
@step An advert reads: “Fresh. Natural. Yours. Only 100 bottles left this week!”
@step “Fresh. Natural. Yours.” are pleasant words with no checkable promise: **glittering generalities**.
@step “Only 100 bottles left” pushes the reader to buy before it is too late: **scarcity**.
@answer The advert combines vague praise with time pressure, so readers feel good about the product and rush to buy it before they think too hard.
:::

:::mistake Look for the label
When an influencer is paid to promote something, the post should say so, for example with “#ad” or “sponsored”. The label matters because it tells you the review may not be impartial, even if it looks like a friendly recommendation.
:::

## Target audiences

:::definition Target audience
A **target audience** is the group of people an advert is designed to appeal to, such as young children, parents, teenagers or business owners.
:::

To find the target audience, look at three things: the **images** (who is shown, and doing what), the **words** (simple or technical, playful or serious) and the **placement** (where and when the advert appears).

| Advert | Target audience | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| bright cartoons during children’s television | young children and the parents who buy for them | the images and the timing |
| a family car shown passing a safety test | parents | it stresses what they value: safety |
| the same car racing along a coast road at sunset | young drivers | it stresses speed and style |

Many adverts sell an **identity**, not just a product. A running-shoe advert that shows a tired office worker turning into a confident athlete is selling the wish to become a better version of yourself.

Online, adverts are often **targeted**: websites track what you browse, so an advert for shoes you looked at can follow you from site to site.

:::summary
- Techniques include **testimonial**, **scarcity**, **bandwagon** and **glittering generalities**; a **slogan** makes a brand memorable.
- Paid promotion should be labelled, for example “#ad”.
- Find the **target audience** from the images, words and placement; many adverts sell an identity.
:::
