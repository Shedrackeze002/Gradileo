Punctuation is not only about being correct. Writers choose dashes, brackets and ellipses to change the pace and the voice of a sentence. In this lesson you will use these marks correctly and explain their **effect**.

## Dashes, brackets and ellipses

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dash** (—) | a sharp pause before an added or surprising point | There was only one thing missing from the party—the guest of honour. |
| **Pair of dashes** | wraps extra information with emphasis | My aunt—who hates flying—flew to Lagos last week. |
| **Brackets** ( ) | wrap extra information more quietly | The concert (if you can call it that) lasted two hours. |
| **Ellipsis** (…) | shows words left out of a quotation, or a trailing pause | I thought I heard… no, it was nothing. |
| **Hyphen** (-) | joins words; short, with no spaces | a well-known writer |

A useful way to remember the difference: **dashes raise the volume, brackets lower it**.

:::example Choosing a mark
@step The extra information is “who hates flying”.
@step With brackets: “My aunt (who hates flying) flew to Lagos.” The detail sounds like a quiet aside.
@step With dashes: “My aunt—who hates flying—flew to Lagos.” The detail jumps out.
@answer Both are correct. Choose dashes if the detail is the surprising point, and brackets if it is a side note.
:::

:::mistake Close the pair
If you open a pair of dashes or brackets, close it with the same mark. “My aunt—who hates flying, flew to Lagos” starts with a dash and ends with a comma.
:::

## Punctuation for effect

| Choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| “He opened the box—empty.” | the dash makes a sharper pause, so the reveal hits harder than with a comma |
| “I thought I heard… no.” | the ellipsis shows hesitation or builds suspense |
| “The concert (if you can call it that)…” | the brackets carry a sly, ironic aside |
| “Run! Now! They’re coming!” | repeated exclamation marks sound frantic and urgent |
| many semicolons in a calm description | a measured, balanced rhythm that suits the calm mood |

Punctuation shapes rhythm, and rhythm shapes mood. When you analyse a text, ask how a mark changes the pause or the voice.
