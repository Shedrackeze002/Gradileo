How much fence does a rancher need, how should a pop can be shaped, what should a gym charge? Each question asks for the **best** value of something: the largest area, the least material, the most revenue. Calculus finds these best values exactly, and this process is called **optimization**.

## A strategy for optimization problems

:::definition Objective function and constraint
The **objective function** is the quantity to be maximized or minimized. A **constraint** is a condition the variables must satisfy, such as a fixed amount of fencing or a fixed volume. The constraint lets you write the objective as a function of one variable.
:::

1. Draw a diagram and label the quantities with variables.
2. Write the objective function.
3. Use the constraint to rewrite it as a function of **one** variable.
4. State the domain: the values that make sense in the problem.
5. Find the critical numbers in the domain.
6. Verify that you have a maximum or a minimum (see the next section).
7. Answer the question that was asked, with units.

:::example Fencing beside a river
Rashid has $600$ m of fencing to enclose a rectangular pasture beside a straight river. No fence is needed along the river. What dimensions give the largest area?
@step Let $x$ be the length of each side perpendicular to the river and $y$ the side along it. Objective: $A = xy$.
@step Constraint: $2x + y = 600$, so $y = 600 - 2x$ and $A(x) = x(600 - 2x) = 600x - 2x^{2}$.
@step Domain: $0 < x < 300$ (both sides must be positive).
@step $A'(x) = 600 - 4x = 0$ gives $x = 150$; $A''(x) = -4 < 0$, so it is a maximum.
@step $y = 600 - 2(150) = 300$ and $A = 150 \times 300 = 45{,}000$.
@answer $150$ m by $300$ m (the long side along the river), for an area of $45{,}000$ m².
:::

## Verifying a maximum or a minimum

A critical number is only a candidate. Confirm it in one of three ways:

- **Closed interval method:** if the domain is a closed interval, compare the objective at the critical numbers and at the endpoints.
- **First derivative test:** if the objective's derivative changes from $+$ to $-$ (or $-$ to $+$) at the only critical number in the domain, that critical number gives the absolute maximum (or minimum).
- **Second derivative test:** if the second derivative has one sign throughout the domain (always negative, say), the only critical number gives the absolute maximum (or minimum, if always positive).

:::example The largest box
An open-top box is made from a $24$ cm by $15$ cm sheet of cardboard by cutting a square of side $x$ cm from each corner and folding up the sides. Find the largest possible volume.
@step $V(x) = x(24 - 2x)(15 - 2x) = 4x^{3} - 78x^{2} + 360x$, with domain $0 < x < 7.5$.
@step $V'(x) = 12x^{2} - 156x + 360 = 12(x - 3)(x - 10)$, so $x = 3$ or $x = 10$.
@step Only $x = 3$ is in the domain. $V$ is $0$ at both ends of the domain and positive between them, so $x = 3$ gives the maximum.
@step $V(3) = 3(18)(9) = 486$
@answer Cut $3$ cm squares; the largest volume is $486$ cm³.
:::

## Revenue and profit

**Revenue** is (price) × (number sold) and **profit** is revenue minus cost. When a price change affects sales, let $x$ be the number of price changes.

:::example Setting a fee
A climbing gym in Hillcrest has $270$ members who each pay \$50 per month. A survey shows that for every \$2 increase in the fee, $6$ members would leave. What fee maximizes the monthly revenue?
@step With $x$ increases of \$2, the fee is $50 + 2x$ dollars and there are $270 - 6x$ members.
@step $R(x) = (50 + 2x)(270 - 6x) = 13{,}500 + 240x - 12x^{2}$, for $0 \le x \le 45$.
@step $R'(x) = 240 - 24x = 0$ gives $x = 10$; $R''(x) = -24 < 0$, so it is a maximum.
@step Fee: $50 + 2(10) = 70$ dollars; members: $270 - 6(10) = 210$; revenue: $70 \times 210 = 14{,}700$ dollars.
@answer Charge \$70 a month, for a maximum revenue of \$14,700 a month.
:::

:::mistake Answering with x
Here $x = 10$ is the number of \$2 increases, not the fee. Reread the question and answer it in its own terms: the best fee is \$70.
:::

## Least material

:::example A pop can
A closed cylindrical can must hold $500$ cm³. What radius and height use the least metal?
@step Constraint: $\pi r^{2}h = 500$, so $h = \dfrac{500}{\pi r^{2}}$.
@step Objective (surface area): $S = 2\pi r^{2} + 2\pi rh = 2\pi r^{2} + \dfrac{1000}{r}$, for $r > 0$.
@step $S'(r) = 4\pi r - \dfrac{1000}{r^{2}} = 0$ gives $r^{3} = \dfrac{250}{\pi}$, so $r = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{250}{\pi}} \approx 4.30$.
@step $S''(r) = 4\pi + \dfrac{2000}{r^{3}} > 0$ for $r > 0$, so this is a minimum.
@step $h = \dfrac{500}{\pi r^{2}} \approx 8.60$, which is exactly $2r$.
@answer Radius about $4.30$ cm and height about $8.60$ cm: the height equals the diameter.
:::

## Distance and time

:::tip Minimize the square of a distance
A distance $D = \sqrt{\ldots}$ is smallest exactly where $D^{2}$ is smallest, and $D^{2}$ has no square root to differentiate.
:::

For example, the point on $y = \sqrt{x}$ closest to $(3, 0)$ minimizes $D^{2} = (x - 3)^{2} + x$: the derivative $2(x - 3) + 1 = 0$ gives $x = 2.5$, so the point is $(2.5, \sqrt{2.5})$, at distance $\sqrt{2.75} \approx 1.66$.

:::example Swim, then run
Hana is on one bank of a river $300$ m wide and wants to reach a cabin on the other bank, $800$ m downstream. She swims at $1.2$ m/s and runs at $2$ m/s. Where should she land to arrive as soon as possible?
@step Let $x$ be the distance downstream from the point directly across to her landing point $P$, $0 \le x \le 800$. Then $T(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{300^{2} + x^{2}}}{1.2} + \dfrac{800 - x}{2}$.
@step $T'(x) = \dfrac{x}{1.2\sqrt{90{,}000 + x^{2}}} - \dfrac{1}{2} = 0$ gives $2x = 1.2\sqrt{90{,}000 + x^{2}}$; squaring, $4x^{2} = 1.44(90{,}000 + x^{2})$, so $2.56x^{2} = 129{,}600$ and $x = 225$.
@step Compare: $T(0) = 650$ s, $T(225) = \dfrac{375}{1.2} + \dfrac{575}{2} = 600$ s, $T(800) \approx 712$ s.
@answer Land $225$ m downstream; the trip takes $600$ s, or $10$ minutes.
:::
