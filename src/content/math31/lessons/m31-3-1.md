If you drive the 300 km from Brookfield to Riverton in 3 hours, your **average** speed is 100 km/h — but your speedometer showed many different speeds along the way, each one an **instantaneous** rate of change. This lesson connects the two, and limits are the bridge.

## Average rate of change

:::definition Average rate of change
The average rate of change of $f$ on the interval $[a, b]$ is
$$\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.$$
:::

:::definition Secant line
A line through two points on a curve. The average rate of change on $[a, b]$ is the slope of the secant line through $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$.
:::

:::example From a table
The temperature in Ashford one spring day is shown. Find the average rate of change of the temperature from 2 to 6 hours after 6:00 a.m.
@step Change in temperature: $16 - 7 = 9$ °C.
@step Change in time: $6 - 2 = 4$ h.
@answer $\dfrac{9}{4} = 2.25$ °C/h: on average, the temperature rose $2.25$ °C each hour.
:::

The units of a rate of change are always (units of the output) per (unit of the input).

:::example From a formula
Find the average rate of change of $f(x) = x^2 - 4x$ on $[1, 4]$.
@step $f(4) = 16 - 16 = 0$ and $f(1) = 1 - 4 = -3$.
@step $\dfrac{f(4) - f(1)}{4 - 1} = \dfrac{0 - (-3)}{3}$
@answer $1$, the slope of the secant line $PQ$ below.

## Average velocity

When $s(t)$ is the position of an object at time $t$, the average rate of change of $s$ is its **average velocity**. Velocity has a sign: positive when the object moves in the positive direction (up, for a height), negative when it moves the other way.

:::example A ball thrown upward
A ball's height, in metres, after $t$ seconds is $h(t) = 2 + 19.6t - 4.9t^2$. Find its average velocity from $t = 1$ to $t = 2$ and from $t = 2$ to $t = 3$.
@step $h(1) = 2 + 19.6 - 4.9 = 16.7$, $h(2) = 2 + 39.2 - 19.6 = 21.6$ and $h(3) = 2 + 58.8 - 44.1 = 16.7$.
@step From $t = 1$ to $t = 2$: $\dfrac{21.6 - 16.7}{2 - 1} = 4.9$ m/s.
@step From $t = 2$ to $t = 3$: $\dfrac{16.7 - 21.6}{3 - 2} = -4.9$ m/s.
@answer $4.9$ m/s (rising), then $-4.9$ m/s (falling)
:::

:::mistake Average is not total
An average rate of change describes the whole interval. A ball with average velocity $0$ m/s on $[1, 3]$ was certainly moving — it went up and came back down.
:::

## From secant lines to the tangent line

To find the rate of change at a single instant, shrink the interval. Fix the point $P(a, f(a))$ and move $Q(a + h, f(a + h))$ toward it. The secant slopes $\dfrac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$ approach the slope of the **tangent line** at $P$.

For $f(x) = x^2$ at $P(1, 1)$, the secant slopes are $4$ (to $Q_1$), $3$ (to $Q_2$) and $2.5$ (to $Q_3$). They are approaching $2$, the slope of the tangent.

:::definition Tangent line and instantaneous rate of change
The **tangent line** at $P(a, f(a))$ is the line through $P$ with slope
$$m = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h},$$
provided the limit exists. This slope is the **instantaneous rate of change** of $f$ at $x = a$.
:::

:::example Estimating with a table
Estimate the slope of the tangent to $f(x) = x^2$ at $x = 3$.
@step Compute $\dfrac{f(3 + h) - f(3)}{h}$ for small positive and negative $h$ (the table below).
@step The values $6.1, 6.01, 6.001$ and $5.9, 5.99, 5.999$ approach $6$ from both sides.
@step Algebra confirms it: $\dfrac{(3 + h)^2 - 9}{h} = \dfrac{6h + h^2}{h} = 6 + h \to 6$.
@answer The slope of the tangent at $x = 3$ is $6$.
:::

:::example Instantaneous velocity
A stone falls $s(t) = 4.9t^2$ metres in $t$ seconds. Find its velocity at $t = 2$ s.
@step Average velocity on $[2, 2 + h]$: $\dfrac{4.9(2 + h)^2 - 4.9(4)}{h} = \dfrac{19.6h + 4.9h^2}{h} = 19.6 + 4.9h$
@step As $h \to 0$, $19.6 + 4.9h \to 19.6$.
@answer $19.6$ m/s
:::

:::mistake Setting h = 0 too early
Putting $h = 0$ into $\dfrac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$ gives $\frac{0}{0}$. Simplify first, or use a table of small non-zero values of $h$ on both sides.
:::

:::summary
- Average rate of change on $[a, b]$: $\dfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$, the slope of a secant line, in (output units) per (input unit).
- For a position function, the average rate of change is the average velocity.
- The instantaneous rate of change at $x = a$ is $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$, the slope of the tangent line.
- Estimate it with a table of small positive and negative $h$, or find it exactly by simplifying before letting $h \to 0$.
:::
