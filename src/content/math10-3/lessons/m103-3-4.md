An oven from the United States is marked in degrees Fahrenheit, but your recipe says $180^\circ\text{C}$. A truck's coolant gauge reads $230^\circ\text{F}$: is that too hot? Cooks, mechanics, HVAC technicians and nurses all need to move between the two temperature scales.

## Two temperature scales

:::definition Celsius and Fahrenheit
The **Celsius** scale (°C) is the SI scale: water freezes at $0^\circ\text{C}$ and boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$. The **Fahrenheit** scale (°F) is the imperial scale: water freezes at $32^\circ\text{F}$ and boils at $212^\circ\text{F}$.
:::

:::definition Referent
A **referent** is a familiar temperature you can use to judge whether another temperature makes sense.
:::

| Referent | Celsius | Fahrenheit |
|---|---|---|
| Water freezes | $0^\circ\text{C}$ | $32^\circ\text{F}$ |
| Comfortable room | $20^\circ\text{C}$ | $68^\circ\text{F}$ |
| Normal body temperature | $37^\circ\text{C}$ | $98.6^\circ\text{F}$ |
| Water boils (sea level) | $100^\circ\text{C}$ | $212^\circ\text{F}$ |
| Moderate oven | $180^\circ\text{C}$ | $350^\circ\text{F}$ |

Notice that a Fahrenheit degree is smaller than a Celsius degree: from freezing to boiling is $100$ Celsius degrees but $180$ Fahrenheit degrees.

:::mistake Right number, wrong scale
$37^\circ\text{F}$ is not body temperature; it is just above freezing! Always check the scale before you decide whether a temperature is reasonable.

## Celsius to Fahrenheit

$$F = \tfrac{9}{5}C + 32$$

Multiply by $\tfrac{9}{5}$ (the same as $1.8$) to change the size of the degrees, then add 32 because the Fahrenheit scale starts 32 degrees higher at freezing.

:::example A warm day
The forecast for Lakeside is $25^\circ\text{C}$. What is that in degrees Fahrenheit?
@step $F = \tfrac{9}{5}(25) + 32$
@step $F = 45 + 32 = 77$
@answer $25^\circ\text{C} = 77^\circ\text{F}$
:::

:::example A cold morning
A walk-in freezer is at $-20^\circ\text{C}$. What is that in degrees Fahrenheit?
@step $F = \tfrac{9}{5}(-20) + 32 = -36 + 32$
@answer $F = -4$, so the freezer is at $-4^\circ\text{F}$.
:::

## Fahrenheit to Celsius

To go the other way, **rearrange** the formula by undoing its steps in reverse order.

:::example Rearranging the formula
Solve $F = \tfrac{9}{5}C + 32$ for $C$.
@step The formula multiplies by $\tfrac{9}{5}$, then adds 32. Undo the last step first: subtract 32 from both sides. $F - 32 = \tfrac{9}{5}C$
@step Undo the multiplication: multiply both sides by $\tfrac{5}{9}$.
@answer $C = \tfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)$
:::

:::example An American oven
A recipe says to bake at $350^\circ\text{F}$. What is that in degrees Celsius, to the nearest degree?
@step $C = \tfrac{5}{9}(350 - 32) = \tfrac{5}{9}(318)$
@step $C \approx 176.7$
@answer About $177^\circ\text{C}$ (ovens are usually set to $180^\circ\text{C}$).
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the brackets
$C = \tfrac{5}{9}F - 32$ is **wrong**. For $350^\circ\text{F}$ it gives $\tfrac{5}{9}(350) - 32 \approx 162$, which is too low. Subtract 32 **first**, then multiply by $\tfrac{5}{9}$. On a calculator, type the brackets.
:::

## Estimates and changes

For a quick estimate from Celsius to Fahrenheit, **double it and add 30**. To go back, **subtract 30 and halve**. The estimate is good for weather, but use the formula for cooking, medicine or equipment.

:::example Checking the estimate
Estimate $30^\circ\text{C}$ in Fahrenheit, then compare with the exact value.
@step Estimate: $2(30) + 30 = 90^\circ\text{F}$
@step Exact: $\tfrac{9}{5}(30) + 32 = 54 + 32 = 86^\circ\text{F}$
@answer The estimate is $4$ degrees too high, but close enough to know it is a hot day.
:::

A **change** in temperature is different from a temperature. One Celsius degree is the same size as $\tfrac{9}{5} = 1.8$ Fahrenheit degrees, so to convert a change you only multiply by $\tfrac{9}{5}$ (or by $\tfrac{5}{9}$ to go back). There is **no** $+32$, because the 32 only shifts where the scale starts.

:::example A warm wind
A warm wind raised the temperature in Riverton from $-25^\circ\text{C}$ to $5^\circ\text{C}$. By how many Fahrenheit degrees did it rise?
@step Rise in Celsius: $5 - (-25) = 30$ Celsius degrees.
@step $\tfrac{9}{5} \times 30 = 54$
@answer The temperature rose by $54$ Fahrenheit degrees.
:::

:::tip Check with a referent
After any conversion, compare with a referent. If you convert a summer temperature and get $5^\circ\text{F}$, something went wrong.
:::

:::summary
- Referents: freezing $0^\circ\text{C} = 32^\circ\text{F}$, room $20^\circ\text{C} \approx 68^\circ\text{F}$, body $37^\circ\text{C} \approx 98.6^\circ\text{F}$, boiling $100^\circ\text{C} = 212^\circ\text{F}$, oven $180^\circ\text{C} \approx 350^\circ\text{F}$.
- Celsius to Fahrenheit: $F = \tfrac{9}{5}C + 32$.
- Fahrenheit to Celsius: $C = \tfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)$. Subtract 32 first.
- Quick estimate: double and add 30 (or subtract 30 and halve).
- A temperature **change** converts with $\tfrac{9}{5}$ or $\tfrac{5}{9}$ only, no 32.
:::
