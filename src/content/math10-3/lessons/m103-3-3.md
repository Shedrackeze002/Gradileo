A welder orders steel from a supplier in the United States, a trucker crosses a border where the speed signs switch to miles per hour, and a cook follows an American recipe in cups. Converting between SI and imperial units is an everyday part of many jobs.

## Conversion factors

A **conversion factor** tells you how a unit in one system compares with a unit in the other. Some are exact and some are rounded.

:::definition Conversion factor
A **conversion factor** is a relationship such as $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$. Written as a fraction, $\dfrac{2.54\ \text{cm}}{1\ \text{in}}$ or $\dfrac{1\ \text{in}}{2.54\ \text{cm}}$, it is equal to 1, so multiplying by it changes the units but not the size.
:::

| Quantity | Conversion factor |
|---|---|
| Length | $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$ (exact), $1\ \text{ft} = 30.48\ \text{cm}$ (exact) |
| Length | $1\ \text{m} \approx 3.28\ \text{ft}$, $1\ \text{km} \approx 0.621\ \text{mi}$, $1\ \text{mi} \approx 1.609\ \text{km}$ |
| Mass | $1\ \text{kg} \approx 2.2\ \text{lb}$, $1\ \text{oz} \approx 28.35\ \text{g}$ |
| Capacity | $1\ \text{L} \approx 0.264$ US gal, $1$ US gal $\approx 3.785\ \text{L}$ |

Questions will always tell you which factor to use. Pick the fraction that **cancels the unit you start with**.

:::tip Unit fractions
To change kilograms to pounds, the kilograms must cancel, so put kilograms on the bottom: $\text{kg} \times \dfrac{2.2\ \text{lb}}{1\ \text{kg}}$. To change pounds to kilograms, put pounds on the bottom: $\text{lb} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{kg}}{2.2\ \text{lb}}$, which is the same as dividing by 2.2.
:::

## SI to imperial

:::example Kilograms to pounds
A bag of fertilizer has a mass of $75\ \text{kg}$. What is its mass in pounds? Use $1\ \text{kg} \approx 2.2\ \text{lb}$.
@step $75\ \text{kg} \times \dfrac{2.2\ \text{lb}}{1\ \text{kg}}$
@step $75 \times 2.2 = 165$
@answer The bag is about $165\ \text{lb}$.
:::

:::example Centimetres to inches
A shelf from a Canadian supplier is $180\ \text{cm}$ tall. What is this in inches, to the nearest tenth? Use $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$.
@step Inches are bigger than centimetres, so divide: $180 \div 2.54 \approx 70.866$
@answer About $70.9\ \text{in}$
:::

## Imperial to SI

:::example Feet and inches to centimetres
A basketball player is $6\ \text{ft}\ 2\ \text{in}$ tall. What is this height in centimetres, to the nearest whole number?
@step Change to inches first: $6 \times 12 + 2 = 74\ \text{in}$.
@step $74 \times 2.54 = 187.96$
@answer About $188\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::example Choosing a wrench
A mechanic needs a metric wrench for a $\tfrac{3}{4}$-inch bolt head. What size in millimetres is closest? Use $1\ \text{in} = 25.4\ \text{mm}$.
@step $\tfrac{3}{4} = 0.75\ \text{in}$
@step $0.75 \times 25.4 = 19.05\ \text{mm}$
@answer The closest metric wrench is $19\ \text{mm}$.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying by the wrong factor
To change $165\ \text{lb}$ to kilograms, some students multiply by 2.2 and get $363\ \text{kg}$. A kilogram is **heavier** than a pound, so there must be **fewer** kilograms: $165 \div 2.2 = 75\ \text{kg}$.
:::

## Is the answer reasonable?

Rounded factors make quick mental checks easy:

- $1\ \text{in}$ is about $2.5\ \text{cm}$.
- $1\ \text{kg}$ is about $2\ \text{lb}$.
- $1\ \text{mi}$ is about $1.6\ \text{km}$, so $100\ \text{km/h}$ is about $60\ \text{mph}$.
- $1$ US gallon is about $4\ \text{L}$.

Before you write down an answer, compare it with your estimate. If they are far apart, you probably multiplied when you should have divided.

:::example A truck load in tonnes
A truck from a country that uses pounds carries $44\,000\ \text{lb}$ of lumber. The scale on this side of the border reads in tonnes. What is the load? Use $1\ \text{kg} \approx 2.2\ \text{lb}$ and $1\ \text{t} = 1000\ \text{kg}$.
@step Pounds to kilograms: $44\,000 \div 2.2 = 20\,000\ \text{kg}$
@step Kilograms to tonnes: $20\,000 \div 1000 = 20$
@step Check: $1\ \text{kg} \approx 2\ \text{lb}$, so $44\,000\ \text{lb}$ is about $22\,000\ \text{kg}$. Close enough.
@answer About $20\ \text{t}$
:::

:::example A recipe in cups
A US recipe calls for $2$ cups of milk. Use $1$ US cup $= 8$ US fl oz and $1$ US fl oz $\approx 29.57\ \text{mL}$. How many millilitres is that, to the nearest whole number?
@step $2 \times 8 = 16$ US fl oz
@step $16 \times 29.57 = 473.12$
@answer About $473\ \text{mL}$
:::

:::summary
- Use the conversion factor given in the question, written as a fraction that cancels the starting unit.
- $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$ exactly; most other factors are rounded, so answers are approximate.
- Convert in steps (for example, lb to kg to t, or ft and in to in to cm) and round only at the end.
- Estimate with rounded factors to check that your answer is reasonable.
:::
