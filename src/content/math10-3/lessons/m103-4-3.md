How much fencing goes around a pasture? How much trim goes around a window, or edging around a round flower bed? Each of these jobs is about the distance around a shape.

## Perimeter of polygons

:::definition Perimeter
The **perimeter** of a shape is the total distance around its outside edge. It is a length, so it is measured in units such as metres, feet or inches.
:::

To find any perimeter, add the lengths of all the sides. For shapes you see often, there are shortcuts:

| Shape | Perimeter |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $P = 2(l + w)$ |
| Square | $P = 4s$ |
| Triangle | $P = a + b + c$ |
| Regular polygon with $n$ sides | $P = ns$ |

:::example Railing for a deck
A rectangular deck is $16\ \text{ft}$ long and $12\ \text{ft}$ wide. What is its perimeter?
@step $P = 2(l + w)$
@step $P = 2(16 + 12) = 2(28) = 56$
@answer The perimeter is $56\ \text{ft}$.
:::

:::example A triangular flower bed
A flower bed has sides of $4.2\ \text{m}$, $3.5\ \text{m}$ and $5.8\ \text{m}$. How much edging goes around it?
@step $P = 4.2 + 3.5 + 5.8$
@answer $P = 13.5\ \text{m}$ of edging.
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
Before you add, every side must be in the **same** unit. A table top $1.8\ \text{m}$ by $70\ \text{cm}$ has perimeter $2(1.8 + 0.7) = 5\ \text{m}$, not $2(1.8 + 70)$.
:::

## Composite shapes

A **composite shape** is made of simpler shapes joined together, like an L-shaped room. To find its perimeter, sketch it, label every outside edge, and work out any missing sides by adding or subtracting.

:::example Baseboard for an L-shaped room
The room above is a $7\ \text{m}$ by $5\ \text{m}$ rectangle with a $3\ \text{m}$ by $2\ \text{m}$ corner cut out. What is its perimeter?
@step The room has six sides. Two are given in full: $7$ and $5$. The cut-out sides are $3$ and $2$.
@step Missing sides: $7 - 3 = 4$ and $5 - 2 = 3$
@step $P = 7 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 24$
@answer The perimeter is $24\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::tip A shortcut for corner cut-outs
Cutting a rectangle out of a **corner** does not change the perimeter: $2(7 + 5) = 24\ \text{m}$ too. The cut-out edges just move inward. A notch cut into the **middle** of a side does add length: it adds two extra depths.
:::

:::mistake Missing a side
An L-shape has **six** sides, not four. Trace the whole outline with your finger and count the sides before you add.
:::

## Circumference

:::definition Circumference, diameter and radius
The **circumference** is the perimeter of a circle. The **diameter** $d$ is the distance across the circle through its centre. The **radius** $r$ is the distance from the centre to the edge, so $d = 2r$.
:::

For every circle, the circumference is a little more than $3$ times the diameter. That number is $\pi \approx 3.14159$. Use the $\pi$ key on your calculator and round only at the end.

$$C = \pi d \qquad\text{or}\qquad C = 2\pi r$$

:::example A steel band for a grain bin
A grain bin on a farm has a diameter of $8.2\ \text{m}$. How long is a steel band that goes once around it? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step $C = \pi d = \pi \times 8.2$
@step $C \approx 25.761$
@answer The band is about $25.8\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

:::mistake Radius or diameter?
$C = \pi d$ uses the **diameter**. If you are given the radius, double it first, or use $C = 2\pi r$. Using the radius in $C = \pi d$ gives an answer half as big as it should be.
:::

Each time a wheel turns once, it rolls forward one circumference.

:::example How far does the wheel roll?
A bicycle wheel has a diameter of $66\ \text{cm}$. How far does it roll in $500$ turns? Give the answer in metres, to the nearest tenth.
@step One turn: $C = \pi \times 66 \approx 207.345\ \text{cm}$
@step $500$ turns: $500 \times 207.345 \approx 103\,672.6\ \text{cm}$
@step Change to metres: $103\,672.6 \div 100 \approx 1036.7$
@answer The wheel rolls about $1036.7\ \text{m}$.
:::

## Working backwards: rearranging a formula

Sometimes you know the perimeter and need a side. Write the formula, put in what you know, and **undo** the operations to get the unknown by itself.

:::example Finding the width of a dog run
A rectangular dog run uses $46\ \text{m}$ of fencing. It is $14\ \text{m}$ long. How wide is it?
@step $P = 2(l + w)$
@step Divide both sides by $2$: $\dfrac{P}{2} = l + w$, so $w = \dfrac{P}{2} - l$
@step $w = \dfrac{46}{2} - 14 = 23 - 14 = 9$
@answer The dog run is $9\ \text{m}$ wide.
:::

:::example The diameter of a tree
An arborist wraps a tape once around a spruce trunk and reads $150\ \text{cm}$. What is the diameter, to the nearest centimetre?
@step $C = \pi d$, so divide both sides by $\pi$: $d = \dfrac{C}{\pi}$
@step $d = \dfrac{150}{\pi} \approx 47.75$
@answer The trunk is about $48\ \text{cm}$ across.
:::

:::summary
- Perimeter is the distance around a shape: add all the sides, in the same unit.
- Rectangle $P = 2(l + w)$; square $P = 4s$; regular polygon $P = ns$.
- For composite shapes, sketch, find the missing sides, and add every outside edge.
- Circumference $C = \pi d = 2\pi r$. One turn of a wheel moves it one circumference.
- To find a side or radius, rearrange the formula: $w = \dfrac{P}{2} - l$, $s = \dfrac{P}{4}$, $d = \dfrac{C}{\pi}$, $r = \dfrac{C}{2\pi}$.
:::
