Most first jobs pay by the hour. Whether you cook on the line in a busy restaurant or haul lumber on a job site, you should be able to check that your pay is right. All it takes is careful multiplication and reading a timecard.

## Gross pay from an hourly wage

When you are paid by the hour, every hour you work earns the same amount. To find your pay, multiply.

:::definition Hourly wage and gross pay
An **hourly wage** is the amount you earn for each hour of work. **Gross pay** is the total amount you earn before anything is taken off:
$$\text{gross pay} = \text{hourly wage} \times \text{hours worked}$$
:::

:::example Pay for a week
Amara works as a line cook for \$17.50 per hour. Last week Amara worked 32 hours. What was the gross pay?
@step Gross pay $=$ hourly wage $\times$ hours worked
@step $17.50 \times 32 = 560.00$
@answer Amara’s gross pay was \$560.00.
:::

## Hours and minutes

Hours are often not whole numbers. Before you multiply, change the minutes to a decimal part of an hour by dividing by 60.

| Minutes | 15 min | 30 min | 45 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours | 0.25 h | 0.5 h | 0.75 h |

:::example Weekend shifts
Tariq works at a tire shop for \$16.40 per hour. Tariq worked 6 h 45 min on Saturday and 5 h 30 min on Sunday. What was the gross pay for the weekend?
@step Saturday: 6 h 45 min $= 6.75\ \text{h}$. Sunday: 5 h 30 min $= 5.5\ \text{h}$.
@step Total hours: $6.75 + 5.5 = 12.25\ \text{h}$
@step Gross pay: $12.25 \times 16.40 = 200.90$
@answer Tariq earned \$200.90 for the weekend.
:::

:::mistake 7 h 30 min is not 7.30 h
The digits after the decimal point are parts of an hour, not minutes. 7 h 30 min is half an hour past 7, so it is $7.5\ \text{h}$. Writing 7.30 would cheat you out of pay.
:::

:::check m103-gross-hourly 2
:::

## Reading a timecard

A **timecard** records when each shift starts and ends. Most breaks for meals are **unpaid**, so subtract them. To find the time between a start and an end, count up in easy steps.

:::tip Count up across noon
From 7:45 a.m. to 3:15 p.m.: 7:45 to 8:00 is 15 min, 8:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. is 7 h, and 3:00 to 3:15 is 15 min. That is 7 h 30 min in all.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Three shifts
Jordan earns \$18.20 per hour at a warehouse. Use the timecard above to find the gross pay.
@step Monday: 8 h 30 min minus 30 min $= 8\ \text{h}$.
@step Tuesday: 7 h 30 min minus 30 min $= 7\ \text{h}$.
@step Wednesday: 5 h 45 min minus 15 min $=$ 5 h 30 min $= 5.5\ \text{h}$.
@step Total: $8 + 7 + 5.5 = 20.5\ \text{h}$, and $20.5 \times 18.20 = 373.10$
@answer Jordan’s gross pay is \$373.10.
:::

## Overtime: time-and-a-half and double time

Working extra hours often earns a higher rate. Each workplace has a rule for when overtime starts, such as hours over 8 in a day or over 40 in a week. Employment standards and union agreements set these rules, and in this course every question tells you the rule to use.

:::definition Overtime
**Overtime** is time worked beyond the regular hours. **Time-and-a-half** pays $1.5$ times the regular hourly wage. **Double time** pays $2$ times the regular wage.
:::

:::example Overtime in a week
Mateo earns \$22.40 per hour as a tire technician. Overtime is paid at time-and-a-half for hours over 40 in a week. Last week Mateo worked 46 hours. Find the gross pay.
@step Regular pay: $40 \times 22.40 = 896.00$
@step Overtime rate: $1.5 \times 22.40 = 33.60$
@step Overtime pay: $(46 - 40) \times 33.60 = 6 \times 33.60 = 201.60$
@step Gross pay: $896.00 + 201.60 = 1{,}097.60$
@answer Mateo’s gross pay is \$1,097.60.
:::

:::mistake Only the extra hours get the higher rate
Do not multiply all 46 hours by the overtime rate. The first 40 hours are paid at the regular wage. Only the hours past the limit earn time-and-a-half.
:::

## Overtime on a weekly timecard

When the rule is **over 8 hours in a day**, check each day separately. A long Monday earns overtime even if the week is short.

:::example A daily overtime rule
Kiona earns \$19.60 per hour as a farm hand. Hours over 8 in a day are paid at time-and-a-half. Kiona worked 9, 8, 10.5, 7.5 and 9.5 hours from Monday to Friday. Find the gross pay.
@step Overtime each day: $1 + 0 + 2.5 + 0 + 1.5 = 5\ \text{h}$. Regular hours: $8 + 8 + 8 + 7.5 + 8 = 39.5\ \text{h}$.
@step Regular pay: $39.5 \times 19.60 = 774.20$
@step Overtime pay: $5 \times (1.5 \times 19.60) = 5 \times 29.40 = 147.00$
@step Gross pay: $774.20 + 147.00 = 921.20$
@answer Kiona’s gross pay is \$921.20.
:::

:::note Daily and weekly rules give different answers
Kiona worked 44.5 hours in all. With a weekly rule of “over 40 hours”, only 4.5 hours would be overtime. With the daily rule it is 5 hours. Always read the rule carefully.
:::

:::check m103-overtime 3
:::

:::summary
- Gross pay $=$ hourly wage $\times$ hours worked, before anything is taken off.
- Change minutes to hours by dividing by 60: 15 min $= 0.25$ h, 30 min $= 0.5$ h, 45 min $= 0.75$ h.
- On a timecard, find the time from start to end, then subtract unpaid breaks.
- Time-and-a-half is $1.5 \times$ the wage; double time is $2 \times$ the wage.
- Only the hours past the overtime limit earn the higher rate. Check whether the rule is daily or weekly.
:::
