Two job offers can look very different: one pays \$18.50 an hour, the other pays a weekly base plus commission. Self-employed tradespeople, like a welder who builds gates for cattle ranches, set their own price for every job. To choose well, you have to compare the offers fairly.

## Comparing job offers

To compare two ways of being paid, work out what each one pays **for the same situation**: the same week, the same hours and the same expected sales.

:::example Hourly or commission?
Job A at a hardware store pays \$18.50 per hour for 40 hours a week. Job B at a furniture store pays \$450 per week plus 5% commission on sales. Ahmed expects to sell about \$6,200 a week at Job B. Which job pays more?
@step Job A: $18.50 \times 40 = 740.00$
@step Job B: $450 + 0.05 \times 6{,}200 = 450 + 310 = 760.00$
@step Difference: $760.00 - 740.00 = 20.00$
@answer Job B pays \$20.00 more per week, if Ahmed’s sales estimate is right.
:::

:::tip Money is not the only thing
Commission pay can drop in a slow month, while hourly pay is steady. Also think about benefits, travel time, safety and how many hours you want to work.
:::

:::mistake Comparing different time periods
You cannot compare \$1,950 every two weeks with \$4,100 a month directly. Change both to the same period first, such as a year: $1{,}950 \times 26 = 50{,}700$ and $4{,}100 \times 12 = 49{,}200$.
:::

## The break-even point

With commission, how much you earn depends on your sales. The **break-even point** tells you the sales at which two offers pay exactly the same.

:::definition Break-even point
The **break-even point** is the value (sales, hours or pieces) where two ways of being paid give the same amount. Above it, one option pays more; below it, the other one does.
:::

:::example Salary or salary plus commission?
Job A pays \$3,000 per month. Job B pays \$1,200 per month plus 6% commission on sales. What monthly sales make the jobs pay the same?
@step Let $S$ be the monthly sales. Set the pays equal: $1{,}200 + 0.06S = 3{,}000$
@step Subtract 1,200 from both sides: $0.06S = 1{,}800$
@step Divide by 0.06: $S = 1{,}800 \div 0.06 = 30{,}000$
@answer At \$30,000 in sales, both jobs pay \$3,000. With higher sales, Job B pays more.
:::

:::mistake Subtract the base first
Dividing \$3,000 by 0.06 gives \$50,000, which ignores Job B’s \$1,200 base pay. Undo the addition before you undo the multiplication.
:::

:::

## Working on contract

Many tradespeople are **self-employed**. Instead of a wage, they agree on a price for a whole job and pay for the materials themselves.

:::definition Contract, expenses and profit
A **contract** is an agreement to do a job for a set amount of money. A **self-employed** worker runs their own business. **Expenses** are the costs of doing the job, such as materials, fuel and rentals. The **profit** (income) is the contract amount minus the expenses.
:::

:::example A painting contract
Harpreet, a self-employed painter, agrees to paint the inside of a house for \$3,600. Paint and supplies cost \$742.35, a rented sprayer costs \$185.00 and fuel costs \$64.90. The job takes 52 hours. Find Harpreet’s income and the hourly equivalent.
@step Total expenses: $742.35 + 185.00 + 64.90 = 992.25$
@step Income: $3{,}600 - 992.25 = 2{,}607.75$
@step Per hour: $2{,}607.75 \div 52 \approx 50.149$, which rounds to $50.15$
@answer Harpreet earns \$2,607.75, which is about \$50.15 per hour.
:::

:::note No boss, no benefits
A self-employed worker has no employer paying for vacation time, benefits or tools. That is why a contractor’s hourly equivalent needs to be higher than a typical wage.
:::

## Bidding on a job

Before a job starts, a contractor offers a price called a **bid**. A good bid covers the expenses and pays the worker a fair rate for every hour:
$$\text{bid} = \text{hourly rate} \times \text{hours} + \text{expenses}$$

:::example A welding bid
Kwame is a welder who wants to earn \$55 per hour. A ranch gate will take 18 hours, and steel and supplies cost \$640.50. What should Kwame bid?
@step Pay for the work: $55 \times 18 = 990$
@step Add the expenses: $990 + 640.50 = 1{,}630.50$
@answer Kwame should bid \$1,630.50.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the expenses
If Kwame bid only \$990, the materials would come out of the pay: $990 - 640.50 = 349.50$, which is only about \$19.42 per hour.
:::

:::check m103-self-employed 3
:::

:::summary
- Compare offers for the same situation and the same time period.
- The break-even point is where two offers pay the same; set the pays equal and solve.
- Contract income (profit) $=$ contract amount $-$ expenses.
- Hourly equivalent $=$ income $\div$ hours worked.
- Bid $=$ hourly rate $\times$ hours $+$ expenses.
:::
