A hockey team drives to a tournament in the United States. A cook sends money to family in the Philippines. A mechanic orders a part from a supplier in the United States. Each of them has to change Canadian dollars into another currency, or back again.

## Exchange rates

Every country has its own money, or **currency**. An **exchange rate** tells you how much one currency is worth in another. Exchange rates change every day, so a question will always give you the rate to use.

:::definition Exchange rate
An **exchange rate** is the value of one unit of a currency in another currency. For example, $1$ US dollar $= 1.36$ Canadian dollars (CAD).
:::

A rate can be written either way round. "$1$ euro $= 1.52$ CAD" and "$1$ CAD $= 13.85$ Mexican pesos" are both common. Read which currency has the $1$.

:::example US dollars to Canadian dollars
A part costs US\$85. Suppose $1$ US dollar $= 1.36$ CAD. What is the cost in Canadian dollars?
@step Each US dollar is worth $1.36$ Canadian dollars, so multiply.
@step $85 \times 1.36 = 115.60$
@answer The part costs \$115.60 CAD.
:::

## Converting the other way

When you start with the currency that is **not** the "$1$" in the rate, divide by the rate instead.

:::example Canadian dollars to euros
Mei has \$500 CAD to spend in France. Suppose $1$ euro $= 1.52$ CAD. How many euros is that?
@step Each euro costs $1.52$ Canadian dollars, so divide.
@step $500 \div 1.52 \approx 328.947$
@answer Mei will have about €328.95.
:::

:::example Pesos to Canadian dollars
A hotel room in Mexico costs 2400 pesos. Suppose $1$ CAD $= 13.85$ Mexican pesos. What is the cost in Canadian dollars?
@step Each Canadian dollar buys $13.85$ pesos, so divide the pesos by $13.85$.
@step $2400 \div 13.85 \approx 173.285$
@answer The room costs about \$173.29 CAD.
:::

:::tip Does the answer make sense?
A euro is worth **more** than a Canadian dollar, so you get **fewer** euros than dollars. A peso is worth much **less**, so a price in pesos is a much **bigger** number than the same price in dollars. Check your answer against this before you move on.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying when you should divide
$500 \times 1.52 = 760$ euros cannot be right: you would get more euros than dollars, even though each euro costs more than a dollar. Use the units: $\dfrac{500\ \text{CAD}}{1.52\ \text{CAD per euro}}$ leaves euros.
:::

## Buy rates and sell rates

Banks and exchange booths do not use just one rate. They post two, both written from the **bank's** point of view.

:::definition Buy rate and sell rate
The **buy rate** is what the bank pays you, in CAD, for one unit of foreign money you sell to it.
The **sell rate** is what the bank charges you, in CAD, for one unit of foreign money you buy from it.
The sell rate is always higher than the buy rate.
:::

:::

:::example Buying US dollars
Kai buys US\$400 in cash before a trip to Seattle, using the rates in the table. How much does it cost?
@step Kai is **buying** US dollars, so the bank is **selling** them. Use the bank sells rate: $1.3975$.
@step $400 \times 1.3975 = 559$
@answer It costs Kai \$559.00 CAD.
:::

:::example Selling US dollars back
Kai comes home with US\$65 and sells it back to the bank. How much does Kai get?
@step Kai is **selling** US dollars, so the bank is **buying** them. Use the bank buys rate: $1.3125$.
@step $65 \times 1.3125 = 85.3125$
@answer Kai gets about \$85.31 CAD.
:::

:::mistake Using the wrong rate
Ask "what is the **bank** doing?" If you buy, the bank sells (higher rate). If you sell, the bank buys (lower rate). Either way, the bank comes out ahead. That difference is how it makes money.
:::

## Fees, commission and round trips

Some exchange booths also charge a flat fee or a **commission**. Changing money and then changing it back always costs you something.

:::definition Commission
A **commission** is a fee charged as a percent of the amount exchanged.
:::

:::example A cancelled trip
Using the table above, Hana buys €300 for a trip, then the trip is cancelled and she sells all €300 back. How much does she lose?
@step Cost to buy: $300 \times 1.5810 = 474.30$
@step Money back: $300 \times 1.4870 = 446.10$
@step Loss: $474.30 - 446.10 = 28.20$
@answer Hana loses \$28.20 without spending a single euro.
:::

:::example An exchange booth with commission
An airport booth sells US dollars for $1.42$ CAD each and charges a $2.5\%$ commission on the Canadian dollar amount. What do US\$200 cost?
@step Cost before commission: $200 \times 1.42 = 284$
@step Commission: $0.025 \times 284 = 7.10$
@step Total: $284 + 7.10 = 291.10$
@answer The US\$200 cost \$291.10 CAD.
:::

:::check m103-exchange-buy-sell 3
:::

:::summary
- An exchange rate gives the value of one unit of a currency in another. Questions always state the rate.
- If the rate starts with $1$ of the currency you have, multiply. If not, divide.
- Check that the size of your answer makes sense.
- Buying foreign money: use the bank's **sell** rate (higher). Selling it back: use the bank's **buy** rate (lower).
- Fees and commissions add to the cost, and a round trip always loses money.
:::
