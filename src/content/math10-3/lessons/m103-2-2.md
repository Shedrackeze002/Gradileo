Not every job pays by the hour. A store manager earns a salary, a truck salesperson earns commission, and a tree planter up north is paid for every seedling in the ground. Each way of being paid has its own simple calculation.

## Salary and pay periods

A salaried worker is paid a set amount for the year, no matter how many hours a particular week takes. The yearly amount is split into equal pay cheques.

:::definition Salary and pay period
A **salary** is a fixed amount of pay for a year of work. A **pay period** is the length of time each pay cheque covers.
:::

| Pay period | How often | Pays per year |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | every week | 52 |
| Biweekly | every two weeks | 26 |
| Semi-monthly | twice a month | 24 |
| Monthly | once a month | 12 |

:::example Salary to pay cheque
Mei is a safety coordinator with a salary of \$58,500 a year. How much is each pay cheque if Mei is paid biweekly? What if Mei is paid semi-monthly?
@step Biweekly means 26 pays: $58{,}500 \div 26 = 2{,}250.00$
@step Semi-monthly means 24 pays: $58{,}500 \div 24 = 2{,}437.50$
@answer Biweekly cheques are \$2,250.00 each; semi-monthly cheques are \$2,437.50 each.
:::

:::mistake Biweekly is not semi-monthly
“Biweekly” means every two weeks (26 pays). “Semi-monthly” means twice a month (24 pays). The yearly total is the same, but each semi-monthly cheque is bigger because there are fewer of them.
:::

## Commission

Salespeople are often paid a percent of what they sell. This rewards them for selling more.

:::definition Commission
**Commission** is pay calculated as a percent of sales. With **straight commission**, all of your pay is commission. With **salary plus commission**, you get a base salary and commission on top.
:::

:::example Salary plus commission
Jaxon sells hot tubs at a pool-and-spa store for \$1,800 per month plus 4% commission on sales. Last month Jaxon sold \$42,650 worth of hot tubs. What were Jaxon’s total earnings?
@step Change the percent to a decimal: $4\% = 0.04$
@step Commission: $0.04 \times 42{,}650 = 1{,}706.00$
@step Total: $1{,}800 + 1{,}706.00 = 3{,}506.00$
@answer Jaxon earned \$3,506.00 last month.
:::

:::mistake Change the percent first
$4\%$ means $\dfrac{4}{100} = 0.04$. Multiplying the sales by 4 instead of 0.04 gives an answer 100 times too big.
:::

## Graduated commission

Some employers pay a **higher rate on higher sales**. Each rate applies only to its own part of the sales, like steps on a staircase.

:::example Two commission rates
A furniture salesperson earns 3% on the first \$10,000 of sales and 5% on all sales above \$10,000. This month the sales were \$26,400. Find the commission.
@step First part: $0.03 \times 10{,}000 = 300.00$
@step Sales above \$10,000: $26{,}400 - 10{,}000 = 16{,}400$
@step Second part: $0.05 \times 16{,}400 = 820.00$
@step Total: $300.00 + 820.00 = 1{,}120.00$
@answer The commission is \$1,120.00.
:::

:::mistake Applying the higher rate to everything
Using 5% on all \$26,400 gives \$1,320, which is too much. The 5% rate only applies to the sales **above** \$10,000.
:::

## Piecework

In some jobs you are paid for each item you finish, not for your time. Tree planting, sewing, roofing and flyer delivery often pay this way.

:::definition Piecework
**Piecework** is pay based on the number of items (pieces) completed:
$$\text{earnings} = \text{number of pieces} \times \text{pay per piece}$$
:::

:::example Planting trees
A tree planter in a northern forest is paid \$0.17 per tree. On a good day the planter puts in 1,850 trees. What does the planter earn that day?
@step $1{,}850 \times 0.17 = 314.50$
@answer The planter earns \$314.50.
:::

:::example A bonus after a quota
Simran sews hockey jerseys for \$3.20 each, plus a bonus of \$0.80 for each jersey over 40 in a day. Simran sews 52 jerseys. Find the day’s earnings.
@step Base pay: $52 \times 3.20 = 166.40$
@step Jerseys over the quota: $52 - 40 = 12$, so the bonus is $12 \times 0.80 = 9.60$
@step Total: $166.40 + 9.60 = 176.00$
@answer Simran earns \$176.00 that day.
:::

:::tip Piecework rewards speed
A fast, careful worker can earn more with piecework than with an hourly wage. A beginner may earn less. Compare both before choosing.
:::

:::summary
- Salary per pay = annual salary ÷ pays per year (52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 semi-monthly, 12 monthly).
- Commission = rate (as a decimal) $\times$ sales. Salary plus commission adds a base amount.
- With graduated commission, each rate applies only to its own part of the sales.
- Piecework earnings = number of pieces $\times$ pay per piece, plus any bonus over a quota.
:::
