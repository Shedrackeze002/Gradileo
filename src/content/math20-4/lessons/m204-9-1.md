Managers, landlords and banks all use graphs. A line graph shows if sales are going up. A circle graph shows where your paycheque goes. In this lesson you will read these graphs and turn their percents into real amounts.

## Line graphs

:::definition Line graph
A **line graph** shows how something changes over time. Each point is one value. Time goes along the bottom. The line joins the points so you can see the trend.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":1,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":200,"xStep":1,"yStep":20,"xLabel":"Week","yLabel":"Iced coffees sold"},"polylines":[{"points":[[1,60],[2,80],[3,110],[4,140],[5,150],[6,130],[7,100],[8,90]]}],"points":[{"x":1,"y":60},{"x":2,"y":80},{"x":3,"y":110},{"x":4,"y":140},{"x":5,"y":150},{"x":6,"y":130},{"x":7,"y":100},{"x":8,"y":90}]}
:::

The graph shows the iced coffees a café sold each week of the summer. The grid lines go up by $20$. A point halfway between two lines is halfway between their numbers.

:::example Reading a change
By how much did weekly sales go up from week 2 to week 5?
@step Week 2: the point is on the $80$ line.
@step Week 5: the point is halfway between $140$ and $160$, so it is $150$.
@step Change: $150 - 80 = 70$
@answer Sales went up by $70$ iced coffees a week.
:::

:::mistake Reading the wrong point
Go **straight up** from the time on the bottom axis to the point, then **straight across** to the scale. Use a ruler or the edge of a card if it helps.
:::

## Circle graphs and percents

:::definition Circle graph
A **circle graph** shows how one whole is split into parts. Each slice is a percent of the whole, and all the slices add to $100\%$.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"circleGraph","slices":[{"label":"Rent","value":35},{"label":"Food","value":20},{"label":"Transport","value":15},{"label":"Other","value":15},{"label":"Savings","value":10},{"label":"Phone","value":5}],"show":"percent"}
:::

Jordan takes home \$2400 a month. The circle graph shows Jordan's budget. To turn a percent into dollars, change the percent to a decimal and multiply.

:::example Rent
How much does Jordan budget for rent each month?
@step Rent is $35\% = 0.35$.
@step $0.35 \times 2400 = 840$
@answer Jordan budgets \$840 a month for rent.
:::

:::example Two steps
How much more goes to rent than to food each month?
@step Food: $0.20 \times 2400 = 480$
@step $840 - 480 = 360$
@answer Jordan budgets \$360 more for rent than for food.
:::

:::tip A shortcut
Subtract the percents first: $35\% - 20\% = 15\%$, and $0.15 \times 2400 = 360$. You get the same answer.
:::

:::check m204-display-data 2
:::

## Finding a percent

To find what percent one part is of the whole, divide the part by the whole. Then multiply by $100$.
$$\text{percent} = \dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100$$

:::example Paying with cash
A food truck had $80$ customers at lunch. $28$ paid with cash. What percent paid with cash?
@step $28 \div 80 = 0.35$
@step $0.35 \times 100 = 35$
@answer $35\%$ of the customers paid with cash.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
Always divide the **part** by the **whole**. $80 \div 28$ gives a number bigger than $1$, and a part can never be more than $100\%$ of the whole.
:::

## Making a circle graph

A full circle has $360^\circ$. Each slice gets the same part of $360^\circ$ as its part of the data.
$$\text{slice angle} = \dfrac{\text{count}}{\text{total}} \times 360^\circ$$

:::figure
{"type":"circleGraph","slices":[{"label":"Basic","value":24},{"label":"Deluxe","value":18},{"label":"Premium","value":12},{"label":"Interior","value":6}],"show":"value"}
:::

:::example Car washes sold
A car wash sold $24$ basic, $18$ deluxe, $12$ premium and $6$ interior washes. What angle should the basic slice have?
@step Total: $24 + 18 + 12 + 6 = 60$
@step Basic: $\dfrac{24}{60} \times 360 = 144$
@answer The basic slice should be $144^\circ$. Check: $144 + 108 + 72 + 36 = 360$.
:::

| Use a... | when the data... |
|---|---|
| bar graph | compares separate groups |
| line graph | changes over time |
| circle graph | shows the parts of one whole |

:::check m204-display-data 3
:::

:::summary
- On a line graph, go straight up from the time, then straight across to the scale.
- A circle-graph amount: change the percent to a decimal and multiply by the whole.
- Percent $= \dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100$
- Slice angle $= \dfrac{\text{count}}{\text{total}} \times 360^\circ$. All the angles add to $360^\circ$.
- Bar graph: compare groups. Line graph: change over time. Circle graph: parts of a whole.
:::
