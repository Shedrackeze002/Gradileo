A cousin in Texas asks how tall you are in feet. A recipe from a US website uses pounds. A truck from Montana has a gas tank measured in gallons. To change between SI and imperial units, you use a conversion factor.

## Conversion factors

:::definition Conversion factor
A **conversion factor** tells you how many of one unit make one of another unit. For example, $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$.
:::

These are the factors you will use in this lesson. A question will always tell you which one to use.

:::tip Quick estimates
An inch is about $2.5\ \text{cm}$. A kilogram is about $2$ pounds. A mile is about $1.5\ \text{km}$. A US gallon is about $4\ \text{L}$. Use these to check your answers.
:::

:::example A ruler from the United States
A ruler is $12\ \text{in}$ long. About how many centimetres is that?
@step Estimate: each inch is about $2.5\ \text{cm}$, so $12 \times 2.5 = 30$.
@step Exact: $12 \times 2.54 = 30.48$
@answer The ruler is $30.48\ \text{cm}$ long, close to the estimate of $30\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::mistake Reading the factor backwards
$1\ \text{kg} \approx 2.2\ \text{lb}$ means one kilogram is about two pounds. It does **not** mean one pound is $2.2\ \text{kg}$. A kilogram is the bigger unit.
:::

## Multiply or divide?

Ask the same question as before: is the new unit **bigger** or **smaller**?

- If the new unit is smaller, you need more of them. **Multiply** by the factor.
- If the new unit is bigger, you need fewer of them. **Divide** by the factor.

:::example A TV in centimetres
A TV from a US store is $50\ \text{in}$ across. How many centimetres is that? Use $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$.
@step A centimetre is smaller than an inch, so multiply.
@step $50 \times 2.54 = 127$
@answer The TV is $127\ \text{cm}$ across.
:::

:::example Dog food in kilograms
A bag of dog food from a US store weighs $30\ \text{lb}$. What is its mass in kilograms, to the nearest tenth? Use $1\ \text{kg} \approx 2.2\ \text{lb}$.
@step A kilogram is bigger than a pound, so divide.
@step $30 \div 2.2 \approx 13.636$
@answer The bag has a mass of about $13.6\ \text{kg}$.
:::

:::mistake Dividing when you should multiply
$50 \div 2.54 \approx 19.7$, but a $50$-inch TV is not $20\ \text{cm}$ across. That is narrower than a sheet of paper! Before you calculate, decide if the number should get bigger or smaller.
:::

## Rounding and two-step conversions

Round only at the end, to the place the question asks for.

:::example A speed limit in miles per hour
The speed limit on a highway is $110\ \text{km/h}$. What is this in miles per hour, to the nearest whole number? Use $1\ \text{mi} \approx 1.6\ \text{km}$.
@step A mile is longer than a kilometre, so divide.
@step $110 \div 1.6 = 68.75$
@answer The limit is about $69$ miles per hour.
:::

Some conversions take two steps. Do one step at a time.

:::example A height in centimetres
A hockey player from the United States is $5\ \text{ft}\ 9\ \text{in}$ tall. What is this in centimetres, to the nearest tenth?
@step Change to inches: $5 \times 12 + 9 = 69\ \text{in}$
@step Change to centimetres: $69 \times 2.54 = 175.26$
@answer The player is about $175.3\ \text{cm}$ tall.
:::

:::example Filling a gas tank
Jordan's pickup truck has a $20$-gallon (US) gas tank. Gas at a station in town costs \$1.50 per litre. How much does it cost to fill the empty tank? Use $1$ US gallon $\approx 3.79\ \text{L}$.
@step Change to litres: $20 \times 3.79 = 75.8\ \text{L}$
@step Find the cost: $75.8 \times 1.50 = 113.70$
@answer It costs about \$113.70 to fill the tank.
:::

:::mistake Rounding too early
If you round $75.8\ \text{L}$ to $76\ \text{L}$ before you multiply, the cost comes out as \$114.00 instead of \$113.70. Round only at the end.
:::

:::

:::summary
- A conversion factor links two units, such as $1\ \text{in} = 2.54\ \text{cm}$ or $1\ \text{kg} \approx 2.2\ \text{lb}$.
- If the new unit is smaller, multiply. If the new unit is bigger, divide.
- Estimate first so you can catch a wrong step.
- Round only at the end.
- For two-step problems, do one conversion at a time.
:::
