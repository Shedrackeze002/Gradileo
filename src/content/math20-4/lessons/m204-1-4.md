"30% off!" "Rent goes up 4%." "Leave a 15% tip." Percents are everywhere you shop and work. This lesson shows four kinds of percent problems and how to solve each one.

## Percent of a number: a quick review

:::definition Percent
**Percent** means "out of 100". $25\%$ means $25$ out of $100$, which is $\frac{25}{100}$ or $0.25$.
:::

In Math 10 Essentials you found a percent of an amount. Change the percent to a decimal, then multiply.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Percent","Decimal","Fraction"],"rows":[["10%","0.1","1/10"],["25%","0.25","1/4"],["50%","0.5","1/2"],["75%","0.75","3/4"],["5%","0.05","1/20"]]}
:::

:::example Riding the bus
At a warehouse in Edmonton, $25\%$ of the $80$ workers take the bus. How many workers is that?
@step Change the percent to a decimal: $25\% = 0.25$
@step Multiply: $0.25 \times 80 = 20$
@answer $20$ workers take the bus.
:::

:::tip Tips without a calculator
To find $10\%$, move the decimal point one place left: $10\%$ of \$45 is \$4.50. Half of that is $5\%$, \$2.25. Add them for $15\%$: \$6.75.
:::

## What percent is one number of another?

To find what percent a part is of a whole, divide the part by the whole. Then multiply by $100$.

:::example A practice test
Sana got $18$ out of $20$ on a practice test for her driver's licence. What percent is that?
@step Part over whole: $18 \div 20 = 0.9$
@step Multiply by $100$: $0.9 \times 100 = 90$
@answer Sana scored $90\%$.
:::

:::example Flu shots at work
At a hotel, $37$ of the $52$ staff got a flu shot. What percent is that, to the nearest whole percent?
@step $37 \div 52 \approx 0.7115$
@step $0.7115 \times 100 \approx 71$
@answer About $71\%$ of the staff got a flu shot.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
$20 \div 18$ gives about $111\%$, which cannot be a test score. The **part** goes first: part $\div$ whole.
:::

## Percent increase and decrease

:::definition Percent increase and percent decrease
A **percent increase** tells how much an amount went up, as a percent of the original amount. A **percent decrease** tells how much it went down.
$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{amount of change}}{\text{original amount}} \times 100$$
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example A bus pass goes up
A monthly bus pass went from \$80 last year to \$92 this year. What is the percent increase?
@step Amount of change: $92 - 80 = 12$
@step Divide by the original: $12 \div 80 = 0.15$
@step Multiply by $100$: $0.15 \times 100 = 15$
@answer The price went up $15\%$.
:::

:::example A new rent
Rent of \$1150 a month goes up $4\%$. What is the new rent?
@step Find the increase: $0.04 \times 1150 = 46$
@step Add it on: $1150 + 46 = 1196$
@answer The new rent is \$1196 a month.
:::

:::example A sale price
A jacket costs \$89.99. It is $30\%$ off. What is the sale price, to the nearest cent?
@step Find the discount: $0.30 \times 89.99 = 26.997$
@step Subtract: $89.99 - 26.997 = 62.993$
@answer The sale price is about \$62.99.
:::

:::mistake Dividing by the new amount
Always divide by the **original** (the "before") amount. For the bus pass, $12 \div 92$ gives about $13\%$, which is wrong.
:::

## Finding the whole from a part

Sometimes you know the part and the percent, and you need the whole. Divide the part by the percent written as a decimal.

:::example A deposit on a couch
Liam pays a deposit of \$45 on a couch. The deposit is $15\%$ of the full price. What is the full price? How much does he still owe?
@step $15\%$ of the price is \$45, so $0.15 \times \text{price} = 45$
@step Divide: $45 \div 0.15 = 300$
@step Still to pay: $300 - 45 = 255$
@answer The couch costs \$300. Liam still owes \$255.
:::

:::check m204-percent-problems 3
:::

:::summary
- Percent of a number: change the percent to a decimal and multiply.
- What percent: part $\div$ whole, then $\times\ 100$.
- Percent change: change $\div$ original amount, then $\times\ 100$.
- New amount: find the change, then add it (increase) or subtract it (decrease).
- Finding the whole: part $\div$ percent as a decimal.
:::
