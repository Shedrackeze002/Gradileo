How much will you earn for a 6-hour shift? How much is a 10 km taxi ride? A table or a graph can answer questions like these at a glance, without doing the math each time.

## Tables of values: a quick review

:::definition Relationship
A **relationship** shows how one amount depends on another. Your pay depends on the hours you work.
:::

:::definition Table of values
A **table of values** lists pairs of numbers that follow a rule. The top row is what you choose, and the bottom row is what you get.
:::

In Math 10 Essentials you found patterns in tables. Now you will build a table from a rule.

:::example Pay at \$15 per hour
Tariq earns \$15 per hour. The rule is $P = 15h$. Make a table for $1$ to $5$ hours.
@step Substitute each number of hours: $15 \times 1 = 15$, $15 \times 2 = 30$, $15 \times 3 = 45$
@step $15 \times 4 = 60$ and $15 \times 5 = 75$
@answer The table is shown below.
:::

## Reading a graph

:::definition Graph
A **graph** shows a relationship as a picture. The horizontal axis shows what you choose. The vertical axis shows what you get.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":120,"xStep":1,"yStep":15,"xLabel":"Hours worked","yLabel":"Pay ($)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,0],[8,120]]}],"points":[{"x":6,"y":90,"label":"(6, 90)","color":"accent"}]}
:::

:::example Reading the pay graph
Use the graph of $P = 15h$. How much does Tariq earn for $6$ hours?
@step Find $6$ on the horizontal axis (hours worked).
@step Go straight up to the line. Then go straight across to the vertical axis.
@step The line is at $90$.
@answer Tariq earns \$90 for $6$ hours. Check: $15 \times 6 = 90$.
:::

You can also read a graph backwards. To find how many hours give \$75, start at $75$ on the vertical axis. Go across to the line, then down. The answer is $5$ hours.

## Rules with a start value

Some rules have a **start value** that you pay even before you use anything, like a taxi's starting fee. The graph then starts **above** zero.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":20,"xStep":1,"yStep":2,"xLabel":"Distance (km)","yLabel":"Fare ($)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,4],[8,20]]}],"points":[{"x":0,"y":4,"label":"start fee","color":"accent"}]}
:::

:::example A taxi fare
A taxi charges \$4 to start plus \$2 per kilometre: $C = 4 + 2d$. What is the fare for $6\ \text{km}$?
@step From the graph: go up from $6$ to the line, then across. The fare is $16$.
@step Check with the rule: $C = 4 + 2 \times 6 = 4 + 12 = 16$
@answer The fare is \$16.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the start value
$2 \times 6 = 12$ leaves out the \$4 start fee. On the graph, the line meets the vertical axis at $4$, not at $0$.
:::

:::

## Matching a rule to a graph

To check which graph shows a rule, find two points from the rule. Then look for the line that goes through both.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":250,"xStep":1,"yStep":25,"xLabel":"Time (h)","yLabel":"Cost ($)"},"lines":[{"through":[[0,50],[8,250]],"label":"A"},{"through":[[0,25],[4.5,250]],"label":"B","color":"accent"}]}
:::

:::example Booking a hall
A community hall charges \$50 plus \$25 per hour: $C = 50 + 25h$. Which line, A or B, shows this rule?
@step When $h = 0$: $C = 50 + 25 \times 0 = 50$. The line must start at $50$.
@step When $h = 4$: $C = 50 + 25 \times 4 = 150$.
@step Line A starts at $50$ and goes through $(4, 150)$. Line B starts at $25$.
@answer Line A shows the rule.
:::

:::tip What the graph tells you
The **start value** is where the line meets the vertical axis. The **rate** is how much the line goes up for each step of $1$ to the right. For line A, that is \$25 per hour.
:::

:::check m204-table-graph 3
:::

:::summary
- A table of values lists pairs of numbers that follow a rule. Substitute each value into the rule.
- To read a graph, go up from the horizontal axis to the line, then across to the vertical axis.
- A rule with a start value, like $C = 4 + 2d$, has a graph that starts above zero.
- To match a rule to a graph, find two points from the rule and look for the line through both.
:::
