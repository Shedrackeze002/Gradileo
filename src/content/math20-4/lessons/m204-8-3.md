Kiona drives from Riverton to Lakeside for a new job. How long will it take, and when will she get there? Drivers, dispatchers and anyone planning a road trip use one simple formula to answer these questions.

## Speed and distance

:::definition Speed
**Speed** tells how far something goes in one unit of time. A speed of $90\ \text{km/h}$ (kilometres per hour) means $90$ kilometres in each hour.
:::

If you know the speed and the time, multiply to find the **distance**:

$$\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}$$

:::example A delivery run
A delivery truck travels for $3$ hours at an average speed of $90\ \text{km/h}$. How far does it go?
@step $\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}$
@step $90 \times 3 = 270$
@answer The truck goes $270\ \text{km}$.
:::

## Finding the time or the speed

The same formula can be turned around.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["To find","Use"],"rows":[["Distance","speed × time"],["Time","distance ÷ speed"],["Speed","distance ÷ time"]]}
:::

:::definition Travel time
The **travel time** is how long a trip takes. $\text{time} = \text{distance} \div \text{speed}$.
:::

:::example Riverton to Lakeside
The drive from Riverton to Lakeside is about $300\ \text{km}$. Kiona drives at an average speed of $100\ \text{km/h}$. How long does the drive take?
@step $\text{time} = \text{distance} \div \text{speed}$
@step $300 \div 100 = 3$
@answer The drive takes about $3$ hours.
:::

:::example Average speed
A bus travels $240\ \text{km}$ in $3$ hours. What is its average speed?
@step $\text{speed} = \text{distance} \div \text{time}$
@step $240 \div 3 = 80$
@answer The average speed is $80\ \text{km/h}$.
:::

## Hours and minutes

A travel time often comes out as a decimal, like $2.5$ hours. To change the decimal part to minutes, multiply it by $60$.

:::example A trip to Hillcrest
A driver goes $210\ \text{km}$ at an average speed of $90\ \text{km/h}$. How long does the trip take, in hours and minutes?
@step $210 \div 90 \approx 2.3333$ hours
@step That is $2$ hours and $0.3333$ of an hour.
@step $0.3333 \times 60 \approx 20$ minutes
@answer The trip takes $2$ hours $20$ minutes.
:::

:::mistake Decimal hours are not minutes
$2.5$ hours is **not** $2$ hours $50$ minutes. Half an hour is $30$ minutes, so $2.5$ hours is $2$ hours $30$ minutes. Always multiply the decimal part by $60$.
:::

To go the other way, divide the minutes by $60$. For example, $2$ h $15$ min is $2 + \frac{15}{60} = 2.25$ hours.

## Planning a trip

To find the arrival time, add the driving time **and** any stops to the start time.

:::example Leaving Riverton
Kiona leaves Riverton at 8:30 a.m. She drives $300\ \text{km}$ to Lakeside at $100\ \text{km/h}$. She stops for $30$ minutes for lunch. When does she arrive?
@step Driving time: $300 \div 100 = 3$ hours.
@step Total time: $3$ h $+ 30$ min $= 3$ h $30$ min.
@step 8:30 a.m. $+ 3$ h is 11:30 a.m. Then $+ 30$ min is 12:00 noon.
@answer Kiona arrives at 12:00 noon (12:00 p.m.).
:::

:::tip Estimate first
Before you calculate, make a quick estimate. At about $100\ \text{km/h}$, each $100\ \text{km}$ takes about one hour. This helps you catch big mistakes.
:::

:::check m204-travel-time 3
:::

:::summary
- $\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}$
- $\text{time} = \text{distance} \div \text{speed}$ and $\text{speed} = \text{distance} \div \text{time}$
- Change decimal hours to minutes by multiplying the decimal part by $60$.
- Arrival time = start time + driving time + stops.
:::
