Recipes and tape measures are full of fractions: $2\frac{1}{4}$ cups of flour, a board $6\frac{1}{2}$ feet long. At work you often need to add them, take one away from another, or double them.

## Mixed numbers and improper fractions

:::definition Mixed number
A **mixed number** has a whole number and a fraction, like $2\frac{1}{4}$.
:::

:::definition Improper fraction
An **improper fraction** has a numerator (top) that is as big as or bigger than its denominator (bottom), like $\frac{9}{4}$.
:::

A mixed number and an improper fraction can name the same amount. Each whole is $\frac{4}{4}$, so $2\frac{1}{4}$ is $\frac{4}{4} + \frac{4}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}$.

:::figure

:::

:::example Changing $2\frac{1}{4}$ to an improper fraction
Write $2\frac{1}{4}$ as an improper fraction.
@step Multiply the whole number by the denominator: $2 \times 4 = 8$
@step Add the numerator: $8 + 1 = 9$
@step Keep the same denominator: $\frac{9}{4}$
@answer $2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}$
:::

To go back, divide. $\frac{15}{4}$ is $15 \div 4 = 3$ with $3$ left over, so $\frac{15}{4} = 3\frac{3}{4}$.

:::mistake Adding instead of multiplying
$2\frac{1}{4}$ is **not** $\frac{2 + 1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$. Multiply first: $2 \times 4 + 1 = 9$, so $2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}$.
:::

## Adding and subtracting: a quick review

In Math 10 Essentials you added and subtracted fractions. When the denominators match, add or subtract the whole numbers, then the fractions.

:::example Two pieces of trim
Jun cuts two pieces of trim. One is $1\frac{3}{8}$ feet long and the other is $2\frac{1}{8}$ feet long. What is the total length?
@step Whole numbers: $1 + 2 = 3$
@step Fractions: $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$
@answer The total length is $3\frac{1}{2}$ feet.
:::

:::definition Common denominator
A **common denominator** is a number that both denominators divide into evenly. For $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$, a common denominator is $4$, because $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4}$.
:::

When the denominators are different, change to improper fractions with a common denominator. This method always works.

:::example Cutting a board
A board is $6\frac{1}{2}$ feet long. Kai cuts off a piece $2\frac{3}{4}$ feet long. How much is left? (Ignore the saw cut.)
@step Change to improper fractions: $6\frac{1}{2} = \frac{13}{2}$ and $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$
@step Use the common denominator $4$: $\frac{13}{2} = \frac{26}{4}$
@step Subtract the numerators: $\frac{26}{4} - \frac{11}{4} = \frac{15}{4}$
@step Change back: $\frac{15}{4} = 3\frac{3}{4}$
@answer $3\frac{3}{4}$ feet of the board is left.
:::

## Multiplying fractions and mixed numbers

Here is the new step for this year. To multiply:
1. Change any mixed numbers to improper fractions.
2. Multiply the numerators. Multiply the denominators.
3. Simplify, and change back to a mixed number.

:::example Doubling a recipe
A bannock recipe uses $2\frac{1}{4}$ cups of flour. Talia makes a double batch. How much flour does she need?
@step Change to an improper fraction: $2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}$
@step Multiply by $2$: $2 \times \frac{9}{4} = \frac{18}{4}$
@step Simplify: $\frac{18}{4} = \frac{9}{2} = 4\frac{1}{2}$
@answer Talia needs $4\frac{1}{2}$ cups of flour.
:::

:::example Part of a shift
Mason works a $2\frac{1}{2}$-hour shift. He spends $\frac{3}{4}$ of it stocking shelves. How long is that?
@step "Of" means multiply: $\frac{3}{4} \times 2\frac{1}{2}$
@step Change to an improper fraction: $2\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}$
@step Multiply: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{3 \times 5}{4 \times 2} = \frac{15}{8}$
@step Change back: $\frac{15}{8} = 1\frac{7}{8}$
@answer Mason stocks shelves for $1\frac{7}{8}$ hours, a little less than $2$ hours.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying the parts separately
For $1\frac{1}{2} \times 2\frac{1}{2}$, you cannot multiply $1 \times 2$ and $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2}$ to get $2\frac{1}{4}$. Change to improper fractions first: $\frac{3}{2} \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{15}{4} = 3\frac{3}{4}$.
:::

## Two-step problems

Many real jobs need two steps. Decide what to find first, then use that answer.

:::example Cutting shelves
Sofia has an $8$-foot board. She cuts $2$ shelves, each $3\frac{1}{4}$ feet long. How much of the board is left?
@step Length of the shelves: $2 \times 3\frac{1}{4} = 2 \times \frac{13}{4} = \frac{26}{4} = 6\frac{1}{2}$ feet
@step Write $8$ with the same denominator: $8 = \frac{16}{2}$, and $6\frac{1}{2} = \frac{13}{2}$
@step Subtract: $\frac{16}{2} - \frac{13}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1\frac{1}{2}$
@answer $1\frac{1}{2}$ feet of the board is left.
:::

:::tip Check with an estimate
$3\frac{1}{4}$ is a bit more than $3$, so two shelves use a bit more than $6$ feet. That leaves a bit less than $2$ feet. The answer $1\frac{1}{2}$ makes sense.
:::

:::

:::summary
- A mixed number like $2\frac{1}{4}$ can be written as an improper fraction: $2 \times 4 + 1 = 9$, so $\frac{9}{4}$.
- To add or subtract, use a common denominator. Then add or subtract the numerators.
- To multiply, change to improper fractions, multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms, then simplify.
- "Of" means multiply: $\frac{3}{4}$ of $2\frac{1}{2}$ is $\frac{3}{4} \times 2\frac{1}{2}$.
- For two-step problems, find the first amount, then use it. Check with an estimate.
:::
