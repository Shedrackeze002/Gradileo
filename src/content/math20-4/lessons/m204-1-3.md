Concrete, two-stroke fuel and frozen juice all come with mixing instructions such as $1 : 2 : 3$ or $50 : 1$. Get the mix wrong and the concrete cracks, the engine seizes or the juice tastes like water. Ratios, rates and proportions help you get it right.

## Ratios

:::definition Ratio
A **ratio** compares two amounts of the same kind. The ratio $2 : 3$ is read "2 to 3". It can also be written as the fraction $\frac{2}{3}$.
:::

A ratio is in **simplest form** when no number except $1$ divides into both terms. To simplify, divide both terms by the same number. This works just like simplifying a fraction, which you did in Math 10 Essentials.

:::figure

:::

:::example Cashiers to clerks
A grocery store has $12$ cashiers and $18$ stock clerks. What is the ratio of cashiers to clerks in simplest form?
@step Write the ratio in the order asked: $12 : 18$
@step Both numbers divide by $6$: $12 \div 6 = 2$ and $18 \div 6 = 3$
@answer The ratio is $2 : 3$. For every $2$ cashiers there are $3$ clerks.
:::

:::mistake The order matters
"Cashiers to clerks" is $2 : 3$. "Clerks to cashiers" is $3 : 2$. Always write the terms in the order the question names them.
:::

## Rates and unit rates

:::definition Rate
A **rate** compares two amounts with different units, such as \$12 for $4\ \text{kg}$, or $240\ \text{km}$ in $3$ hours.
:::

A **unit rate** is a rate for **one** unit: one kilogram, one hour, one litre. To find it, divide by the number of units.

:::example Price per kilogram
A $3\ \text{kg}$ bag of apples costs \$8.40. What is the price per kilogram?
@step Divide the price by the kilograms: $8.40 \div 3 = 2.80$
@answer The apples cost \$2.80 per kilogram.
:::

:::example Pay per hour
Hana earns \$136 for an $8$-hour shift. What is her hourly wage?
@step Divide the pay by the hours: $136 \div 8 = 17$
@answer Hana earns \$17 per hour.
:::

:::tip "Per" means "for each one"
\$17 **per** hour means \$17 for each hour. When you see "per", think: divide to find the amount for one.
:::

:::

## Proportions

:::definition Proportion
A **proportion** says two ratios are equal, such as $1 : 3 = 4 : 12$. Whatever you multiply one term by, you multiply the other term by too.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Juice for a team
Frozen juice is mixed $1 : 3$ (concentrate to water). How many cans of water go with $4$ cans of concentrate?
@step Concentrate goes from $1$ to $4$: that is $\times 4$.
@step Water: $3 \times 4 = 12$
@answer Use $12$ cans of water.
:::

:::example Two-stroke fuel
A chainsaw uses gas and oil mixed $50 : 1$. How much oil goes with $4\ \text{L}$ of gas?
@step Change the gas to millilitres: $4\ \text{L} = 4000\ \text{mL}$
@step Oil is $\frac{1}{50}$ of the gas: $4000 \div 50 = 80$
@answer Mix in $80\ \text{mL}$ of oil.
:::

:::example Scaling a recipe
A stew for $6$ people uses $450\ \text{g}$ of beef. How much beef is needed for $10$ people?
@step Beef for $1$ person: $450 \div 6 = 75\ \text{g}$
@step Beef for $10$ people: $75 \times 10 = 750\ \text{g}$
@answer Use $750\ \text{g}$ of beef.
:::

:::mistake Adding instead of multiplying
Going from $6$ to $10$ people is **not** "add $4$ grams". A proportion grows by multiplying. Find the amount for one, then multiply.
:::

:::

## Sharing a total in a ratio

Sometimes you know the total amount of mix and need each part. Add the terms of the ratio to find how many equal parts there are.

:::example Concrete for a patio
Concrete is mixed $1 : 2 : 3$ (cement to sand to gravel). A crew needs $36$ buckets of mix. How many buckets of each?
@step Number of parts: $1 + 2 + 3 = 6$
@step One part: $36 \div 6 = 6$ buckets
@step Cement: $1 \times 6 = 6$. Sand: $2 \times 6 = 12$. Gravel: $3 \times 6 = 18$.
@answer $6$ buckets of cement, $12$ of sand and $18$ of gravel. Check: $6 + 12 + 18 = 36$.
:::

:::check m204-ratio-rate 3
:::

:::summary
- A ratio compares amounts of the same kind. Keep the order, and divide both terms by the same number to simplify.
- A rate compares amounts with different units. A unit rate is the amount for one unit: divide.
- In a proportion, multiply both terms by the same number.
- To share a total in a ratio, add the terms to find the number of parts, find one part, then multiply.
:::
