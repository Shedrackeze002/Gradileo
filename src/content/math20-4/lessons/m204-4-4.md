Your first vehicle and your first place are big steps. Each one costs much more than the price tag or the rent. Work out the real monthly cost before you sign anything.

## The monthly cost of a vehicle

A car payment is only part of the cost. You also pay for insurance, fuel and maintenance, like oil changes and tires.

:::definition Insurance
**Insurance** is a plan you pay for that covers the cost if something goes wrong, like a crash, a fire or a theft. In many places, the law says every vehicle on the road must be insured.
:::

Insurance is often quoted **per year**. Divide by 12 to get the monthly cost.

:::example The real cost of a car
Sofia’s costs for her car are in the table. What does the car cost her each month?
@step Insurance per month: $1740 \div 12 = 145$
@step Total: $285 + 145 + 160 + 50 = 640$
@answer The car costs Sofia \$640 a month, much more than the \$285 payment.
:::

## Fuel costs

:::definition Fuel economy
**Fuel economy** tells how much fuel a vehicle uses. In Canada it is given in **litres per 100 km** (L/100 km). A smaller number means the vehicle uses less fuel.
:::

$$\text{litres used} = \text{distance} \div 100 \times \text{L/100 km}$$

:::example Gas for one month
Arjun drives about $1500\ \text{km}$ a month. His car uses $8.2\ \text{L/100 km}$, and gas costs \$1.45 per litre. What does his gas cost each month?
@step Litres used: $1500 \div 100 \times 8.2 = 15 \times 8.2 = 123\ \text{L}$
@step Cost: $123 \times 1.45 = 178.35$
@answer Arjun’s gas costs \$178.35 a month.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to divide by 100
$1500 \times 8.2 = 12\,300$ litres is far too much. The rate is per **100** km, so first find how many hundreds of kilometres you drive.
:::

:::check m204-vehicle-cost 2
:::

:::example A car or a pickup truck?
A pickup truck uses $13.6\ \text{L/100 km}$. How much more would Arjun pay for gas each month with the truck?
@step Truck: $15 \times 13.6 = 204\ \text{L}$, and $204 \times 1.45 = 295.80$
@step Difference: $295.80 - 178.35 = 117.45$
@answer The truck costs \$117.45 more each month. That is \$1409.40 more per year.
:::

## Renting a home

:::definition Rent and utilities
**Rent** is what you pay each month to live in a home you do not own. **Utilities** are services like power, heat and water. Sometimes they are included in the rent, and sometimes you pay them yourself.
:::

:::definition Damage deposit
A **damage deposit** is money you pay the landlord when you move in. You get it back when you move out, as long as there is no damage. A deposit is often about one month’s rent.
:::

You will also want **tenant insurance**. It covers your things if they are stolen or damaged.

:::example Sharing a place
Leila and a roommate rent a basement suite. Rent is \$1100, utilities are \$165 and tenant insurance is \$22 a month. They split the costs equally. What does each person pay?
@step Total: $1100 + 165 + 22 = 1287$
@step Each share: $1287 \div 2 = 643.50$
@answer Each person pays \$643.50 a month.
:::

:::example Move-in costs
To move in, they must pay the first month’s rent and a damage deposit equal to one month’s rent. How much is that?
@step $1100 + 1100 = 2200$
@answer They need \$2200 before they get the keys.
:::

## How much rent can you afford?

A common **rule of thumb** says to spend no more than about **30%** of your income on rent. Then there is enough left for food, transportation and savings.

:::example Using the 30% rule
Grace takes home \$2600 a month. She finds an apartment for \$950. Is it within the 30% guideline?
@step Most rent: $0.3 \times 2600 = 780$
@step Rent as a percent of income: $950 \div 2600 \times 100 \approx 36.5\%$
@answer \$950 is more than \$780 (36.5% is more than 30%), so it is too much on her own. With a roommate, her share is \$475, about 18.3% of her income.
:::

:::check m204-housing-cost 3
:::

:::summary
- A vehicle costs its payment **plus** insurance, fuel and maintenance. Divide yearly costs by 12.
- Litres used $=$ distance $\div 100 \times$ L/100 km. Then multiply by the price per litre.
- Housing costs rent **plus** utilities and tenant insurance. Roommates split the total.
- Move-in costs are the first month’s rent plus the damage deposit.
- Try to keep rent to about 30% of your income or less.
:::
