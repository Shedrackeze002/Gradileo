A payroll clerk works out pay. A taxi meter works out a fare. A landscaper works out how much fence a yard needs. Each one uses a **formula**: a short rule that turns the numbers you know into the number you need.

## Formulas and variables: a quick review

:::definition Formula
A **formula** is a rule written with letters and numbers, such as $E = rh$. It shows how one amount is found from other amounts.
:::

:::definition Variable
A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number that can change, such as the hours you work.
:::

:::definition Substitute
To **substitute** means to replace each letter with its number.
:::

In Math 10 Essentials you substituted into simple formulas. Remember: two letters side by side, like $rh$, mean multiply: $r \times h$.

:::example Pay for a shift
Pay is found with $E = rh$. $E$ is the pay in dollars, $r$ is the hourly wage and $h$ is the hours worked. Kiona earns \$16.50 per hour and works $6$ hours. What is her pay?
@step Write the formula: $E = rh$
@step Substitute $r = 16.50$ and $h = 6$: $E = 16.50 \times 6$
@step Calculate: $E = 99$
@answer Kiona earns \$99.
:::

## Formulas with more than one step

Many formulas have two operations. Use the order of operations: **multiply first, then add**.

:::

:::example Fencing a yard
A rectangular yard is $12.5\ \text{m}$ long and $8.4\ \text{m}$ wide. Use $P = 2l + 2w$ to find the perimeter.
@step Write the formula: $P = 2l + 2w$
@step Substitute $l = 12.5$ and $w = 8.4$: $P = 2 \times 12.5 + 2 \times 8.4$
@step Multiply first: $P = 25 + 16.8$
@step Add: $P = 41.8$
@answer The yard needs $41.8\ \text{m}$ of fence.
:::

:::example A taxi fare
A taxi in the city charges $C = 4 + 2.5d$, where $C$ is the fare in dollars and $d$ is the distance in kilometres. What is the fare for a $7.4\ \text{km}$ ride?
@step Substitute $d = 7.4$: $C = 4 + 2.5 \times 7.4$
@step Multiply first: $2.5 \times 7.4 = 18.5$
@step Add: $C = 4 + 18.5 = 22.5$
@answer The fare is \$22.50.
:::

:::mistake Adding first
In $C = 4 + 2.5 \times 7.4$, adding first gives $6.5 \times 7.4 = 48.10$. That fare is more than double the right answer. Multiply before you add.
:::

## The temperature formula

To change degrees Celsius to degrees Fahrenheit, use $F = 1.8C + 32$.

:::example A warm day
It is $25^\circ\text{C}$ outside. What is that in degrees Fahrenheit?
@step Substitute $C = 25$: $F = 1.8 \times 25 + 32$
@step Multiply first: $1.8 \times 25 = 45$
@step Add: $F = 45 + 32 = 77$
@answer $25^\circ\text{C}$ is $77^\circ\text{F}$.
:::

:::example A cold morning
It is $-20^\circ\text{C}$ on a winter morning. What is that in degrees Fahrenheit?
@step Substitute $C = -20$: $F = 1.8 \times (-20) + 32$
@step Multiply first: $1.8 \times (-20) = -36$
@step Add: $F = -36 + 32 = -4$
@answer $-20^\circ\text{C}$ is $-4^\circ\text{F}$.
:::

:::tip Negative numbers on a calculator
Put a negative number in brackets, like $1.8 \times (-20)$. Use the $(-)$ key, not the subtract key.
:::

## Formulas in two-step jobs

Real jobs often use a formula, then one more step, like finding a cost.

:::example New flooring
A bedroom is $4.5\ \text{m}$ long and $3.2\ \text{m}$ wide. Flooring costs \$28.50 per square metre. What will the flooring cost?
@step Area: $A = lw = 4.5 \times 3.2 = 14.4\ \text{m}^2$
@step Cost: $14.4 \times 28.50 = 410.40$
@answer The flooring costs \$410.40.
:::

:::check m204-use-formula 3
:::

:::summary
- A formula is a rule with variables. Substitute a number for each letter, then calculate.
- Letters side by side mean multiply: $rh = r \times h$.
- Use the order of operations: multiply before you add.
- Put negative numbers in brackets when you substitute.
- For two-step jobs, use the formula first, then use its answer.
:::
