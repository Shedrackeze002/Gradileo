Moving out, buying a car, or just making it to the next payday all start with one plan: a budget. A budget tells your money where to go. Then you do not have to wonder where it went.

## Income and expenses

:::definition Budget
A **budget** is a plan for how you will use your money each month. It lists the money coming in and the money going out.
:::

:::definition Income
Your **income** is the money you get, usually from a job. In a budget, use your **take-home pay**. That is the money that lands in your bank account after deductions like income tax, CPP and EI.
:::

An **expense** is something you spend money on, like rent or groceries. To balance a budget, add up the expenses. Then subtract the total from the income.

$$\text{money left over} = \text{income} - \text{total expenses}$$

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Expense","Cost per month"],"rows":[["Rent","$750"],["Phone plan","$50"],["Bus pass","$100"],["Groceries","$300"],["Entertainment","$60"]]}
:::

:::example Money left over
Jasmine works at a coffee shop. She takes home \$1600 each month. Her expenses are in the table above. How much money is left over each month?
@step Add the expenses: $750 + 50 + 100 + 300 + 60 = 1260$
@step Subtract from the take-home pay: $1600 - 1260 = 340$
@answer Jasmine has \$340 left over each month. She can save it or plan how to use it.
:::

:::mistake Using gross pay
A pay stub shows **gross pay** (before deductions) and **net pay** (take-home pay). Gross pay is bigger, but you never get all of it. Build your budget with take-home pay.
:::

## Fixed and variable expenses

:::definition Fixed expense
A **fixed expense** costs the same amount every month. Rent, a phone plan, a bus pass and car insurance are fixed expenses.
:::

:::definition Variable expense
A **variable expense** changes from month to month. Groceries, gas for a car, eating out and clothing are variable expenses.
:::

Fixed expenses are easy to plan for. Variable expenses are where you can cut back when money is tight.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Expense","Cost per month"],"rows":[["Rent","$875.00"],["Groceries","$312.65"],["Phone plan","$54.99"],["Gas for car","$148.20"],["Car insurance","$163.50"],["Eating out","$96.35"]]}
:::

:::example Sorting a budget
Omar works at a warehouse and takes home \$2150.40 a month. His expenses are in the table. Find his fixed total, his variable total and the money left over.
@step Fixed (the same every month): rent, phone plan and car insurance. $875 + 54.99 + 163.50 = 1093.49$
@step Variable (they change): groceries, gas and eating out. $312.65 + 148.20 + 96.35 = 557.20$
@step Left over: $2150.40 - 1093.49 - 557.20 = 499.71$
@answer Fixed expenses are \$1093.49, variable expenses are \$557.20, and Omar has \$499.71 left over.
:::

:::tip Track your spending
For one month, write down everything you buy, even a \$3 coffee. Small variable expenses add up fast.
:::

## Budgets as percents

A **circle graph** shows how a whole budget is split up. The whole circle is 100% of the income.

:::figure
{"type":"circleGraph","slices":[{"label":"Rent","value":40},{"label":"Food","value":20},{"label":"Transportation","value":15},{"label":"Savings","value":10},{"label":"Other","value":15}],"show":"percent","caption":"Mateo’s monthly budget"}
:::

:::example Reading a circle graph
Mateo takes home \$2400 a month. How much does his plan set aside for food?
@step The graph shows food is 20%. Write it as a decimal: $20\% = 0.2$
@step $0.2 \times 2400 = 480$
@answer Mateo’s plan sets aside \$480 for food each month.
:::

To find what percent of your income an expense takes, divide the expense by the income. Then multiply by 100.

:::example Rent as a percent of income
Priya takes home \$2860 a month and pays \$975 for rent. What percent of her take-home pay goes to rent?
@step $975 \div 2860 \times 100 \approx 34.09$
@answer About 34.1% of Priya’s take-home pay goes to rent.
:::

## Saving for a goal

A budget helps you reach a goal, like a damage deposit or a used car. Split the goal into equal monthly amounts.

$$\text{save each month} = \text{goal} \div \text{number of months}$$

:::example Saving for winter tires
Daniel wants \$900 for winter tires in 6 months. Right now he has \$110 left over each month. How much less must he spend each month?
@step Save each month: $900 \div 6 = 150$
@step Extra needed: $150 - 110 = 40$
@answer Daniel needs to save \$150 a month. He must spend \$40 less each month, for example on eating out.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting costs that do not come every month
Car repairs, gifts and yearly fees are easy to forget. Divide a yearly cost by 12 and put that amount in your monthly budget.
:::

:::tip Pay yourself first
On payday, move your savings into a separate account right away. Then live on what is left.
:::

:::check m204-budget 3
:::

:::summary
- A budget lists your income (use take-home pay) and your expenses.
- Money left over $=$ income $-$ total expenses.
- Fixed expenses stay the same each month. Variable expenses change, so they are easier to cut.
- Percent of income $=$ expense $\div$ income $\times 100$.
- To save for a goal, divide the goal by the number of months. If you are short, spend less on variable expenses.
:::
