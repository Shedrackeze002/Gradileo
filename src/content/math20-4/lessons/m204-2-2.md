A sign that says "25% off" sounds great. But how much will you really pay? At the till, a sales tax is added too. In this lesson you will find sale prices and the total on a receipt.

## Discounts and sale prices

A sale takes a **percent** off the regular price. To use a percent, change it to a decimal first.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Percent","Decimal"],"rows":[["10%","0.1"],["20%","0.2"],["25%","0.25"],["30%","0.3"],["50%","0.5"]]}
:::

:::definition Discount and sale price
The **discount** is the amount of money you save. The **sale price** is what you pay after the discount.
$$\text{discount} = \text{percent off} \times \text{regular price}$$
$$\text{sale price} = \text{regular price} - \text{discount}$$
:::

:::example A hoodie on sale
A hoodie is regularly \$40. It is on sale for 25% off. How much do you save, and what is the sale price?
@step Change the percent to a decimal: $25\% = 0.25$
@step Discount: $0.25 \times 40 = 10.00$
@step Sale price: $40.00 - 10.00 = 30.00$
@answer You save \$10.00, and the sale price is \$30.00.
:::

:::example Jeans at 30% off
A pair of jeans is regularly \$59.99. It is on sale for 30% off.
@step Discount: $0.3 \times 59.99 = 17.997$, which rounds to \$18.00.
@step Sale price: $59.99 - 18.00 = 41.99$
@answer You save \$18.00, and the sale price is \$41.99.
:::

:::mistake Stopping too soon
$0.3 \times 59.99$ is the money you **save**, not the price you pay. Subtract it from the regular price to get the sale price.
:::

## Two deals at once

Some sales are trickier. Work one step at a time.

**Buy one, get the second at 50% off.** Only the second item (usually the cheaper one) is half price.

:::example Two pairs of shoes
Shoes are on sale: buy one, get the second at 50% off. Maya buys a pair for \$80 and a pair for \$60.
@step The cheaper pair is half price: $60 \div 2 = 30$, so Maya saves \$30.00.
@step Total: $80 + 60 - 30 = 110$
@answer Maya pays \$110.00 before tax.
:::

**Two discounts in a row.** The second discount is taken off the **sale price**, not the regular price.

:::example 20% off, then 10% off
A jacket is regularly \$100. It is 20% off, and the store takes another 10% off at the till.
@step After 20% off, you pay 80%: $0.8 \times 100 = 80.00$
@step After another 10% off, you pay 90% of that: $0.9 \times 80 = 72.00$
@answer The final price is \$72.00.
:::

:::mistake 20% then 10% is not 30% off
30% off \$100 would be \$70. But the second 10% comes off \$80, not \$100, so you save only \$8 more. The final price is \$72.
:::

## Subtotal, GST and total

At the till, the store adds up your items and then adds tax.

:::definition Subtotal and GST
The **subtotal** is the total of the prices **before** tax. Many places add a **sales tax** to most things you buy. Depending on where you live, it may be called sales tax, GST (Goods and Services Tax) or VAT. In this lesson the tax is called GST, and it is 5% of the subtotal. Each question tells you the rate to use.
$$\text{GST} = 0.05 \times \text{subtotal} \qquad \text{total} = \text{subtotal} + \text{GST}$$
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Reading a receipt
Find the subtotal, the GST and the total for the receipt above.
@step Subtotal: $15.00 + 12.50 + 9.99 = 37.49$
@step GST: $0.05 \times 37.49 = 1.8745$, which rounds to \$1.87.
@step Total: $37.49 + 1.87 = 39.36$
@answer The subtotal is \$37.49, the GST is \$1.87 and the total is \$39.36.
:::

:::note Not everything is taxed
In many places, basic groceries, like milk, bread and vegetables, have no sales tax or a lower rate, while restaurant meals, clothes and tools are taxed. The rules depend on where you live, so each question tells you what is taxed.

## Paying and getting change

When you pay cash, check your change. Change $=$ amount paid $-$ total.

:::example Paying with a \$50 bill
The total on the receipt above is \$39.36. You pay with a \$50 bill. How much change should you get?
@step $50.00 - 39.36 = 10.64$
@answer You should get \$10.64 in change.
:::

:::tip Count up to check
Cashiers often count up from the total: \$39.36 plus 4 cents is \$39.40, plus 60 cents is \$40, plus \$10 is \$50. The change is $0.04 + 0.60 + 10 = 10.64$ dollars.
:::

To compare two stores, find the total at each store. The cheaper store overall may not have the lowest price on every item.

:::check m204-total-cost 3
:::

:::summary
- Discount $=$ percent off $\times$ regular price. Sale price $=$ regular price $-$ discount.
- For "second item 50% off", only one item is half price.
- Two discounts in a row are not added together. Take the second one off the sale price.
- Subtotal $=$ the prices added up. GST $= 0.05 \times$ subtotal. Total $=$ subtotal $+$ GST.
- Change $=$ amount paid $-$ total.
:::
