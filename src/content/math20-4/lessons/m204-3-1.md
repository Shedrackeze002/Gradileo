Most first jobs pay by the hour. When payday comes, you should be able to check that your pay is right. All you need is a timecard, your hourly wage and a calculator.

## Gross pay from an hourly wage

If you are paid by the hour, every hour of work earns the same amount. To find your pay, multiply.

:::definition Hourly wage and gross pay
An **hourly wage** is the money you earn for each hour of work. **Gross pay** is the total you earn **before** anything is taken off.
$$\text{gross pay} = \text{hourly wage} \times \text{hours worked}$$
:::

:::example Pay for one week
Amara works as a cashier for \$16.50 per hour. Last week she worked 24 hours. What was her gross pay?
@step Gross pay $=$ hourly wage $\times$ hours worked
@step $16.50 \times 24 = 396.00$
@answer Amara’s gross pay was \$396.00.
:::

:::

## Reading a timecard

A **timecard** shows when each shift starts and ends. Meal breaks are often **unpaid**, so take them away.

:::definition Timecard
A **timecard** is a record of the times you start and finish work each day.
:::

To find the time between a start and an end, count up in easy steps. Then change any minutes to part of an hour.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Minutes","15 min","30 min","45 min"],"rows":[["Hours","0.25 h","0.5 h","0.75 h"]]}
:::

:::tip Count up across noon
From 9:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.: 9:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon is 3 h. Noon to 3:00 p.m. is 3 h more. Then 30 min more. That is 6 h 30 min, or 6.5 h.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Day","Start","End","Unpaid break"],"rows":[["Monday","9:00 a.m.","3:30 p.m.","none"],["Tuesday","12:00 p.m.","8:00 p.m.","30 min"],["Thursday","7:30 a.m.","12:30 p.m.","none"]]}
:::

:::example Pay from a timecard
Jordan earns \$17.20 per hour at a warehouse. Use the timecard above to find the gross pay.
@step Monday: 9:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. is 6 h 30 min $= 6.5\ \text{h}$.
@step Tuesday: 12:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. is 8 h. Take away the 30 min break: $7.5\ \text{h}$.
@step Thursday: 7:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. is $5\ \text{h}$.
@step Total hours: $6.5 + 7.5 + 5 = 19$
@step Gross pay: $19 \times 17.20 = 326.80$
@answer Jordan worked 19 hours and earned \$326.80.
:::

:::mistake 6 h 30 min is not 6.30 h
The digits after the decimal point are parts of an hour, not minutes. Half an hour is $0.5$, so 6 h 30 min is $6.5\ \text{h}$.
:::

:::check m204-gross-pay 2
:::

Some jobs pay more for weekend or evening shifts. Find the hours at each rate, pay them separately, then add.

:::example Two rates
Kai earns \$16.00 per hour on weekdays and \$17.50 per hour on weekends. This week Kai worked 18 weekday hours and 8 weekend hours.
@step Weekday pay: $18 \times 16.00 = 288.00$
@step Weekend pay: $8 \times 17.50 = 140.00$
@step Gross pay: $288.00 + 140.00 = 428.00$
@answer Kai earned \$428.00.
:::

:::check m204-gross-pay 3
:::

## Overtime

If you work extra hours, you may earn **overtime** pay. Your job tells you when overtime starts, and the rules are different from place to place. Some jobs count the hours over 8 in each day. Some count the hours over a set number in the week, such as 44. Some count both and pay for whichever number is bigger. In this course, each question tells you the rule to use.

:::definition Overtime
**Overtime** is time worked past the usual hours. It is often paid at **time-and-a-half**, which is $1.5$ times your regular wage. Some jobs pay **double time**, which is $2$ times your regular wage.
:::

:::example A long day
Rosa earns \$18.00 per hour. Hours over 8 in a day are paid at time-and-a-half. On Saturday she worked 11 hours.
@step Regular pay: $8 \times 18.00 = 144.00$
@step Overtime rate: $1.5 \times 18.00 = 27.00$. Overtime hours: $11 - 8 = 3$.
@step Overtime pay: $3 \times 27.00 = 81.00$
@step Gross pay: $144.00 + 81.00 = 225.00$
@answer Rosa earned \$225.00 for the day.
:::

## Overtime on a weekly timecard

When overtime is counted **by the day**, check each day on its own.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Day","Hours worked"],"rows":[["Monday","9"],["Tuesday","8"],["Wednesday","10.5"],["Thursday","7"],["Friday","8"]]}
:::

:::example Overtime day by day
Liam earns \$20.00 per hour. Hours over 8 in a day are paid at time-and-a-half. Use the timecard above.
@step Overtime: Monday $9 - 8 = 1$ and Wednesday $10.5 - 8 = 2.5$. That is $3.5$ overtime hours.
@step Regular hours: $8 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 8 = 39$, so regular pay is $39 \times 20.00 = 780.00$.
@step Overtime pay: $3.5 \times (1.5 \times 20.00) = 3.5 \times 30.00 = 105.00$
@step Gross pay: $780.00 + 105.00 = 885.00$
@answer Liam earned \$885.00 for the week.
:::

:::mistake Using only the weekly total
Liam worked 42.5 hours in all, which is less than a weekly limit of 44. But with a **daily** rule, the long days still earn overtime. Read the rule in the question carefully.
:::

:::check m204-overtime 3
:::

:::summary
- Gross pay $=$ hourly wage $\times$ hours worked.
- On a timecard, count up from start to end, take away unpaid breaks, and change minutes to hours (30 min $= 0.5$ h).
- Pay hours at different rates separately, then add.
- Time-and-a-half $= 1.5 \times$ wage. Double time $= 2 \times$ wage.
- Gross pay with overtime $=$ regular pay $+$ overtime pay.
:::
