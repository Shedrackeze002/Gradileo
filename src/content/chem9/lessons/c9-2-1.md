Salt melts at over 800 °C, but water ice melts at 0 °C. Both are compounds, so why are they so different? The answer is the way their atoms are held together: by ionic bonds or by covalent bonds.

## Two kinds of bond

:::definition Ionic and covalent bonding
**Ionic bonding** happens between a **metal** and a **non-metal**. The metal atoms **transfer** electrons to the non-metal atoms, making positive and negative ions that attract each other strongly.

**Covalent bonding** happens between **non-metal** atoms. The atoms **share** pairs of electrons. Each shared pair is one covalent bond.
:::

| Compound | Elements | Bonding |
|---|---|---|
| sodium chloride | metal + non-metal | ionic |
| magnesium oxide | metal + non-metal | ionic |
| water | non-metals only | covalent |
| carbon dioxide | non-metals only | covalent |
| methane | non-metals only | covalent |

:::example Making magnesium oxide
Magnesium (2, 8, 2) reacts with oxygen (2, 6). Describe what happens to the electrons.
@step Magnesium is a metal and oxygen a non-metal, so the bonding is ionic.
@step Each magnesium atom transfers its 2 outer electrons to an oxygen atom.
@answer Magnesium becomes $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ and oxygen becomes $\text{O}^{2-}$; the opposite charges attract.
:::

:::example Sharing in a water molecule
How are the atoms in a water molecule held together?
@step Hydrogen and oxygen are both non-metals.
@answer By covalent bonds: the oxygen atom shares a pair of electrons with each hydrogen atom.
:::

## Bonding and properties

:::definition Structures and properties
**Ionic compounds** form a **giant lattice** of ions held by strong forces in every direction. They have **high melting points**, are solids at room temperature, and **conduct electricity only when melted or dissolved**, when their ions can move.

**Small covalent molecules**, such as water and carbon dioxide, have strong bonds inside each molecule but only **weak forces between molecules**. They have **low melting and boiling points**, and they **do not conduct** electricity.
:::

| Property | Ionic (salt) | Small covalent molecules (water) |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | high (801 °C) | low (0 °C) |
| state at room temperature | solid | often liquid or gas |
| conducts when solid? | no | no |
| conducts when melted or dissolved? | yes | no |

:::mistake Solid salt does not conduct
In solid salt the ions are locked in place. They must be free to move, in a melt or a solution, to carry a current.
:::

:::example Identifying an unknown
A white solid melts at 770 °C and its solution conducts electricity. What kind of bonding does it have?
@step A high melting point points to a lattice held by strong forces.
@step Conducting in solution means there are ions that can move.
@answer Ionic bonding.
:::

:::summary
- **Ionic**: metal + non-metal; electrons are transferred, making ions that attract.
- **Covalent**: non-metals only; atoms share pairs of electrons.
- Ionic compounds have high melting points and conduct only when melted or dissolved.
- Small covalent molecules have low melting points and do not conduct.
:::
