Why is salt NaCl, but calcium chloride CaCl₂? An ionic compound has no overall charge, so its ions must come in just the right numbers for the charges to cancel. Once you know the charges, you can write the formula of any ionic compound.

## Balancing the charges

:::definition The formula of an ionic compound
The formula of an ionic compound shows the **ratio of ions** in it. The total positive charge must **equal** the total negative charge, so the compound is neutral. Use the **fewest** ions that make the charges cancel.
:::

:::example Calcium chloride
Calcium forms $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions and chlorine forms $\text{Cl}^{-}$ ions. What is the formula of calcium chloride?
@step One $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ has a charge of +2.
@step It takes two $\text{Cl}^{-}$ ions to give −2.
@answer $\ce{CaCl2}$
:::

:::example Aluminium oxide
What is the formula of aluminium oxide, made of $\text{Al}^{3+}$ and $\text{O}^{2-}$ ions?
@step The lowest common multiple of 3 and 2 is 6.
@step Two $\text{Al}^{3+}$ give +6, and three $\text{O}^{2-}$ give −6.
@answer $\ce{Al2O3}$
:::

:::tip The swap-and-drop shortcut
Write each charge number as the subscript of the other ion, then simplify: $\text{Al}^{3+}$ and $\text{O}^{2-}$ give $\ce{Al2O3}$. Always simplify: $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ and $\text{O}^{2-}$ give MgO, not Mg₂O₂.
:::

## Polyatomic ions

:::definition Polyatomic ion
A **polyatomic ion** is a group of atoms joined together that carries a charge as a whole.

| Ion | Formula | Charge |
|---|---|---|
| hydroxide | $\ce{OH-}$ | 1− |
| nitrate | $\ce{NO3-}$ | 1− |
| sulfate | $\ce{SO4^2-}$ | 2− |
| carbonate | $\ce{CO3^2-}$ | 2− |
| phosphate | $\ce{PO4^3-}$ | 3− |
| ammonium | $\ce{NH4+}$ | 1+ |
:::

Balance the charges in the same way. When you need **more than one** polyatomic ion, put it in **brackets**, with the number outside.

:::example Calcium hydroxide
What is the formula of calcium hydroxide, made of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\ce{OH-}$?
@step One $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ needs two $\ce{OH-}$ ions.
@step Two hydroxide ions are written $\ce{(OH)2}$.
@answer $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$
:::

:::mistake Never change the inside of a polyatomic ion
$\ce{CaOH2}$ would mean one oxygen and two hydrogens. The brackets show two whole hydroxide ions.
:::

:::summary
- In an ionic compound, total positive charge = total negative charge; use the fewest ions.
- Polyatomic ions (hydroxide, nitrate, sulfate, carbonate, phosphate, ammonium) are charged groups of atoms.
- Bracket a polyatomic ion when there is more than one: $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$, $\ce{Mg(NO3)2}$.
:::
