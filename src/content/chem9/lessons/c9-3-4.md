Some reactions are over in a flash, like a firework. Others take years, like rusting. Chemists can speed reactions up or slow them down, and collision theory explains how.

## What changes the rate

:::definition Rate of reaction
The **rate of reaction** is how fast reactants are used up or products are made. The mean rate is

$$\text{mean rate} = \dfrac{\text{amount of product made}}{\text{time taken}}$$
:::

Four factors increase the rate:

| Change | Effect on rate |
|---|---|
| higher **temperature** | faster |
| higher **concentration** of a solution (or pressure of a gas) | faster |
| larger **surface area** of a solid (smaller pieces, powder) | faster |
| adding a **catalyst** | faster |

:::definition Catalyst
A **catalyst** speeds up a reaction without being used up. It is still there, unchanged, at the end. Enzymes are the catalysts in living things.
:::

:::example Mean rate
A reaction gives off 60 cm³ of gas in 20 seconds. What is the mean rate?
@step Mean rate = gas made ÷ time.
@answer $\dfrac{60}{20} = 3$ cm³/s
:::

## Collision theory

:::definition Collision theory
Particles react only when they **collide** with **enough energy** (the activation energy). Anything that makes successful collisions **more frequent** makes the reaction **faster**.
:::

| Change | Why it is faster |
|---|---|
| higher temperature | particles move faster, so they collide more often and with more energy |
| higher concentration | more particles in the same volume, so more frequent collisions |
| larger surface area | more particles exposed, so more collisions at once |
| catalyst | gives an easier route that needs less energy |

:::example Powder or lump?
Why does powdered marble react with acid faster than a single lump?
@step The powder has a much larger surface area.
@answer More marble particles are exposed to the acid, so there are more collisions each second.
:::

:::mistake Hotter does not just mean more collisions
At a higher temperature, particles collide more often **and** more of the collisions have enough energy to react. Both make the rate rise.
:::

:::summary
- Rate is how fast products form; mean rate = amount of product ÷ time.
- Higher temperature, higher concentration, larger surface area and catalysts all increase the rate.
- Collision theory: particles must collide with enough energy; more frequent successful collisions mean a faster reaction.
:::
