Why is sodium so like potassium, and why do the noble gases refuse to react? The answer lies in how an atom's electrons are arranged in shells. Once you can write those arrangements, the layout of the periodic table makes sense.

## Filling the shells

:::definition Electron arrangement
Electrons occupy **shells** around the nucleus. For the first 20 elements:
- the **first** shell holds up to **2** electrons;
- the **second** shell holds up to **8**;
- the **third** shell holds up to **8** (for these elements);
- any remaining electrons go into the **fourth** shell.

Shells fill from the inside out. The **electron arrangement** lists the electrons in each shell, such as 2, 8, 1.
:::

:::example Writing an arrangement
Write the electron arrangement of aluminium (atomic number 13).
@step 13 electrons. The first shell takes 2, leaving 11.
@step The second shell takes 8, leaving 3.
@step The last 3 go into the third shell.
@answer 2, 8, 3
:::

| Element | Atomic number | Arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| helium | 2 | 2 |
| carbon | 6 | 2, 4 |
| neon | 10 | 2, 8 |
| sodium | 11 | 2, 8, 1 |
| chlorine | 17 | 2, 8, 7 |
| argon | 18 | 2, 8, 8 |
| calcium | 20 | 2, 8, 8, 2 |

:::mistake Fill the inner shells first
Do not put 3 electrons in the first shell, or more than 8 in the second. A quick check: the numbers must add up to the atomic number.

## Shells and the periodic table

The electron arrangement tells you where an element sits in the periodic table:

- The **number of occupied shells** is the **period**. Sodium (2, 8, 1) has three shells, so it is in period 3.
- The **number of outer electrons** gives the **group**: 1 outer electron means group 1, 2 means group 2, and 3 to 8 outer electrons mean groups 13 to 18.

:::example Finding the place
An element has the arrangement 2, 8, 6. Which group and period is it in?
@step Three shells, so period 3.
@step Six outer electrons, so group 16.
@answer Period 3, group 16. It is sulfur.
:::

:::definition Why groups behave alike
Chemical reactions involve the **outer electrons**. Elements in the same group have the same number of outer electrons, so they react in similar ways. The noble gases have **full outer shells**, which are very stable, so they hardly react at all.

:::summary
- Shells hold 2, then 8, then 8 electrons (for the first 20 elements), filling from the inside out.
- The number of shells gives the **period**; the number of outer electrons gives the **group**.
- Elements in a group have the same number of outer electrons, so they react alike; full outer shells make noble gases unreactive.
:::
