When wood burns, a big log turns into a small pile of ash. Has mass been destroyed? No: the missing mass has gone into the air as gases. In every chemical reaction, mass is conserved, and knowing this lets you work out masses you never measured.

## The law of conservation of mass

:::definition Conservation of mass
In a chemical reaction, **mass is not created or destroyed**. The total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants.

$$\text{total mass of reactants} = \text{total mass of products}$$
:::

This is because a reaction only **rearranges atoms** into new substances. Every atom that goes in comes out again.

:::example Iron sulfide
14 g of iron reacts completely with 8 g of sulfur. What mass of iron sulfide is made?
@step Mass of products = mass of reactants.
@answer $14 + 8 = 22$ g of iron sulfide.
:::

:::example Finding a missing mass
6 g of magnesium burns to make 10 g of magnesium oxide. What mass of oxygen reacted?
@step Mass of magnesium + mass of oxygen = mass of magnesium oxide.
@answer Oxygen = $10 - 6 = 4$ g.
:::

## Open and closed systems

:::definition Open and closed systems
In a **closed system**, such as a sealed flask, nothing can get in or out, so the measured mass **stays the same**.

In an **open system**, such as an open beaker, gases can escape or enter, so the measured mass can **seem** to change, even though mass is still conserved.
:::

| Reaction in an open container | Reading on the balance | Why |
|---|---|---|
| marble chips fizzing in acid | goes down | carbon dioxide escapes into the air |
| magnesium burning in a crucible | goes up | oxygen from the air joins the magnesium |
| the same reactions in a sealed flask | stays the same | no gas can enter or leave |

:::example A fizzing tablet
An antacid tablet is dropped into water in an open cup on a balance. The reading falls from 152.4 g to 151.6 g. Explain.
@step The tablet reacts to make carbon dioxide gas.
@step The gas escapes from the open cup.
@answer $152.4 - 151.6 = 0.8$ g of carbon dioxide escaped. Mass was conserved; it just left the cup.
:::

:::mistake Mass is never really lost
When a balance reading drops, mass has not been destroyed. It has moved somewhere you are not measuring.

:::summary
- Mass is conserved in every reaction: atoms are rearranged, not made or destroyed.
- Total mass of reactants = total mass of products.
- In an open container, gases escaping or joining make the mass seem to change; in a closed one it stays the same.
:::
