Atoms are far too small to weigh one at a time, but chemists can still compare their masses. Using relative atomic masses, you can work out how heavy a molecule is compared with others, and what fraction of a compound is made of each element.

## Relative formula mass

:::definition Relative atomic mass and relative formula mass
The **relative atomic mass** ($A_r$) of an element compares the mass of its atoms with other atoms. For example, $A_r$ of carbon is 12 and of hydrogen is 1, so a carbon atom is about 12 times as heavy as a hydrogen atom.

The **relative formula mass** ($M_r$) of a compound is the **sum** of the relative atomic masses of **all the atoms** in its formula.
:::

:::example Water
Find $M_r$ of water, $\ce{H2O}$. ($A_r$: H = 1, O = 16.)
@step Two hydrogen atoms: $2 \times 1 = 2$.
@step One oxygen atom: 16.
@answer $M_r = 2 + 16 = 18$
:::

:::example Calcium hydroxide
Find $M_r$ of $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$. ($A_r$: Ca = 40, O = 16, H = 1.)
@step The bracket doubles O and H: 2 O and 2 H.
@step $40 + (2 \times 16) + (2 \times 1) = 40 + 32 + 2$.
@answer $M_r = 74$
:::

:::mistake Count every atom
Multiply each $A_r$ by the number of atoms. In $\ce{CO2}$, oxygen counts twice: $12 + 2 \times 16 = 44$, not $12 + 16 = 28$.
:::

## Percentage by mass

:::definition Percentage by mass
The percentage of a compound's mass that comes from one element is

$$\% \text{ by mass} = \dfrac{A_r \times \text{number of atoms of the element}}{M_r} \times 100$$
:::

:::example Oxygen in carbon dioxide
What percentage of the mass of $\ce{CO2}$ is oxygen? ($A_r$: C = 12, O = 16.)
@step $M_r = 12 + 2 \times 16 = 44$.
@step Oxygen contributes $2 \times 16 = 32$.
@step $\dfrac{32}{44} \times 100 = 72.7$
@answer 72.7% (to 1 decimal place)
:::

This is useful in real life: a farmer comparing fertilizers wants the one with the highest percentage of nitrogen.

:::summary
- $M_r$ is the sum of the relative atomic masses of every atom in the formula.
- A number after a bracket multiplies everything inside it.
- % by mass = (mass of that element in the formula ÷ $M_r$) × 100.
:::
