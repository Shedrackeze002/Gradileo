There are millions of chemical reactions, but most of them follow a handful of patterns. Spotting the pattern lets you predict what a reaction will make, and the reactivity series tells you whether some reactions will happen at all.

## Four types of reaction

:::definition Reaction types
- **Synthesis**: two or more substances join to make one product. $\text{A} + \text{B} \rightarrow \text{AB}$
- **Decomposition**: one substance breaks down into two or more simpler ones, often when heated. $\text{AB} \rightarrow \text{A} + \text{B}$
- **Displacement**: a more reactive element takes the place of a less reactive one in a compound. $\text{A} + \text{BC} \rightarrow \text{AC} + \text{B}$
- **Combustion**: a fuel reacts with oxygen, giving out heat. Fuels containing carbon and hydrogen make carbon dioxide and water.
:::

| Reaction | Type |
|---|---|
| iron + sulfur → iron sulfide | synthesis |
| calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide | decomposition |
| zinc + copper sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper | displacement |
| methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water | combustion |

:::example Classifying a reaction
What type of reaction is $\ce{2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2}$?
@step One substance, hydrogen peroxide, breaks down into two.
@answer Decomposition.
:::

## The reactivity series

:::definition Reactivity series
The **reactivity series** lists metals from most reactive to least reactive:

potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, zinc, iron, lead, copper, silver, gold

A metal **displaces** another metal from its compound only if it is **higher** in the series.
:::

:::example Iron in copper sulfate
An iron nail is put into blue copper sulfate solution. What happens?
@step Iron is above copper, so iron displaces copper.
@step iron + copper sulfate → iron sulfate + copper
@answer A brown coating of copper forms on the nail, and the blue colour fades.
:::

:::example No reaction
Is there a reaction when copper is put into zinc sulfate solution?
@step Copper is below zinc in the series.
@answer No: a less reactive metal cannot displace a more reactive one.
:::

:::tip Gold and silver are found as metals
The least reactive metals, such as gold, are found in the ground as the pure metal, because they hardly react with anything.

:::summary
- Synthesis joins; decomposition breaks apart; displacement swaps a more reactive element in; combustion burns a fuel in oxygen.
- The reactivity series runs from potassium (most reactive) to gold (least).
- A metal displaces another from its compound only if it is higher in the series.
:::
