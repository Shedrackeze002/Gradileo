Lemon juice, vinegar and cola taste sour. Soap and oven cleaner feel slippery. These are two families of substances, acids and bases, and chemists use coloured dyes called indicators to tell them apart safely.

## Acids, bases and alkalis

:::definition Acids and bases
An **acid** is a substance that releases **hydrogen ions**, $\ce{H+}$, in water. Acids taste sour, turn blue litmus red, and have a pH below 7.

A **base** is a substance that **neutralizes** an acid. Bases taste bitter and many feel soapy.

An **alkali** is a base that **dissolves in water**. Alkalis release **hydroxide ions**, $\ce{OH-}$, and have a pH above 7.
:::

| Acids | Bases and alkalis |
|---|---|
| lemon juice, vinegar, cola | soap, baking soda, oven cleaner |
| hydrochloric acid ($\ce{HCl}$) | sodium hydroxide ($\ce{NaOH}$), an alkali |
| sulfuric acid ($\ce{H2SO4}$) | calcium hydroxide (limewater) |
| nitric acid ($\ce{HNO3}$) | copper oxide, a base that does not dissolve |

:::mistake Never taste or touch to test
Strong acids and alkalis are corrosive: they damage skin and eyes. Use indicators, and wear eye protection.
:::

:::example Base or alkali?
Copper oxide neutralizes acids but does not dissolve in water. Is it a base, an alkali, or both?
@step It neutralizes acids, so it is a base.
@step It does not dissolve, so it is not an alkali.
@answer A base, but not an alkali.
:::

## Indicators

:::definition Indicator
An **indicator** is a dye that has **different colours** in acids and alkalis.
:::

| Indicator | In acid | Neutral | In alkali |
|---|---|---|---|
| blue litmus | red | blue | blue |
| red litmus | red | red | blue |
| phenolphthalein | colourless | colourless | pink |
| universal indicator | red, orange or yellow | green | blue or purple |

Litmus only tells you acid or alkali. **Universal indicator** is a mixture of dyes whose colour shows **how** acidic or alkaline a solution is.

:::example Two tests
A solution turns red litmus blue and phenolphthalein pink. What is it?
@step Both changes happen only in alkalis.
@answer An alkali.
:::

:::tip Red cabbage
Juice from boiled red cabbage is a natural indicator: red in acids, purple when neutral, and green or yellow in alkalis.

:::summary
- Acids release $\ce{H+}$ ions, taste sour and turn blue litmus red.
- Bases neutralize acids; alkalis are soluble bases that release $\ce{OH-}$ ions.
- Litmus: red in acid, blue in alkali. Phenolphthalein: pink in alkali. Universal indicator shows how strong.
:::
