Add an alkali to an acid a little at a time, and something remarkable happens: the acid's sourness and the alkali's soapiness both disappear. The two cancel out, and what is left is a salt and water. This reaction, neutralization, is at work in your stomach, on farms and in your toothpaste.

## Neutralization

:::definition Neutralization
**Neutralization** is the reaction of an **acid** with a **base**. It makes a **salt** and **water**.

$$\text{acid} + \text{base} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water}$$

The hydrogen ions from the acid and the hydroxide ions from an alkali join to make water: $\ce{H+ + OH- -> H2O}$.
:::

The salt takes its **first name from the metal in the base** and its **second name from the acid**:

| Acid | Makes salts called | Example |
|---|---|---|
| hydrochloric acid | chlorides | sodium chloride |
| sulfuric acid | sulfates | copper sulfate |
| nitric acid | nitrates | potassium nitrate |

:::example Naming the salt
Sulfuric acid is neutralized by magnesium oxide. Write the word equation.
@step The metal is magnesium; sulfuric acid gives sulfates.
@answer sulfuric acid + magnesium oxide → magnesium sulfate + water
:::

:::mistake Salt does not only mean table salt
In chemistry, a **salt** is any compound made when an acid is neutralized. Table salt, sodium chloride, is just one of them.
:::

## Neutralization in everyday life

| Problem | Neutralized with |
|---|---|
| indigestion (too much stomach acid) | antacid tablets containing a base, such as magnesium hydroxide |
| acidic soil | lime (calcium hydroxide or calcium carbonate) |
| bee sting (acidic) | baking soda, a weak base |
| wasp sting (alkaline) | vinegar, a weak acid |
| acids made by bacteria on teeth | slightly alkaline toothpaste |

:::example Following the pH
Acid is added slowly to an alkali containing universal indicator. How does the pH change?
@step At first the alkali is in excess: pH above 7, blue or purple.
@step When exactly enough acid has been added, the solution is neutral: pH 7, green.
@answer The pH falls to 7, and below 7 if more acid is added.
:::

:::summary
- Neutralization: acid + base → salt + water; $\ce{H+}$ and $\ce{OH-}$ make water.
- Hydrochloric acid makes chlorides, sulfuric acid sulfates, nitric acid nitrates; the metal comes from the base.
- Antacids, lime for soil and remedies for stings all use neutralization.
:::
