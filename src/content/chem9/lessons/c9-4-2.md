"Mildly acidic" and "strongly alkaline" are useful words, but chemists want a number. The pH scale gives every solution a number that says how acidic or alkaline it is, from battery acid to drain cleaner.

## The pH scale

:::definition pH
The **pH scale** measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is. It usually runs from **0 to 14**.
- **pH below 7**: acidic. The lower the pH, the more strongly acidic.
- **pH 7**: neutral.
- **pH above 7**: alkaline. The higher the pH, the more strongly alkaline.
:::

| pH | Example | Universal indicator |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | stomach acid | red |
| 3 | vinegar, cola | orange |
| 5 | black coffee | yellow |
| 7 | pure water | green |
| 9 | baking soda solution | blue |
| 11 | household ammonia | dark blue |
| 13 | oven cleaner | purple |

pH can be measured with universal indicator and a colour chart, or more precisely with an electronic **pH meter**.

:::example Reading pH
A shampoo has a pH of 6. Is it acidic, neutral or alkaline?
@step 6 is below 7.
@answer Slightly acidic.
:::

## Comparing pH

:::definition A tenfold scale
Each step of **1** on the pH scale is a **tenfold** change. A solution of pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than one of pH 4, and $10 \times 10 = 100$ times more acidic than one of pH 5.
:::

:::example How many times?
Lemon juice has a pH of 2 and tomato juice a pH of 4. How many times more acidic is lemon juice?
@step The difference is $4 - 2 = 2$ steps.
@answer $10^2 = 100$ times more acidic.
:::

:::mistake A lower number means more acidic
pH 2 is more acidic than pH 5. It is easy to get this backwards.
