From the top of a cliff, a lookout or a grain elevator, you can measure distances you could never reach with a tape. Many of these jobs use **two** right triangles that share a side. The key skill is spotting the shared side and deciding which triangle to solve first.

## Angles of elevation and depression

:::definition Angle of elevation and angle of depression
- The **angle of elevation** is measured **up** from the horizontal to your line of sight.
- The **angle of depression** is measured **down** from the horizontal to your line of sight.
:::

The horizontal line at the top and the ground are parallel, so the angle of depression from the top **equals** the angle of elevation from the bottom (they are alternate angles). That lets you put the angle inside the triangle, at the bottom.

:::example One triangle first
From the top of a $28\ \text{m}$ grain elevator, a worker sees a truck at an angle of depression of $31^\circ$. How far is the truck from the base of the elevator? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step The angle at the truck is also $31^\circ$. The height is opposite it and the distance $d$ is adjacent: TOA.
@step $\tan 31^\circ = \dfrac{28}{d}$, so $d = \dfrac{28}{\tan 31^\circ}$
@answer $d \approx 46.6\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake Measuring from the wall
An angle of depression is measured from the **horizontal**, not from the wall or cliff face. Putting the $31^\circ$ between the line of sight and the wall gives the wrong triangle.
:::

## Two triangles that share a side

Use this plan for every two-triangle problem:

1. **Sketch** both triangles and mark the right angles.
2. **Find the shared side.** Is it a height, a ground distance or a ladder?
3. **Solve one triangle at a time**, using SOH CAH TOA or the Pythagorean theorem.
4. **Combine**: decide whether to add or subtract the pieces.

:::example Opposite sides of a tower
Two survey technicians stand on opposite sides of a $45\ \text{m}$ wind turbine tower, in line with its base. The angles of elevation to the top are $38^\circ$ and $52^\circ$. How far apart are they, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step The shared side is the tower. In each triangle it is opposite the angle, and the ground distance is adjacent: TOA.
@step Left: $d_1 = \dfrac{45}{\tan 38^\circ} \approx 57.60\ \text{m}$
@step Right: $d_2 = \dfrac{45}{\tan 52^\circ} \approx 35.16\ \text{m}$
@answer The technicians are on opposite sides, so add: $x \approx 57.60 + 35.16 \approx 92.8\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::example A ladder in an alley
The foot of a $6\ \text{m}$ ladder stands between two buildings. Leaned on one wall it makes a $72^\circ$ angle with the ground; swung to the other wall it makes a $64^\circ$ angle. How wide is the alley?
@step The shared side is the ladder, the hypotenuse of both triangles. Each ground distance is adjacent: CAH.
@step $d_1 = 6\cos 72^\circ \approx 1.85\ \text{m}$ and $d_2 = 6\cos 64^\circ \approx 2.63\ \text{m}$
@answer Add: the alley is about $1.85 + 2.63 \approx 4.5\ \text{m}$ wide.
:::

## Adding or subtracting

When both triangles are on the **same side**, one triangle sits inside the other. Then you usually **subtract**.

:::figure
{"type":"twoTriangles","arrangement":"same","labels":{"h":"50 m","a1":"40°","a2":"18°","x":"x"}}
:::

:::example Two boats from a cliff
From the top of a $50\ \text{m}$ cliff above a mountain lake, the angles of depression to two boats in line are $40^\circ$ and $18^\circ$. How far apart are the boats?
@step Each angle of depression equals the angle at that boat. The shared side is the cliff, $50\ \text{m}$.
@step Near boat: $\dfrac{50}{\tan 40^\circ} \approx 59.59\ \text{m}$. Far boat: $\dfrac{50}{\tan 18^\circ} \approx 153.88\ \text{m}$
@answer Subtract: the boats are about $153.88 - 59.59 \approx 94.3\ \text{m}$ apart.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"twoTriangles","arrangement":"stacked","labels":{"d":"40 m","a1":"30°","a2":"38°","x":"x"}}
:::

:::example An antenna on a roof
From $40\ \text{m}$ away, the angle of elevation to the bottom of a rooftop antenna is $30^\circ$ and to its top is $38^\circ$. How tall is the antenna?
@step The shared side is the ground distance, $40\ \text{m}$, adjacent to both angles: TOA.
@step To the top: $40\tan 38^\circ \approx 31.25\ \text{m}$. To the roof: $40\tan 30^\circ \approx 23.09\ \text{m}$
@answer Subtract: the antenna is about $31.25 - 23.09 \approx 8.2\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

:::tip Keep the digits
Carry at least two decimal places (better, every digit on your calculator) through the middle steps. Round only the final answer.
:::

## Find the shared side first

Sometimes the shared side is **not given**. Look for the triangle where you know an angle **and** a side, solve it for the shared side, then move to the other triangle.

:::example A buoy and a boat
A buoy floats $90\ \text{m}$ from the base of a cliff. From the top of the cliff, the angle of depression to the buoy is $35^\circ$ and to a boat farther out, in line with it, is $20^\circ$. How far is the boat from the buoy, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step The shared side is the cliff height $h$, which is not given. The buoy's triangle has an angle and a side, so start there.
@step Buoy triangle (TOA): $h = 90\tan 35^\circ \approx 63.02\ \text{m}$
@step Boat triangle (TOA): $d = \dfrac{h}{\tan 20^\circ} \approx \dfrac{63.02}{\tan 20^\circ} \approx 173.14\ \text{m}$
@answer The boat is about $173.14 - 90 \approx 83.1\ \text{m}$ from the buoy.
:::

:::example A second guy wire
A $30\ \text{m}$ guy wire runs from the top of a tower to an anchor, at $60^\circ$ to the ground. A second wire runs from the same anchor to a point $6\ \text{m}$ below the top. How long is the second wire?
@step First wire: the tower height is $h = 30\sin 60^\circ \approx 25.98\ \text{m}$ and the shared ground distance is $d = 30\cos 60^\circ = 15\ \text{m}$.
@step The second wire is attached $25.98 - 6 = 19.98\ \text{m}$ up.
@answer Pythagorean theorem: $\sqrt{15^2 + 19.98^2} \approx 25.0\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::summary
- Angles of elevation and depression are measured from the horizontal. The angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the bottom.
- Sketch both triangles and find the side they share: a height, a ground distance or a hypotenuse.
- Solve one triangle at a time, then add (opposite sides) or subtract (one triangle inside the other).
- If the shared side is not given, find it first from the triangle where you know an angle and a side.
- Keep all the digits until the final answer.
:::
