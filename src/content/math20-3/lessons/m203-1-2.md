How fast does a generator burn fuel? How much does each extra kilometre of a rental cost? Both questions ask for a **rate of change**, and a rate of change is just a slope with units.

## Rate of change from a table

:::definition Rate of change
A **rate of change** compares how much one quantity changes for each unit of change in another:
$$\text{rate of change} = \dfrac{\text{change in output}}{\text{change in input}}$$
Its units are "output units per input unit", such as litres per hour or dollars per kilometre.
:::

To find a rate of change from a table, pick two rows, subtract, and divide. Subtract in the **same order** on the top and the bottom.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Time running (h)","Fuel in tank (L)"],"rows":[["0","120"],["2","111"],["5","97.5"],["8","84"]]}
:::

:::example Fuel in a generator
A diesel generator powers a remote work camp. Use the table to find the rate of change of the fuel in its tank.
@step Use the rows for $0$ h and $2$ h: $\dfrac{111 - 120}{2 - 0} = \dfrac{-9}{2} = -4.5$
@step Check with two other rows: $\dfrac{84 - 97.5}{8 - 5} = \dfrac{-13.5}{3} = -4.5$. The rate is the same, so it is constant.
@answer The rate of change is $-4.5\ \text{L/h}$: the tank loses $4.5$ litres every hour.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting in different orders
If you subtract "later minus earlier" on top, you must do the same on the bottom. $\dfrac{111 - 120}{0 - 2}$ gives $+4.5$, which wrongly says the fuel is going **up**. A quantity that goes down has a **negative** rate of change.
:::

## What the rate means

A rate of change always comes with units, and the units tell you what it means.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Distance driven (km)","Total cost (\\$)"],"rows":[["100","65.00"],["250","98.00"],["400","131.00"]]}
:::

:::example Renting a cargo van
Priya rents a cargo van. The table shows the total cost for different distances. Find the rate of change, explain what it means, and find the cost of driving $320\ \text{km}$.
@step Rate: $\dfrac{98.00 - 65.00}{250 - 100} = \dfrac{33.00}{150} = 0.22$ dollars per kilometre.
@step Meaning: each kilometre driven adds \$0.22 to the cost.
@step Fixed part of the cost: $65.00 - 0.22 \times 100 = 43.00$, so the van costs \$43.00 before any driving.
@step For $320\ \text{km}$: $43.00 + 0.22 \times 320 = 43.00 + 70.40 = 113.40$
@answer The rate is \$0.22 per kilometre, and $320\ \text{km}$ costs \$113.40.
:::

:::tip Read the units aloud
"Dollars per kilometre" means "dollars for each kilometre". If your sentence does not make sense with the units, check whether you divided the right way.
:::

## The slope of a line graph

On a graph, the rate of change is the **slope** of the line. Pick two points where the line crosses grid lines exactly, then find rise over run using the scales on the axes.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":12,"yMin":0,"yMax":500,"xStep":1,"yStep":50,"xLabel":"Time (min)","yLabel":"Water in tank (L)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,480],[12,0]]}],"points":[{"x":2,"y":400,"label":"(2, 400)"},{"x":8,"y":160,"label":"(8, 160)"}]}
:::

:::example Draining a stock tank
The graph shows the water left in a stock tank on a farm as it drains. Find the rate of change.
@step Use the marked points $(2, 400)$ and $(8, 160)$.
@step $\text{slope} = \dfrac{160 - 400}{8 - 2} = \dfrac{-240}{6} = -40$
@answer The rate of change is $-40\ \text{L/min}$: the tank loses $40$ litres each minute.
:::

:::mistake Counting grid squares
The two axes often have different scales. Here each square across is $1$ minute but each square up is $50$ litres. Always use the numbers on the axes, not the number of squares.
:::

## Graphs with several pieces

Many real graphs are made of straight pieces. Each piece has its own rate of change.

- A **steeper** piece means a **faster** change.
- A piece that goes **down** has a **negative** rate.
- A **flat** piece means **no change**: the rate is $0$.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":7,"yMin":0,"yMax":200,"xStep":1,"yStep":20,"xLabel":"Time (h)","yLabel":"Distance from warehouse (km)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,0],[2,180],[3,180],[6,0]]}],"points":[{"x":0,"y":0},{"x":2,"y":180},{"x":3,"y":180},{"x":6,"y":0}],"segmentLabels":["A","B","C"]}
:::

:::example A delivery truck
The graph shows a truck's distance from its warehouse. Find the rate of change for each piece, and say when the truck was moving fastest.
@step Piece A: $\dfrac{180 - 0}{2 - 0} = 90\ \text{km/h}$ (driving away)
@step Piece B: $\dfrac{180 - 180}{3 - 2} = 0\ \text{km/h}$ (stopped while it is unloaded)
@step Piece C: $\dfrac{0 - 180}{6 - 3} = -60\ \text{km/h}$ (driving back)
@answer The truck moved fastest during piece A, at $90\ \text{km/h}$.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Rate of change $= \dfrac{\text{change in output}}{\text{change in input}}$, with units such as L/h or \$/km.
- Subtract in the same order on the top and the bottom. A decreasing quantity has a negative rate.
- The slope of a line graph is its rate of change. Use points on grid lines and the scales on the axes.
- Steeper means faster; flat means no change.
- Use the rate to make predictions beyond the table or graph.
:::
