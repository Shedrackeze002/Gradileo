Every ramp, roof, road and staircase has a steepness. Builders, roofers and road crews describe it with one number, the **slope**, and building codes set limits on it. In this lesson you will find slopes and use them to plan real jobs.

## Rise over run

:::definition Slope, rise and run
The **rise** is the vertical change. The **run** is the horizontal change. The **slope** compares them:
$$\text{slope} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$$
:::

A bigger slope means a steeper surface. A slope of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ climbs $1$ unit for every $2$ units across, which is much steeper than a slope of $\dfrac{1}{12}$.

:::example A wheelchair ramp
A wheelchair ramp at a clinic rises $45\ \text{cm}$ over a horizontal run of $540\ \text{cm}$. What is its slope?
@step $\text{slope} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \dfrac{45}{540}$
@step Divide the top and the bottom by $45$: $\dfrac{45 \div 45}{540 \div 45} = \dfrac{1}{12}$
@answer The slope is $\dfrac{1}{12}$: the ramp rises $1$ cm for every $12$ cm of run.
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
The rise and the run must be in the **same unit** before you divide. A ramp that rises $30\ \text{cm}$ over $3.6\ \text{m}$ has slope $\dfrac{30}{360} = \dfrac{1}{12}$, not $\dfrac{30}{3.6}$. Change $3.6\ \text{m}$ to $360\ \text{cm}$ first.
:::

## Finding a missing rise or run

If you know the slope and one of the two lengths, rearrange the formula:

$$\text{rise} = \text{slope} \times \text{run} \qquad\qquad \text{run} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{slope}}$$

:::example How high does the roof rise?
A garage roof has a slope of $\dfrac{5}{12}$. Each rafter covers a horizontal run of $14\ \text{ft}$. How high does the roof rise? Round to the nearest tenth of a foot.
@step $\text{rise} = \text{slope} \times \text{run} = \dfrac{5}{12} \times 14$
@step $= \dfrac{70}{12} \approx 5.833$
@answer The roof rises about $5.8\ \text{ft}$.
:::

:::example How long must the ramp be?
Suppose the building code allows a ramp slope of at most $\dfrac{1}{12}$. A door is $54\ \text{cm}$ above the sidewalk. What is the shortest run the ramp can have?
@step A slope of $\dfrac{1}{12}$ means the run is $12$ times the rise.
@step $\text{run} = 54 \times 12 = 648\ \text{cm}$
@answer The ramp needs a run of at least $648\ \text{cm}$, or $6.48\ \text{m}$.
:::

Stairs work the same way. For one step, the **riser** is the rise and the **tread** is the run.

## Percent grade

Road signs and trail maps give slope as a percent.

:::definition Percent grade
The **grade** of a road or path is its slope written as a percent:
$$\text{grade} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} \times 100\%$$
:::

:::example A highway grade
A section of highway climbs $42\ \text{m}$ over a horizontal distance of $700\ \text{m}$. What is its grade?
@step $\text{slope} = \dfrac{42}{700} = 0.06$
@step $0.06 \times 100\% = 6\%$
@answer The highway has a $6\%$ grade.
:::

:::example Working backwards from a grade
A sign warns of an $8\%$ grade for the next $2.5\ \text{km}$, measured horizontally. How far does the road drop?
@step An $8\%$ grade is a slope of $0.08$. Change the run to metres: $2.5\ \text{km} = 2500\ \text{m}$.
@step $\text{drop} = 0.08 \times 2500 = 200$
@answer The road drops $200\ \text{m}$.
:::

## Roof pitch

:::definition Pitch
Roofers give the **pitch** of a roof as the rise for every $12$ units of run. A **6/12 pitch** rises $6$ in for every $12$ in (one foot) of run, so its slope is $\dfrac{6}{12} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

:::example Measuring a pitch
A farmhouse roof rises $4$ ft $6$ in over a horizontal run of $9\ \text{ft}$. What is its pitch?
@step Change the rise to inches: $4 \times 12 + 6 = 54\ \text{in}$
@step Rise for each foot of run: $54 \div 9 = 6\ \text{in}$
@answer The roof rises $6$ in for every $12$ in of run: a 6/12 pitch.
:::

:::example Using a pitch
A house has a 7/12 pitch, and each rafter covers a run of $15\ \text{ft}$. How much does the roof rise?
@step The roof rises $7$ in for each foot of run.
@step $15 \times 7 = 105\ \text{in}$
@answer The roof rises $105$ in, which is $8$ ft $9$ in.
:::

:::tip Compare slopes as decimals
To compare slopes written in different forms, change each one to a decimal. A 5/12 pitch is $5 \div 12 \approx 0.417$, a $40\%$ grade is $0.40$, and a slope of $\dfrac{3}{8}$ is $0.375$. The roof is the steepest.
:::

:::summary
- $\text{slope} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$, with the rise and run in the same unit.
- Rearrange to find a missing length: $\text{rise} = \text{slope} \times \text{run}$ and $\text{run} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{slope}}$.
- Percent grade is the slope times $100\%$: a slope of $0.06$ is a $6\%$ grade.
- Pitch is the rise for every $12$ units of run: a 6/12 pitch is a slope of $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- To compare slopes, change them all to decimals.
:::
