Interest is the price of using someone else’s money. When you save, the bank pays you interest for using your money. When you borrow, you pay interest for using theirs. How that interest is worked out makes a big difference over time.

## Simple interest

:::definition Principal, interest rate and simple interest
The **principal** $P$ is the amount saved or borrowed. The **interest rate** $r$ is the percent charged or paid per year. **Simple interest** is paid on the principal only:
$$I = Prt$$
where $r$ is written as a decimal and $t$ is the time in **years**. The total amount at the end is $A = P + I$.
:::

:::example A GIC with simple interest
Priya puts \$2,500 from her summer job on a farm into a GIC (guaranteed investment certificate) that pays 3.5% simple interest per year. How much interest does it earn in 4 years, and what is it worth then?
@step Rate as a decimal: $3.5\% = 0.035$
@step $I = Prt = 2{,}500 \times 0.035 \times 4 = 350$
@step $A = 2{,}500 + 350 = 2{,}850$
@answer The GIC earns \$350.00 and is then worth \$2,850.00.
:::

:::mistake Using the percent instead of the decimal
In the formula, the rate must be a decimal: $3.5\% = 3.5 \div 100 = 0.035$. Using $3.5$ gives $2{,}500 \times 3.5 \times 4 = 35{,}000$, which would mean the GIC earned fourteen times what was put in. If an interest answer looks huge, check the rate.
:::

## Time in months

The rate is **per year**, so the time must be in years too. Change months to years by dividing by 12.

:::example A short loan
Tyler borrows \$1,800 for 9 months to take a welding course. The loan charges 7.5% simple interest per year. How much does he pay back?
@step Time in years: $t = \dfrac{9}{12} = 0.75$
@step $I = 1{,}800 \times 0.075 \times 0.75 = 101.25$
@step $A = 1{,}800 + 101.25 = 1{,}901.25$
@answer Tyler pays back \$1,901.25, including \$101.25 in interest.
:::

:::mistake Mixing months and years
Putting $t = 9$ into $I = Prt$ treats 9 months as 9 **years** and gives $1{,}800 \times 0.075 \times 9 = 1{,}215$ in interest, twelve times too much. Always match the time to the rate: a yearly rate needs the time in years.
:::

:::check m203-simple-interest 2
:::

## Rearranging the formula

If you know three of $I$, $P$, $r$ and $t$, you can find the fourth. Divide both sides of $I = Prt$ by the two values you know:
$$r = \dfrac{I}{Pt} \qquad t = \dfrac{I}{Pr} \qquad P = \dfrac{I}{rt}$$

:::example Finding the rate
A \$3,000 deposit earned \$315 in simple interest over 3 years. What was the rate?
@step $r = \dfrac{I}{Pt} = \dfrac{315}{3{,}000 \times 3} = \dfrac{315}{9{,}000} = 0.035$
@step As a percent: $0.035 \times 100 = 3.5\%$
@answer The rate was 3.5% per year.
:::

:::example Finding the time
Wyatt borrowed \$2,400 from his aunt at 5% simple interest per year and paid \$150 in interest. How many months did he have the loan?
@step $t = \dfrac{I}{Pr} = \dfrac{150}{2{,}400 \times 0.05} = \dfrac{150}{120} = 1.25$ years
@step In months: $1.25 \times 12 = 15$
@answer Wyatt had the loan for 15 months.
:::

:::check m203-simple-interest 3
:::

## Compound interest

With **compound interest**, the interest is added to the balance, and the next period earns interest on the new, bigger balance. You earn interest on your interest.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Year","Balance at start","Interest at 5%","Balance at end"],"rows":[["1","$1,000.00","$50.00","$1,050.00"],["2","$1,050.00","$52.50","$1,102.50"],["3","$1,102.50","$55.13","$1,157.63"]]}
:::

The interest grows every year: \$50.00, then \$52.50, then \$55.13. With simple interest it would stay at \$50.00.

:::definition Compound interest formula
$$A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$
$A$ is the final amount, $P$ the principal, $r$ the yearly rate as a decimal, $t$ the time in years, and $n$ the number of **compounding periods** per year: $n = 1$ (annually), $2$ (semi-annually), $4$ (quarterly) or $12$ (monthly).
:::

Check the table with the formula: $1{,}000(1 + 0.05)^3 = 1{,}157.625 \approx 1{,}157.63$.

:::example Compounded monthly
Dakota puts \$4,000 into a term deposit at Riverbend Savings that earns 4.8% per year, compounded monthly. What is it worth after 5 years, and how much interest is that?
@step $P = 4{,}000$, $r = 0.048$, $n = 12$, $t = 5$, so $nt = 60$.
@step $A = 4{,}000\left(1 + \dfrac{0.048}{12}\right)^{60} = 4{,}000(1.004)^{60} \approx 5{,}082.56$
@step Interest: $5{,}082.56 - 4{,}000 = 1{,}082.56$
@answer The deposit is worth \$5,082.56, including \$1,082.56 in interest.
:::

:::tip Calculator keystrokes
Type it in one line: `4000 × (1 + 0.048 ÷ 12) ^ (12 × 5)`. Do not round $\frac{r}{n}$ along the way. At 5% compounded monthly, $\frac{0.05}{12} = 0.004166\ldots$; rounding it to $0.0042$ would make the 5-year amount on \$4,000 about \$10 too high. Round only the final amount to the cent.
:::

## Simple and compound interest compared

At first the two kinds of interest are close. Over many years, compound interest pulls far ahead, because each year’s interest earns interest of its own.

:::example Ten years at 4%
Compare \$5,000 for 10 years at 4% simple interest and at 4% compounded annually.
@step Simple: $5{,}000 + 5{,}000 \times 0.04 \times 10 = 7{,}000$
@step Compound: $5{,}000(1 + 0.04)^{10} \approx 7{,}401.22$
@step Difference: $7{,}401.22 - 7{,}000 = 401.22$
@answer Compound interest earns \$401.22 more over 10 years.
:::

:::summary
- Simple interest: $I = Prt$ and $A = P + I$, with $r$ as a decimal and $t$ in years.
- Change months to years by dividing by 12. Rearrange $I = Prt$ to find $P$, $r$ or $t$.
- Compound interest: $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$, where $n$ is 1, 2, 4 or 12 for annual, semi-annual, quarterly or monthly compounding.
- Compound interest earns interest on interest, so it grows faster than simple interest, and more often compounding earns a little more.
- Keep every digit on the calculator and round only the final answer to the cent.
:::
