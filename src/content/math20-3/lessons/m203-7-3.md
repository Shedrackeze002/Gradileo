Credit lets you buy a truck today and pay for it over five years, or put a new laptop on a card and pay next month. It is useful, but it is almost never free. Knowing how to work out the real cost helps you choose the cheapest way to borrow.

## How credit cards charge interest

:::definition Credit, credit limit and finance charge
**Credit** is money you borrow now and pay back later. A credit card has a **credit limit**, the most you can owe at one time. A **finance charge** is the cost of using credit: interest plus any fees.
:::

Most cards give you an interest-free period: if you pay the **whole** statement balance by the due date, you pay no interest on purchases. If you leave part of it unpaid, the card charges interest every day on what you owe. Each card has its own rules, so every question tells you the rule to use.

$$\text{interest} = \text{balance} \times \text{yearly rate (as a decimal)} \div 365 \times \text{number of days}$$

:::example Interest for one month
Cole has an unpaid balance of \$850 on a card that charges 19.99% per year, charged daily. How much interest is charged for 30 days?
@step Rate as a decimal: $19.99\% = 0.1999$
@step Interest: $850 \times 0.1999 \div 365 \times 30 \approx 13.97$
@answer Cole is charged \$13.97 in interest for the month.
:::

:::mistake Using 19.99 instead of 0.1999
Using the percent gives $850 \times 19.99 \div 365 \times 30 \approx 1{,}396.56$, more than the balance itself. Divide the percent by 100 first: $19.99\% = 0.1999$.
:::

## The statement: new balance and minimum payment

Each month’s statement shows the new balance and a **minimum payment**, the least you must pay by the due date. Paying only the minimum keeps the account in good standing, but the rest of the balance keeps collecting interest.

:::example Next month’s statement
Mei’s statement balance was \$1,240.00 and she paid \$400.00. For this example, interest is charged on the unpaid amount only, at 20.99% per year for 30 days. She also made \$186.45 in new purchases. The minimum payment is 3% of the new balance or \$10, whichever is greater. Find the new balance and the minimum payment.
@step Unpaid amount: $1{,}240.00 - 400.00 = 840.00$
@step Interest: $840.00 \times 0.2099 \div 365 \times 30 \approx 14.49$
@step New balance: $840.00 + 14.49 + 186.45 = 1{,}040.94$
@step Minimum payment: $1{,}040.94 \times 0.03 \approx 31.23$, which is more than \$10.
@answer The new balance is \$1,040.94 and the minimum payment is \$31.23.
:::

:::tip Cash advances cost more
Taking cash from an ATM with a credit card usually has a fee, and interest starts the same day, with no interest-free period. It is one of the most expensive ways to borrow.
:::

:::

## The cost of carrying a balance

When you carry a balance, each month’s interest is added to what you owe, and the next month charges interest on that interest too.

:::example Two months on the card
Jamal buys a \$1,200 laptop on a card that charges 19.99% per year, charged daily, and makes no payments for two months (31 days, then 30 days).
@step Month 1: $1{,}200 \times 0.1999 \div 365 \times 31 \approx 20.37$, so he owes $1{,}220.37$.
@step Month 2: $1{,}220.37 \times 0.1999 \div 365 \times 30 \approx 20.05$, so he owes $1{,}240.42$.
@step Cost: $20.37 + 20.05 = 40.42$
@answer Jamal owes \$1,240.42. Waiting two months cost him \$40.42.
:::

If you often carry a balance, a low-rate card with an annual fee can be cheaper. For an average balance of \$1,500 for a year, a card at 19.99% with no fee costs about $1{,}500 \times 0.1999 = 299.85$ dollars, while a card at 12.99% with a \$49 fee costs about $1{,}500 \times 0.1299 + 49 = 243.85$ dollars.

## Loans: total paid and finance charge

A **loan** is paid back in equal payments over a set time, called the **term**. The lender tells you the payment; you can work out what the loan really costs.

:::definition Cost of a loan
$$\text{total paid} = \text{payment} \times \text{number of payments}$$
$$\text{finance charge} = \text{total paid} - \text{amount borrowed}$$
:::

:::example A truck loan
Simran borrows \$24,000 from Prairie Credit Union for a truck. The payments are \$480.80 a month for 5 years. What does the loan cost?
@step Number of payments: $5 \times 12 = 60$
@step Total paid: $480.80 \times 60 = 28{,}848.00$
@step Finance charge: $28{,}848.00 - 24{,}000 = 4{,}848.00$
@answer Simran pays \$28,848.00 in all, so the loan costs \$4,848.00.
:::

:::mistake Mixing months and years
Monthly payments for 5 years means $5 \times 12 = 60$ payments, not 5. Using 5 gives a total of \$2,404.00, far less than the \$24,000 borrowed. Check that the total paid is **more** than the amount borrowed.
:::

## Comparing credit options

A lower monthly payment often means more payments, and a bigger total cost. Always compare the totals.

:::example Dealer or bank?
Marcus needs \$20,000 for a truck. The dealer offers 84 monthly payments of \$311.63. Northern Bank offers 60 monthly payments of \$391.23. Which costs less?
@step Dealer: $311.63 \times 84 = 26{,}176.92$
@step Bank: $391.23 \times 60 = 23{,}473.80$
@step Difference: $26{,}176.92 - 23{,}473.80 = 2{,}703.12$
@answer The bank loan costs \$2,703.12 less, even though its payment is higher.
:::

:::definition Rent-to-own
With **rent-to-own**, you make weekly or every-two-weeks payments and own the item after the last one. The finance charge is the total of the payments minus the cash price, and it is often very high.
:::

:::example A rent-to-own TV
A store offers a TV with a cash price of \$899.99 for 78 weekly payments of \$24.99. Find the finance charge.
@step Total of payments: $24.99 \times 78 = 1{,}949.22$
@step Finance charge: $1{,}949.22 - 899.99 = 1{,}049.23$
@step As a percent of the cash price: $1{,}049.23 \div 899.99 \times 100 \approx 116.6\%$
@answer The finance charge is \$1,049.23, more than the TV itself. Saving \$24.99 a week for 37 weeks would buy it outright.
:::

:::check m203-loan-cost 3
:::

:::summary
- Credit card interest: balance × yearly rate (as a decimal) ÷ 365 × days. Pay the full balance on time to avoid it.
- New balance = unpaid amount + interest + new purchases. The minimum payment follows the rule on the statement.
- Carrying a balance means paying interest on interest.
- Loans: total paid = payment × number of payments (years × 12 for monthly payments); finance charge = total paid − amount borrowed.
- Compare credit options by their total cost, not by the size of the payment.
:::
