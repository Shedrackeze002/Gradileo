:::note Spoiler
This lesson follows the whole plot of *Romeo and Juliet*, including the ending. The Prologue gives the ending away too, so you lose little by knowing it: the play is about how and why it happens.
:::

In the first scene of the play, two servants of the Capulets meet two servants of the Montagues in the street, and an insult starts a brawl:

> **Abram.** Do you bite your thumb at us, sir?
>
> **Sampson.** I do bite my thumb, sir.
>
> — *Romeo and Juliet*, Act 1, Scene 1

Biting your thumb at someone was a rude gesture, and within minutes the heads of both families are calling for their swords. The feud is so old that no one remembers how it began, yet it drives everything that follows. In this lesson you will meet the characters on each side, follow the story act by act, and explain why the characters act as they do.

## The two households

| The Montagues | The Capulets | Outside the families |
|---|---|---|
| **Montague** and **Lady Montague** | **Capulet** and **Lady Capulet** | **Prince Escalus**, the ruler of Verona |
| **Romeo**, their son | **Juliet**, their daughter, not yet fourteen | **Mercutio**, Romeo’s witty friend, a kinsman of the Prince |
| **Benvolio**, Montague’s nephew, Romeo’s cousin and friend | **Tybalt**, Lady Capulet’s nephew, Juliet’s hot-tempered cousin | **Paris**, a young nobleman, also a kinsman of the Prince |
| **Balthasar**, Romeo’s servant | **the Nurse**, who has looked after Juliet since she was a baby | **Friar Lawrence**, a Franciscan friar and Romeo’s adviser |

Before he meets Juliet, Romeo is lovesick for **Rosaline**, a young woman who will not return his love. She never appears on stage, but she explains why Romeo goes to the Capulets’ feast at all.

## The story, act by act

The whole play takes place in less than a week.

| Act | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | A street fight between the two households; the Prince threatens death to anyone who fights again. Capulet invites Paris, who wants to marry Juliet, to a feast. Romeo, Benvolio and Mercutio go to the feast in masks; Romeo and Juliet meet and fall in love, and only then learn each other’s family names. |
| 2 | That night, Romeo overhears Juliet at her window, and they promise to marry. Friar Lawrence agrees to marry them in secret, and they are married the next day. |
| 3 | Tybalt challenges Romeo; Romeo refuses to fight, so Mercutio fights Tybalt instead and is killed. Romeo kills Tybalt and is banished from Verona. Capulet arranges for Juliet to marry Paris on Thursday, and threatens to throw her out when she refuses. |
| 4 | Friar Lawrence gives Juliet a potion that will make her seem dead for “two and forty hours” and plans to send word to Romeo. Juliet pretends to agree to the marriage; Capulet moves it forward a day; Juliet drinks the potion and is found, apparently dead, on her wedding morning. |
| 5 | The Friar’s letter never reaches Romeo. Told that Juliet is dead, Romeo buys poison and goes to her tomb, where he kills Paris and drinks the poison. Juliet wakes, finds Romeo dead, and stabs herself. Over their bodies, the two families make peace. |

## Motive

A **motive** is the reason behind what a character does. To explain a motive, you need **evidence**: the character’s own words, what others say about them, or what they do. Characters can have more than one motive, and they can act on mistaken beliefs.

:::definition Tragedy and the tragic hero
*Romeo and Juliet* is a **tragedy**: a serious play that ends in the deaths of its main characters. In a tragedy, the downfall usually comes partly from outside (fate, chance, other people) and partly from the characters’ own choices and weaknesses. Romeo’s haste and his fury after Mercutio’s death are part of what destroys him.
:::

:::example Why does Friar Lawrence agree to marry them?
Romeo asks Friar Lawrence to marry him to Juliet that same day. The Friar has just teased him for forgetting Rosaline so quickly (“Young men’s love then lies / Not truly in their hearts, but in their eyes”). Then he says:

> In one respect I’ll thy assistant be;\
> For this alliance may so happy prove,\
> To turn your households’ rancour to pure love.
>
> — Friar Lawrence, *Romeo and Juliet*, Act 2, Scene 3

Why does the Friar agree?
@step Find the moment: Act 2, Scene 3, the morning after the balcony scene. The Friar doubts that Romeo’s love is deep.
@step Explain the key words: an “alliance” is a joining of families, and “rancour” is bitter hatred.
@step Notice “in one respect”: he agrees for one reason only, not because he trusts Romeo’s love, but because the marriage might end the feud.
@answer The Friar agrees because he hopes that marrying a Montague to a Capulet will turn the families’ hatred into love, even though he doubts that Romeo’s love runs deep.
:::

:::mistake Characters only know what they have seen
Tybalt, Mercutio, Lady Capulet and Paris never learn that Romeo and Juliet are married. When you explain their motives, use only what they know. Tybalt challenges Romeo because Romeo came uninvited to the Capulets’ feast, not because of Juliet.
:::

## Foils

A **foil** is a character whose differences from another character make that character’s qualities stand out. Shakespeare places several pairs side by side:

- **Benvolio and Tybalt**: Benvolio tries to stop the first fight (“I do but keep the peace”); Tybalt hates “the word” peace.
- **Mercutio and Romeo**: Mercutio jokes about love and dreams; Romeo is lovesick and believes his dreams.
- **The Nurse and Lady Capulet**: the Nurse is warm, chatty and close to Juliet; her mother is distant and formal.

:::tip Ask what the contrast shows
When two characters react differently to the same moment, ask what the contrast tells you. When Tybalt insults Romeo, Romeo answers gently and Mercutio draws his sword: the audience sees at once how Romeo has changed, and why his friend cannot understand him.
:::

:::summary
- The Montagues include Romeo, Benvolio and Balthasar; the Capulets include Juliet, Tybalt and the Nurse; Mercutio and Paris are kinsmen of the Prince, and Friar Lawrence stands outside both families.
- Act 1: the feud and the feast. Act 2: the balcony and the secret wedding. Act 3: the deaths of Mercutio and Tybalt, and Romeo’s banishment. Act 4: the potion. Act 5: Mantua and the tomb.
- A motive is the reason behind an action; support it with the character’s words, and remember what each character does and does not know.
- A foil, such as Benvolio beside Tybalt, makes another character’s qualities stand out by contrast.
:::
