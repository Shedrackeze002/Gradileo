*The box of old photographs sit on the top shelf.* Many readers would pass over that sentence without noticing anything wrong, because the word right before the verb, *photographs*, is plural. But the box is doing the sitting, and there is only one box. Agreement errors hide in exactly this way: something comes between the words that belong together. In this lesson you will make verbs agree with their subjects, choose pronouns that agree with the words they replace, and keep verb tense steady.

## Subject–verb agreement

:::definition Subject–verb agreement
A verb must **agree** with its subject in number: a singular subject takes a singular verb (*the box sits*), and a plural subject takes a plural verb (*the boxes sit*). In the present tense, the singular verb usually ends in *-s*, the opposite of nouns.
:::

The rule is simple; finding the subject is the hard part. Here are the situations that cause most errors.

- **A phrase between the subject and the verb.** Cover up the phrase: *The keys ~~to the science lab~~ are missing.* The subject is *keys*.
- **Subjects joined by *and*** are plural: *Kenji and Liam play in the orchestra.*
- **Subjects joined by *or* or *nor***: the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. *Either the coach or the players carry the equipment. Neither the players nor the coach was happy.*
- ***One of …*** is singular, because the subject is *one*: *One of the windows is cracked.*
- **Sentences that begin with *there* or *here***: the subject comes after the verb. *There are three letters on the table.*

:::definition Indefinite pronouns
Some pronouns that do not name a particular person or thing are always singular: *each*, *every one*, *everyone*, *everybody*, *nobody*, *no one*, *someone*, *anyone*, *neither*, *either*. Others are always plural: *both*, *few*, *many*, *several*. *Each of the students has a locker. Several of the runners have blisters.*
:::

:::example Finding the real subject
Choose the verb: *The cost of the concert tickets (is / are) too high for most students.*
@step Cover the phrase that begins with *of*: *The cost ~~of the concert tickets~~ (is / are) too high.*
@step The subject is *cost*, which is singular.
@answer *The cost of the concert tickets **is** too high for most students.*
:::

:::mistake “Along with” is not “and”
Phrases such as *along with*, *as well as* and *together with* add information but do not make the subject plural. *Wei, along with his teammates, **is** ready for the final.* Only *and* joins subjects into a plural.
:::

:::tip Nouns that only look plural
*News*, *mathematics* and *physics* end in s but are singular: *The news from the hospital is good.*
:::

## Pronouns

A pronoun stands in for a noun, called its **antecedent**. The pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number, and it must be in the right **case** for its job in the sentence.

:::definition Pronoun agreement and case
- **Agreement:** a plural antecedent takes a plural pronoun (*The students handed in **their** projects*); a single thing takes *it* or *its* (*The tree lost **its** leaves*).
- **Subject case** (*I, he, she, we, they, who*) is used when the pronoun does the action: ***She** and I set up the chairs.*
- **Object case** (*me, him, her, us, them, whom*) is used when the pronoun receives the action or follows a preposition such as *to*, *for*, *with* or *between*: *The coach gave the trophy to Fatima and **me**.*
:::

:::example Two people and a pronoun
Choose the pronoun: *The teacher asked Diego and (I / me) to hand out the books.*
@step Cover the other person: *The teacher asked \_\_\_\_\_ to hand out the books.*
@step *The teacher asked I* sounds wrong; *the teacher asked me* sounds right.
@answer *The teacher asked Diego and **me** to hand out the books.*
:::

:::mistake “Between you and I”
After a preposition, use the object case: *between you and **me***, *for him and me*, *to my brother and me*. And *myself* is not a polite form of *me*. Use it only when the subject does something to itself: *I taught myself to juggle.*
:::

:::note Singular “they”
When a person’s gender is unknown or not relevant, *they* and *their* are accepted as singular in all varieties of English: *Everyone should bring their own lunch.* This course does not treat that as an error.
:::

## Consistent verb tense

A story or an essay chooses a tense, past or present, and stays in it. Switching without a reason confuses the reader about when things happen.

:::example Spotting a tense shift
Find the verb that shifts tense: *Hana opened the door and looked around the empty classroom. On the desk, she notices a note and picked it up.*
@step List the verbs: *opened*, *looked*, *notices*, *picked*.
@step Three are in the past tense; *notices* is in the present.
@answer Change *notices* to *noticed*.
:::

:::tip Essays about literature
When you write about what happens in a story, the usual convention is the present tense: *In the story, the narrator hears a heartbeat.* Whichever tense you choose, keep it.
:::

:::summary
- Find the true subject before choosing the verb: ignore phrases in between, look after *there* and *here*, and remember that *one of*, *each*, *everyone* and *neither* are singular.
- *And* makes a plural subject; with *or* and *nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject; *along with* and *as well as* do not change the subject.
- Pronouns agree in number with their antecedents. Use the subject case for subjects and the object case after verbs and prepositions; cover the other person to test.
- Keep verb tense consistent within a passage.
:::
