A poem’s lines are grouped into **stanzas**, and the pattern of rhymes at the ends of the lines is its **rhyme scheme**. Together they give a poem its shape on the page and its music in the ear.

## Naming stanzas

| Stanza | Number of lines |
|---|---|
| **Couplet** | 2 |
| **Tercet** | 3 |
| **Quatrain** | 4 |
| **Sestet** | 6 |

Each stanza of Tennyson’s “The Eagle” is a **tercet**. Carl Sandburg’s “Fog” has a couplet followed by a quatrain. Poets choose stanza lengths to fit their ideas, and free verse can mix them.

## Writing a rhyme scheme

To write a rhyme scheme, give the sound at the end of the first line the letter **A**. Every line that rhymes with it is also **A**. The next new sound is **B**, then **C**, and so on.

:::example A rhyme scheme with a couplet
> I wandered lonely as a cloud\
> That floats on high o’er vales and hills,\
> When all at once I saw a crowd,\
> A host, of golden daffodils;\
> Beside the lake, beneath the trees,\
> Fluttering and dancing in the breeze.
>
> — William Wordsworth, “I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud” (1815), first stanza

What is the rhyme scheme?
@step *cloud* is A, *hills* is B, *crowd* rhymes with *cloud* (A), *daffodils* with *hills* (B).
@step *trees* is a new sound, C, and *breeze* rhymes with it (C).
@answer ABABCC: a quatrain’s pattern followed by a rhyming couplet.
:::

| Rhyme scheme | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| AABB | rhyming couplets | Stevenson’s *witches / ditches, battle / cattle* |
| ABAB | alternate rhyme | *night / street / white / feet* |
| ABCB | only lines 2 and 4 rhyme | many ballads and songs |
| ABBA | enclosed rhyme | the outer lines rhyme and frame the inner pair |

:::mistake Rhyme is about sound
Lines whose last words look alike may not rhyme (*love* and *move*), and lines whose last words look different may rhyme (*cloud* and *crowd*). Say the words aloud before you give them letters.
:::

:::summary
- A **couplet** has 2 lines, a **tercet** 3, a **quatrain** 4 and a **sestet** 6.
- A **rhyme scheme** uses letters for end sounds: the first sound is A, each new sound gets the next letter.
- Common schemes are AABB, ABAB, ABCB and ABBA.
:::
