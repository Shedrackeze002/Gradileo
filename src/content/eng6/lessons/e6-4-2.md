A photograph seems to show the plain truth, and a headline seems to just name the news. But every image is **chosen** and every headline is **written** by someone. In this lesson you will learn to read the messages they send.

## Reading images

When you look at an image in the news or an advert, ask:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **What is shown?** | The people, objects and setting carry a message: a trophy and confetti suggest success. |
| **What is left out?** | An advert for a water park may show empty slides and sunshine but not the queues. |
| **How is it framed?** | A wide shot of a crowd makes a protest look huge; a close-up of a few people makes it look small. |
| **From what angle?** | Looking **up** at something makes it seem big and powerful; looking **down** makes it seem small. |
| **What does the caption say?** | A caption tells you how to read the picture, and can change its meaning. |

:::example Reading an angle
A news photo of a forest fire is taken from a low angle, with flames towering over a firefighter. What does the angle suggest?
@step A low angle looks up, which makes things above seem larger.
@step The firefighter looks small beside the flames.
@answer The fire is huge and powerful.
:::

## Loaded words in headlines

:::definition Loaded and neutral words
A **loaded word** carries strong feelings, positive or negative: *heroic*, *shameful*, *slams*, *crush*. A **neutral word** simply reports: *helps*, *votes*, *says*, *wins*.
:::

| Loaded headline | Neutral headline |
|---|---|
| *Heroic Pupils Save Stranded Dog* | *Pupils Help Dog Off Ice* |
| *Council Slams Door on Book Lovers* | *Council Votes to Close Library on Sundays* |
| *Tigers Crush Lions 5–0* | *Tigers Beat Lions 5–0* |

:::example Rewriting a headline
> Greedy Developers Destroy Beloved Woods

Rewrite it in neutral words.
@step *Greedy* and *beloved* are judgements; *destroy* is a strong, negative verb.
@step Keep the facts: developers are clearing woods.
@answer *Developers Clear Woods for New Housing.*
:::

:::mistake Loaded is not the same as false
A loaded headline may report true facts. The problem is that it tells you how to *feel* about them before you have read the story. Notice the feeling, then decide for yourself.
:::
