*Slender*, *thin* and *scrawny* all describe someone without much fat on them. But if a friend called you scrawny, you would probably feel very differently than if they called you slender. Words have a dictionary meaning and also a feeling attached to them. Choosing words for their feelings is one of a writer’s most powerful tools.

## Denotation and connotation

:::definition Denotation and connotation
- The **denotation** of a word is its dictionary meaning.
- The **connotation** of a word is the feeling or idea it suggests. A connotation can be **positive**, **negative** or **neutral**.
:::

| Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| slender | thin | scrawny |
| curious | interested | nosy |
| determined | firm | stubborn |
| thrifty | careful with money | stingy |
| home | house | shack |
| aroma | smell | stench |

:::example Naming a connotation
What is the connotation of *stubborn*?
@step Its denotation is “not willing to change one’s mind”.
@step Would you be pleased to be called stubborn? Probably not: it suggests being difficult for no good reason.
@answer Negative. (*Determined* is a positive word for a similar trait.)
:::

## Choosing words for their connotation

Writers choose connotations on purpose. An estate agent calls a small house *cosy*, not *cramped*. A news report on an angry crowd might say *mob*; a friendly report might say *crowd* or *fans*.

:::example Choosing the right word
A writer wants readers to admire a character who asks lots of questions. Which word fits: *nosy*, *prying* or *inquisitive*?
@step *Nosy* and *prying* suggest interfering in other people’s business.
@step *Inquisitive* suggests a healthy wish to learn.
@answer *inquisitive*
:::

:::mistake Synonyms are not always swappable
A thesaurus lists *stench* as a synonym for *smell*, but “the stench of baking bread” sounds disgusting. Check the connotation before you swap one word for another.
:::

:::summary
- **Denotation** is the dictionary meaning; **connotation** is the feeling a word carries.
- Connotations can be positive, negative or neutral.
- Choose words for the feeling you want the reader to have, and check connotations before swapping synonyms.
:::
