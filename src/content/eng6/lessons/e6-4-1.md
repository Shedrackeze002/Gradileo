Advertisements are everywhere: on buses, in apps, before videos and on the backs of cereal boxes. Every one of them is designed to persuade a particular group of people to buy, do or believe something. Learning the tricks advertisers use makes you a sharper, more independent viewer.

## Advertising techniques

| Technique | How it persuades | Example (invented) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bandwagon** | everyone is doing it, so you should too | *Everyone at school is wearing ZoomKicks. Don’t be left out!* |
| **Testimonial** | a famous or expert person recommends it | *“I eat Crunch-O’s before every match,” says football star Leo Diaz.* |
| **Emotional appeal** | makes you feel something: happy, sad, scared or proud | *A lonely puppy in the rain: “Give him a warm home.”* |
| **Facts and figures** | uses numbers and test results | *9 out of 10 users had fewer fillings.* |
| **Slogan** | a short, catchy phrase that sticks in your mind | *FreshFarm: milk the way it should be.* |

:::example Naming the technique
> Join the millions of families who have already switched to FreshFarm milk.

@step The advert does not give facts about the milk or use a famous person.
@step It says that lots of other people are doing it.
@answer Bandwagon.
:::

:::mistake Numbers can mislead too
“9 out of 10 users” sounds convincing, but ask: who tested it, how many people were asked, and compared with what? Facts and figures in an advert are chosen to persuade.
:::

## The target audience

The **target audience** is the group of people an advert is aimed at. Advertisers choose words, pictures, music and even the time and place an advert appears to suit that audience.

| Clue | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| bright colours, cartoons, a free toy | young children |
| talk of safety, value and family | parents |
| sports stars, music, “stand out” | teenagers |
| an advert shown during a Saturday-morning cartoon show | children |

:::example Finding the audience
> SafeRide car seats: because nothing matters more than keeping your little one safe.

@step A car seat is bought by adults, not children.
@step “Your little one” speaks to someone who looks after a small child.
@answer Parents of young children.
:::

:::summary
- Advertisers use **bandwagon**, **testimonials**, **emotional appeals**, **facts and figures** and **slogans**.
- Ask who made the advert and what it wants you to do.
- The **target audience** is the group an advert is aimed at; look at its words, pictures and where it appears.
:::
