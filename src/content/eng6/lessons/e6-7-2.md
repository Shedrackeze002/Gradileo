An informational piece, such as a report on wolves or an article about the solar system, teaches the reader about a topic. To do that well, it needs a clear **structure** and **transitions** that lead the reader from one idea to the next.

## Introduction, body and conclusion

| Part | Its job |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | names the topic, hooks the reader with an interesting fact or question, and often says what the piece will cover |
| **Body paragraphs** | each covers one **subtopic**, starting with a topic sentence and giving facts, examples and details |
| **Conclusion** | sums up the main ideas and leaves the reader with a final thought |

:::example Planning a report
A student is writing about the solar system. Which plan is clearest?
- *Intro; Mars; the Sun; Saturn; Earth; my opinion*
- *Intro; the Sun; the rocky planets; the gas giants; conclusion*

@step The first jumps between topics in no clear order and ends with an opinion.
@step The second groups related planets together and moves from the centre outwards.
@answer The second plan.
:::

:::mistake "The end" is not a conclusion
“Thank you for reading my report” tells the reader nothing. A conclusion should remind them of the big idea: *Recycling is a small habit that makes a big difference.*
:::

## Transitions

**Transitions** are words and phrases that show how ideas connect.

| To show | Transitions |
|---|---|
| time or order | *first, next, then, afterwards, finally* |
| an example | *for example, for instance, such as* |
| addition | *also, in addition, furthermore, another* |
| contrast | *however, in contrast, on the other hand, instead* |
| cause and effect | *because, as a result, therefore, so* |
| a conclusion | *in summary, in conclusion, overall* |

:::example Choosing a transition
> Frogs have smooth skin. ___, toads have dry, bumpy skin.

@step The second sentence shows a difference from the first.
@answer *In contrast* (or *However*).
:::

:::summary
- An informational piece has an **introduction**, **body paragraphs** (one subtopic each) and a **conclusion**.
- Each body paragraph starts with a topic sentence.
- **Transitions** show time, examples, addition, contrast, cause and effect, and conclusions.
:::
