Verbs tell us not only *what* happens but *when*. In Grade 5 you used the simple past, present and future. Now you will learn tenses that show an action **in progress** or an action **completed** before a certain time, and how to keep your tenses steady.

## Progressive and perfect tenses

| Tense | Form | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present progressive | am / is / are + -ing | *She **is reading**.* | happening right now |
| Past progressive | was / were + -ing | *She **was reading** when the phone rang.* | in progress at a past moment |
| Future progressive | will be + -ing | *She **will be reading** at eight.* | in progress at a future moment |
| Present perfect | has / have + past participle | *We **have visited** Paris twice.* | past action connected to now |
| Past perfect | had + past participle | *The film **had started** when we arrived.* | completed before another past event |
| Future perfect | will have + past participle | *I **will have finished** by tomorrow.* | completed before a future time |

:::example Choosing a tense
> By the time we arrived, the film (started / had started).

@step There are two past events: the film started, and we arrived.
@step The film started first, so it needs the past perfect.
@answer By the time we arrived, the film **had started**.
:::

:::mistake "Since" and the present perfect
Use the present perfect with *since* and *for* when the action continues up to now: *I **have lived** here since 2020.* Not *I live here since 2020*.
:::

## Keeping tenses consistent

When you tell a story or describe events, keep the verbs in the same tense unless the time really changes. A sudden, unnecessary shift confuses the reader.

| Shifts in error | Consistent |
|---|---|
| *The wind howled, the rain fell, and the trees **bend**.* | *The wind howled, the rain fell, and the trees **bent**.* |
| *She **runs** to the stop, but the bus left.* | *She **ran** to the stop, but the bus **had left**.* |

A change of tense is right when the time changes: *Yesterday I **was** nervous, but today I **feel** confident.*

:::summary
- **Progressive** tenses (be + -ing) show actions in progress.
- **Perfect** tenses (have, has, had + past participle) show actions completed before a time.
- Use the **past perfect** for the earlier of two past events.
- Keep tenses consistent unless the time really changes.
:::
