Two small marks cause a great many mistakes: the **apostrophe** and the **comma**. In this lesson you will learn exactly when each one is needed and, just as important, when it is not.

## Apostrophes

An apostrophe has two jobs.

| Job | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Contraction** | it shows where letters are left out | *do not* → *don’t*; *it is* → *it’s*; *they are* → *they’re* |
| **Possession** (one owner) | add **‘s** | *the dog’s bone*; *Mia’s coat* |
| **Possession** (plural ending in s) | add only **’** | *the girls’ coats*; *the dogs’ bowls* |
| **Possession** (plural not ending in s) | add **‘s** | *the children’s toys*; *the men’s team* |

:::example Choosing the possessive
> The (girl’s / girls’) coats were hanging on the hooks. (several girls)

@step The owners are several girls, a plural that ends in s.
@step Add only an apostrophe after the s.
@answer The **girls’** coats were hanging on the hooks.
:::

:::mistake It’s and its
*It’s* always means *it is* or *it has*. *Its* (no apostrophe) means *belonging to it*, like *his* and *hers*. And a plain plural never takes an apostrophe: *Fresh apples for sale*, not *apple’s*.
:::

## Commas in sentences

| Use a comma | Example |
|---|---|
| before a FANBOYS conjunction joining two independent clauses | *I like apples, but my sister likes pears.* |
| after an introductory word, phrase or clause | *However, the bus was late.* *After the game, we went for pizza.* *When the bell rang, we left.* |
| around extra information in the middle of a sentence | *My cousin, who lives in Halifax, is visiting.* |
| around a phrase that renames a noun | *Sam, our team captain, scored twice.* |

:::example Extra information
> My cousin who lives in Halifax is visiting.

Where do the commas go if the speaker has only one cousin?
@step “Who lives in Halifax” is extra information: the sentence makes sense without it.
@step Extra information needs a comma on **both** sides.
@answer *My cousin, who lives in Halifax, is visiting.*
:::

:::mistake One subject, two verbs: no comma
*The dog barked and wagged its tail* has one subject doing two things, so it needs no comma before *and*. Use the comma only when *and* joins two complete sentences.
:::

:::summary
- Apostrophes show **contractions** and **possession**: *dog’s* (one), *dogs’* (plural in s), *children’s* (plural not in s).
- *It’s* = it is; *its* = belonging to it; plurals take no apostrophe.
- Use commas before FANBOYS joining two sentences, after introductory parts, and on both sides of extra information.
:::
