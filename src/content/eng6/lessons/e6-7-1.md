In Grade 5 you learned to tell events in order and to show feelings instead of naming them. Now you will learn two more ways to make a story grip its reader: a strong **opening** and lively **dialogue**.

## Hooking the reader

The first sentence of a story is its **hook**. It should make the reader want to read the second sentence.

| Kind of hook | Example |
|---|---|
| **Action** | *The floor beneath me cracked, and I was falling.* |
| **Dialogue** | *“Don’t open that box!” Grandma shouted.* |
| **A question** | *Have you ever wondered what it would be like to fly?* |
| **A mysterious detail** | *The letter had no name on it, only a black feather.* |
| **A sound** | *Crash! The whole shelf of plates hit the floor.* |

:::example Choosing a hook
Which is the stronger opening?
- *This is a story about a girl and a long trip she took.*
- *The floor beneath me cracked, and I was falling.*

@step The first announces that a story is coming, but nothing happens.
@step The second drops the reader into danger and raises a question: what happens next?
@answer The second.
:::

:::mistake Don’t explain everything first
You do not need to start with the narrator’s name, age and home. Begin in the action and slip the background in later, a sentence at a time.
:::

## Writing dialogue

Good dialogue does at least one of two jobs: it **shows character** (how someone feels or what they are like), or it **moves the plot** (it gives new information or creates a problem).

| Dull dialogue | Dialogue that works |
|---|---|
| *“Hello. How are you?” “Fine, thanks.”* | *“The bridge is out. We’ll have to swim across.”* (moves the plot) |
| *“I won,” said Sam.* | *“I won,” Sam whispered, tears in his eyes.* (shows feeling) |

**Punctuating dialogue**

- Put the spoken words inside quotation marks.
- If the dialogue tag comes after the speech, end the speech with a comma (or ? or !) inside the quotation marks: *“Let’s go,” said Nia.*
- If the tag interrupts a sentence, use commas on both sides of the tag: *“Wait,” Nia said, “I forgot my bag.”*
- Start a **new paragraph** each time a different character speaks.

:::example Reading between the lines
> “Oh, sure, I’d love to clean the bathroom,” Omar muttered, rolling his eyes.

What does this show about Omar?
@step His words say he would love to, but *muttered* and *rolling his eyes* say the opposite.
@answer He does not want to clean it at all: he is being sarcastic.
:::

:::summary
- A **hook** grabs the reader: start with action, dialogue, a question, a mysterious detail or a sound.
- Good dialogue shows character or moves the plot.
- Punctuate speech inside quotation marks, and start a new paragraph for each new speaker.
:::
