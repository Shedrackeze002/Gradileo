Most stories follow a shape. They begin quietly, build up tension, reach a turning point, and then wind down to an ending. Knowing this shape, called the **plot**, helps you understand where you are in a story and why each part matters. At the heart of every plot is a **conflict**: a struggle the main character must face.

## The five stages of a plot

:::definition The plot stages
- **Exposition**: the beginning, which introduces the characters, the setting and the situation.
- **Rising action**: events that build tension as the problem grows.
- **Climax**: the turning point, the moment of greatest tension, when the main problem is faced.
- **Falling action**: the events just after the climax, as loose ends are tied up.
- **Resolution**: the ending, which shows how things are once the problem is over.
:::

Many readers picture the plot as a mountain. You climb up through the rising action, reach the top at the climax, and come down the other side to the resolution.

:::example Placing an event
> At last Mira reached the top of the cliff, grabbed the rope with both hands and pulled the lost climber to safety as the rock gave way beneath them.

Which stage of the plot is this?
@step The whole story has built towards the rescue, and the danger is at its greatest.
@step This is the moment when the main problem is faced.
@answer The climax.
:::

:::mistake The climax is not always the end
The climax is the turning point, and it usually comes near the end, but it is not the last part. After it come the falling action and the resolution, where we see what happens because of the climax.
:::

## Kinds of conflict

A **conflict** is a struggle between opposing forces. Without one there is no story, because nothing would need to happen.

| Conflict | The character struggles against | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Character against character | another person or creature | two friends competing to be captain |
| Character against nature | weather, animals, the land or the sea | a sailor caught in a storm |
| Character against self | their own fears, doubts or choices | a boy deciding whether to tell the truth |
| Character against society | the rules, laws or beliefs of a group | a girl who wants to be a knight in a kingdom that says girls cannot |

The first, second and fourth are **external** conflicts, because the struggle is with something outside the character. Character against self is an **internal** conflict, because it happens inside the character’s mind.

:::example Naming the conflict
> Everyone in the kingdom believed that girls could not be knights. Ana practised with a sword every night, determined to prove them wrong.

@step Ana is not fighting one person or the weather, and the passage does not describe her doubting herself.
@step She is pushing against what the whole community believes.
@answer Character against society.
:::

:::summary
- A plot has five stages: **exposition**, **rising action**, **climax**, **falling action** and **resolution**.
- The **climax** is the turning point, where the main problem is faced.
- A **conflict** is the struggle at the heart of a story: against another character, nature, society or oneself.
- Character against self is an **internal** conflict; the others are **external**.
:::
