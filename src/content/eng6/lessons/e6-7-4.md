Revising means making your writing better, not just fixing mistakes. Two of the quickest improvements are choosing **precise words** and giving your sentences **variety**. Small changes like these can make a piece of writing feel completely different.

## Precise words

**Vague** words such as *nice*, *good*, *thing*, *stuff*, *went* and *got* tell the reader very little. **Precise** words paint an exact picture.

| Vague | Precise |
|---|---|
| *The dog went across the field.* | *The terrier sprinted across the meadow.* |
| *We had a nice time.* | *We had a relaxing afternoon.* |
| *The food was really very good.* | *The soup was rich and creamy.* |

:::mistake "Very" does not fix a vague word
“Very good” and “really nice” are still vague. Instead of adding *very*, replace the weak word: *very big* → *enormous*; *very tired* → *exhausted*.
:::

Revising also means cutting **wordy** phrases that add no meaning:

| Wordy | Concise |
|---|---|
| *We made the decision that we would leave early.* | *We decided to leave early.* |
| *at this point in time* | *now* |
| *due to the fact that* | *because* |

## Sentence variety

Writing in which every sentence is the same length, and starts the same way, quickly becomes dull. Vary your sentences:

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| Mix long and short | *We searched the cave for an hour, our torches flickering on the wet walls. Nothing.* |
| Start with a phrase | *After breakfast, we set off.* |
| Start with an adverb | *Slowly, the door creaked open.* |
| Start with a dependent clause | *Although the cave was dark and cold, we went in anyway.* |

:::example Revising choppy sentences
> I woke up. I got dressed. I ate breakfast.

@step Every sentence is short and starts with *I*.
@step Join the ideas and vary the openings.
@answer *When I woke up, I got dressed. After breakfast, I left for school.*
:::

:::summary
- Replace vague words (*nice, good, went*) with precise ones, and cut wordy phrases.
- Don’t fix a weak word with *very*; choose a stronger word.
- Vary sentence length and openings: phrases, adverbs and dependent clauses.
- A short sentence after long ones adds emphasis.
:::
