In Grade 5 you wrote opinion pieces with reasons. A **persuasive** piece goes further: it makes a clear claim, supports it with evidence, and answers the people who disagree. That last step is what makes an argument truly convincing.

## A clear, arguable claim

:::definition Claim
The **claim** (sometimes called the **thesis**) states exactly what the writer wants the reader to believe or do. A good claim is:
- **arguable**: reasonable people could disagree;
- **specific**: it says exactly what should happen;
- **manageable**: it can be supported well in the space you have.
:::

| Weak claim | Why | Stronger claim |
|---|---|---|
| *Gardens are places where plants grow.* | a fact, not arguable | *Our school should plant a vegetable garden.* |
| *Something should be done about our town.* | too vague | *Our town should build a skate park in Elm Field.* |
| *Schools around the world should be completely changed.* | too big for one page | *Pupils should have ten more minutes of break each day.* |

## Counterclaims and rebuttals

:::definition Counterclaim and rebuttal
- A **counterclaim** is an argument against your claim, one that someone on the other side might make.
- A **rebuttal** is your answer to it, with reasons or evidence.
:::

:::example Answering the other side
Claim: *Pupils should have a longer lunch.*

@step A counterclaim: *A longer lunch would make the school day end later.*
@step A rebuttal answers the concern reasonably: *Five minutes could come from the morning assembly instead, so the day would end at the same time.*
@answer State the counterclaim fairly, then answer it with a reasonable solution or evidence.
:::

Introduce a counterclaim respectfully, with phrases such as *Some people argue that…* or *It is true that…*, and then turn back to your side with *However…* or *But…*.

:::mistake Insults are not rebuttals
“Only foolish people think homework is useful” does not answer anything; it just attacks. A rebuttal must give a reason. Treating the other side fairly also makes readers trust you more.
:::

:::summary
- A strong **claim** is arguable, specific and manageable.
- A **counterclaim** is an argument against your claim; a **rebuttal** answers it.
- Introduce the other side fairly, then answer it with reasons, not insults.
:::
