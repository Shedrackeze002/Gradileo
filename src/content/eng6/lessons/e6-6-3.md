Good writers use sentences of different shapes. Some are short and simple; others join two or more ideas. To build them well, you need to know about **clauses** and the words that join them.

## Clauses and sentence kinds

:::definition Clause
A **clause** is a group of words with a subject and a verb.
- An **independent clause** can stand alone as a sentence: *the dog barked*.
- A **dependent clause** cannot stand alone: *because it was raining*.
:::

| Kind | What it has | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | one independent clause | *The dog barked at the mail carrier.* |
| **Compound** | two independent clauses joined by a comma and a conjunction | *The dog barked, and the cat ran away.* |
| **Complex** | an independent clause and a dependent clause | *Because it was raining, we stayed inside.* |

:::example Is it simple or compound?
> Sam and Mia laughed and clapped.

@step There are two subjects (Sam, Mia) and two verbs (laughed, clapped).
@step But they form one clause: there is no second subject-and-verb pair that could stand alone.
@answer A simple sentence.
:::

:::mistake A phrase is not a clause
“After lunch, we played football” has a comma, but “after lunch” has no verb, so it is a phrase. The sentence is simple.
:::

## Coordinating conjunctions

The seven **coordinating conjunctions** join independent clauses into compound sentences. Remember them as **FANBOYS**:

| Conjunction | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **f**or | gives a reason | *We stayed inside, for it was raining.* |
| **a**nd | adds | *I played, and she watched.* |
| **n**or | adds another negative | *He did not call, nor did he write.* |
| **b**ut | contrasts | *I like maths, but I don’t like science.* |
| **o**r | gives a choice | *We could walk, or we could take the bus.* |
| **y**et | contrasts, often surprisingly | *It was late, yet we kept playing.* |
| **s**o | shows a result | *I was hungry, so I made a sandwich.* |

:::example Avoiding a comma splice
> The film was long, I enjoyed it.

@step Two independent clauses are joined by only a comma. That is a **comma splice**.
@step Add a conjunction that shows the contrast.
@answer *The film was long, but I enjoyed it.*
:::

:::summary
- A **simple** sentence has one independent clause; a **compound** sentence joins two; a **complex** sentence adds a dependent clause.
- Join independent clauses with a comma and a **FANBOYS** conjunction.
- A comma alone between two sentences is a **comma splice**.
:::
