Many English words were built from pieces of two ancient languages, **Greek** and **Latin**. These pieces, called **roots**, still carry their old meanings. If you know that *tele* means far and *scope* means see, you already know what a *telescope* does. Learning a handful of roots unlocks hundreds of words.

## Common roots

| Root | From | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| bio | Greek | life | biology, biography |
| geo | Greek | earth | geography, geology |
| graph | Greek | write or draw | autograph, paragraph |
| tele | Greek | far | telescope, television |
| micro | Greek | small | microscope, microchip |
| phon | Greek | sound | microphone, symphony |
| auto | Greek | self | automatic, autobiography |
| aqua | Latin | water | aquarium, aquatic |
| port | Latin | carry | transport, portable |
| aud | Latin | hear | audience, audible |
| vis | Latin | see | vision, visible |
| dict | Latin | say or speak | dictionary, predict |
| rupt | Latin | break | erupt, interrupt |
| struct | Latin | build | construct, structure |
| scrib | Latin | write | scribble, describe |

## Building meaning from parts

A long word is often a root with a **prefix** (at the front) and a **suffix** (at the end). Split the word, find the meaning of each part, and put them together.

:::example Taking a word apart
What does *interrupt* mean?
@step Split it: *inter* + *rupt*.
@step *inter-* means between; *rupt* means break.
@answer To break in between, as when you speak while someone else is talking.
:::

:::example Three parts
What is an *autobiography*?
@step *auto* = self, *bio* = life, *graphy* = writing.
@answer A book someone writes about their own life.
:::

:::mistake Not every matching letter string is a root
The letters *port* appear in *portrait*, but a portrait is not something you carry. Check that the root’s meaning fits before you rely on it, and use the sentence around the word as a second clue.
:::
