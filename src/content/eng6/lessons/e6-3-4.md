Every author writes for a reason, and every author has a view of their subject. Knowing an author’s **purpose** tells you how to read a text, and noticing their **point of view** helps you judge whether they are being fair.

## The author’s purpose

| Purpose | The author wants to | Clues | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inform** | give the reader facts | neutral words, facts and figures | an encyclopedia entry about whales |
| **Persuade** | change what the reader thinks or does | opinion words, *you*, calls to act | an advert or a letter to the council |
| **Entertain** | amuse, thrill or move the reader | a story, humour, vivid description | a funny poem or an adventure story |
| **Give instructions** | explain how to do something | numbered steps, commands | a recipe or a guide to building a kite |

:::example Naming the purpose
> Every plastic bag you refuse is one less bag in the sea. Bring a cloth bag. The turtles are counting on you.

@step The author speaks directly to the reader (*you*) and gives a command (*Bring a cloth bag*).
@step The last line appeals to the reader’s feelings.
@answer To persuade.
:::

:::mistake Interesting is not the same as entertaining
A fascinating article about monarch butterflies may be enjoyable to read, but its purpose is still to inform. Ask what the author mainly wants: for you to **know**, **do**, **enjoy** or **follow steps**.
:::

## The author’s point of view

An author’s **point of view** is how they feel about their subject. You can usually infer it from their **word choice**.

| Neutral | Shows a point of view |
|---|---|
| *The council voted to close the park after 6 p.m.* | *The council’s **unfair** rule **punishes** children who just want to play.* |
| *A new shopping centre opened on Friday.* | *The **exciting** new centre brings 200 jobs to our town.* |

When an author feels strongly, they may include only the facts that support their side. This is called **bias**. Noticing it helps you ask: what might the author have left out?

:::example Inferring a point of view
> Each year, shops hand out billions of wasteful plastic bags.

What is the author’s point of view on plastic bags?
@step *Billions* is a fact, but *wasteful* is a judgement.
@answer The author disapproves of plastic bags.
:::

:::summary
- Authors write to **inform**, **persuade**, **entertain** or **give instructions**.
- Ask what the author mainly wants the reader to know, do or feel.
- Word choice shows an author’s **point of view**; one-sided writing shows **bias**.
:::
