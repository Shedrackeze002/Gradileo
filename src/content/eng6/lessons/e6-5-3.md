If someone tells you it is raining cats and dogs, you do not look up expecting pets to fall from the sky. You know they mean it is raining hard. Expressions like this, whose meaning is different from the meanings of their separate words, are called **idioms**.

## Idioms

:::definition Idiom
An **idiom** is a phrase whose meaning cannot be worked out from the words on their own. Its meaning is **figurative**, not literal.
:::

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| It’s raining cats and dogs. | It is raining very heavily. |
| a piece of cake | something very easy |
| break a leg | good luck (said to performers) |
| let the cat out of the bag | tell a secret by accident |
| in the same boat | in the same difficult situation |
| hold your horses | wait; slow down |
| bite off more than you can chew | take on more than you can manage |
| on the fence | undecided |
| cost an arm and a leg | be very expensive |

:::example Working out an idiom
> Jamal let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party.

What does the idiom mean?
@step There is no real cat or bag at a surprise party.
@step The sentence is about a surprise, which is a kind of secret.
@answer Jamal told the secret by mistake.
:::

## Literal and figurative

:::definition Literal and figurative meaning
- **Literal** language means exactly what it says: *The cat jumped onto the fence.*
- **Figurative** language means something beyond what the words say: *I’m on the fence about the trip.*
:::

The same words can be literal in one sentence and figurative in another.

| Literal | Figurative |
|---|---|
| *After the snowball fight, my feet were cold.* | *I got cold feet before the concert.* (became nervous) |
| *Mum cut me a piece of cake.* | *The test was a piece of cake.* (easy) |

:::mistake Idioms do not translate
Idioms belong to a language. Translating one word for word into another language usually makes nonsense, and an English learner may find idioms confusing for the same reason. When you write for readers who are still learning English, use idioms with care.
:::

:::summary
- An **idiom** is a phrase whose meaning is different from its separate words.
- **Literal** language means what it says; **figurative** language means something more.
- Use the rest of the sentence to work out what an idiom means.
:::
