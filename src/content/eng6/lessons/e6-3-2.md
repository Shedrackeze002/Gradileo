Writers of non-fiction organize their ideas in patterns called **text structures**. Spotting the structure tells you what kind of information to look for, and a **graphic organizer** that matches the structure helps you take notes.

## Five text structures

| Structure | What it does | Signal words |
|---|---|---|
| **Description** | tells what something is like: its features, parts or qualities | *for example, such as, also, looks like* |
| **Sequence** | gives steps or events in time order | *first, next, then, after, finally, in spring* |
| **Compare and contrast** | shows how things are alike and different | *both, similarly, however, while, unlike* |
| **Cause and effect** | explains why something happens and what it leads to | *because, so, as a result, therefore, led to* |
| **Problem and solution** | presents a problem and how it is or could be solved | *problem, solve, to fix this, one answer is* |

:::example Naming the structure
> The school car park was jammed every morning. To fix this, the school started a “walking bus”: parents take turns walking groups of children to school. Now there are far fewer cars.

@step The first sentence names a problem: a jammed car park.
@step “To fix this” introduces a solution, and the last sentence gives the result.
@answer Problem and solution.
:::

:::mistake Look at the whole text
A description of pandas might use *because* once, but that does not make it cause and effect. Choose the structure that organizes the **whole** passage, not one sentence.
:::

## Graphic organizers

| Structure | Graphic organizer |
|---|---|
| Description | a **web**, with the topic in the centre and details around it |
| Sequence | a **timeline** or numbered **flow chart** |
| Compare and contrast | a **Venn diagram** (two overlapping circles) or a **T-chart** |
| Cause and effect | a **chain** of boxes with arrows from causes to effects |
| Problem and solution | a **chart** with a box for the problem, boxes for solutions, and the result |

:::example Choosing an organizer
A passage explains that a river flooded, so farmers planted late, so the harvest was small. Which organizer fits?
@step Each event causes the next one.
@answer A cause-and-effect chain: flood → late planting → small harvest.
:::

- The five structures are **description**, **sequence**, **compare and contrast**, **cause and effect**, and **problem and solution**.
- Signal words give clues, but choose the structure of the whole text.
- Match the organizer to the structure: web, timeline, Venn diagram, chain or problem-solution chart.
:::
