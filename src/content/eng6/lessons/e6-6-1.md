Pronouns save us from repeating nouns: instead of “Ana said Ana would bring Ana’s ball”, we say “Ana said she would bring her ball”. But pronouns change their form depending on their job in a sentence, and they must clearly point back to the right noun. In this lesson you will learn to choose the right pronoun every time.

## Subject and object pronouns

| Subject pronouns (do the action) | Object pronouns (receive the action, or follow a preposition) | Possessive pronouns |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | my, mine |
| you | you | your, yours |
| he, she, it | him, her, it | his, her, hers, its |
| we | us | our, ours |
| they | them | their, theirs |

:::example The cover-up test
> Grandma made lunch for Leo and (I / me).

@step Cover “Leo and”: *Grandma made lunch for ___.*
@step “for I” sounds wrong; “for me” sounds right. After the preposition *for*, use an object pronoun.
@answer Grandma made lunch for Leo and **me**.
:::

:::mistake "Me and her went" is not standard English
When pronouns are the subject, use subject pronouns: *She and I went*, not *Me and her went*. It is also polite to put yourself last: *Leo and I*, not *I and Leo*.
:::

Reflexive pronouns (*myself*, *himself*, *themselves*) are used when the subject acts on itself: *Kai taught himself to juggle.* They should not replace *I* or *me*: say *Give the book to me*, not *to myself*.

## Pronouns and antecedents

:::definition Antecedent
The **antecedent** is the noun a pronoun refers to. In *The dog wagged its tail*, the antecedent of *its* is *the dog*.
:::

A pronoun must **agree** with its antecedent: singular with singular, plural with plural.

| Sentence | Why |
|---|---|
| *The students handed in **their** projects.* | *students* is plural |
| *Each of the boys brought **his** own lunch.* | *each* is singular |
| *The team celebrated **its** victory.* | the team acts as one group |

Words such as *each*, *every*, *either* and *neither* are singular. When a singular antecedent could be anyone, many writers use *their*: *Everyone brought their lunch.*

:::example Fixing an unclear pronoun
> When the cup hit the plate, it broke.

@step *It* could mean the cup or the plate.
@step Replace the pronoun with the noun, or rewrite the sentence.
@answer *The plate broke when the cup hit it.*
:::

:::summary
- Use **subject pronouns** (I, he, she, we, they) for subjects and **object pronouns** (me, him, her, us, them) for objects and after prepositions.
- Cover the other name in a pair to test which pronoun fits.
- A pronoun must agree with its **antecedent** and point to it clearly.
:::
