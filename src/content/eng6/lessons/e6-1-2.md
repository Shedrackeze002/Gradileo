Characters in good stories do things for reasons, just as real people do. The reason behind what a character does is called their **motive**. Understanding motives helps you predict what characters will do next and explain why the story unfolds as it does. Many characters also **change** as the story goes on, and noticing how and why they change is one of the best ways to find a story’s meaning.

## Motive

:::definition Motive
A **motive** is the reason a character acts the way they do: what they want, fear or care about. Writers rarely state a motive directly, so readers **infer** it from what the character says, does and thinks.
:::

| What the character does | What it suggests about their motive |
|---|---|
| Saves every coin for three months | They want something badly, perhaps a gift for someone |
| Laughs too loudly at others’ jokes | They want to be liked or included |
| Says “Who cares?” but practises every night | They care a great deal and fear failing |

:::example Inferring a motive
> “Who cares about a drawing contest?” Leo shrugged. But every night that week he sat at his desk, drawing until midnight, and on Friday he was the first to check the results board.

Why does Leo pretend not to care?
@step His actions (drawing every night, rushing to see the results) show he cares very much.
@step Saying “Who cares?” protects him in case he loses.
@answer He is afraid of being disappointed.
:::

:::mistake Actions speak louder than words
A character’s words can hide their real motive. When what a character says and what they do disagree, trust the actions.
:::

## How characters change

:::definition Dynamic and static characters
- A **dynamic** character changes during the story, usually because of what happens to them. They might learn a lesson, become braver or see someone differently.
- A **static** character stays the same from beginning to end.
:::

To describe a change, compare the character at the **beginning** and at the **end**, and then find the **event** that caused it.

:::example Explaining a change
> Kai always made jokes about Sam’s old coat. Then, one afternoon, he found Sam crying behind the gym. After that, Kai never made a joke about anyone’s clothes again.

How does Kai change, and why?
@step At the beginning, Kai mocks Sam’s coat.
@step The turning event is finding Sam crying, which shows Kai how much his jokes hurt.
@answer Kai changes from unkind to thoughtful because he sees how his words hurt Sam.
:::

In Aesop’s fable “The Tortoise and the Hare”, the boastful Hare is the one who learns a lesson when he loses the race. The Tortoise is steady and patient from start to finish, so he is static.

:::summary
- A **motive** is the reason behind a character’s actions; infer it from what they say, do and think.
- When words and actions disagree, the actions usually show the real motive.
- A **dynamic** character changes; a **static** character stays the same.
- Explain a change by comparing the beginning and the end, and naming the event that caused it.
:::
