A **summary** is a short retelling of a text’s most important ideas, in your own words. Summarizing is one of the most useful reading skills there is: it shows that you have understood a text, and it helps you remember it. The hard part is deciding what to keep and what to leave out.

## What a good summary does

:::definition Summary
A good summary:
- states the **main idea** of the text;
- includes the **key ideas** that support it;
- leaves out **minor details**, examples and repetition;
- is **objective**: it reports what the text says, without your own opinions;
- is written **in your own words** and is much shorter than the original.
:::

> Every autumn, millions of monarch butterflies leave Canada and the northern United States and fly south to the mountains of central Mexico, a journey of more than 3,000 kilometres. No single butterfly makes the round trip. The monarchs that fly south in autumn spend the winter in the forests, and in spring they start north again, laying eggs on milkweed plants along the way. It takes three or four generations of butterflies to complete the journey back.

:::example Choosing the best summary
Which is the best summary of the passage?
- *Monarch butterflies lay their eggs on milkweed plants.*
- *Monarchs are the most amazing insects in the world.*
- *Monarchs migrate thousands of kilometres over several generations.*

@step The first is a minor detail.
@step The second is an opinion that the passage never states.
@answer The third: it gives the main idea and the key surprise, briefly and objectively.
:::

:::mistake Keep your opinion out
“Monarchs are amazing, and everyone should plant milkweed” may be true, but it is your view, not the passage’s. Save opinions for a response; a summary only reports.
:::

## Key ideas and minor details

A **key idea** is one the text needs to make its point. A **minor detail** adds colour or an example, but the text would still make sense without it.

| In the monarch passage | Key idea or minor detail? |
|---|---|
| Monarchs fly south to Mexico each autumn. | Key idea |
| No single butterfly makes the round trip. | Key idea |
| They lay eggs on milkweed plants. | Minor detail |
| The journey is more than 3,000 kilometres. | Minor detail (it supports how far they travel) |

A quick test: cover the idea up and ask whether the passage would lose its point. If it would, the idea is key.

:::summary
- A **summary** gives the main idea and key ideas, briefly and in your own words.
- Leave out minor details and your own opinions.
- A **key idea** is one the text cannot make its point without.
:::
