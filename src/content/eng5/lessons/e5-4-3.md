Even strong readers meet words they do not know. You do not always need a dictionary: the sentences around a new word often give you enough to work it out. These hints are called **context clues**.

## Kinds of context clues

| Clue | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | the sentence tells you the meaning | A **botanist**, a scientist who studies plants, visited our class. |
| **Synonym** | a word nearby means the same | The path was **narrow**, so thin that we walked in single file. |
| **Antonym** | a word nearby means the opposite | Unlike his **timid** sister, Raj was bold and loud. |
| **Example** | examples show what the word means | **Reptiles**, such as snakes, lizards and turtles, are cold-blooded. |
| **General sense** | the whole situation points to the meaning | After the long hike, we were so **famished** that we ate every crumb. |

:::example Using the clues
> The puppy was so **drowsy** after its walk that it curled up and fell asleep at once.

What does *drowsy* mean?
@step The puppy curled up and fell asleep straight away.
@step So it must have been feeling ready to sleep.
@answer Sleepy.
:::

## Checking your guess

Once you have a guess, put it back into the sentence in place of the hard word. If the sentence still makes sense, your guess is probably right.

:::example An antonym clue
> Mira’s room was usually **spotless**, but today it was messy and covered in toys.

What does *spotless* mean?
@step *But* signals a contrast: *spotless* is the opposite of *messy*.
@step Try “very clean”: *Mira’s room was usually very clean, but today it was messy.* It makes sense.
@answer Very clean.
:::

:::mistake Do not stop at the word itself
Looking only at the word often leads to a wrong guess. *Famished* sounds a bit like *famous*, but the clue “we ate every crumb” tells you it means very hungry. Always read the whole sentence, and the one after it.
:::

:::summary
- **Context clues** are hints in the nearby words: a definition, a synonym, an antonym, examples or the general sense.
- Signal words such as *or*, *such as*, *unlike* and *but* often point to a clue.
- Test your guess by putting it back into the sentence.
:::
