Good writers rarely get everything right in the first draft. They go back and make it better. **Revising** means improving what the writing says and how it sounds. **Editing** means fixing mistakes. In this lesson you will practise both.

## Editing: fixing mistakes

When you edit, check four things, one at a time. A handy way to remember them is **CUPS**.

| Letter | Check | Example fix |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Capitals | *on monday* → *On Monday* |
| **U** | Usage (grammar) | *The dogs runs* → *The dogs run* |
| **P** | Punctuation | *Where are you going.* → *Where are you going?* |
| **S** | Spelling | *becuase* → *because*; *their going* → *they’re going* |

:::example Editing a sentence
> my freind and me went to the park on saturday

@step **C**: *My* starts the sentence, and *Saturday* is a day.
@step **U**: *my friend and I went* (you would say “I went”, not “me went”).
@step **P**: the sentence needs a period. **S**: *freind* is spelled *friend*.
@answer My friend and I went to the park on Saturday.
:::

:::mistake Read it aloud
Many mistakes are easier to hear than to see. Reading your writing aloud, slowly, helps you notice missing words, run-ons and verbs that do not agree.
:::

## Revising: combining sentences

Too many short sentences in a row make writing sound choppy. Combining them makes it flow.

| Choppy | Combined | How |
|---|---|---|
| *The cat is black. The cat is fluffy.* | *The cat is black and fluffy.* | join two describing words |
| *Maya likes soccer. Jun likes soccer.* | *Maya and Jun like soccer.* | join two subjects |
| *It was raining. We stayed inside.* | *It was raining, so we stayed inside.* | join with a comma and *so* |
| *I was tired. I finished my homework.* | *Although I was tired, I finished my homework.* | join with *although* |

:::example Combining two sentences
> The bus was late. We missed the first bell.

@step The first sentence causes the second.
@step Join them with *so* or *because* to show that.
@answer *The bus was late, so we missed the first bell.*
:::

Notice that when two subjects are joined (*Maya and Jun*), the verb changes from *likes* to *like*, because the subject is now plural.

:::summary
- **Revising** improves the writing; **editing** fixes mistakes.
- Edit with **CUPS**: capitals, usage, punctuation, spelling.
- Combine short, choppy sentences by joining describing words or subjects, or with *and, but, so, because* or *although*.
:::
