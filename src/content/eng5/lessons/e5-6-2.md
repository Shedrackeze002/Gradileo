A **narrative** is a piece of writing that tells a story. It can be true, like a story about your first day at a new school, or made up. In this lesson you will learn two tools that make narratives better: **time-order words**, which move the story along, and **showing**, which brings the characters to life.

## Moving the story along

A narrative tells events in order, with a beginning, a middle and an end. **Time-order words** (also called *transition words*) tell the reader when each event happens.

| Beginning | Middle | End |
|---|---|---|
| *First, One morning, At the start* | *Next, Then, After that, Later, Meanwhile, Suddenly* | *Finally, In the end, At last* |

:::example Choosing a time-order word
> \_\_\_\_\_, after three hours of searching, we found the lost kitten asleep in the laundry basket.

Which word fits: *Finally*, *First* or *Meanwhile*?
@step The search took three hours and this is when it ended.
@step The word must show the end of a series of events.
@answer Finally
:::

:::note Meanwhile and suddenly
*Meanwhile* means “at the same time, somewhere else”: *Meanwhile, back at home, Mom was baking.* *Suddenly* shows that something happened quickly and by surprise.
:::

## Show, don’t tell

When a writer **tells**, they name a feeling: *Ana was nervous.* When a writer **shows**, they describe what the character does, says and notices, so the reader works out the feeling: *Ana’s hands shook as she stepped up to the microphone, and her voice came out as a whisper.*

| Telling | Showing |
|---|---|
| Leo was happy. | Leo grinned and punched the air. |
| The room was messy. | Socks and comic books covered every inch of the floor. |
| Grandma was tired. | Grandma yawned and sank into her chair. |

:::example Showing a feeling
Which sentence **shows** that Ben was angry?
@step A telling sentence would use the word *angry* or *mad*.
@step A showing sentence describes actions: a red face, a slammed door, clenched fists.
@answer *Ben’s face turned red, and he slammed his book shut.*
:::

:::mistake Showing is not adding the feeling word twice
“Ben was very, very angry” still only tells. To show, replace the feeling word with actions, words and details that let the reader see it.
:::

:::summary
- A **narrative** tells events in order, with a beginning, a middle and an end.
- **Time-order words** (*first, then, later, finally*) show when events happen.
- **Show, don’t tell**: describe actions and details instead of naming the feeling.
:::
