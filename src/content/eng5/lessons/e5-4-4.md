Some words sound exactly alike but are spelled differently. Others are spelled exactly alike but mean different things. Both can trip up a reader or a writer. In this lesson you will learn to choose the right **homophone** and to tell which meaning of a **multiple-meaning word** a sentence uses.

## Homophones

:::definition Homophones
**Homophones** are words that **sound the same** but have **different spellings and meanings**: *flour* (for baking) and *flower* (in a garden).
:::

| Homophones | Meanings |
|---|---|
| their / there / they’re | belonging to them / in that place / they are |
| to / too / two | towards / also or too much / the number 2 |
| your / you’re | belonging to you / you are |
| piece / peace | a part of something / calm, no fighting |
| weak / week | not strong / seven days |
| hole / whole | an opening / all of something |
| knew / new | did know / not old |

:::example Choosing the right homophone
> The children left \_\_\_\_\_ bikes by the gate.

*their*, *there* or *they’re*?
@step The bikes belong to the children.
@step The word for “belonging to them” is *their*.
@answer their
:::

:::mistake Test the short forms
*They’re* and *you’re* are short for *they are* and *you are*. Say the long form in the sentence: “They are bikes by the gate”? That does not make sense, so *they’re* is wrong.
:::

## Multiple-meaning words

:::definition Multiple-meaning word
A **multiple-meaning word** is spelled and said the same way but has more than one meaning: a baseball **bat** and a **bat** that flies at night.
:::

The rest of the sentence tells you which meaning is used.

| Word | One meaning | Another meaning |
|---|---|---|
| bark | the sound a dog makes | the outside of a tree trunk |
| ring | a band worn on a finger | the sound of a bell |
| light | not heavy | brightness, as from a lamp |
| fair | right and equal for everyone | an outdoor show with rides |

:::example Which meaning?
> The box of feathers was so **light** that the baby could carry it.

Which meaning of *light* is used?
@step The baby could *carry* the box, so the sentence is about weight.
@answer Not heavy.
:::

:::summary
- **Homophones** sound the same but are spelled differently and mean different things.
- Test *they’re* and *you’re* by saying *they are* and *you are*.
- A **multiple-meaning word** has one spelling but several meanings; the sentence shows which one.
:::
