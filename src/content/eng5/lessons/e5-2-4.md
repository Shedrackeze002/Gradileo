Poems come in many shapes. Some follow strict rules about syllables or rhyme, and some follow no rules at all. In this lesson you will learn four forms, and how to talk about the parts of a poem: its **lines** and **stanzas**.

## Lines and stanzas

:::definition Line and stanza
- A **line** is one row of words in a poem. A poem’s lines do not have to be full sentences.
- A **stanza** is a group of lines, set apart from the next group by a blank space. Stanzas work like paragraphs in a story.
:::

:::example Counting lines and stanzas
> The bus is late, the sky is grey,\
> We stamp our feet and blow.\
> At last it rumbles round the bend\
> And splashes through the snow.
>
> We tumble in and find our seats\
> And draw on misty glass;\
> The driver hums a cheerful tune\
> And lets the traffic pass.
>
> At school we jump down one by one\
> And race in through the gate.
>
> — written for Gradileo

How many lines and how many stanzas does this poem have?
@step Count the groups separated by blank space: 3 stanzas.
@step Count the rows of words in each: 4 + 4 + 2.
@answer 10 lines in 3 stanzas.
:::

## Four forms of poetry

| Form | The rules | Example idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Haiku** | 3 lines of 5, 7 and 5 syllables, often about nature; no rhyme needed | a frog jumping into a pond |
| **Limerick** | 5 lines, rhyme pattern AABBA, funny, with a bouncy rhythm | a silly person and a silly problem |
| **Acrostic** | the first letters of the lines spell a word, read downwards | a poem about the SEA |
| **Free verse** | no fixed rhyme or rhythm; lines can be any length | any subject, in natural speech |

Here is a limerick by Edward Lear. Look for the AABBA rhymes: *beard*, *feared*, *Hen*, *Wren*, *beard*.

> There was an Old Man with a beard,\
> Who said, “It is just as I feared!\
> Two Owls and a Hen,\
> Four Larks and a Wren,\
> Have all built their nests in my beard!”
>
> — Edward Lear, A Book of Nonsense (1846)

And here is a haiku written for Gradileo. Count the syllables in each line: 5, 7, 5.

> Old pond, still and green,\
> a frog leaps in with a splash,\
> then silence again.

:::mistake Three lines is not enough for a haiku
A poem with three lines is not a haiku unless its lines have 5, 7 and 5 syllables. Always count the syllables before you name the form.
:::

:::summary
- A **line** is one row of a poem; a **stanza** is a group of lines set apart by space.
- A **haiku** has 3 lines of 5, 7 and 5 syllables.
- A **limerick** has 5 funny lines that rhyme AABBA.
- An **acrostic** spells a word with the first letters of its lines.
- **Free verse** has no fixed rhyme or rhythm.
:::
