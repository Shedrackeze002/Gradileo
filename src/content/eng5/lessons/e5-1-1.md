Every story you have ever enjoyed, from a bedtime fable to a long adventure novel, is built from the same three parts: the people (or animals) it is about, the place and time it happens, and the events that take place. Readers call these the **story elements**. In this lesson you will learn to find each one.

## Characters, setting and plot

:::definition The three story elements
- **Characters** are the people, animals or creatures in a story. The **main character** is the one the story follows most closely.
- The **setting** is *where* and *when* the story happens: a place, a time of day, a season or a time in history.
- The **plot** is *what happens*: the events of the story, one after another.
:::

Here is the opening of a story written for Gradileo:

> Before the sun was up, Mei pulled on her boots and crunched across the frosty yard to the barn. Snow lay on the fence posts, and the cows’ breath hung in clouds.

| Element | In this passage |
|---|---|
| Character | Mei (and the cows) |
| Setting | A farm, early on a winter morning |
| Plot | Mei goes out to the barn |

## Who tells the story?

The voice that tells a story is the **narrator**. If the narrator uses *I* and *my*, the narrator is a character in the story. If the narrator uses *he*, *she* and *they* for everyone, the narrator is outside the story, watching.

:::example Finding the narrator
> I will never forget the day my grandmother’s dog, Biscuit, ate my birthday cake.

Who is telling this story?
@step Look for the words *I* and *my*.
@step The person telling it is part of the events: it was *their* cake.
@answer A narrator who is a character in the story.
:::

## Clues to the setting

Writers rarely say “The setting is a beach in summer.” Instead they leave **clues**: what the characters see, hear and wear, and what the weather is like.

:::example Reading the clues
> Red and gold leaves drifted down from the maple trees, and Kwame zipped up his jacket against the cool wind.

When does this passage take place?
@step Red and gold leaves falling from trees happen in one season.
@step A cool wind and a jacket fit the same season.
@answer In autumn (fall).
:::

:::mistake The setting is more than a place
The setting is *where* **and** *when*. A story set in a city long ago, with horses and candles, feels very different from a story set in the same city today. Look for clues to the time as well as the place.
:::

:::summary
- **Characters** are who the story is about; the **main character** is the one it follows most.
- The **setting** is where and when the story happens; find it from clues in the text.
- The **plot** is what happens, event by event.
- A narrator who says *I* is a character in the story.
:::
