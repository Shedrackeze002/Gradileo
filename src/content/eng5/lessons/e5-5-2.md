A sentence is a group of words that expresses a **complete thought**. Writers sometimes leave a sentence unfinished, or run two sentences together without stopping. In this lesson you will learn what every sentence needs, and how to spot sentences that are broken.

## Subject and predicate

:::definition Subject and predicate
Every complete sentence has two parts.
- The **subject** tells **who or what** the sentence is about.
- The **predicate** tells what the subject **does or is**. It always contains a verb.
:::

| Subject | Predicate |
|---|---|
| My little brother | laughed at the clown. |
| The old wooden bridge | creaked in the wind. |
| We | are going to the museum tomorrow. |

The **simple subject** is the main noun or pronoun in the subject, without its describing words. In *The old wooden bridge creaked*, the simple subject is *bridge*.

:::example Splitting a sentence
> The students in Room 4 planted a garden.

@step Who is the sentence about? *The students in Room 4*: that is the subject.
@step What did they do? *planted a garden*: that is the predicate.
@answer Subject: *The students in Room 4*. Predicate: *planted a garden*. Simple subject: *students*.
:::

## Fragments and run-ons

:::definition Fragment and run-on
- A **fragment** is only part of a sentence. It is missing a subject or a predicate, or it does not finish its thought: *Running down the hill.* *Because it was raining.*
- A **run-on** is two sentences joined with no end mark or joining word: *The bell rang we went outside.*
:::

| Problem | Example | Fixed |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment (no subject) | *Ate all the cookies.* | *Leo ate all the cookies.* |
| Fragment (unfinished) | *When the bus came.* | *When the bus came, we got on.* |
| Run-on | *I like cats my sister likes dogs.* | *I like cats. My sister likes dogs.* or *I like cats, but my sister likes dogs.* |

:::mistake A capital and a period do not make a sentence
*Because the power went out.* starts with a capital and ends with a period, but the thought is not finished: what happened because the power went out? Read it aloud and ask whether it makes sense on its own.
:::

:::summary
- A sentence has a **subject** (who or what) and a **predicate** (what the subject does or is).
- The **simple subject** is the main noun or pronoun.
- A **fragment** is an incomplete sentence; a **run-on** joins two sentences with no break.
- Fix a run-on with a period, or with a comma and *and*, *but* or *so*.
:::
