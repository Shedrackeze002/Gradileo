English has many words that mean nearly the same thing, and many that mean the opposite. Choosing between them is how a writer says exactly what they mean. In this lesson you will learn about **synonyms**, **antonyms** and **shades of meaning**.

## Synonyms and antonyms

:::definition Synonym and antonym
- **Synonyms** are words with the same or nearly the same meaning: *big* and *large*, *quick* and *fast*.
- **Antonyms** are words with opposite meanings: *big* and *small*, *arrive* and *leave*.
:::

| Word | A synonym | An antonym |
|---|---|---|
| happy | glad | sad |
| brave | bold | afraid |
| ancient | old | modern |
| begin | start | finish |

A good test for a synonym is to swap it into a sentence. If the sentence still means the same, you have found a synonym: *The* ***large*** *dog barked* means the same as *The* ***big*** *dog barked*.

## Shades of meaning

Synonyms are rarely exactly the same. Some are stronger than others. Words that mean almost the same thing but differ in strength are called **shades of meaning**, like shades of one colour from light to dark.

| Weaker | Middle | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| warm | hot | boiling |
| like | love | adore |
| damp | wet | soaked |
| glance | look | stare |

:::example Putting words in order
Order these words from the weakest to the strongest: *furious, annoyed, angry*.
@step *Annoyed* is a little bit cross.
@step *Angry* is more; *furious* is very, very angry.
@answer annoyed, angry, furious.
:::

Choosing the right shade matters. Saying you were *terrified* of a small spider tells the reader something very different from saying you were *nervous*.
