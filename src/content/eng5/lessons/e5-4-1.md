Many long words are really a short word with an extra piece stuck on the front or the end. If you know what the pieces mean, you can work out words you have never seen before. In this lesson you will learn the most common of these pieces, **prefixes** and **suffixes**.

## Base words and prefixes

:::definition Base word and prefix
- A **base word** is a word that can stand on its own: *happy*, *read*, *agree*.
- A **prefix** is a word part added to the **front** of a base word. It changes the meaning: *un* + *happy* = *unhappy*, “not happy”.
:::

| Prefix | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| un- | not | unhappy, unkind, unsafe |
| re- | again | retell, rewrite, rebuild |
| pre- | before | preheat, preview |
| mis- | wrongly | misspell, misread |
| dis- | not, or the opposite of | disagree, dishonest, disappear |
| non- | not | nonstop, nonfiction |
| over- | too much | overcooked, overslept |
| sub- | under | submarine, subway |

:::example Working out a new word
What does *reheat* mean?
@step Split it: *re* + *heat*.
@step *re-* means “again”.
@answer To heat again.
:::

:::mistake Not every word that starts with "re" has a prefix
In *read* and *real*, the letters *re* are part of the base word; take them off and nothing is left that makes sense. Check that what is left is a real word before you treat the start as a prefix.
:::

## Suffixes

:::definition Suffix
A **suffix** is a word part added to the **end** of a base word: *hope* + *ful* = *hopeful*, “full of hope”. A suffix often changes the kind of word too: *kind* (adjective) becomes *kindness* (noun).
:::

| Suffix | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -ful | full of | hopeful, joyful, careful |
| -less | without | fearless, careless, endless |
| -er | a person who | teacher, painter, farmer |
| -able | able to be | washable, breakable |
| -ly | in a … way | quickly, softly, bravely |
| -ness | the state of being | kindness, darkness |
| -ment | the act or result of | enjoyment, movement |

Notice that *careful* and *careless* are opposites: the suffix alone flips the meaning.

:::example A word with two parts added
What does *unbreakable* mean?
@step Split it: *un* + *break* + *able*.
@step *break* + *able* = able to be broken; *un-* = not.
@answer Not able to be broken.
:::

:::summary
- A **prefix** goes at the front of a base word; a **suffix** goes at the end.
- *un-, dis-, non-* = not; *re-* = again; *pre-* = before; *mis-* = wrongly.
- *-ful* = full of; *-less* = without; *-er* = a person who; *-able* = able to be.
- Split a long word into its parts, then put the meanings together.
:::
