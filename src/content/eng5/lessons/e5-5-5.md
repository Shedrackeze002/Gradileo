A comma is a short pause. It helps the reader see which words belong together. Quotation marks show the exact words a character says. In this lesson you will learn the main comma rules and how to punctuate dialogue.

## Commas

| Use a comma | Example |
|---|---|
| between items in a list | *We bought apples, pears and grapes.* |
| after an introductory word | *Yes, I would like to come.* *Well, I think so.* |
| after an introductory phrase | *After dinner, we played a board game.* |
| when speaking to someone by name | *Lina, can you help me?* |
| in a date | *Saturday, June 14, 2025* |
| between a city and a province or state | *Halifax, Nova Scotia* |
| before *and*, *but* or *so* joining two sentences | *I wanted to swim, but the pool was closed.* |

:::note The comma before "and" in a list
In this course, lists are written without a comma before the final *and*: *red, white and blue*. Some writers add one (*red, white, and blue*). Either is correct if you use it the same way every time.
:::

:::example Placing the commas
> On Monday June 2 we will visit Victoria British Columbia.

@step *On Monday* begins the sentence, and the date needs a comma after the day: *On Monday, June 2,*.
@step The city and province need a comma between them: *Victoria, British Columbia*.
@answer On Monday, June 2, we will visit Victoria, British Columbia.
:::

:::mistake A name in a sentence is not always spoken to
In *Lina can help me*, we are talking *about* Lina, so no comma is needed. In *Lina, can you help me?* we are talking *to* her, so her name is set off with a comma.
:::

## Quotation marks

:::definition Quotation marks
**Quotation marks** (“ ”) go around the **exact words** a person says. The end mark of the spoken words goes **inside** the closing quotation mark.
:::

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Speaker first | *Dad said, “Dinner is ready.”* |
| Speaker last | *“Dinner is ready,” Dad said.* |
| A question | *“Is dinner ready?” asked Mia.* |

Notice that when the speaker comes last, the spoken sentence ends with a **comma** inside the quotation marks, not a period, because the whole sentence is not finished yet.

:::example Punctuating dialogue
> Can we go to the park asked Omar

@step The exact words are *Can we go to the park*. They ask a question.
@step The question mark goes inside the closing quotation mark, and the sentence ends with a period.
@answer “Can we go to the park?” asked Omar.
:::

:::summary
- Commas go between list items, after introductory words and phrases, around names of people spoken to, in dates and places, and before *and*, *but* or *so* joining two sentences.
- Quotation marks go around a speaker’s exact words.
- The end mark or comma of the spoken words goes inside the closing quotation mark.
:::
