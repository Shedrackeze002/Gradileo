Think of a friend. You could describe what they look like, but what makes them *them* is what they are like inside: funny, kind, brave or stubborn. Characters in stories are the same. In this lesson you will learn to work out what a character is like, and how they feel, even when the writer does not say it.

## Character traits

:::definition Character trait
A **character trait** is a word that describes what a character is like on the inside, such as *honest*, *patient*, *brave*, *selfish* or *curious*. A trait stays fairly steady through a story.
:::

Writers usually **show** traits through what a character **does**, **says** and **thinks**, and how others react to them. You, the reader, have to **infer**: put the clues together with what you already know.

:::example Inferring a trait
> Liam found a ten-dollar note on the floor of the shop. He carried it straight to the counter and said, “Someone must have dropped this.”

Which word best describes Liam?
@step What does Liam do? He hands in money he could have kept.
@step What kind of person does that? Someone who tells the truth and does the right thing.
@answer Liam is **honest**.
:::

## Feelings

A **feeling** is how a character feels at one moment: *excited*, *nervous*, *relieved*, *jealous*. Unlike a trait, a feeling can change from one page to the next.

| The character… | The feeling might be… |
|---|---|
| jumps up and down, grins | excited |
| stomps, slams a door | angry |
| holds the straps of a backpack tightly | nervous |
| lets out a long breath and laughs | relieved |

:::mistake A trait is not the same as a feeling
*Nervous before a test* is a feeling: it passes. *Shy* is a trait: it is how the character usually is. When a question asks what a character is **like**, give a trait. When it asks how a character **feels**, give a feeling.
:::

## Characters change

In many stories the main character changes: a nervous speaker becomes confident, or a selfish character learns to share. Compare how the character acts at the beginning with how they act at the end.

:::summary
- A **trait** is what a character is like; a **feeling** is how they feel at one moment.
- Infer traits and feelings from what characters **do**, **say** and **think**.
- Support your answer with **evidence**: the words in the story that show it.
- Compare the beginning and the end to see how a character **changes**.
:::
