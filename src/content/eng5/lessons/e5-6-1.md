A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about one main idea. Good paragraphs make writing easy to follow, because the reader always knows what each part is about. In this lesson you will learn the three parts of a paragraph and how to keep every sentence on topic.

## The parts of a paragraph

:::definition Topic sentence, body and closing sentence
- The **topic sentence** states the main idea. It usually comes first.
- The **body** sentences give details, examples and reasons that support the main idea.
- The **closing sentence** wraps up the paragraph, often by restating the main idea in new words.
:::

> **Recess is the best part of the school day.** It gives us time to run around after sitting still all morning. We can play games with friends from other classes. Fresh air also helps us feel awake for the afternoon. **For all these reasons, recess is the part of the day I look forward to most.**

The first bold sentence is the topic sentence and the last is the closing sentence. The three sentences between them are the body.

:::example Choosing a topic sentence
Which topic sentence fits this body?

> Some dogs guide people who cannot see. Others sniff out lost hikers in the mountains. Many dogs visit hospitals to cheer up patients.

@step Every body sentence describes a way dogs help people.
@step The topic sentence must cover all three, not just one.
@answer *Dogs do many jobs that help people.*
:::

:::mistake A topic sentence is not a detail
“Some dogs guide people who cannot see” is too narrow to be the topic sentence of that paragraph, because it covers only one of the three jobs. A topic sentence must be wide enough to hold every detail.
:::

## Keeping to the topic

A paragraph has **unity** when every sentence supports the main idea. A sentence that wanders off topic distracts the reader, even if it is interesting.

:::example Finding the sentence that does not belong
> Penguins are birds that cannot fly. Their wings work like flippers for swimming. They waddle on land and slide on their bellies over ice. My uncle saw a penguin at the zoo last year. Under water, a penguin can swim faster than a person can run.

Which sentence does not belong?
@step The main idea is about penguins: how they move, since they cannot fly.
@step One sentence is about the writer’s uncle, not about how penguins move.
@answer *My uncle saw a penguin at the zoo last year.*
:::

:::summary
- A paragraph has a **topic sentence**, **body** sentences and a **closing sentence**.
- The topic sentence must be wide enough to cover every detail.
- Every sentence should support the main idea; take out any that wander off topic.
:::
