Imagine a story where nothing goes wrong: a girl walks to the park, plays, and walks home. It would be very boring! Stories need a **problem**, something that goes wrong or a goal that is hard to reach. In this lesson you will learn to find a story’s problem and solution, and to keep its events in order.

## Problem and solution

:::definition Problem and solution
- The **problem** is what goes wrong, or what the main character wants but cannot easily get.
- The **events** are what the characters do about it.
- The **solution** is how the problem is solved, usually near the end.
:::

:::example The Crow and the Pitcher
> A thirsty crow found a pitcher with a little water at the bottom, but her beak could not reach it. She picked up a pebble and dropped it in, then another, and another. Slowly the water rose until she could drink.
>
> — retold from Aesop

What is the problem, and how is it solved?
@step What goes wrong? The crow is thirsty but cannot reach the water.
@step What does she do? She drops pebbles in, one by one.
@answer The water rises until she can drink: that is the solution.
:::

## Beginning, middle and end

Most stories move in order from a **beginning** (who, where and the problem) through a **middle** (events that try to solve it) to an **end** (the solution). This order is called the **sequence**.

| Part | “The Tortoise and the Hare” |
|---|---|
| Beginning | The hare laughs at the slow tortoise, and they agree to race. |
| Middle | The hare runs ahead and takes a nap; the tortoise keeps going. |
| End | The tortoise passes the sleeping hare and wins. |

Signal words help you follow the sequence: *first*, *then*, *next*, *later*, *after that*, *finally*.

:::mistake A detail is not the problem
In “Ravi has a new costume and his family is in the audience, but he forgets his lines”, the costume and the audience are details. The **problem** is the thing that goes wrong: Ravi forgets his lines.

:::summary
- The **problem** is what goes wrong; the **solution** is how it is fixed.
- Stories move from **beginning** to **middle** to **end**.
- Put events in **sequence** by asking what has to happen before each one.
- Signal words such as *first*, *then* and *finally* show the order.
:::
