Writers organize information in different ways depending on what they want to explain. The way a text is organized is its **structure**. Small **signal words** give clues to the structure, a bit like road signs that tell you which way the ideas are going.

## Four text structures

| Structure | What it does | Signal words |
|---|---|---|
| **Sequence** | tells steps or events in order | *first, next, then, after, finally* |
| **Compare and contrast** | shows how things are alike and different | *both, also, like, but, however, unlike* |
| **Cause and effect** | tells why something happens and what it leads to | *because, so, as a result, since, therefore* |
| **Problem and solution** | names a problem and how it was or could be fixed | *problem, solve, solution, to fix this* |

:::example Naming the structure
> Frogs and toads are both amphibians that lay their eggs in water. However, frogs have smooth, wet skin, while toads have dry, bumpy skin.

What is the structure of this paragraph?
@step *Both* tells how they are alike.
@step *However* and *while* tell how they are different.
@answer Compare and contrast.
:::

:::mistake Cause and effect is not the same as sequence
Two events can come one after another without one *causing* the other. “First we ate lunch, then it rained” is sequence. “Because it rained, the game was cancelled” is cause and effect: the rain made the game stop.
:::

## Using signal words

Signal words help you as a reader and as a writer. When you see *as a result*, get ready for an effect. When you see *however*, get ready for a difference.

:::example Choosing a signal word
> The river flooded the road. \_\_\_\_\_, the school bus had to take a longer route.

Which signal word fits best: *As a result*, *First* or *Both*?
@step The flood *caused* the bus to change its route.
@step A cause-and-effect signal word is needed.
@answer As a result.
:::

:::summary
- **Sequence**: steps in order (*first, next, finally*).
- **Compare and contrast**: alike and different (*both, however, unlike*).
- **Cause and effect**: why something happens (*because, as a result*).
- **Problem and solution**: a problem and its fix (*to solve this*).
:::
