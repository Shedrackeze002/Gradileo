In an **opinion piece** you tell the reader what you think and try to convince them to agree. An opinion on its own is easy to ignore; an opinion with strong reasons is hard to argue with. In this lesson you will learn how to build an opinion piece and link its ideas together.

## Opinion, reasons and conclusion

:::definition The parts of an opinion piece
- The **opinion** states clearly what you think: *Our school should have a vegetable garden.*
- The **reasons** explain why. Each reason is backed by **evidence**: a fact, an example or an expert’s words.
- The **conclusion** restates the opinion and leaves the reader with something to think about.
:::

:::example A strong reason or a weak one?
Opinion: *Our class should go to the science centre for our field trip.*

Which reason is strongest?
@step “I just want to” is about the writer’s wish, not a reason the reader can share.
@step “We are studying electricity, and the science centre has a hands-on electricity exhibit” connects to the class and can be checked.
@answer The reason about the electricity exhibit.
:::

A strong reason matters to the **reader**, not only to the writer, and it can be supported with evidence.

:::mistake Repeating the opinion is not a reason
“We should go to the science centre because it is the best place to go” just says the opinion again in different words. A reason must explain *why*.
:::

## Linking words

**Linking words** join your opinion, reasons and examples so the reader can follow your thinking.

| To do this | Use |
|---|---|
| give a reason | *because, since, so that* |
| add another reason | *also, in addition, another reason is* |
| give an example | *for example, for instance, such as* |
| show the other side | *however, but, although* |
| conclude | *for these reasons, in conclusion, that is why* |

:::example Choosing a linking word
> Dogs make great pets. \_\_\_\_\_, they can be trained to fetch, sit and shake hands.

@step The second sentence gives an example of why dogs are great pets.
@answer For example

:::summary
- An opinion piece has a clear **opinion**, **reasons** backed by **evidence**, and a **conclusion**.
- A strong reason matters to the reader and can be supported; repeating the opinion is not a reason.
- **Linking words** (*because, for example, however, for these reasons*) join the ideas.
:::
