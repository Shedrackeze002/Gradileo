Stories are not the only things we read. Every day we also read to learn: a page about volcanoes, a sign at the zoo, an article about a new playground. These are **informational texts**. The first question to ask about any of them is: *what is this mostly about?*

## The main idea

:::definition Main idea and topic
- The **topic** is the subject of a paragraph, in a word or two: *bees*.
- The **main idea** is the most important point the writer makes about the topic, as a full sentence: *Bees are important because they help plants grow.*
:::

The main idea is often stated in the first or last sentence of a paragraph, but not always. If it is not stated, ask: *what do all the sentences have in common?*

:::example Finding the main idea
> Camels are well suited to life in the desert. Their humps store fat, which their bodies can use for energy when food is hard to find. Thick eyelashes keep sand out of their eyes, and wide feet stop them from sinking into soft sand.

What is the main idea?
@step The topic is camels.
@step Every sentence after the first gives one way camels cope with the desert.
@answer Camels are well suited to life in the desert.
:::

:::mistake A detail is not the main idea
“Camels have thick eyelashes” is true, and it is in the paragraph, but it is only one small part. The main idea covers the whole paragraph, not just one sentence of it.
:::

## Supporting details

:::definition Supporting details
**Supporting details** are the facts, examples and reasons that explain or prove the main idea.
:::

Think of a table: the main idea is the tabletop, and the details are the legs holding it up. In the camel paragraph, the humps, the eyelashes and the wide feet are the three legs.

| Main idea | Supporting details |
|---|---|
| Camels are suited to the desert. | Humps store fat. Eyelashes keep out sand. Wide feet do not sink. |

A sentence that is interesting but does not explain the main idea is not a supporting detail. “Camels can live for about forty years” is a fact about camels, but it does not show how they cope with the desert.

:::summary
- The **topic** is the subject; the **main idea** is the writer’s most important point about it.
- Look for the main idea in the first or last sentence, or ask what all the sentences share.
- **Supporting details** explain or prove the main idea.
:::
