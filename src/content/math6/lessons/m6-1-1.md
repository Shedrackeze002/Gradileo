On a January morning in a northern town, it can be $-25^\circ\text{C}$. A loon can dive under the water of a lake. A friend can owe you \$5. We use negative numbers to describe all of these.

## Numbers below zero

:::definition Positive and negative numbers
A **positive number** is greater than $0$, like $8$. You can write it with or without a plus sign: $+8$ and $8$ are the same number.
A **negative number** is less than $0$. It always has a negative sign, like $-8$.
:::

:::note Zero
Zero is neither positive nor negative. It is the starting point.
:::

Negative numbers tell us about things below a starting point of $0$:

- **Temperature:** $8$ degrees below zero is $-8^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Debt:** owing \$8 is a debt of \$8. We write it as $-8$ dollars.
- **Elevation:** a diver $8$ metres below the surface of a lake is at $-8$ m.

:::example Writing a number for a situation
Write a positive or negative number for each situation: a) a temperature of $15$ degrees below zero, b) having \$40 in a piggy bank, c) an elevator stop $2$ floors below the ground floor.
@step Below zero means negative, so a) is $-15$.
@step Money that is saved is positive, so b) is $40$ (or $+40$).
@step The ground floor is $0$. Below it is negative, so c) is $-2$.
@answer a) $-15$ b) $40$ c) $-2$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the negative sign
"$15$ degrees below zero" is $-15$, not $15$. Without the negative sign, it means $15$ degrees **above** zero. That is a warm spring day, not a cold winter one!
:::

## Horizontal and vertical number lines

A number line can go across or up and down.

On a **horizontal** number line, positive numbers are to the right of $0$. Negative numbers are to the left of $0$.

On a **vertical** number line, positive numbers are above $0$. Negative numbers are below $0$. A thermometer is a vertical number line.

The thermometer shows the temperature in Riverton at 7 a.m. What is the temperature?
@step Find $0$. The top of the coloured bar is below $0$, so the temperature is negative.

## Magnitude

:::definition Magnitude
The **magnitude** of a number is its distance from $0$ on the number line. It counts the units from $0$, so it is never negative.
:::

$-7$ and $7$ are on different sides of $0$, but they are both $7$ units from $0$. They have the same magnitude: $7$.

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-8,"max":8,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-7,"label":"−7"},{"value":7,"label":"7"}]}
:::

The sign tells the **direction** from $0$. The magnitude tells **how far** from $0$.

:::example A diver in a lake
A diver is at $-9$ m. Heights are measured from the surface of the water, which is $0$ m. How far below the surface is the diver?
@step The negative sign tells the direction: below the surface.
@step The magnitude of $-9$ is $9$.
@answer The diver is $9$ m below the surface.
:::

:::mistake A magnitude is not negative
The magnitude of $-9$ is $9$, not $-9$. A distance is never negative. You cannot walk $-9$ steps!
:::

:::example Which has the greater magnitude?
Which number has the greater magnitude: $-12$ or $5$?
@step $-12$ is $12$ units from $0$. $5$ is $5$ units from $0$.
@answer $12 > 5$, so $-12$ has the greater magnitude, even though it is the smaller number.
:::

## Opposites

:::definition Opposite (additive inverse)
Two numbers are **opposites** when they have the same magnitude but different signs, like $3$ and $-3$. A number and its opposite are called **additive inverses**.
:::

The number line is like a mirror at $0$. Every positive number has an opposite negative number the same distance away on the other side.

:::example Finding an opposite
What is the opposite of $-12$?
@step $-12$ is $12$ units to the left of $0$.
@step The number $12$ units to the right of $0$ is $12$.
@answer The opposite of $-12$ is $12$.
:::

:::example Two opposites
Two numbers are opposites. On a number line, they are $10$ units apart. What are the numbers?
@step Opposites are the same distance from $0$, so $0$ is halfway between them.
@step Half of $10$ is $5$. Each number is $5$ units from $0$.
@answer The numbers are $5$ and $-5$.
:::

:::tip Opposites in real life
If Kiona owes \$14, her money is $-14$ dollars. To get back to \$0, she needs to earn the opposite: \$14.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Negative numbers are less than $0$ and have a negative sign. Positive numbers are greater than $0$. Zero is neither.
- We use negative numbers for temperatures below zero, debts, and places below the surface or below ground.
- Negative numbers are left of $0$ on a horizontal number line and below $0$ on a vertical one.
- Magnitude is the distance from $0$. The magnitude of $-9$ is $9$.
- Opposites (additive inverses) have the same magnitude and different signs, like $9$ and $-9$.
:::
