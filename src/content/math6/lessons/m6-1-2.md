Which is colder, $-13^\circ\text{C}$ or $-6^\circ\text{C}$? Who is deeper, a diver at $-8$ m or a diver at $-3$ m? A number line helps you compare positive and negative numbers.

## Integers

:::definition Integer
The **integers** are the natural numbers ($1, 2, 3, \ldots$), their opposites ($-1, -2, -3, \ldots$) and zero.
$$\ldots,\ -3,\ -2,\ -1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \ldots$$
Integers have no fractions or decimal parts.
:::

The number line goes on forever in both directions. There is no greatest integer and no least integer.

## Comparing two numbers

On a horizontal number line, numbers get greater as you move to the **right**. The number farther to the left is less.

- The symbol $<$ means "is less than": $-5 < 2$
- The symbol $>$ means "is greater than": $2 > -5$
- The symbol $=$ means "is equal to": $+6 = 6$

:::example Compare two temperatures
Which symbol makes this true? $-13 \ \square\ -6$
@step On the number line, $-13$ is to the left of $-6$.
@step So $-13$ is less than $-6$.
@answer $-13 < -6$. A temperature of $-13^\circ\text{C}$ is colder.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-20,"max":20,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-13,"label":"−13"},{"value":-6,"label":"−6"}]}
:::

:::mistake Thinking $-13$ is greater than $-6$
$13$ is greater than $6$, but $-13$ is **less** than $-6$. For negative numbers, the farther a number is from $0$, the less it is. Owing \$13 is worse than owing \$6.
:::

:::tip The open side eats the bigger number
The open side of $<$ or $>$ always faces the greater number. The point faces the smaller number.
:::

## Comparing on a vertical number line

On a vertical number line, numbers get greater as you move **up**. A thermometer works this way: higher is warmer.

:::

:::example Heights at Heron Lake
The vertical number line shows heights compared to the surface of Heron Lake ($0$ m). The loon is at $-3$ m and the fish is at $-8$ m. Which is lower?
@step On a vertical number line, the lower number is farther down.
@step $-8$ is below $-3$, so $-8 < -3$.
@answer The fish is lower.
:::

:::example Which place was colder?
At 7 a.m., it was $-15^\circ\text{C}$ in Northfield, $-4^\circ\text{C}$ in Riverton and $2^\circ\text{C}$ in Westbury. Which place was the coldest?
@step Picture a thermometer. The coldest temperature is the lowest one.
@step $-15$ is the lowest of $-15$, $-4$ and $2$.
@answer Northfield was the coldest.
:::

## Ordering numbers

To put numbers in order, picture them on a number line. The least is farthest left (or lowest). The greatest is farthest right (or highest).

:::example Order from least to greatest
Put these numbers in order from least to greatest: $5,\ -9,\ 0,\ -2,\ 11$
@step The negative numbers come first. $-9$ is farther from $0$ than $-2$, so $-9$ is less.
@step Next comes $0$, which is neither positive nor negative.
@step Then the positive numbers: $5$, then $11$.
@answer $-9,\ -2,\ 0,\ 5,\ 11$
:::

:::example Integers between two numbers
How many integers are between $-4$ and $3$? Do not count $-4$ and $3$.
@step List them from left to right: $-3,\ -2,\ -1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2$.
@step Remember that $0$ is an integer.
@answer There are $6$ integers between $-4$ and $3$.
:::

:::example The next integer
What is the greatest integer that is less than $-6$?
@step Integers less than $-6$ are to the left of $-6$: $-7,\ -8,\ -9,\ \ldots$
@step The closest one to $-6$ is one step to the left.
@answer $-7$
:::

:::check m6-compare-integers 3
:::

:::summary
- The integers are the natural numbers, their opposites, and $0$.
- On a horizontal number line, numbers increase to the right. On a vertical number line, they increase going up.
- $<$ means "is less than", $>$ means "is greater than", $=$ means "is equal to".
- For negative numbers, farther from $0$ means less: $-13 < -6$.
- To order numbers, picture them on a number line from left to right.
:::
