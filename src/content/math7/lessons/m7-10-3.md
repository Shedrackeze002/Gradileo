How did your class spend its fundraiser money? How do students in your school get to class? When a whole is split into parts, a circle graph shows each part as a slice of the circle.

## Reading a circle graph

:::definition Circle graph
A **circle graph** shows how a whole is split into parts. The whole circle is $100\%$ of the data. Each part is a **sector**, a slice of the circle. The **legend** tells what each colour means, and often gives its percent.
:::

The circle graph shows the favourite sports of $200$ students at a school.

To find how many are in a sector, find its percent of the total: number $=$ percent $\times$ total.

:::example How many chose hockey?
How many of the $200$ students chose hockey?
@step Hockey is $35\%$. Write it as a decimal: $0.35$.
@step $0.35 \times 200 = 70$
@answer $70$ students chose hockey.
:::

:::example How many more?
How many more students chose soccer than swimming?
@step Subtract the percents: $25\% - 5\% = 20\%$
@step $0.20 \times 200 = 40$
@answer $40$ more students chose soccer.
:::

## Working backward

If you know how many are in one sector and its percent, you can find the total.

:::example Finding the total
At another school, $30$ students chose basketball. That is $20\%$ of the students surveyed. How many students were surveyed?
@step $20\%$ is $30$ students, so $1\%$ is $30 \div 20 = 1.5$ students.
@step $100\%$ is $1.5 \times 100 = 150$ students.
@answer $150$ students were surveyed. Check: $0.20 \times 150 = 30$.
:::

## Central angles

:::definition Central angle
A **central angle** has its vertex at the centre of the circle. The central angles of all the sectors add to $360^\circ$, a full turn.
:::

Each sector's central angle is its percent of $360^\circ$: central angle $=$ percent $\times 360^\circ$.

:::example Angles for the sports graph
Find the central angle for each sport in the graph above.
@step Hockey: $0.35 \times 360^\circ = 126^\circ$. Soccer: $0.25 \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ$.
@step Basketball: $0.20 \times 360^\circ = 72^\circ$. Volleyball: $0.15 \times 360^\circ = 54^\circ$. Swimming: $0.05 \times 360^\circ = 18^\circ$.
@step Check: $126 + 90 + 72 + 54 + 18 = 360$
@answer $126^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $72^\circ$, $54^\circ$ and $18^\circ$.
:::

## Making a circle graph

1. Find the total.
2. Find each category's percent of the total.
3. Find each central angle: percent $\times 360^\circ$, rounded to the nearest degree.
4. Draw a circle and a radius. Use a protractor to draw each angle, one after another.
5. Label each sector or make a legend, and give the graph a title.

:::example Favourite winter activities
Arjun asked $24$ students for their favourite winter activity: skating $9$, skiing $6$, tobogganing $5$ and snowboarding $4$. Find the percents and the central angles.
@step Skating: $9 \div 24 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$, and $0.375 \times 360^\circ = 135^\circ$
@step Skiing: $6 \div 24 = 0.25 = 25\%$, and $0.25 \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ$
@step Tobogganing: $5 \div 24 \approx 20.8\%$, and $5 \div 24 \times 360^\circ = 75^\circ$
@step Snowboarding: $4 \div 24 \approx 16.7\%$, and $4 \div 24 \times 360^\circ = 60^\circ$
@answer The angles are $135^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $75^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. They add to $360^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- A circle graph shows parts of one whole. The whole circle is $100\%$.
- Number in a sector $=$ percent $\times$ total.
- If you know a part and its percent, divide to find $1\%$, then multiply by $100$.
- Central angle $=$ percent $\times 360^\circ$. The angles add to $360^\circ$.
:::
