A hockey team scores 3 goals one game, 5 the next and 2 the next. How many goals does it score in a typical game? Statistics gives you a few numbers that each sum up a whole data set in one value.

## The mean

:::definition Measures of central tendency
A **measure of central tendency** is one number that describes the centre, or a typical value, of a data set. The mean, the median and the mode are measures of central tendency.
:::

:::definition Mean
The **mean** is the sum of the values divided by the number of values. It is what each value would be if the total were shared out equally.
$$\text{mean} = \text{sum of the values} \div \text{number of values}$$
:::

:::example Goals per game
Kai's hockey team scored $3$, $5$, $2$, $4$ and $6$ goals in five games. Find the mean.
@step Add: $3 + 5 + 2 + 4 + 6 = 20$
@step There are $5$ values: $20 \div 5 = 4$
@answer The mean is $4$ goals per game.
:::

:::example Rounding the mean
Simran's points in six basketball games were $14$, $18$, $11$, $16$, $20$ and $13$. Find the mean to the nearest tenth.
@step Add: $14 + 18 + 11 + 16 + 20 + 13 = 92$
@step Divide by $6$: $92 \div 6 = 15.33\ldots$
@answer The mean is about $15.3$ points.
:::

:::mistake Dividing by the wrong number
Divide by **how many values** there are, not by the greatest value or by $10$. Count the values before you divide.
:::

## The median

:::definition Median
The **median** is the middle value when the data are in order from least to greatest.
:::

:::example An odd number of values
Seven students did these numbers of push-ups: $24$, $31$, $18$, $27$, $35$, $22$, $29$. Find the median.
@step In order: $18,\ 22,\ 24,\ 27,\ 29,\ 31,\ 35$
@step There are $7$ values, so the middle one is the $4$th.
@answer The median is $27$ push-ups.
:::

With an **even** number of values, there are two middle values. The median is halfway between them.

:::example An even number of values
Six students read these numbers of books over the summer: $3$, $8$, $5$, $12$, $6$, $9$. Find the median.
@step In order: $3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 8,\ 9,\ 12$
@step The two middle values are $6$ and $8$: $(6 + 8) \div 2 = 7$
@answer The median is $7$ books.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to sort
The median is the middle of the **ordered** list, not the middle of the list as it was written.
:::

:::

## Mode and range

:::definition Mode and range
The **mode** is the value that appears most often. The **range** is the greatest value minus the least value. The range is not a measure of central tendency. It tells how spread out the data are.
:::

:::example Winter temperatures
The daily highs in a northern town for five days were $-12^\circ\text{C}$, $-8^\circ\text{C}$, $-15^\circ\text{C}$, $-8^\circ\text{C}$ and $-3^\circ\text{C}$. Find the mode and the range.
@step $-8$ appears twice. No other value repeats, so the mode is $-8^\circ\text{C}$.
@step Greatest: $-3$. Least: $-15$. Range: $-3 - (-15) = -3 + 15 = 12$
@answer The mode is $-8^\circ\text{C}$ and the range is $12^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::tip No mode, or two modes
If no value repeats, there is no mode. If two values tie for most often, both are modes.
:::

:::example A missing value
The mean of Harpreet's five quiz scores is $16$. Four of the scores are $14$, $18$, $15$ and $14$. What is the fifth score?
@step A mean of $16$ for $5$ scores means the sum is $16 \times 5 = 80$.
@step The four known scores add to $14 + 18 + 15 + 14 = 61$.
@answer The fifth score is $80 - 61 = 19$.
:::

:::check m7-central-tendency 3
:::

## Choosing the best measure

| Use the... | when... |
|---|---|
| mean | the values are close together, with no outliers |
| median | there is an **outlier**: a value much greater or much less than the rest |
| mode | you want the most common value, or the data are words (like favourite colours) |

:::example Babysitting money
Six students earned \$45, \$50, \$48, \$52, \$47 and \$250 babysitting. Which measure best describes a typical amount?
@step Mean: $45 + 50 + 48 + 52 + 47 + 250 = 492$, and $492 \div 6 = 82$, so \$82.
@step Median: in order $45,\ 47,\ 48,\ 50,\ 52,\ 250$. Halfway between $48$ and $50$ is $49$, so \$49.
@step \$250 is an outlier. It pulls the mean up to \$82, more than five of the six students earned.
@answer The median, \$49, describes a typical amount best.
:::

:::example A shoe store
A shoe store wants to know which size to order the most of. Which measure should it use?
@step The store needs the size that sells most often. A mean like $7.4$ might not even be a real size.
@answer The mode.
:::

:::summary
- Mean: add the values, then divide by how many there are. Round to the nearest tenth when needed.
- Median: put the values in order and find the middle. With two middle values, go halfway between them.
- Mode: the value that appears most often. Range: greatest − least.
- Use the mean when values are close together, the median when there is an outlier, and the mode for the most common value.
:::
