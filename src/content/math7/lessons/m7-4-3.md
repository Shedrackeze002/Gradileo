Weather can change fast. Near some mountains, a warm winter wind can raise the temperature by more than $20$ degrees in a few hours. Integers describe changes like this. They also describe heights, hockey plus-minus, golf scores and bank balances.

## Writing integers for changes

A change can go up or down. Use a positive integer for a rise, a gain or money in. Use a negative integer for a drop, a loss or money out.

:::figure

:::

:::example A cold evening
At noon in Riverton the temperature is $+4^\circ\text{C}$. By evening it drops $9$ degrees. What is the temperature in the evening?
@step A drop of $9$ degrees is $-9$. Write the sum: $(+4) + (-9)$.
@step Different signs: $9 - 4 = 5$, and there are more negatives.
@answer The temperature in the evening is $-5^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::example Hockey plus-minus
In hockey, a player’s plus-minus goes up $1$ for each even-strength goal their team scores while the player is on the ice, and down $1$ for each even-strength goal the other team scores. In one game, Talia was on the ice for $2$ goals for her team and $5$ goals against. What was her plus-minus?
@step Goals for: $+2$. Goals against: $-5$.
@step $(+2) + (-5)$: $5 - 2 = 3$, and there are more negatives.
@answer Talia’s plus-minus for the game was $-3$.
:::

## Finding a change or a difference

:::definition Change and difference
The **change** from one value to another is new value $-$ old value. The **difference** between two values is how far apart they are: higher value $-$ lower value.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-20,"max":20,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-18,"label":"before"},{"value":7,"label":"after"}]}
:::

:::example A warm wind
One morning in a town near the mountains, the temperature was $-18^\circ\text{C}$. After a warm wind arrived, it was $+7^\circ\text{C}$. By how many degrees did the temperature rise?
@step Change = new $-$ old: $(+7) - (-18)$.
@step Add the opposite: $(+7) + (+18) = +25$.
@answer The temperature rose $25$ degrees.
:::

:::tip Count through zero
From $-18$ up to $0$ is $18$ degrees. From $0$ up to $+7$ is $7$ more. Altogether, $18 + 7 = 25$ degrees.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting only the digits
$18 - 7 = 11$ is **not** the change. The temperature had to climb $18$ degrees just to reach $0$, and then $7$ more.
:::

:::example Above and below the water
Heights are measured from the surface of a lake, which is $0$ m. A diver is at $-9$ m and an osprey is flying at $+41$ m. How far above the diver is the osprey?
@step Difference = higher $-$ lower: $(+41) - (-9)$.
@step Add the opposite: $(+41) + (+9) = +50$.
@answer The osprey is $50$ m above the diver.
:::

## Several changes in a row

When there are several changes, start at the first value and go one step at a time. Add each change.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Transaction","Change"],"rows":[["Bought a hockey stick","-$130"],["Birthday money","+$35"],["Paid a phone bill","-$70"],["Pay from babysitting","+$100"]]}
:::

:::example Tracking a bank account
Ngozi’s bank account starts the month at \$40. The bank lets the balance go below \$0. The table shows the changes. What is the balance at the end of the month?
@step After the hockey stick: $(+40) + (-130) = -90$
@step After the birthday money: $(-90) + (+35) = -55$
@step After the phone bill: $(-55) + (-70) = -125$
@step After babysitting: $(-125) + (+100) = -25$
@answer The balance is −\$25. Ngozi owes the bank \$25.
:::

:::tip Check with groups
Add all the money in: $35 + 100 = 135$. Add all the money out: $130 + 70 = 200$. Then $(+40) + (+135) + (-200) = -25$. Same answer.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Use positive integers for rises, gains and money in. Use negative integers for drops, losses and money out.
- A change is new $-$ old. A difference is higher $-$ lower.
- Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive. Count through $0$ to check.
- For several changes, go one step at a time, or group the positives and the negatives.
:::
