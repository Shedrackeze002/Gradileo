Fractions show up in recipes, trail signs, schedules and gardens. Often the hardest part of a fraction problem is deciding what to do. This lesson gives you a plan.

## Making a plan

Use these steps for any fraction problem:
1. Read the problem. What do you need to find?
2. Decide: add to find a total, or subtract to find what is left or how much more.
3. Estimate the answer with benchmarks.
4. Calculate, using a common denominator.
5. Check that your answer makes sense.

:::definition Benchmark
A **benchmark** is a friendly number, such as $0$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ or $1$, that you can use to estimate. For example, $\dfrac{7}{10}$ is a little more than $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

:::example The walk to the arena
Kai walks $\dfrac{1}{2}$ km to the bus stop. Then he walks $\dfrac{7}{10}$ km from the bus to the arena. How far does he walk in all?
@step "In all" means a total, so add.
@step Estimate: $\dfrac{1}{2}$ plus a bit more than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a bit more than $1$ km.
@step Common denominator $10$: $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{10}$. Then $\dfrac{5}{10} + \dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{12}{10} = \dfrac{6}{5}$.
@step $\dfrac{6}{5} = 1\dfrac{1}{5}$, which is a bit more than $1$. It matches the estimate.
@answer Kai walks $1\dfrac{1}{5}$ km.
:::

:::mistake "How much more" means subtract
"How much farther did Kai walk than Leah?" asks for a difference. Subtract the smaller distance from the larger one. Do not add.
:::

## Problems with mixed numbers

:::example On the trail
A mountain hiking trail is $6\dfrac{1}{2}$ km long. Rosa’s family has hiked $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ km. How far is it to the end?
@step "How far is it to the end" asks what is left, so subtract: $6\dfrac{1}{2} - 2\dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step Estimate: $6\dfrac{1}{2} - 3 = 3\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the answer is a little more than $3\dfrac{1}{2}$.
@step Common denominator $4$: $6\dfrac{1}{2} = 6\dfrac{2}{4}$. Regroup: $6\dfrac{2}{4} = 5\dfrac{6}{4}$.
@step $5\dfrac{6}{4} - 2\dfrac{3}{4} = 3\dfrac{3}{4}$
@answer It is $3\dfrac{3}{4}$ km to the end of the trail.
:::

:::example Practice time
Arjun practises piano for $1\dfrac{1}{3}$ h on Saturday and $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ h on Sunday. How long does he practise in all?
@step "In all" means add: $1\dfrac{1}{3} + 1\dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step Common denominator $12$: $1\dfrac{4}{12} + 1\dfrac{9}{12}$.
@step Whole numbers: $1 + 1 = 2$. Fractions: $\dfrac{4}{12} + \dfrac{9}{12} = \dfrac{13}{12} = 1\dfrac{1}{12}$.
@step Carry: $2 + 1\dfrac{1}{12} = 3\dfrac{1}{12}$.
@answer Arjun practises for $3\dfrac{1}{12}$ h.
:::

## Two-step problems

:::example Flowers in the garden
Maya plants $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of her garden with carrots and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of it with beans. The rest is for flowers. What fraction of the garden is for flowers?
@step Step 1: Add the vegetable parts. Common denominator $12$: $\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{4}{12} + \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{7}{12}$.
@step Step 2: The whole garden is $1 = \dfrac{12}{12}$. Subtract: $\dfrac{12}{12} - \dfrac{7}{12} = \dfrac{5}{12}$.
@step Check with the picture: $5$ of the $12$ parts are not planted with vegetables.
@answer $\dfrac{5}{12}$ of the garden is for flowers.
:::

:::mistake Stopping after step 1
$\dfrac{7}{12}$ is the part for vegetables, not for flowers. Read the question again before you write your answer.
:::

:::example Is there enough flour?
Leah has $5$ cups of flour. Cookies need $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ cups and pancakes need $1\dfrac{2}{3}$ cups. How much flour is left after she makes both?
@step Step 1: $2\dfrac{1}{4} + 1\dfrac{2}{3} = 2\dfrac{3}{12} + 1\dfrac{8}{12} = 3\dfrac{11}{12}$ cups are used.
@step Step 2: Regroup $5 = 4\dfrac{12}{12}$. Then $4\dfrac{12}{12} - 3\dfrac{11}{12} = 1\dfrac{1}{12}$.
@answer Leah will have $1\dfrac{1}{12}$ cups of flour left.
:::

:::tip Check by adding back
Add your answer to the amount used: $3\dfrac{11}{12} + 1\dfrac{1}{12} = 4\dfrac{12}{12} = 5$. You get back to the $5$ cups Leah started with.
:::

:::summary
- Decide what the question asks: a total means add; what is left, how much more or how much farther means subtract.
- Estimate with benchmarks such as $0$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$ before you calculate.
- Use a common denominator, and carry or regroup with mixed numbers.
- In two-step problems, find the total of the parts first, then subtract from the whole.
- Check that the answer makes sense, for example by adding back.
:::
