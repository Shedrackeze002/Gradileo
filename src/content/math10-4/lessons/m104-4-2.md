The temperature is $-6^\circ\text{C}$ at sunrise and warms up 10 degrees by noon. You pay for groceries and your bank balance drops below \$0. To keep track of changes like these, you add and subtract integers.

## Adding integers on a number line

Start at the first number. Then move:
- Adding a **positive** number moves **right**.
- Adding a **negative** number moves **left**.

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-8,"max":8,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-6,"label":"start"},{"value":4,"label":"end"}]}
:::

:::example Adding a positive
Find $-6 + 10$.
@step Start at $-6$.
@step Adding $10$ moves 10 steps to the right.
@answer $-6 + 10 = 4$
:::

:::example Adding a negative
Find $3 + (-7)$.
@step Start at $3$.
@step Adding $-7$ moves 7 steps to the left.
@answer $3 + (-7) = -4$
:::

For bigger numbers, a number line takes too long. Use these rules instead.

:::note Sign rules for adding
- **Same signs:** add the sizes and keep the sign. $-9 + (-6) = -15$
- **Different signs:** subtract the sizes. Use the sign of the number with the bigger size. $-14 + 9 = -5$
:::

## Subtracting integers

To subtract an integer, **add its opposite**. Change the subtraction to addition, and change the sign of the second number.

:::example Subtracting a positive
Find $-4 - 5$.
@step Add the opposite of $5$: $-4 - 5 = -4 + (-5)$
@step Same signs: $4 + 5 = 9$, and keep the negative sign.
@answer $-4 - 5 = -9$
:::

:::example Subtracting a negative
Find $7 - (-3)$.
@step Add the opposite of $-3$: $7 - (-3) = 7 + 3$
@answer $7 - (-3) = 10$
:::

:::mistake Two minus signs
$7 - (-3)$ is **not** $4$. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive. Think of money: if someone cancels a \$3 debt you owe, you are \$3 better off.
:::

## Finding a change

:::definition Change and difference
A **change** tells how much something goes up or down. The **difference** between two numbers is how far apart they are. To find it, subtract: higher number $-$ lower number.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-20,"max":20,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-18,"label":"before"},{"value":7,"label":"after"}]}
:::

:::example A warm winter wind
A warm wind blew into town on a winter day, and the temperature rose from $-18^\circ\text{C}$ to $7^\circ\text{C}$. How many degrees did the temperature rise?
@step Rise = higher $-$ lower: $7 - (-18)$
@step Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: $7 + 18 = 25$
@answer The temperature rose 25 degrees.
:::

:::tip Count through zero
From $-18$ up to $0$ is 18 degrees. From $0$ up to $7$ is 7 more degrees. Altogether, $18 + 7 = 25$ degrees.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting only the digits
$18 - 7 = 11$ is **not** the change. The temperature had to climb 18 degrees just to reach $0$, and then 7 more.
:::

## Several changes in a row

When there are several changes, start at the first value. Then go one step at a time. Add a rise or a deposit. Subtract a drop or a withdrawal.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Transaction","Amount"],"rows":[["Starting balance","$85"],["Debit: groceries","-$120"],["Deposit: paycheque","+$200"],["Debit: gas","-$45"]]}
:::

:::example Tracking a bank account
The table shows Malik's bank account for one week. What is the balance at the end of the week?
@step After groceries: $85 - 120 = -35$. The account is overdrawn by \$35.
@step After the paycheque: $-35 + 200 = 165$
@step After gas: $165 - 45 = 120$
@answer The balance at the end of the week is \$120.
:::

:::check m104-integer-change 3
:::

:::summary
- Adding a positive moves right on a number line. Adding a negative moves left.
- Same signs: add the sizes and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract the sizes and use the sign of the bigger size.
- To subtract, add the opposite: $7 - (-3) = 7 + 3 = 10$.
- A change or difference is higher $-$ lower. Count through $0$ to check.
- For several changes, go one step at a time.
:::
