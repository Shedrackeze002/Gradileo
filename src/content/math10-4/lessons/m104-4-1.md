On a winter morning in a northern town, it can be $-30^\circ\text{C}$. A bank account can drop below \$0. A diver can swim $10$ metres below the surface of a lake. We use negative numbers to describe all of these.

## What is an integer?

:::definition Integer
An **integer** is a whole number or its opposite: $\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ Integers have no fractions or decimals.
:::

:::definition Positive and negative
A **positive** number is greater than $0$, like $8$ or $+8$. A **negative** number is less than $0$ and has a minus sign, like $-8$. Zero is neither positive nor negative.
:::

Words like *below*, *loss*, *drop* and *withdrawal* usually mean a negative number. Words like *above*, *gain*, *rise* and *deposit* usually mean a positive number.

:::example Writing an integer
Write an integer for each situation: a) a temperature of 15 degrees below zero, b) putting \$40 into a bank account.
@step Below zero means negative, so a) is $-15$.
@step Putting money in makes the balance go up, so b) is $+40$, or just $40$.
@answer a) $-15$ b) $40$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the minus sign
"15 degrees below zero" is $-15$, not $15$. Without the minus sign, it means 15 degrees **above** zero, which is a warm spring day.
:::

## Integers on a number line

On a number line, positive integers are to the right of $0$ and negative integers are to the left. A thermometer is a number line standing up: warmer is higher, colder is lower.

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-8,"max":8,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-5,"label":"−5"},{"value":5,"label":"5"}]}
:::

:::definition Opposite
**Opposite** numbers are the same distance from $0$, on opposite sides of $0$. The opposite of $5$ is $-5$. The opposite of $-5$ is $5$.
:::

:::example Finding an opposite
What is the opposite of $-12$?
@step $-12$ is 12 units to the left of $0$.
@step The number 12 units to the right of $0$ is $12$.
@answer The opposite of $-12$ is $12$.
:::

## Comparing integers

On a number line, numbers get bigger as you move to the right. So the number farther to the **right** is greater, and the number farther to the **left** is less.

- The symbol $<$ means "is less than". $-6 < 2$
- The symbol $>$ means "is greater than". $2 > -6$

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-20,"max":20,"step":1,"points":[{"value":-15,"label":"Ashford"},{"value":-4,"label":"Lakeside"}]}
:::

:::example Which town is colder?
At 7 a.m. it is $-15^\circ\text{C}$ in Ashford and $-4^\circ\text{C}$ in Lakeside. Which town is colder?
@step On the number line, $-15$ is to the left of $-4$.
@step So $-15 < -4$. The lower temperature is colder.
@answer Ashford is colder.
:::

:::mistake Thinking $-15$ is bigger than $-4$
$15$ is bigger than $4$, but $-15$ is **less** than $-4$. With negative numbers, the bigger the number after the minus sign, the smaller the value. Owing \$15 is worse than owing \$4.
:::

:::tip Read the symbol from the small end
The small, pointed end of $<$ or $>$ always points at the smaller number.
:::

## Ordering integers

To put integers in order, picture them on a number line. The least is farthest left. The greatest is farthest right.

:::example Ordering bank balances
Put these bank balances in order from least to greatest: \$25, −\$40, \$0, −\$5, \$10.
@step The negative balances are the least. −\$40 is farther left than −\$5.
@step Next comes \$0. Then the positive balances: \$10, then \$25.
@answer −\$40, −\$5, \$0, \$10, \$25
:::

:::example Golf scores
In golf, the lowest score wins. A score of $-3$ means 3 strokes under par. Who won, and who came last?
@step From lowest to highest: $-5, -3, 0, +2$
@answer Kim won with $-5$. Sam came last with $+2$.
:::

:::summary
- An integer is a whole number or its opposite. Negative integers are less than $0$.
- Below zero, a loss, a drop or a withdrawal is negative. Above zero, a gain, a rise or a deposit is positive.
- Opposites are the same distance from $0$ on opposite sides: $-7$ and $7$.
- On a number line, the number farther right is greater. $-15 < -4$.
- To order integers, picture them on a number line from left to right.
:::
