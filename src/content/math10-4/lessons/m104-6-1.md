A recipe asks for $250\ \text{mL}$ of milk. A bag of potatoes says $5\ \text{kg}$. A highway sign says Brookfield is $45\ \text{km}$ away. Canada uses the metric system, so you see these units at home and at work every day.

## Length, mass and capacity

Canada measures with **SI units**. SI is the short name for the metric system. There are three kinds of measurement you will use most.

:::definition Length, mass and capacity
**Length** is how long, tall or far something is. The basic unit is the **metre** (m).
**Mass** is how heavy something is. We use the **gram** (g) and the kilogram (kg).
**Capacity** is how much a container holds. The basic unit is the **litre** (L).
:::

A **prefix** goes at the front of a unit. It tells you how big the unit is.

:::definition kilo-, centi- and milli-
**kilo-** means $1000$. A kilometre is $1000$ metres.
**centi-** means one hundredth. A centimetre is $\tfrac{1}{100}$ of a metre.
**milli-** means one thousandth. A millilitre is $\tfrac{1}{1000}$ of a litre.
:::

To choose a unit, first decide what you are measuring. Then pick a unit that gives a sensible number.

:::example Choosing a unit
Which unit is best for measuring the amount of gas in a car's tank: millilitres, litres, grams or kilograms?
@step Gas fills a tank, so this is a capacity. Grams and kilograms are for mass, so they are out.
@step A tank holds a lot of gas. In millilitres the number would be huge.
@answer Use **litres**. In most countries, gas pumps show the price per litre.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up mass and capacity
"How heavy?" is a mass question: use g or kg. "How much does it hold?" is a capacity question: use mL or L. A jug holds $4\ \text{L}$ of milk. It does **not** hold $4\ \text{kg}$.
:::

## Estimating with referents

You do not always need a ruler or a scale. You can **estimate** with things you already know.

:::definition Referent
A **referent** is a familiar object you use to estimate a measurement. For example, a door is about $2\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Unit","Referent (about this much)"],"rows":[["1 mm","the thickness of a dime"],["1 cm","the width of your little finger"],["1 m","one big step"],["1 km","a 12-minute walk"],["1 g","a paper clip"],["1 kg","a 1 L carton of milk"],["5 mL","one teaspoon"],["1 L","a carton of milk"]]}
:::

:::example Estimating a mass
A paper clip is about $1\ \text{g}$. A carton of milk is about $1\ \text{kg}$. Which is the best estimate for the mass of an apple: $150\ \text{g}$, $15\ \text{kg}$ or $1.5\ \text{g}$?
@step An apple is much heavier than one paper clip, so $1.5\ \text{g}$ is too small.
@step An apple is lighter than a carton of milk, so $15\ \text{kg}$ is far too big.
@answer The best estimate is $150\ \text{g}$.
:::

:::mistake Right number, wrong unit
An apple is about $150\ \text{g}$, not $150\ \text{kg}$. That is heavier than a fridge! Check that the number **and** the unit make sense together.
:::

:::tip Count the referents
A warehouse wall is about $4$ doors tall. Each door is about $2\ \text{m}$, so the wall is about $4 \times 2 = 8\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

## Converting units

To **convert** means to change a measurement to a different unit. You need to know how the units are related.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Measure","Unit facts"],"rows":[["Length","1 km = 1000 m"],["Length","1 m = 100 cm"],["Length","1 m = 1000 mm"],["Length","1 cm = 10 mm"],["Mass","1 kg = 1000 g"],["Capacity","1 L = 1000 mL"]]}
:::

- From a **bigger** unit to a **smaller** unit, **multiply**. You will get more of the small units.
- From a **smaller** unit to a **bigger** unit, **divide**. You will get fewer of the big units.

:::example Metres to centimetres
A shelf board is $2.35\ \text{m}$ long. How many centimetres is that?
@step Metres to centimetres is big to small, so multiply. $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$.
@step $2.35 \times 100 = 235$
@answer The board is $235\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

:::example Millilitres to litres
A carton holds $750\ \text{mL}$ of cream. How many litres is that?
@step Millilitres to litres is small to big, so divide. $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{mL}$.
@step $750 \div 1000 = 0.75$
@answer The carton holds $0.75\ \text{L}$.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying the wrong way
$750\ \text{mL}$ is **not** $750\,000\ \text{L}$. A litre is a big unit, so you need fewer of them. Ask: should my answer be bigger or smaller than the number I started with?
:::

## Two-step jobs

At work, you often convert first and then do the job. Put everything in the **same unit** before you add, subtract or multiply.

:::example Cutting a board
A carpenter cuts $12$ pieces, each $35\ \text{cm}$ long, from a board that is $5\ \text{m}$ long. How many centimetres of the board are left?
@step Convert the board to centimetres: $5 \times 100 = 500\ \text{cm}$.
@step Length cut off: $12 \times 35 = 420\ \text{cm}$.
@step Left over: $500 - 420 = 80$
@answer $80\ \text{cm}$ of the board is left.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting different units
$5 - 420$ makes no sense, because $5$ is in metres and $420$ is in centimetres. Convert first, then subtract.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Length uses mm, cm, m and km. Mass uses g and kg. Capacity uses mL and L.
- kilo- means $1000$, centi- means one hundredth, milli- means one thousandth.
- Use referents, like a big step for $1\ \text{m}$ or a paper clip for $1\ \text{g}$, to estimate.
- Big unit to small unit: multiply. Small unit to big unit: divide.
- In a two-step job, change everything to the same unit first.
:::
