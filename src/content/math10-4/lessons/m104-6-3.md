How long is your shift? Will you be paid for your lunch break? How much colder will it be tonight? Time and temperature questions come up every day at work and at home.

## Elapsed time

:::definition Elapsed time
**Elapsed time** is how much time passes between a start time and an end time. A shift from 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. has an elapsed time of $8$ hours.
:::

The easiest way to find elapsed time is to **count up** in pieces: first to the next full hour, then the whole hours, then the extra minutes.

:::example Counting up
Kwame's shift at a grocery store starts at 7:45 a.m. and ends at 4:15 p.m. How long is the shift?
@step From 7:45 a.m. to 8:00 a.m. is $15$ min.
@step From 8:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon is $4$ h. From noon to 4:00 p.m. is $4$ h more. That is $8$ h.
@step From 4:00 p.m. to 4:15 p.m. is $15$ min.
@step Add: $8$ h $+ 15$ min $+ 15$ min $= 8$ h $30$ min.
@answer The shift is $8$ h $30$ min long.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["From","To","Time"],"rows":[["7:45 a.m.","8:00 a.m.","15 min"],["8:00 a.m.","4:00 p.m.","8 h"],["4:00 p.m.","4:15 p.m.","15 min"],["Total","","8 h 30 min"]]}
:::

:::mistake Subtracting times like decimals
Time does not work like decimals, because $1$ hour is $60$ minutes, not $100$. Do not try $4.15 - 7.45$. Count up in pieces instead.
:::

## The 24-hour clock

:::definition 24-hour clock
The **24-hour clock** counts the hours from $0$ at midnight to $23$. It does not use a.m. or p.m. Bus schedules, hospitals and many workplaces use it.
:::

Morning times look the same, with a $0$ in front: 7:30 a.m. is 07:30. For times after noon, add $12$ to the hour: 3:00 p.m. is 15:00.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["12-hour clock","24-hour clock"],"rows":[["12:00 midnight","00:00"],["6:45 a.m.","06:45"],["12:00 noon","12:00"],["3:15 p.m.","15:15"],["10:30 p.m.","22:30"]]}
:::

:::example A night shift
Ana works a night shift at a hospital from 22:30 to 06:15 the next morning. How long is her shift?
@step From 22:30 to 23:00 is $30$ min.
@step From 23:00 to 00:00 (midnight) is $1$ h. From 00:00 to 06:00 is $6$ h. That is $7$ h.
@step From 06:00 to 06:15 is $15$ min.
@step Add: $7$ h $+ 30$ min $+ 15$ min $= 7$ h $45$ min.
@answer The shift is $7$ h $45$ min long.
:::

Many jobs have an **unpaid break**. Find the whole shift first, then subtract the break.

:::example An unpaid lunch break
Kwame works from 7:45 a.m. to 4:15 p.m. with a $30$-minute unpaid lunch. How long is he paid for?
@step The whole shift is $8$ h $30$ min (from the first example).
@step $8$ h $30$ min $- 30$ min $= 8$ h.
@answer Kwame is paid for $8$ hours.
:::

## Reading temperatures

:::definition Degrees Celsius
In Canada, temperature is measured in **degrees Celsius** (°C). Water freezes at $0^\circ\text{C}$ and boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$. Temperatures below zero are negative, like $-20^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Temperature","What it feels like"],"rows":[["-30 °C","a very cold January night"],["0 °C","water freezes"],["4 °C","inside a fridge"],["21 °C","a comfortable room"],["30 °C","a hot summer day"],["37 °C","your body"],["100 °C","water boils"]]}
:::

A thermometer is like a number line standing up. On the number line below, each tick is $2$ degrees.

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-14,"max":6,"step":2,"points":[{"value":-9,"label":"7 a.m."}]}
:::

:::example Reading below zero
What temperature does the number line show at 7 a.m.?
@step The dot is halfway between $-10$ and $-8$.
@step Halfway between them is $-9$. It is left of $0$, so it is below zero.
@answer The temperature is $-9^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the minus sign
$9^\circ\text{C}$ is a cool spring day. $-9^\circ\text{C}$ is a cold winter morning. Always check which side of $0$ you are on.
:::

## Changes in temperature

To find how much the temperature **rose** or **dropped**, find the distance between the two temperatures. When one is below zero and one is above, count to $0$ first.

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":-15,"max":10,"step":5,"points":[{"value":-14,"label":"6 a.m."},{"value":5,"label":"3 p.m."}]}
:::

:::example A warm wind
In one town it was $-14^\circ\text{C}$ at 6 a.m. A warm wind blew in, and by 3 p.m. it was $5^\circ\text{C}$. How many degrees did the temperature rise?
@step From $-14$ up to $0$ is $14$ degrees.
@step From $0$ up to $5$ is $5$ degrees.
@step $14 + 5 = 19$
@answer The temperature rose $19$ degrees.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting the numbers without signs
$14 - 5 = 9$ is wrong here. The two temperatures are on opposite sides of $0$, so the distances add: $14 + 5 = 19$.
:::

:::summary
- Elapsed time: count up to the next hour, then whole hours, then the extra minutes. $60$ min $= 1$ h.
- On the 24-hour clock, add $12$ to afternoon hours: 3:00 p.m. is 15:00.
- For a shift with an unpaid break, find the whole shift, then subtract the break.
- Temperatures below $0^\circ\text{C}$ are negative.
- To find a change across zero, count to $0$, then past it, and add the two parts.
:::
