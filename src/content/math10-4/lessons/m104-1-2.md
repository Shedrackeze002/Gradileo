Every shift at work has number questions. How many cans are on the pallets? How many boxes can you fill? What does this order cost? The four operations answer these questions, and the order of operations makes sure everyone gets the same answer.

## Choosing the operation

There are four operations: add, subtract, multiply and divide. Each answer has its own name.

:::definition Sum, difference, product and quotient
The **sum** is the answer to an addition. The **difference** is the answer to a subtraction.
The **product** is the answer to a multiplication. The **quotient** is the answer to a division.
:::

The words in a problem give clues about which operation to use.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Clue words","Operation"],"rows":[["in all, total, altogether","add (+)"],["left, how many more, difference","subtract (−)"],["each, equal groups, times","multiply (×)"],["share, split, how many groups","divide (÷)"]]}
:::

:::example Equal groups
A case holds $24$ cans of juice. How many cans are in $6$ cases?
@step There are $6$ equal groups of $24$, so **multiply**.
@step $6 \times 24 = 144$
@answer There are $144$ cans.
:::

:::example Finding the difference
At the start of a trip, an odometer reads $48{,}236$ km. At the end, it reads $49{,}015$ km. How far was the trip?
@step The distance is the **difference** between the readings, so subtract.
@step $49{,}015 - 48{,}236 = 779$
@answer The trip was $779$ km.
:::

## When division has a remainder

Things do not always divide evenly. The part that is left over is the remainder.

:::definition Remainder
The **remainder** is the amount left over when a number does not divide evenly. For example, $250 \div 12 = 20$ remainder $10$, because $20 \times 12 = 240$ and $250 - 240 = 10$.
:::

What you do with the remainder depends on the question.

:::example How many full cartons?
A farm worker packs $250$ eggs into cartons of $12$. How many **full** cartons can she fill?
@step $250 \div 12 = 20$ remainder $10$. A calculator shows about $20.83$.
@step The $10$ extra eggs cannot fill a carton. Round **down**.
@answer She can fill $20$ full cartons.
:::

:::example How many vans?
A hockey team has $39$ people going to a tournament in another town. Each van holds $12$ people. How many vans are needed?
@step $39 \div 12 = 3$ remainder $3$. A calculator shows $3.25$.
@step The $3$ extra people still need a seat. Round **up**.
@answer They need $4$ vans.
:::

:::mistake Rounding the wrong way
Do not just round the calculator answer the usual way. Ask: "Does the leftover part need one more?" People need a seat, so round up. A half-full box is not a full box, so round down.
:::

## The order of operations

What is $5 + 3 \times 4$? If you add first, you get $32$. If you multiply first, you get $17$. Only one answer can be right, so everyone follows the same rules.

:::definition Order of operations
The **order of operations** is the order for doing the steps in an expression:
1. **B**rackets first.
2. Then multiply and divide, working from left to right.
3. Then add and subtract, working from left to right.
:::

:::example Multiply before you add
Find the value of $5 + 3 \times 4$.
@step Multiply first: $3 \times 4 = 12$.
@step Then add: $5 + 12 = 17$.
@answer $5 + 3 \times 4 = 17$
:::

:::example Brackets first
Find the value of $(6 + 2) \times 5$.
@step Brackets first: $6 + 2 = 8$.
@step Then multiply: $8 \times 5 = 40$.
@answer $(6 + 2) \times 5 = 40$
:::

:::example Left to right
Find the value of $20 - 8 + 2$.
@step There are only $-$ and $+$, so work from left to right.
@step $20 - 8 = 12$, then $12 + 2 = 14$.
@answer $20 - 8 + 2 = 14$
:::

:::mistake Adding before subtracting
Addition does **not** always come before subtraction. In $20 - 8 + 2$, adding first gives $20 - 10 = 10$, which is wrong. Add and subtract from left to right.
:::

## A receipt as one expression

A cashier can write a whole order as one expression.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Item","Number","Price each"],"rows":[["Coffee","3","$4"],["Muffin","2","$3"]]}
:::

:::example A coffee order
Malik buys $3$ coffees at \$4 each and $2$ muffins at \$3 each. Find the total.
@step The expression is $3 \times 4 + 2 \times 3$.
@step Multiply first: $3 \times 4 = 12$ and $2 \times 3 = 6$.
@step Then add: $12 + 6 = 18$.
@answer The total is \$18.
:::

:::example Splitting the bill
Malik and a friend split that bill equally. How much does each person pay?
@step Use brackets so the whole bill is found first: $(3 \times 4 + 2 \times 3) \div 2$.
@step Inside the brackets: $12 + 6 = 18$.
@step Then divide: $18 \div 2 = 9$.
@answer Each person pays \$9.
:::

:::tip Brackets change the order
Without brackets, $3 \times 4 + 2 \times 3 \div 2$ only splits the cost of the muffins. Use brackets around the part you want to do first.
:::

:::check m104-order-ops 3
:::

:::summary
- Sum, difference, product and quotient are the answers to $+$, $-$, $\times$ and $\div$.
- Look for clue words to choose the operation.
- With a remainder, decide whether to round up (everyone needs a seat) or down (only full boxes count).
- Order of operations: brackets, then $\times$ and $\div$ left to right, then $+$ and $-$ left to right.
:::
