A graph can show a whole price list or pay chart in one picture. Workers read graphs to check pay, plan deliveries and compare costs. You do not need to calculate; you just need to read carefully.

## Reading values from a graph

:::definition Relationship and graph
A **relationship** shows how one amount depends on another, like pay depending on hours worked. A **graph** shows a relationship as a picture.
:::

:::definition Axis
An **axis** is one of the two number lines on a graph. The **horizontal axis** goes across. The **vertical axis** goes up. The label on each axis tells you what it measures.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":160,"xStep":1,"yStep":20,"xLabel":"Hours worked","yLabel":"Pay ($)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,0],[8,160]]}],"caption":"Jordan's pay"}
:::

The graph shows Jordan's pay at a car wash. Each grid line across is $1$ hour. Each grid line up is \$20.

:::example Pay for 5 hours
How much does Jordan earn for $5$ hours?
@step Find $5$ on the horizontal axis (hours).
@step Go straight up to the line, then straight across to the vertical axis.
@step The line is at $100$.
@answer Jordan earns \$100 for 5 hours.
:::

:::example Hours for \$140
How many hours must Jordan work to earn \$140?
@step This time, start on the vertical axis. Find $140$.
@step Go straight across to the line, then straight down.
@answer Jordan must work $7$ hours.
:::

:::mistake Not checking the scale
The grid lines do not always go up by $1$. On this graph, each line up is $20$. Read the numbers on the axis before you count lines.
:::

## Describing a graph

Read a graph from **left to right**, the same way you read a sentence.

- A line that goes **up** means both amounts grow together. More hours, more pay.
- A line that goes **down** means one amount shrinks as the other grows.
- A **flat** line means nothing is changing.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":20,"xStep":1,"yStep":2,"xLabel":"Distance (km)","yLabel":"Fare ($)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,4],[8,20]]}],"caption":"Taxi fares"}
:::

:::example Where does the line start?
The graph shows taxi fares in one city. What does the point where the line meets the vertical axis tell you?
@step At $0\ \text{km}$, the line is at $4$ on the vertical axis.
@step The taxi has not moved yet, but the fare is already \$4.
@answer The starting fee is \$4. The line goes up as the distance grows, so longer trips cost more.
:::

:::example How much per kilometre?
Use the taxi graph. How much does the fare go up for each kilometre?
@step At $2\ \text{km}$ the fare is \$8. At $5\ \text{km}$ the fare is \$14.
@step Change in fare: $14 - 8 = 6$. Change in distance: $5 - 2 = 3$.
@step $6 \div 3 = 2$
@answer The fare goes up \$2 for each kilometre.
:::

:::

## Distance–time graphs

A distance–time graph shows how far someone is from a place as time goes on.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":60,"yMin":0,"yMax":2,"xStep":10,"yStep":0.5,"xLabel":"Time (min)","yLabel":"Distance from home (km)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,0],[20,1.5],[30,1.5],[60,0]]}],"caption":"Ravi's walk with a dog"}
:::

Ravi walks a neighbour's dog. The graph has three parts:
- From $0$ to $20$ minutes, the line goes **up**: Ravi walks away from home.
- From $20$ to $30$ minutes, the line is **flat**: Ravi stops at the dog park.
- From $30$ to $60$ minutes, the line goes **down**: Ravi walks back home.

:::example How long was the stop?
For how many minutes did Ravi stop?
@step The flat part starts at $20$ minutes and ends at $30$ minutes.
@step $30 - 20 = 10$
@answer Ravi stopped for $10$ minutes.
:::

:::example How far in total?
How far did Ravi walk in total?
@step The highest point is $1.5\ \text{km}$, so he walked $1.5\ \text{km}$ out.
@step The line comes back down to $0$, so he walked $1.5\ \text{km}$ back.
@step $1.5 + 1.5 = 3$
@answer Ravi walked $3\ \text{km}$ in total.
:::

:::mistake Reading "down" as "slower"
A line going down does not mean Ravi slowed down. It means he is getting **closer to home**. A flat line means he is not moving.
:::

:::check m104-read-relation-graph 3
:::

:::summary
- Read each axis label and scale before you read a value.
- To read a value, go up from the horizontal axis to the line, then across. To work backwards, go across from the vertical axis, then down.
- Up means both amounts grow. Down means one shrinks. Flat means no change.
- Where a line starts on the vertical axis is often a starting fee.
- On a distance–time graph, flat parts are stops and parts going down mean coming back.
:::
