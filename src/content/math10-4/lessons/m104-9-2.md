How much does a server usually make in tips? How long is a typical bus ride to work? One number can sum up a whole list of data. In this lesson you will learn four of these numbers.

## The mean

:::definition Mean
The **mean** (also called the **average**) is the total of all the values divided by how many values there are.
$$\text{mean} = \text{total} \div \text{number of values}$$
:::

:::example Tips for five shifts
Jasmine is a server at a diner. Her tips for five shifts were \$32, \$45, \$28, \$50 and \$40. What is her mean tip amount per shift?
@step Add: $32 + 45 + 28 + 50 + 40 = 195$
@step There are $5$ values. Divide: $195 \div 5 = 39$
@answer Her mean is \$39 per shift.
:::

:::example Rounding the mean
Ravi bowled six games. His scores were $112$, $135$, $98$, $124$, $141$ and $117$. Find his mean score to the nearest tenth.
@step Add: $112 + 135 + 98 + 124 + 141 + 117 = 727$
@step Divide by $6$: $727 \div 6 \approx 121.17$
@answer His mean score is about $121.2$.
:::

:::mistake Dividing by the wrong number
Divide by **how many values** there are, not by the biggest value or by $10$. Count the values before you divide.
:::

## The median

:::definition Median
The **median** is the middle value when the data is in order from least to greatest.
:::

:::example An odd number of values
Nadia worked these hours each week: $18$, $25$, $12$, $22$, $30$, $20$, $16$. Find the median.
@step Put them in order: $12,\ 16,\ 18,\ 20,\ 22,\ 25,\ 30$
@step There are $7$ values. The middle one is the 4th value.
@answer The median is $20$ hours.
:::

If there is an **even** number of values, there are two middle values. The median is halfway between them.

:::example An even number of values
A bus ride took these numbers of minutes: $24$, $31$, $19$, $28$, $35$, $22$, $26$, $30$. Find the median.
@step In order: $19,\ 22,\ 24,\ 26,\ 28,\ 30,\ 31,\ 35$
@step The two middle values are $26$ and $28$.
@step Halfway: $(26 + 28) \div 2 = 27$
@answer The median is $27$ minutes.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to sort
The median is the middle of the **ordered** list. The middle of the list as it was written is not the median.
:::

:::check m104-central-tendency 2
:::

## Mode and range

:::definition Mode and range
The **mode** is the value that appears most often. The **range** is the highest value minus the lowest value. The range tells you how spread out the data is.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Lawns mowed in a week
The table shows how many lawns a landscaping crew mowed each day. Find the mode and the range.
@step $9$ appears three times. No other value appears more than once.
@step Highest: $12$. Lowest: $5$. Range: $12 - 5 = 7$
@answer The mode is $9$ lawns. The range is $7$ lawns.
:::

:::tip No mode, or two modes
If no value repeats, there is no mode. If two values tie for most often, both are modes.
:::

## Which measure is best?

:::definition Outlier
An **outlier** is a value that is much higher or much lower than the rest of the data.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"barChart","categories":["Mon","Tue","Wed","Thu","Fri","Sat"],"values":[40,42,38,45,41,150],"valueLabel":"Tips ($)","categoryLabel":"Shift","max":160,"step":20,"showValues":true}
:::

On Saturday, Jasmine served a big party and got \$150 in tips. That one shift is an outlier.

:::example An outlier pulls the mean
Find the mean and the median of the six shifts in the graph. Which one better describes a typical shift?
@step Mean: $40 + 42 + 38 + 45 + 41 + 150 = 356$, and $356 \div 6 \approx 59.33$
@step Median: in order $38,\ 40,\ 41,\ 42,\ 45,\ 150$. Halfway between $41$ and $42$ is $41.5$.
@answer The median, \$41.50, is closer to most shifts. The outlier pulls the mean up to about \$59.33.
:::

| Use the... | when... |
|---|---|
| mean | the values are close together, with no outliers |
| median | there is an outlier |
| mode | you want the most common value, like the shoe size a store sells most |

:::example What score is needed?
Ravi's scores in five games were $120$, $135$, $110$, $125$ and $140$. He wants a mean of $130$ for six games. What does he need in game 6?
@step Total needed: $130 \times 6 = 780$
@step Total so far: $120 + 135 + 110 + 125 + 140 = 630$
@step $780 - 630 = 150$
@answer He needs a score of $150$ in game 6.
:::

:::check m104-central-tendency 3
:::

:::summary
- Mean: add all the values, then divide by how many there are.
- Median: put the values in order and find the middle. With two middle values, go halfway between them.
- Mode: the value that appears most often.
- Range: highest value minus lowest value.
- An outlier pulls the mean. Then the median describes a typical value better.
:::
