A pancake recipe for $6$ people uses $4$ eggs. How many eggs do you need for $15$ people? Questions like this come up in recipes, on maps and when you travel. You can answer them with **proportional reasoning**.

## Proportions

:::definition Proportion
A **proportion** says that two ratios are equal, such as $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{15}{20}$ or $3 : 4 = 15 : 20$.
:::

To solve a proportion, find the missing term.

:::example A missing numerator
Solve $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{x}{20}$.
@step The denominator went from $4$ to $20$, and $4 \times 5 = 20$.
@step Multiply the numerator by $5$ too: $3 \times 5 = 15$.
@answer $x = 15$
:::

## When there is no whole-number multiplier

Sometimes no whole number takes one term to the other. Here are two ways to solve the proportion.

:::example Write the ratio in lowest terms first
Solve $\dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{x}{12}$.
@step No whole number times $9$ gives $12$. Write $\dfrac{6}{9}$ in lowest terms: $\dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
@step Now $3 \times 4 = 12$, so multiply the numerator by $4$: $x = 2 \times 4 = 8$.
@answer $x = 8$
:::

:::example Multiply both sides
Solve $\dfrac{5}{4} = \dfrac{x}{7}$.
@step To get $x$ by itself, multiply both sides by $7$: $x = 7 \times \dfrac{5}{4}$.
@step $x = \dfrac{35}{4} = 8.75$
@answer $x = 8.75$
:::

:::tip Check your answer
Divide each ratio. $8.75 \div 7 = 1.25$ and $5 \div 4 = 1.25$. The ratios are equal, so $x = 8.75$ is right.
:::

## The unit-rate method

In the **unit-rate method**, find the amount for one unit first. Then multiply.

:::example Hockey tape
$4$ rolls of hockey tape cost \$12. At the same price, how much do $7$ rolls cost?
@step One roll: $12 \div 4 = 3$, so the tape is \$3 per roll.
@step Seven rolls: $7 \times 3 = 21$.
@answer $7$ rolls cost \$21.
:::

:::example A road trip
A family drives $270$ km in $3$ hours. At the same speed, how long will it take to drive $405$ km?
@step Unit rate: $270 \div 3 = 90$, so the speed is $90$ km/h.
@step Time: $405 \div 90 = 4.5$.
@answer It will take $4.5$ hours ($4$ h $30$ min).
:::

## The equivalent-ratio method

In the **equivalent-ratio method**, write a proportion with the same units in the same order on both sides. Then solve it.

:::example Eggs for pancakes
A recipe for $6$ servings uses $4$ eggs. How many eggs are needed for $15$ servings?
@step Eggs to servings: $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{x}{15}$.
@step In lowest terms, $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. Since $3 \times 5 = 15$, $x = 2 \times 5 = 10$.
@answer $10$ eggs are needed.
:::

:::example A road map
On a road map, $1$ cm represents $25$ km. Two towns are $6.8$ cm apart on the map. How far apart are they?
@step Map distance to real distance: $\dfrac{1\ \text{cm}}{25\ \text{km}} = \dfrac{6.8\ \text{cm}}{x}$.
@step The map distance is $6.8$ times as big, so $x = 25 \times 6.8 = 170$.
@answer The towns are $170$ km apart.
:::

:::example Changing money
Suppose $1$ Canadian dollar (CAD) buys $0.73$ US dollars (USD). How many US dollars do you get for \$150 CAD?
@step US dollars to Canadian dollars: $\dfrac{0.73}{1} = \dfrac{x}{150}$.
@step $x = 150 \times 0.73 = 109.50$
@answer You get \$109.50 USD.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up the order
Keep the same order on both sides. If the left ratio is eggs over servings, the right ratio must also be eggs over servings.
:::

## Sharing and mixing in a ratio

:::example Sharing a profit
Amara and Jun share a profit of \$84 in the ratio $3 : 4$. How much does each person get?
@step The ratio has $3 + 4 = 7$ equal parts.
@step One part: $84 \div 7 = 12$, so each part is \$12.
@step Amara: $3 \times 12 = 36$. Jun: $4 \times 12 = 48$.
@answer Amara gets \$36 and Jun gets \$48. Check: $36 + 48 = 84$.
:::

:::example Iced tea
Iced tea is made by mixing concentrate and water in the ratio $1 : 4$. How much concentrate is in $2$ L of iced tea?
@step $2$ L $= 2000$ mL. The ratio has $1 + 4 = 5$ parts.
@step One part: $2000 \div 5 = 400$ mL. Concentrate is $1$ part.
@answer There are $400$ mL of concentrate (and $1600$ mL of water).
:::

:::check m8-proportion-problems 3
:::

:::summary
- A proportion says two ratios are equal. Solve it by finding the scale factor, writing a ratio in lowest terms first, or multiplying both sides.
- Unit-rate method: find the amount for one unit, then multiply.
- Equivalent-ratio method: set up a proportion with the same units in the same order on both sides.
- To share in a ratio, add the terms to find the number of parts, then find one part.
:::
