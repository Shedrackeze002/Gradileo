A taxi charges \$4 to start plus \$2 for every kilometre. A 1 km ride costs \$6, a 2 km ride costs \$8, and every extra kilometre adds the same \$2. A pattern that changes by the same amount at every step can be described with an equation, and the equation lets you find any value you want.

## Relations and equations

:::definition Relation
A **relation** connects two variables. For example, the fare for a taxi ride depends on the distance. The equation $F = 2d + 4$ gives the fare $F$, in dollars, for a ride of $d$ kilometres.
:::

In the equation $y = 3x - 2$, you choose a value for $x$ and calculate $y$. Remember that $3x$ means $3 \times x$.

:::definition Linear relation
A **linear relation** is a relation where one variable changes by the **same amount** each time the other variable goes up by $1$. When you graph it, the points lie on a straight line.
:::

## Making a table of values

:::definition Table of values
A **table of values** lists some values of $x$ and the matching values of $y$.
:::

To fill in a table, **substitute** each value of $x$ into the equation. That means you replace $x$ with the number, then calculate. Multiply first, then add or subtract.

:::example A table for y = 3x − 2
Complete a table of values for $y = 3x - 2$, using $x = 0$, $1$, $2$ and $3$.
@step $x = 0$: $y = 3(0) - 2 = 0 - 2 = -2$
@step $x = 1$: $y = 3(1) - 2 = 3 - 2 = 1$
@step $x = 2$: $y = 3(2) - 2 = 6 - 2 = 4$
@step $x = 3$: $y = 3(3) - 2 = 9 - 2 = 7$
@answer The values of $y$ are $-2$, $1$, $4$ and $7$.
:::

## Negative values of x

Tables often use negative values of $x$ too. Use your integer rules: a positive times a negative is negative, and a negative times a negative is positive. Put brackets around a negative number when you substitute it.

:::example Negative inputs
Complete a table of values for $y = -2x - 1$, using $x = -2$, $-1$, $0$, $1$ and $2$.
@step $x = -2$: $y = -2(-2) - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3$
@step $x = -1$: $y = -2(-1) - 1 = 2 - 1 = 1$
@step $x = 0$: $y = -2(0) - 1 = 0 - 1 = -1$
@step $x = 1$: $y = -2(1) - 1 = -2 - 1 = -3$
@step $x = 2$: $y = -2(2) - 1 = -4 - 1 = -5$
@answer The values of $y$ are $3$, $1$, $-1$, $-3$ and $-5$.
:::

:::mistake Losing a negative sign
For $x = -2$ in $y = -2x - 1$, some students write $-2(-2) = -4$. A negative times a negative is **positive**, so $-2(-2) = 4$ and $y = 4 - 1 = 3$.
:::

## Describing the pattern

Read a table from left to right. In a linear relation, $y$ changes by the same amount every time $x$ goes up by $1$.

:::example Describe the pattern
Describe the pattern in the table for $y = 3x - 2$.
@step The values of $y$ are $-2$, $1$, $4$ and $7$.
@step Find each change: $1 - (-2) = 3$, $4 - 1 = 3$ and $7 - 4 = 3$.
@step The change is always $3$, which is the number that multiplies $x$.
@answer As $x$ increases by $1$, $y$ increases by $3$.
:::

For $y = -2x - 1$, the values of $y$ go $3$, $1$, $-1$, $-3$, $-5$. As $x$ increases by $1$, $y$ **decreases** by $2$.

:::tip Watch the steps in x
If the values of $x$ go up by $2$ each time, the change in $y$ is for two steps. Divide it by $2$ to find the change for each $1$.
:::

## Linear relations in real life

:::example A burning candle
A candle is $20$ cm tall. It burns down $2$ cm every hour, so its height after $t$ hours is $h = 20 - 2t$. Make a table for $t = 0$, $1$, $2$ and $3$, and describe the pattern.
@step $t = 0$: $h = 20 - 2(0) = 20 - 0 = 20$
@step $t = 1$: $h = 20 - 2(1) = 20 - 2 = 18$
@step $t = 2$: $h = 20 - 2(2) = 20 - 4 = 16$
@step $t = 3$: $h = 20 - 2(3) = 20 - 6 = 14$
@answer The heights are $20$, $18$, $16$ and $14$ cm. As the time increases by $1$ h, the height decreases by $2$ cm.
:::

:::example A warm wind
At 6 a.m. it was $-12^\circ\text{C}$. A warm wind from the mountains made the temperature rise $3^\circ\text{C}$ every hour, so $T = 3h - 12$, where $h$ is the number of hours after 6 a.m. What was the temperature at 10 a.m.?
@step 10 a.m. is $4$ hours after 6 a.m., so substitute $h = 4$.
@step $T = 3(4) - 12 = 12 - 12 = 0$
@answer At 10 a.m. the temperature was $0^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::summary
- A linear relation changes by the same amount each time $x$ goes up by $1$.
- To make a table of values, substitute each value of $x$ into the equation. Multiply first, then add or subtract.
- Put brackets around negative numbers when you substitute, and use the integer sign rules.
- The number that multiplies $x$ tells you how much $y$ changes for each $1$: up if it is positive, down if it is negative.
:::
