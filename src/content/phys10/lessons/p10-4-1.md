Drop a pebble into a still pond and ripples spread outwards. A leaf floating on the pond bobs up and down as they pass, but it does not travel to the bank with them. Something moves across the pond, yet the water itself stays where it was. That something is a wave.

## Waves carry energy

:::definition Wave
A **wave** is a vibration that travels, transferring **energy** from one place to another without transferring matter.
:::

There are two kinds of wave, named by the direction of the vibrations.

| | Transverse wave | Longitudinal wave |
|---|---|---|
| Vibrations | at right angles to the direction of travel | along the direction of travel |
| Looks like | crests and troughs | compressions (squashed) and rarefactions (stretched) |
| Examples | ripples on water, a wave on a rope, light | sound, a pulse pushed along a spring |

Flick the end of a rope up and down and a transverse wave runs along it: the rope moves up and down while the wave moves along. Push and pull the end of a long spring and a longitudinal wave runs along it: the coils move backwards and forwards along the same line as the wave.

:::example What kind of wave?
A loudspeaker cone moves in and out, pushing the air in front of it backwards and forwards. The sound travels straight out from the cone. What kind of wave is it?
@step The air vibrates in and out, along the line from the cone.
@step The sound travels along that same line.
@answer The vibrations are along the direction of travel, so sound is a longitudinal wave.
:::

:::mistake Thinking the material travels with the wave
A wave across a pond does not carry water to the bank, and a sound does not carry air from the speaker to your ear. Each particle only vibrates about its own rest position and passes the energy on to its neighbour.
:::

## Describing a wave

:::definition Amplitude, wavelength, frequency and period
- The **amplitude** is the greatest distance of a point on the wave from its rest position.
- The **wavelength** is the length of one complete wave: from one crest to the next crest.
- The **frequency** is the number of complete waves each second, in **hertz (Hz)**.
- The **period** is the time for one complete wave to pass.
:::

:::summary
- A wave transfers energy without transferring matter.
- Transverse: vibrations at right angles to the direction of travel (water ripples, light).
- Longitudinal: vibrations along the direction of travel (sound).
- Amplitude: rest position to crest. Wavelength: crest to crest.
- Frequency: complete waves each second, in hertz. Period: the time for one wave.
:::
