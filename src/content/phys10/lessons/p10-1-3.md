Walk 50 m to a shop and 50 m back home. You have walked 100 m, yet you are exactly where you started. Physics needs two different words for these two ideas: the ground you covered, and how far you ended from where you began.

## Distance and displacement

:::definition Displacement
**Displacement** is the change in an object's position: how far it ends from its starting point, and in which direction. It is a **vector**, measured in metres.

**Distance** is all the ground covered along the way, whatever the direction. It is a **scalar**.

We need displacement because it tells us where something ends up, which distance alone cannot.
:::

For motion along a straight line, choose one direction as **positive** and the opposite direction as **negative**. Then add the parts of the journey with their signs.

:::example Walking there and part of the way back
Priya walks 80 m east and then 30 m west. Find the distance walked and the displacement.
@step Distance: add every part. $80 + 30 = 110$ m.
@step Displacement: take east as positive. $80 - 30 = 50$ m.
@answer Priya walks 110 m, and the displacement is 50 m east.
:::

:::example Ending behind the start
Mateo walks 20 m north and then 70 m south. What is Mateo’s displacement?
@step Take north as positive: $20 - 70 = -50$ m.
@step The minus sign means the answer points south.
@answer 50 m south.
:::

:::example A full lap
A runner completes one lap of a 400 m track and stops at the start line. Find the distance and the displacement.
@step The runner covers the whole track: the distance is 400 m.
@step The runner ends at the starting point, so the position has not changed.
@answer Distance 400 m, displacement 0 m.
:::

:::mistake Giving a displacement without a direction
"50 m" is a distance. A displacement needs its direction too: "50 m east", or "+50 m" once you have said that east is positive.
:::

## Velocity

:::definition Velocity
**Velocity** is the displacement each second: speed in a stated direction. It is a **vector**, measured in m/s.

$$\text{velocity} = \dfrac{\text{displacement}}{\text{time}} \qquad v = \dfrac{s}{t}$$

Here $s$ stands for displacement. Two cars travelling at 20 m/s in opposite directions have the same speed but different velocities: $+20$ m/s and $-20$ m/s.
:::

:::example A velocity with a sign
Take east as positive. A cart moves 48 m west in 8 s at a steady rate. What is its velocity?
@step West is the negative direction, so the displacement is $-48$ m.
@step $v = \dfrac{-48}{8}$.
@answer $v = -6$ m/s, which means 6 m/s to the west.
:::

Because distance and displacement can differ, so can average speed and average velocity.

$$\text{average speed} = \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} \qquad \text{average velocity} = \dfrac{\text{displacement}}{\text{total time}}$$

:::example Average speed and average velocity
Leila cycles 80 m north in 12 s, then turns and cycles 60 m south in 8 s. Take north as positive. Find the average speed and the average velocity.
@step Total time: $12 + 8 = 20$ s.
@step Distance: $80 + 60 = 140$ m, so the average speed is $\dfrac{140}{20} = 7$ m/s.
@step Displacement: $80 - 60 = 20$ m north, so the average velocity is $\dfrac{20}{20} = 1$ m/s north.
@answer Average speed 7 m/s; average velocity 1 m/s north.
:::

:::mistake Thinking a fast trip has a large velocity
Leila rode quickly, at 7 m/s on average, but ended only 20 m from the starting point, so the average velocity is small. After a full round trip the average velocity is zero, however fast the trip was.
:::

:::summary
- Distance is the ground covered (a scalar). Displacement is the change in position, with its direction (a vector).
- On a straight line, call one direction positive and add the parts with their signs.
- Velocity = displacement ÷ time. Its sign gives its direction.
- Average speed uses the total distance; average velocity uses the displacement. A round trip has an average velocity of zero.
:::
