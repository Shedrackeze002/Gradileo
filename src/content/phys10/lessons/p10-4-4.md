A mirror shows your face, and a straw in a glass of water looks broken at the surface. Both effects come from light changing direction: by bouncing off a surface, or by passing from one material into another.

## Reflection

We draw the path of light as a straight line called a **ray**. To describe how a ray meets a surface, we draw the **normal**: a line at right angles to the surface at the point where the ray hits it. Angles are always measured from the normal.

:::definition The law of reflection
When light reflects from a surface, the **angle of reflection** equals the **angle of incidence**. Both angles are measured between the ray and the normal.
:::

In the diagram, the incoming (incident) ray on the left makes 40° with the normal, so the reflected ray on the right also makes 40° with the normal.

:::example Using the law of reflection
A ray of light hits a plane mirror with an angle of incidence of 25°. What is the angle of reflection?
@answer The angle of reflection is 25°, equal to the angle of incidence.
:::

:::example An angle given from the mirror
A ray of light makes an angle of 30° with the surface of a plane mirror. What is the angle of reflection?
@step The normal is at 90° to the mirror, so the angle of incidence is $90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$.
@answer The angle of reflection is 60°.
:::

:::example The angle between the two rays
A ray hits a plane mirror with an angle of incidence of 35°. What is the angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray?
@step The angle of reflection is also 35°.
@step The two angles sit side by side, one on each side of the normal: $35^\circ + 35^\circ$.
@answer The angle between the rays is 70°.
:::

A flat (plane) mirror forms an **image** that is the same size as the object, upright, swapped left to right, and as far **behind** the mirror as the object is in front of it. A person standing 2 m from a mirror sees their image 2 m behind it, so the image is 4 m from the person.

:::mistake Measuring from the mirror
The angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the normal, not from the surface of the mirror. A ray that skims the mirror at 10° to its surface has an angle of incidence of 80°.
:::

## Refraction

Light travels more slowly in glass and in water than in air. When a ray crosses the boundary at an angle, the change of speed makes it change direction.

:::definition Refraction
**Refraction** is the change in direction of a wave when it passes into a material where its speed is different.

- Passing into a material where it travels **more slowly** (air to glass), a ray bends **towards the normal**.
- Passing into a material where it travels **faster** (glass to air), a ray bends **away from the normal**.
- A ray travelling along the normal changes speed but does not bend.
:::

Refraction changes the speed and the wavelength of the light, but not its frequency. The source sets how many waves leave each second, and the same number must arrive. Since $v = f\lambda$, a lower speed with the same frequency means a shorter wavelength.

:::example Which way does it bend?
A ray of light passes from air into a glass block with an angle of incidence of 40°. Is the angle of refraction larger or smaller than 40°?
@step Light slows down in glass.
@step A ray that slows down bends towards the normal.
@answer The angle of refraction is smaller than 40° (about 25° for ordinary glass).
:::

:::example Water waves slowing down
Water waves of frequency 5 Hz travel at 60 cm/s in deep water and slow to 40 cm/s in shallow water. Find the wavelength in each.
@step The frequency stays at 5 Hz.
@step Deep: $\lambda = \dfrac{60}{5} = 12$ cm. Shallow: $\lambda = \dfrac{40}{5} = 8$ cm.
@answer 12 cm in deep water and 8 cm in shallow water. The slower waves are closer together.
:::

Refraction explains why a pool looks shallower than it is and why a straw in water looks bent. Light from under the water bends away from the normal as it leaves, so it seems to come from a different place.

:::mistake Thinking the frequency changes
When light enters glass, its speed and wavelength both decrease, but its frequency stays the same. This is why the colour of the light does not change.
:::

:::summary
- Angles are measured from the normal, a line at right angles to the surface.
- Law of reflection: angle of reflection = angle of incidence.
- A plane mirror image is the same size, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
- Refraction: light bends towards the normal when it slows down, and away when it speeds up.
- In refraction the speed and wavelength change. The frequency does not.
:::
