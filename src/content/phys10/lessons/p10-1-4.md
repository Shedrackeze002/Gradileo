A car pulling away from traffic lights and a car cruising on a highway are both moving, but only one of them is changing its velocity. Acceleration measures how quickly velocity changes, and it is the link between motion and the forces you will meet in the next unit.

## Acceleration

:::definition Acceleration
**Acceleration** is the change in velocity each second. It is a **vector**.

$$\text{acceleration} = \dfrac{\text{change in velocity}}{\text{time}} \qquad a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$$

Here $u$ is the starting velocity and $v$ is the final velocity. Velocity is in m/s and time in s, so acceleration is in **metres per second squared (m/s²)**.

An acceleration of 3 m/s² means the velocity grows by 3 m/s every second: 0, 3, 6, 9 m/s and so on.
:::

An object that is not moving is **at rest**, with a velocity of 0 m/s. An object moving at a steady velocity has an acceleration of **zero**, however fast it is going, because its velocity is not changing.

:::example Starting from rest
A motorbike starts from rest and reaches 20 m/s in 5 s. What is its acceleration?
@step Change in velocity: $v - u = 20 - 0 = 20$ m/s.
@step $a = \dfrac{20}{5}$.
@answer $a = 4$ m/s².
:::

:::example Speeding up
A car speeds up from 8 m/s to 20 m/s in 4 s. What is its acceleration?
@step Change in velocity: $20 - 8 = 12$ m/s.
@answer $a = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ m/s².
:::

:::example Slowing down
A train slows down from 30 m/s to 10 m/s in 5 s. What is its acceleration?
@step Change in velocity: $10 - 30 = -20$ m/s.
@step $a = \dfrac{-20}{5}$.
@answer $a = -4$ m/s². The minus sign shows that the train is slowing down. This is sometimes called a **deceleration** of 4 m/s².
:::

:::mistake Subtracting the wrong way round
The change in velocity is always final minus starting, $v - u$. If you subtract the other way, a car that is slowing down seems to be speeding up.
:::

## Using acceleration

If we know the acceleration, we can predict the velocity at any later time. In $t$ seconds the velocity changes by $a \times t$.

:::definition The velocity equation
$$v = u + at$$

The final velocity is the starting velocity plus the acceleration times the time. Rearranged, it gives the time or the starting velocity:

$$t = \dfrac{v - u}{a} \qquad u = v - at$$
:::

:::example Finding the final velocity
A cyclist moving at 4 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. What is the final velocity?
@step The velocity changes by $a \times t = 2 \times 5 = 10$ m/s.
@answer $v = u + at = 4 + 10 = 14$ m/s.
:::

:::example Finding the time
A car accelerates at 3 m/s² from 6 m/s to 21 m/s. How long does this take?
@step Change in velocity: $21 - 6 = 15$ m/s.
@answer $t = \dfrac{15}{3} = 5$ s.
:::

:::example Braking to a stop
A bus moving at 18 m/s brakes with an acceleration of $-3$ m/s². How long does it take to stop?
@step When it has stopped, $v = 0$. Change in velocity: $0 - 18 = -18$ m/s.
@answer $t = \dfrac{-18}{-3} = 6$ s.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the minus sign when braking
With an acceleration of $-3$ m/s², the velocity falls by 3 m/s each second. Adding 3 m/s each second instead would make the bus speed up.
:::

:::summary
- Acceleration is the change in velocity each second: $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$, in m/s².
- A steady velocity means zero acceleration. A negative acceleration means slowing down.
- $v = u + at$ gives the final velocity; rearranged, it gives the time or the starting velocity.
:::
