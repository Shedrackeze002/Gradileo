Physics describes the world with measurements: how far, how fast, how heavy, how long. Before we can describe how anything moves, we need to agree on what we measure, the units we measure it in, and which measurements need a direction as well as a size.

## Quantities and SI units

:::definition Physical quantity
A **physical quantity** is anything that can be measured. It is written as a **number** and a **unit**, such as 5 m, 12 s or 60 kg. The number without the unit means nothing: "the race took 12" does not say whether it was seconds or hours.

We use quantities so that everyone can describe the same event in the same way and check each other's results.
:::

Scientists everywhere use one agreed set of units, the **SI units**, so that a measurement made in one country means the same in every other.

| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| length (distance) | metre | m |
| mass | kilogram | kg |
| time | second | s |
| speed | metre per second | m/s |

## Prefixes and converting units

:::definition Prefix
A **prefix** is put in front of a unit to make it larger or smaller. **Kilo** (k) means one thousand times, **centi** (c) means one hundredth and **milli** (m) means one thousandth.
:::

| From | To | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| km | m | multiply by 1000 |
| m | km | divide by 1000 |
| m | cm | multiply by 100 |
| min | s | multiply by 60 |
| h | s | multiply by 3600 |
| km/h | m/s | divide by 3.6 |

Changing to a **smaller** unit gives a **bigger** number, so you multiply. Changing to a **larger** unit gives a smaller number, so you divide.

:::example Kilometres to metres
A cycle path is 3.5 km long. How long is it in metres?
@step 1 km = 1000 m, and a metre is the smaller unit, so multiply by 1000.
@answer $3.5 \times 1000 = 3500$ m.
:::

:::example Hours and minutes to seconds
A film lasts 1 h 30 min. How long is it in seconds?
@step Change the hours: $1 \times 3600 = 3600$ s.
@step Change the minutes: $30 \times 60 = 1800$ s.
@answer $3600 + 1800 = 5400$ s.
:::

:::example Kilometres per hour to metres per second
A car travels at 72 km/h. What is its speed in m/s?
@step 72 km/h means 72 000 m in 3600 s.
@step Dividing by 3.6 does both changes at once.
@answer $72 \div 3.6 = 20$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying when you should divide
Check that your answer makes sense. 250 m is a short walk, so it must be less than one kilometre: 0.25 km, not 250 000 km.

## Scalars and vectors

Some quantities are fully described by a size. Others also need a direction. "Walk 50 m" does not tell you where you end up; "walk 50 m north" does.

:::definition Scalar and vector
A **scalar** is a quantity with a **size only**, such as distance, speed, mass, time and temperature.

A **vector** is a quantity with a **size and a direction**, such as displacement, velocity, acceleration and force.
:::

| Scalar (size only) | Vector (size and direction) |
|---|---|
| distance: 50 m | displacement: 50 m north |
| speed: 20 m/s | velocity: 20 m/s east |
| mass: 5 kg | weight: 49 N downwards |
| time: 12 s | force: 30 N to the left |

:::example Scalar or vector?
Sort these: a temperature of 25 °C, a push of 40 N to the right, a time of 9 s.
@step Ask of each one: does it have a direction?
@step A temperature and a time have a size only. The push has a size (40 N) and a direction (right).
@answer The temperature and the time are scalars. The push is a force, a vector.
:::

:::mistake A unit is not a direction
Every quantity has a unit, scalar or vector. What makes a vector is a direction, such as north, left or downwards.
:::

:::summary
- A physical quantity is a number with a unit. The SI units of length, mass and time are the metre, the kilogram and the second.
- Kilo means × 1000, centi means ÷ 100 and milli means ÷ 1000. Multiply to change to a smaller unit and divide to change to a larger one.
- To change km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6.
- A scalar has a size only. A vector has a size and a direction.
:::
