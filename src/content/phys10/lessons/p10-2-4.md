On the Moon an astronaut can leap several metres with a heavy backpack that would be a struggle to lift on Earth. The backpack has not lost any of its contents. What has changed is how hard gravity pulls on it. To make sense of this we need two different ideas that everyday speech mixes up: mass and weight.

## Mass and weight

:::definition Mass and weight
**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object. It is a scalar, measured in **kilograms (kg)**, and it is the same wherever the object is.

**Weight** is the force of gravity on an object. It is a vector that points towards the centre of the planet, measured in **newtons (N)**, and it changes from place to place.
:::

| | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | amount of matter | pull of gravity |
| Unit | kilogram (kg) | newton (N) |
| Scalar or vector | scalar | vector |
| On the Moon | the same | smaller |
| Measured with | a balance | a spring balance (newton meter) |

:::example An astronaut on the Moon
An astronaut has a mass of 70 kg on Earth. What is the astronaut's mass on the Moon?
@step Mass is the amount of matter, and the astronaut is made of the same matter everywhere.
@answer Still 70 kg. Only the weight is smaller on the Moon.
:::

:::mistake "I weigh 60 kg"
Kilograms measure mass. A person with a mass of 60 kg has a weight of about 588 N on Earth. In physics, a weight is always in newtons.
:::

## Gravitational field strength

:::definition Gravitational field strength
The **gravitational field strength**, $g$, is the force of gravity on each kilogram of mass. On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg, so every kilogram is pulled down with 9.8 N.

$$\text{weight} = \text{mass} \times g \qquad W = mg$$
:::

Other worlds pull differently: on the Moon $g = 1.6$ N/kg, on Mars $g = 3.7$ N/kg and on Jupiter $g = 24.8$ N/kg.

:::example Weight on Earth
On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg. What is the weight of a 50 kg mass?
@step $W = mg = 50 \times 9.8$.
@answer $W = 490$ N.
:::

:::example Mass from weight
On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg. A crate has a weight of 196 N. What is its mass?
@step $m = \dfrac{W}{g} = \dfrac{196}{9.8}$.
@answer $m = 20$ kg.
:::

:::example The same rover on two worlds
A rover has a weight of 490 N on Earth, where $g = 9.8$ N/kg. What is its weight on Mars, where $g = 3.7$ N/kg?
@step Find its mass: $m = \dfrac{490}{9.8} = 50$ kg. The mass is the same on Mars.
@step Weight on Mars: $W = 50 \times 3.7$.
@answer $W = 185$ N.
:::

:::example Mass and weight far from home
An astronaut has a mass of 80 kg. Find the astronaut's mass and weight on the Moon, where $g = 1.6$ N/kg.
@step The mass does not change: 80 kg.
@step Weight: $W = 80 \times 1.6$.
@answer Mass 80 kg, weight 128 N.
:::

:::summary
- Mass is the amount of matter, in kg. It is the same everywhere.
- Weight is the pull of gravity, in N. It depends on where the object is.
- $W = mg$, where $g$ is the gravitational field strength: 9.8 N/kg on Earth.
- To compare two worlds, find the mass first, then use the new value of $g$.
:::
