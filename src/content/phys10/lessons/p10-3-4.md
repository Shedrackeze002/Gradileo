A roller coaster is pulled to the top of the first hill and then let go. From there no engine drives it, yet it races down, climbs the next hill and loops the loop. It runs on energy that is passed back and forth between height and motion. The rule behind it is one of the most important in all of science.

## Energy stores and transfers

:::definition Conservation of energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be **transferred** from one store to another. The total amount of energy stays the same.
:::

| Energy store | What has it |
|---|---|
| kinetic energy | anything that is moving |
| gravitational potential energy | anything lifted above the ground |
| elastic potential energy | anything stretched, squashed or bent that will spring back |
| chemical energy | food, fuel and batteries |
| thermal energy | anything warm |

A falling ball loses height and gains speed: gravitational potential energy is transferred to kinetic energy. A ball thrown upwards does the opposite, slowing down as it rises.

:::example A pendulum
A pendulum swings from side to side. Describe its energy at the ends of the swing and at the bottom.
@step At each end it is at its highest and, for an instant, at rest: all gravitational potential energy, no kinetic energy.
@step At the bottom it is at its lowest and moving fastest: the most kinetic energy, the least gravitational potential energy.
@answer The energy moves from the gravitational store to the kinetic store and back again on every swing.
:::

A real pendulum slowly stops. Its energy has not been destroyed. Air resistance and friction at the pivot transfer it, a little on each swing, to thermal energy in the surroundings. The same happens when a car brakes: its kinetic energy ends up as heat in the brakes.

:::mistake Saying energy is "used up"
Energy is never used up. When something stops moving, its kinetic energy has been transferred somewhere else, usually to heat that spreads into the surroundings where it is hard to use again.
:::

## Calculating with conservation of energy

When friction and air resistance are small enough to ignore, the kinetic and gravitational potential energy of an object add up to a total that does not change:

$$\text{kinetic energy} + \text{gravitational potential energy} = \text{constant}$$

:::example Sharing a total
A roller coaster car at rest at the top of a hill has 400 J of gravitational potential energy. Part of the way down, with no friction, it has 150 J of gravitational potential energy left. How much kinetic energy does it have there?
@step The total stays at 400 J.
@step $400 - 150$.
@answer It has 250 J of kinetic energy.
:::

For an object that falls from rest, all the gravitational potential energy it loses becomes kinetic energy:

$$mgh = \tfrac12 mv^2$$

:::example The speed after a fall
On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg. A 2 kg ball is dropped from rest from a height of 2.5 m. Ignore air resistance. Find its kinetic energy just before it lands, and its speed then.
@step Energy lost from the gravitational store: $mgh = 2 \times 9.8 \times 2.5 = 49$ J. This is the kinetic energy at the bottom.
@step $\tfrac12 mv^2 = 49$, so $v^2 = \dfrac{2 \times 49}{2} = 49$.
@answer The kinetic energy is 49 J and the speed is $\sqrt{49} = 7$ m/s.
:::

:::example How high a ball rises
On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg. A ball is thrown straight up at 14 m/s. Ignore air resistance. How high does it rise?
@step All the kinetic energy at the start becomes gravitational potential energy at the top: $\tfrac12 mv^2 = mgh$.
@step The mass cancels: $h = \dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{14^2}{2 \times 9.8} = \dfrac{196}{19.6}$.
@answer $h = 10$ m.
:::

Notice that the mass cancelled in the last example. With no air resistance, a heavy ball and a light ball thrown up at the same speed rise to the same height, and dropped from the same height they land at the same speed.

:::mistake Forgetting the square root
After finding $v^2$, take the square root to get the speed. In the falling ball example, $v^2 = 49$ gives $v = 7$ m/s, not 49 m/s.
:::

:::summary
- Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred between stores.
- Falling: gravitational potential energy becomes kinetic energy. Rising: the reverse.
- With no friction, kinetic energy + gravitational potential energy stays constant.
- For a fall from rest, $mgh = \tfrac12 mv^2$, and the mass cancels.
- Friction and air resistance transfer energy to heat in the surroundings.
:::
