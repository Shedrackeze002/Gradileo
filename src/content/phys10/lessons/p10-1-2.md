A sprinter and a snail can both travel one metre. What differs is how long it takes. Speed puts a number on "how fast", and with it you can work out how far something will go or how long a journey will take.

## Speed

:::definition Speed
**Speed** is the distance travelled each second. It tells us how fast something moves.

$$\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \qquad v = \dfrac{d}{t}$$

With distance in metres and time in seconds, speed is in **metres per second (m/s)**. A person walks at about 1.5 m/s, a cyclist rides at about 6 m/s, and a car on a highway travels at about 30 m/s.
:::

An object moves at a **steady speed** when it covers the same distance in every second.

:::example Finding a speed
A cyclist rides 120 m in 20 s at a steady speed. What is the speed?
@step Write the formula: $v = \dfrac{d}{t}$.
@step Put in the numbers: $v = \dfrac{120}{20}$.
@answer $v = 6$ m/s.
:::

The same formula gives a distance or a time when it is rearranged.

$$d = v \times t \qquad t = \dfrac{d}{v}$$

:::example Finding a distance
A train travels at a steady 25 m/s for 40 s. How far does it go?
@step $d = v \times t = 25 \times 40$.
@answer $d = 1000$ m.
:::

:::example Finding a time
A runner runs 300 m at a steady 5 m/s. How long does it take?
@step $t = \dfrac{d}{v} = \dfrac{300}{5}$.
@answer $t = 60$ s.
:::

:::example Changing units first
A bus travels 9 km in 15 min. What is its speed in m/s?
@step Change the distance to metres: $9 \times 1000 = 9000$ m.
@step Change the time to seconds: $15 \times 60 = 900$ s.
@answer $v = \dfrac{9000}{900} = 10$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
Kilometres divided by minutes does not give metres per second. Change the distance to metres and the time to seconds before you divide.

## Average speed

On a real journey the speed changes all the time: a bus speeds up, slows down and stops. The **average speed** describes the whole journey with one number.

:::definition Average speed
The **average speed** of a journey is the total distance divided by the total time, including any time spent at rest.

$$\text{average speed} = \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$$
:::

:::example A journey in two parts
Amara jogs 150 m in 40 s, then another 90 m in 20 s. What is Amara’s average speed?
@step Total distance: $150 + 90 = 240$ m.
@step Total time: $40 + 20 = 60$ s.
@answer Average speed $= \dfrac{240}{60} = 4$ m/s.
:::

:::example A journey with a rest
Kenji cycles 200 m in 30 s, rests for 20 s, then cycles 300 m in 50 s. What is Kenji’s average speed?
@step Total distance: $200 + 300 = 500$ m.
@step Total time, with the rest: $30 + 20 + 50 = 100$ s.
@answer Average speed $= \dfrac{500}{100} = 5$ m/s.
:::

:::example Two speeds for different times
A car travels at 10 m/s for 30 s, then at 20 m/s for 10 s. What is its average speed?
@step Find each distance: $10 \times 30 = 300$ m and $20 \times 10 = 200$ m.
@step Total distance 500 m, total time 40 s.
@answer Average speed $= \dfrac{500}{40} = 12.5$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Averaging the speeds
The average speed is not the mean of the speeds. In the last example the mean of 10 and 20 is 15 m/s, but the car spent longer at the slower speed, so the true average, 12.5 m/s, is lower.
:::

:::summary
- Speed is the distance travelled each second: $v = \dfrac{d}{t}$, in m/s.
- Rearranged: $d = v \times t$ and $t = \dfrac{d}{v}$.
- Change distances to metres and times to seconds before calculating.
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time, with any rests counted in the time.
:::
