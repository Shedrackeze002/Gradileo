Stand on a pier and watch the waves roll past a post. You can count how many pass each second, and you can see how far apart the crests are. Those two numbers are enough to tell you how fast the waves are travelling.

## Frequency and period

:::definition Frequency and period
The **frequency** $f$ is the number of complete waves each second, in hertz (Hz). The **period** $T$ is the time for one complete wave, in seconds.

$$f = \dfrac{\text{number of waves}}{\text{time}} \qquad T = \dfrac{1}{f} \qquad f = \dfrac{1}{T}$$
:::

:::example Counting waves
A float bobs up and down 20 times in 5 s as waves pass it. What is the frequency of the waves?
@step $f = \dfrac{20}{5}$.
@answer $f = 4$ Hz.
:::

:::example From frequency to period
A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz. What is its period?
@step $T = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{50}$.
@answer $T = 0.02$ s.
:::

:::example From period to frequency
A wave has a period of 0.25 s. What is its frequency?
@step $f = \dfrac{1}{T} = \dfrac{1}{0.25}$.
@answer $f = 4$ Hz.
:::

:::example Kilohertz and milliseconds
A sound has a frequency of 2 kHz. What is its period, in milliseconds?
@step 2 kHz = 2000 Hz.
@step $T = \dfrac{1}{2000} = 0.0005$ s.
@answer $T = 0.5$ ms, since 1 s = 1000 ms.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up frequency and period
Frequency is how many waves in one second. Period is how many seconds for one wave. A high frequency means a short period: 100 Hz is a period of only 0.01 s.
:::

## The wave equation

Each second, $f$ waves pass a point, and each one is $\lambda$ long. So the wave moves forward a distance of $f \times \lambda$ every second, and that is its speed.

:::definition The wave equation
$$\text{wave speed} = \text{frequency} \times \text{wavelength} \qquad v = f\lambda$$

With frequency in hertz and wavelength in metres, the speed is in m/s. The Greek letter $\lambda$ (lambda) stands for wavelength.
:::

$$f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} \qquad \lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$$

:::example Finding the speed
A water wave has a frequency of 2 Hz and a wavelength of 1.5 m. What is its speed?
@step $v = f\lambda = 2 \times 1.5$.
@answer $v = 3$ m/s.
:::

:::example Finding the frequency
A wave on a rope travels at 6 m/s and has a wavelength of 1.5 m. What is its frequency?
@step $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} = \dfrac{6}{1.5}$.
@answer $f = 4$ Hz.
:::

:::example Finding the wavelength
A sound wave travels at 340 m/s in air and has a frequency of 680 Hz. What is its wavelength?
@step $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f} = \dfrac{340}{680}$.
@answer $\lambda = 0.5$ m.
:::

:::example Changing units first
A sound wave of frequency 1.7 kHz travels at 340 m/s. What is its wavelength, in centimetres?
@step 1.7 kHz = 1700 Hz.
@step $\lambda = \dfrac{340}{1700} = 0.2$ m.
@answer $\lambda = 20$ cm.
:::

For waves travelling at the same speed, a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength. A high-pitched sound has waves packed closer together than a low-pitched one.

:::mistake Using kilohertz in the equation
The wave equation needs the frequency in hertz. For the last example, $340 \div 1.7 = 200$ is a thousand times too large. Change 1.7 kHz to 1700 Hz first.
:::

:::summary
- Frequency = number of waves ÷ time, in hertz.
- Period = 1 ÷ frequency, and frequency = 1 ÷ period.
- The wave equation: $v = f\lambda$.
- Use hertz and metres to get a speed in m/s.
- At the same speed, a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength.
:::
