The light you see is only a thin slice of a much larger family of waves. Radio signals, the microwaves in an oven, the warmth from a fire and the X-rays that show a broken bone are all the same kind of wave as light. They differ only in wavelength and frequency.

## The electromagnetic spectrum

:::definition Electromagnetic waves
**Electromagnetic waves** are transverse waves that can travel through a vacuum. In a vacuum they all travel at the same speed, the **speed of light**, $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s. Arranged in order of wavelength or frequency, they form the **electromagnetic spectrum**.
:::

| Wave | Typical use | Danger |
|---|---|---|
| radio waves | radio and television broadcasts | |
| microwaves | cooking, mobile phones, satellites | heating of body tissue |
| infrared | remote controls, heaters, thermal cameras | skin burns |
| visible light | seeing, photography, optical fibres | very bright light damages the eyes |
| ultraviolet | sterilising water, security marking | sunburn, skin cancer, eye damage |
| X-rays | images of bones, airport security | damage to cells, cancer |
| gamma rays | sterilising medical equipment, treating cancer | damage to cells, cancer |

The table runs from the **longest wavelength and lowest frequency** (radio) at the top to the **shortest wavelength and highest frequency** (gamma) at the bottom. Visible light itself runs from red (longest wavelength) to violet (shortest).

The higher the frequency, the more energy the wave can deliver. Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays carry enough to damage living cells, which is why sunscreen, lead aprons and thick shielding are used with them.

:::example Placing a wave
Which has the higher frequency, infrared or ultraviolet?
@step The order of increasing frequency is radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
@answer Ultraviolet comes after infrared in that order, so it has the higher frequency and the shorter wavelength.
:::

:::mistake Thinking gamma rays travel faster
Every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed in a vacuum. Gamma rays have a higher frequency than radio waves, not a higher speed. Their wavelength is shorter to match.
:::

## Calculating with the speed of light

Electromagnetic waves obey the same equations as every other wave: distance = speed × time, and $v = f\lambda$. The speed is very large, so the numbers are written in scientific notation.

:::example How long does a signal take?
All electromagnetic waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s in a vacuum. A radio signal is sent to a spacecraft $6.0 \times 10^8$ m away. How long does it take to arrive?
@step $t = \dfrac{d}{v} = \dfrac{6.0 \times 10^8}{3.0 \times 10^8}$.
@answer $t = 2$ s.
:::

:::example How far does light travel?
All electromagnetic waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s in a vacuum. Light from the Sun takes 500 s to reach the Earth. How far away is the Sun?
@step $d = vt = 3.0 \times 10^8 \times 500$.
@answer $d = 1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m.
:::

:::example Finding a wavelength
All electromagnetic waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s in a vacuum. A radio wave has a frequency of $1.5 \times 10^8$ Hz. What is its wavelength?
@step $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.5 \times 10^8}$.
@answer $\lambda = 2$ m.
:::

:::example Finding a frequency
All electromagnetic waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s in a vacuum. Orange light has a wavelength of $6.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m. What is its frequency?
@step $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{6.0 \times 10^{-7}}$.
@step Divide the numbers and subtract the powers: $0.5 \times 10^{15}$.
@answer $f = 5.0 \times 10^{14}$ Hz.
:::

To type an answer in scientific notation, write $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ as `1.5e11` or `1.5*10^11`.

:::mistake Adding the powers when dividing
When dividing, subtract the powers of ten: $10^8 \div 10^{-7} = 10^{8-(-7)} = 10^{15}$. Adding them would give $10^1$, which is far too small for the frequency of light.
:::

:::summary
- Electromagnetic waves are transverse and can cross a vacuum, all at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s.
- In order of increasing frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
- Higher frequency means shorter wavelength and more energy delivered.
- Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays can damage living cells.
- Use distance = speed × time and $v = f\lambda$ with the speed of light.
:::
