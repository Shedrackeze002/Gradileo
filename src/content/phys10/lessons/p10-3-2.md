A moving object can do things a resting one cannot: a rolling bowling ball knocks down pins, and a moving hammer drives in a nail. The energy an object has because it is moving is called kinetic energy.

## Kinetic energy

:::definition Kinetic energy
**Kinetic energy** is the energy an object has because of its motion.

$$\text{kinetic energy} = \tfrac12 \times \text{mass} \times \text{speed}^2 \qquad E_k = \tfrac12 mv^2$$

With mass in kg and speed in m/s, the energy is in joules. An object at rest has no kinetic energy.
:::

Work out the speed squared first, then multiply by the mass, then halve.

:::example Finding the kinetic energy
A trolley of mass 4 kg moves at 5 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?
@step Square the speed: $5^2 = 25$.
@step $E_k = \tfrac12 \times 4 \times 25$.
@answer $E_k = 50$ J.
:::

:::example A car
A car of mass 1000 kg moves at 20 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?
@step $20^2 = 400$.
@step $E_k = \tfrac12 \times 1000 \times 400$.
@answer $E_k = 200\,000$ J, which is 200 kJ.
:::

To find the mass or the speed, rearrange the equation:

$$m = \dfrac{2E_k}{v^2} \qquad v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2E_k}{m}}$$

:::example Finding the mass
An object moving at 6 m/s has 360 J of kinetic energy. What is its mass?
@step $m = \dfrac{2E_k}{v^2} = \dfrac{2 \times 360}{36}$.
@answer $m = 20$ kg.
:::

:::example Finding the speed
A 2 kg ball has 100 J of kinetic energy. How fast is it moving?
@step $v^2 = \dfrac{2E_k}{m} = \dfrac{2 \times 100}{2} = 100$.
@step Take the square root.
@answer $v = 10$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Squaring everything
Only the speed is squared. For the trolley above, $\tfrac12 \times 4 \times 5^2 = 50$ J. Squaring the whole product, $(\tfrac12 \times 4 \times 5)^2 = 100$, is wrong.
:::

## How mass and speed change kinetic energy

Kinetic energy is proportional to the **mass**, and to the **square of the speed**. So speed matters much more than mass.

| Change | Kinetic energy becomes |
|---|---|
| double the mass (same speed) | 2 times as large |
| double the speed (same mass) | 4 times as large |
| triple the speed (same mass) | 9 times as large |

:::example Doubling the speed
A car of mass 1000 kg speeds up from 10 m/s to 20 m/s. By how much does its kinetic energy increase?
@step At 10 m/s: $\tfrac12 \times 1000 \times 10^2 = 50\,000$ J.
@step At 20 m/s: $\tfrac12 \times 1000 \times 20^2 = 200\,000$ J.
@answer It increases by $150\,000$ J, or 150 kJ. Twice the speed gives four times the energy.
:::

This is why speed is so dangerous on the road. To stop a car, the brakes must take away all of its kinetic energy. At twice the speed there is four times as much to remove, so the car needs about four times the distance to stop.

:::mistake Thinking twice the speed is twice the energy
Doubling the speed does not double the kinetic energy: it makes it $2^2 = 4$ times as large. Doubling the **mass** is what doubles it.
:::

:::summary
- Kinetic energy is the energy of motion: $E_k = \tfrac12 mv^2$, in joules.
- Square the speed first, and only the speed.
- Double the mass gives double the kinetic energy.
- Double the speed gives four times the kinetic energy.
:::
