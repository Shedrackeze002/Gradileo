The first law says what happens when the net force is zero. The second law says what happens when it is not: the object accelerates. It also says exactly how much, which makes it the most used equation in mechanics.

## Newton's second law

:::definition Newton's second law of motion
A net force makes an object **accelerate** in the direction of the force. The acceleration is larger for a larger force and smaller for a larger mass.

$$\text{net force} = \text{mass} \times \text{acceleration} \qquad F = ma$$

With mass in kg and acceleration in m/s², the force is in newtons. One newton is the net force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².
:::

| Change | Effect on the acceleration |
|---|---|
| double the net force (same mass) | doubles |
| double the mass (same net force) | halves |
| net force zero | zero: the first law |

$$a = \dfrac{F}{m} \qquad m = \dfrac{F}{a}$$

:::example Finding the acceleration
A trolley of mass 5 kg has a net force of 20 N on it. What is its acceleration?
@step $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{20}{5}$.
@answer $a = 4$ m/s².
:::

:::example Finding the force
A go-kart of mass 40 kg accelerates at 2.5 m/s². What is the net force on it?
@step $F = ma = 40 \times 2.5$.
@answer $F = 100$ N.
:::

:::example Finding the mass
A net force of 60 N gives an object an acceleration of 3 m/s². What is its mass?
@step $m = \dfrac{F}{a} = \dfrac{60}{3}$.
@answer $m = 20$ kg.
:::

:::example From force to final velocity
A 4 kg cart starts from rest. A net force of 12 N acts on it for 5 s. How fast is it moving at the end?
@step Acceleration: $a = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ m/s².
@step Velocity: $v = u + at = 0 + 3 \times 5$.
@answer $v = 15$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Using grams
The mass must be in kilograms. A 500 g toy car has a mass of 0.5 kg, so a net force of 2 N gives it $2 \div 0.5 = 4$ m/s², not $2 \div 500$.

## Using the net force

The $F$ in $F = ma$ is the **net** force. When friction acts against a push, find the net force first.

:::example A push against friction
A 10 kg box is pushed along a floor with 50 N. Friction of 20 N acts against the motion. What is the acceleration?
@step Net force: $50 - 20 = 30$ N.
@step $a = \dfrac{30}{10}$.
@answer $a = 3$ m/s².
:::

:::example Finding the friction
A 5 kg crate is pushed with 40 N and accelerates at 2 m/s². How large is the friction?
@step Net force: $ma = 5 \times 2 = 10$ N.
@step Net force = push − friction, so friction $= 40 - 10$.
@answer Friction $= 30$ N.
:::

:::example When the push stops
A 4 kg sledge is sliding when the push on it stops. Only friction of 8 N acts along the ground. What is its acceleration?
@step The net force is the friction alone, backwards: $-8$ N.
@step $a = \dfrac{-8}{4}$.
@answer $a = -2$ m/s². The sledge slows down.
:::

:::mistake Putting the push alone into F = ma
In the first example, $50 \div 10 = 5$ m/s² is wrong, because 20 N of the push only cancels friction. Always use the net force.

:::summary
- Newton's second law: net force = mass × acceleration, $F = ma$.
- More force gives more acceleration; more mass gives less.
- Use kilograms for the mass and the net force for $F$.
- With friction, net force = push − friction.
:::
