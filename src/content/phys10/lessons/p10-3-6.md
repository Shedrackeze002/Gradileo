A petrol engine gets hot, and a motor hums and warms up. That heat and sound carry away energy that was meant to do something else. No machine transfers all the energy it is given in the way we want, and efficiency tells us how close it comes.

## Efficiency

:::definition Efficiency
The **efficiency** of a device is the fraction of the energy supplied to it that is transferred usefully.

$$\text{efficiency} = \dfrac{\text{useful energy out}}{\text{total energy in}} \times 100\%$$

The rest of the energy is **wasted**: it is transferred in ways that are not wanted, usually as heat and sound.
:::

Because energy is conserved, the useful and wasted energy always add up to the energy supplied:

$$\text{total energy in} = \text{useful energy out} + \text{wasted energy}$$

:::example Finding the efficiency
A motor is supplied with 500 J of energy and transfers 400 J usefully. What is its efficiency?
@step $\dfrac{400}{500} = 0.8$.
@step $0.8 \times 100\%$.
@answer The efficiency is 80%.
:::

:::example Useful and wasted energy
A petrol engine is 25% efficient and is supplied with 2000 J of energy. How much energy is transferred usefully, and how much is wasted?
@step Useful: $0.25 \times 2000 = 500$ J.
@step Wasted: $2000 - 500$.
@answer 500 J is useful and 1500 J is wasted.
:::

:::example Finding the energy supplied
A lamp is 40% efficient and transfers 200 J of energy usefully as light. How much energy was it supplied with?
@step Useful energy = efficiency × total energy in, so total energy in $= \dfrac{200}{0.4}$.
@answer It was supplied with 500 J.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way round
Efficiency is useful energy **out** divided by total energy **in**. The useful energy is always the smaller number, so the answer can never be more than 100%. If you get 125%, the fraction is upside down.
:::

## Useful and wasted energy transfers

Which transfer counts as useful depends on what the device is for.

| Device | Energy supplied | Useful transfer | Wasted transfers |
|---|---|---|---|
| lamp | electrical | light | heat |
| electric motor lifting a load | electrical | gravitational potential energy of the load | heat in the wires, sound |
| car engine | chemical (fuel) | kinetic energy of the car | heat, sound |
| electric heater | electrical | heat | almost none |

Wasted energy is not destroyed. It spreads out into the surroundings as heat, where it is too thinly spread to be useful again. No machine can be more than 100% efficient, because that would mean giving out more energy than it takes in.

Machines are made more efficient by cutting down the wasted transfers: oil reduces friction between moving parts, a streamlined shape reduces air resistance, and insulation keeps heat where it is wanted.

:::example Following all the energy
Each second a machine is supplied with 100 J of energy. It transfers 30 J usefully and wastes 10 J as sound. The rest is wasted as heat. Find the energy wasted as heat each second, and the efficiency.
@step Heat: $100 - 30 - 10 = 60$ J.
@step Efficiency: $\dfrac{30}{100} \times 100\%$.
@answer 60 J is wasted as heat each second, and the efficiency is 30%.
:::

:::mistake Thinking wasted energy disappears
The total energy out always equals the total energy in. "Wasted" only means the energy went somewhere that is not useful, such as warming the air.
:::

:::summary
- Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ total energy in × 100%.
- Total energy in = useful energy out + wasted energy.
- Wasted energy usually spreads into the surroundings as heat and sound.
- No machine is more than 100% efficient, because energy cannot be created.
- Less friction and less air resistance make a machine more efficient.
:::
