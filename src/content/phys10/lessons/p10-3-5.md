Two people carry identical boxes up the same staircase. One walks and one runs. They do exactly the same work, because the force and the height are the same. What differs is how quickly they do it, and that is what power measures.

## Power

:::definition Power
**Power** is the energy transferred, or the work done, each second.

$$\text{power} = \dfrac{\text{energy transferred}}{\text{time}} \qquad P = \dfrac{E}{t}$$

With energy in joules and time in seconds, power is in **watts (W)**. One watt is one joule each second, and 1 kW = 1000 W.
:::

$$E = Pt \qquad t = \dfrac{E}{P}$$

:::example Finding the power
A motor transfers 3000 J of energy in 20 s. What is its power?
@step $P = \dfrac{E}{t} = \dfrac{3000}{20}$.
@answer $P = 150$ W.
:::

:::example Finding the energy
A lamp has a power of 60 W. How much energy does it transfer in 30 s?
@step $E = Pt = 60 \times 30$.
@answer $E = 1800$ J.
:::

:::example Finding the time
A pump has a power of 250 W. How long does it take to transfer 5000 J?
@step $t = \dfrac{E}{P} = \dfrac{5000}{250}$.
@answer $t = 20$ s.
:::

:::example Kilowatts and minutes
A kettle has a power of 2 kW. How much energy does it transfer in 5 minutes, in kilojoules?
@step Change the units: 2 kW = 2000 W, and 5 min = 300 s.
@step $E = Pt = 2000 \times 300 = 600\,000$ J.
@answer $E = 600$ kJ.
:::

:::mistake Leaving the time in minutes
The watt is a joule each **second**. Change minutes to seconds before using $E = Pt$: for the kettle, $2000 \times 5$ would be 60 times too small.
:::

## Power and lifting

Lifting a load does work against gravity. The work equals the gravitational potential energy gained, $mgh$, and the power is that work divided by the time taken.

$$P = \dfrac{mgh}{t}$$

:::example A motor lifting a load
On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg. A motor lifts a 50 kg load through 10 m in 20 s at a steady speed. Find the work done against gravity and the useful power of the motor.
@step Work: $mgh = 50 \times 9.8 \times 10 = 4900$ J.
@step Power: $\dfrac{4900}{20}$.
@answer The work is 4900 J and the power is 245 W.
:::

:::example Running up stairs
On Earth, $g = 9.8$ N/kg. A student of mass 60 kg runs up a staircase 5 m high in 4 s. What is the student's useful power?
@step Work: $60 \times 9.8 \times 5 = 2940$ J.
@step Power: $\dfrac{2940}{4}$.
@answer $P = 735$ W.
:::

The same student walking up in 12 s does the same 2940 J of work, but with a power of only $2940 \div 12 = 245$ W. More time for the same work means less power.

:::mistake Mixing up work and power
A powerful machine does not always do more work. It does its work faster. Two cranes that lift identical loads to the same height do the same work, and the one that finishes in half the time has twice the power.
:::

:::summary
- Power is the energy transferred each second: $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$, in watts.
- 1 W = 1 J/s, and 1 kW = 1000 W. Use seconds for the time.
- Energy transferred = power × time.
- For lifting, work = $mgh$ and power = $\dfrac{mgh}{t}$.
:::
