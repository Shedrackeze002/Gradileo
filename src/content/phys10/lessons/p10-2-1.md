Nothing starts moving, stops, turns or changes shape by itself. Whenever the motion of an object changes, something has pushed or pulled it. This unit is about those pushes and pulls, and it begins by naming them and adding them up.

## What a force is

:::definition Force
A **force** is a push or a pull on an object. It can change the object's **speed**, its **direction** or its **shape**.

Force is a **vector**: it has a size and a direction. It is measured in **newtons (N)**. Lifting a small apple takes about 1 N.

We draw a force as an **arrow**: the arrow points the way the force acts, and a longer arrow means a larger force.
:::

| Force | What it is | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| weight | the pull of gravity on an object | downwards, towards the centre of the Earth |
| normal force | the push of a surface on an object resting on it | at right angles to the surface |
| friction | the grip between two surfaces | against the sliding |
| tension | the pull of a stretched rope or cable | along the rope |
| air resistance | the push of the air on a moving object | against the motion |

A **contact force** acts only where two things touch: friction, tension, the normal force and air resistance. A **non-contact force** acts across a gap: weight (gravity), magnetic forces and the forces between electric charges.

:::example Naming the forces
A box is pulled along a rough floor by a rope. Which forces act on the box?
@step What pulls on it from a distance? The Earth: its **weight** acts downwards.
@step What touches it? The floor pushes up with the **normal force** and rubs against it with **friction**. The rope pulls it with **tension**.
@answer Four forces: weight, the normal force, friction and tension.
:::

:::mistake Inventing a "force of motion"
A ball flying through the air is not being pushed along by a force from the kick. Once the foot stops touching it, that force is gone. Only gravity and the air act on the ball. Motion does not need a force to carry on.
:::

## Net force

Usually several forces act on an object at once. What matters for its motion is their combined effect.

:::definition Net force
The **net force** (or resultant force) is the single force that has the same effect as all the forces on an object acting together.

Along one line, forces in the **same direction add** and forces in **opposite directions subtract**. When the net force is zero, the forces are **balanced**. Otherwise they are **unbalanced**.
:::

:::example Opposite forces
A box is pushed to the right with 60 N while friction pulls to the left with 25 N. What is the net force?
@step The forces are opposite, so subtract: $60 - 25 = 35$ N.
@step The net force points the way of the larger force.
@answer 35 N to the right. The forces are unbalanced.
:::

:::example Three forces
Two people push a cart east with 40 N and 30 N, and friction acts west with 50 N. What is the net force?
@step Take east as positive: $40 + 30 - 50$.
@answer $+20$ N: a net force of 20 N east.
:::

:::example Balanced forces
A sledge is pulled forwards with 80 N and friction pulls backwards with 80 N. What is the net force?
@step $80 - 80 = 0$ N.
@answer Zero. The forces are balanced.
:::

:::example Finding a missing force
A crate is pushed to the right with 90 N, and the net force on it is 20 N to the right. How large is the friction?
@step Net force = push − friction, so $90 - \text{friction} = 20$.
@answer Friction $= 90 - 20 = 70$ N.
:::

:::summary
- A force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons. It is a vector, drawn as an arrow.
- Weight, magnetic and electric forces act without contact. The normal force, friction, tension and air resistance need contact.
- The net force is the combined effect of all the forces: add those in the same direction, subtract those in opposite directions.
- Zero net force means the forces are balanced.
:::
