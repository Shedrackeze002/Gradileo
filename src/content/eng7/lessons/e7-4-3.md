Some sentences can be checked; others are judgements. Some writers tell both sides; others lean heavily towards one. Telling **fact** from **opinion**, and spotting **bias**, are two of the most useful reading skills you can have.

## Fact and opinion

:::definition Fact and opinion
- A **fact** is a statement that can be checked and proved true or false. *Water freezes at 0 °C at sea level.*
- An **opinion** is a judgement or belief that people can disagree with. *Winter is the best season of the year.*
:::

A fact does not have to be true to be a *statement of fact*: “The stadium cost 30 million dollars” can be checked against records, even if the figure turns out to be wrong. An opinion can never be proved, only argued for.

Words that often signal an opinion include *best*, *worst*, *should*, *beautiful*, *terrible* and *a waste*. Words that often signal a fact include *measured*, *recorded*, *counted* and specific numbers and dates.

:::mistake Opinions dressed up as facts
Writers sometimes make an opinion sound like a fact: *Clearly, everyone agrees that homework is pointless.* Words like *clearly*, *obviously* and *everyone agrees* do not turn a judgement into a fact.
:::

## Bias

:::definition Bias
A text is **biased** when it favours one side unfairly. A **balanced** text gives more than one side fairly.
:::

Bias can show in three ways.

- **What is included and left out.** An article about a new road that lists only the benefits, and none of the noise or lost trees, is one-sided.
- **How things are worded.** Calling one group “protesters” and another “thugs” for doing the same thing reveals the writer’s view.
- **Who is writing.** A review of a phone written by the company that makes it has a reason to praise it.

:::example Spotting bias in a headline
Which headline is most balanced?

@step *Heartless council destroys our beloved pool* uses loaded words against the council.
@step *Council finally rids town of useless pool* uses loaded words for it.
@step *Council votes 5–4 to close the old pool* simply states what happened.
@answer The last headline is the most balanced.
:::

To check for bias, compare a text with other sources that might see things differently. What one source leaves out, another may include.

:::summary
- A **fact** can be checked; an **opinion** is a judgement people can disagree with.
- Words like *best*, *should* and *clearly* often signal opinions.
- **Bias** is an unfair lean towards one side, shown by what is left out, loaded words, or who is writing.
- Compare sources to check for bias.
:::
