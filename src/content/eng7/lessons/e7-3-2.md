In a story, a narrator can tell us what a character is thinking. In a play there is usually no narrator, so playwrights use other tools: what characters say to each other, and special speeches in which they speak to the audience or to themselves.

## Dialogue reveals character and moves the action

Dialogue in a play does two jobs at once. It **reveals character**, because how people speak shows who they are, and it **moves the action**, because what they say sets up the next event.

> MIRA: (whispering) Grandma said never to open the trunk.\
> JONAH: Then why are we here?\
> MIRA: (kneeling by the trunk) Because she never said why.

| Line | What it reveals | How it moves the action |
|---|---|---|
| *Grandma said never to open the trunk.* | Mira knows the rule | sets up the conflict: a rule about to be broken |
| *Then why are we here?* | Jonah is unsure, maybe scared | raises the question of what they will do |
| *Because she never said why.* | Mira’s curiosity beats her obedience | she is about to open the trunk |

Sometimes what matters most is *not* said. Two characters may chat about the weather while one keeps glancing at a letter on the table. The real subject, unspoken, is called **subtext**.

## Asides, monologues and soliloquies

:::definition Three special speeches
- An **aside** is a short remark to the audience that the other characters on stage are taken not to hear.
- A **monologue** is a long speech by one character to other characters.
- A **soliloquy** is a speech a character makes alone (or believing they are alone), thinking aloud.
:::

> KING: Welcome, my loyal friend!\
> DUKE: (aside) Loyal? If only he knew what I have planned.

The Duke’s aside lets the audience in on his secret. Now we know something the King does not. When the audience knows more than a character, it is called **dramatic irony**, and it makes us watch every scene with tension.

:::example Monologue or soliloquy?
> (The coach stands before the whole team.)\
> COACH: Listen, all of you. Last season we lost every game. Every single one. But this season, I promise you, things will be different…

@step One character gives a long speech.
@step The coach is talking to the team, not alone.
@answer A monologue. (If the coach were alone, thinking aloud, it would be a soliloquy.)
:::

:::mistake Monologue is not the same as soliloquy
Both are long speeches by one character. The difference is who is listening: a monologue is spoken *to others*; a soliloquy is spoken *alone*. Every soliloquy is a kind of monologue, but not every monologue is a soliloquy.
:::

:::summary
- Dialogue in a play reveals character and moves the action.
- **Subtext** is what characters mean but do not say.
- An **aside** is a quick word to the audience; a **monologue** is a long speech to others; a **soliloquy** is a character alone, thinking aloud.
- **Dramatic irony** is when the audience knows something a character does not.
:::
