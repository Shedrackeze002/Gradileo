Anyone can publish anything online. That makes the internet wonderful for finding information, and risky too, because not everything you read is true, up to date or honest about what it wants. In this lesson you will learn to check a source and its purpose.

## Is this source reliable?

Ask four questions of any source.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Who wrote it?** | A named author with the right qualifications, or a respected organization, is more trustworthy than an anonymous post. |
| **When was it written?** | A page about phones from 2009 may describe phones that no longer exist. |
| **How do they know?** | Good sources show their evidence: studies, data, named experts. |
| **Do other reliable sources agree?** | If several independent sources say the same, the fact is more likely true. This is called **cross-checking**. |

The web address can be a clue. In many countries, universities use addresses ending in *.edu* or *.ac.uk*, and government sites often end in *.gov* or *.gov.uk*. But an address is only one clue, not proof.

:::example A suspicious post
A post says: *Scientists prove chocolate cures colds!* It names no scientists and links to no study.

@step Who wrote it? We do not know.
@step How do they know? No study is named, so the claim cannot be checked.
@answer Treat it with doubt until a reliable source confirms it.
:::

## What does this text want?

Every online text has a **purpose**. Most aim to **inform**, **persuade**, **sell** or **entertain**.

- *Library opening hours: Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.* informs.
- *Write to your council today and demand safer crossings* persuades.
- *Get 50% off all trainers this weekend only!* sells.
- *Ten photos of cats who think they are dogs* entertains.

Some texts hide their purpose. A post that looks like a friendly personal review may actually be paid for by a company. Look for labels such as **Sponsored**, **Ad** or **Paid partnership**, and for discount codes and “buy now” links.

:::mistake A review is not always a review
*I tried GlowSkin cream for a week, and wow, my skin has never looked better! Use my code for 10% off.* If this is labelled “Sponsored”, the writer was paid. Its real purpose is to sell.
:::

:::summary
- Check **who** wrote a source, **when**, **how they know**, and whether other reliable sources **agree**.
- **Cross-checking** means comparing a fact with other independent sources.
- Online texts **inform**, **persuade**, **sell** or **entertain**.
- *Sponsored* and *Ad* mean someone paid for the post.
:::
