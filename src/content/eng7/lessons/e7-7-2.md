An essay is a group of paragraphs working together to explain or argue one main point. If a paragraph is a brick, an essay is a small building, and its plan always has the same three parts.

## The thesis statement

:::definition Thesis statement
A **thesis statement** is one or two sentences, usually at the end of the introduction, that state the main point of the whole essay.
:::

A strong thesis is:

- **specific**: *Team sports teach young people cooperation, discipline and resilience*, not *Sports are good*;
- **arguable** (for an argument essay): something a reader could disagree with, not a plain fact;
- often a **preview** of the main reasons, which become the body paragraphs.

| Weak | Why | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| *This essay is about cooking classes.* | announces a topic | *Schools should offer cooking classes because they teach health, budgeting and independence.* |
| *Zoos keep animals in enclosures.* | a fact, not arguable | *Zoos do more good than harm for endangered animals.* |
| *Technology is good and bad.* | too vague | *Video games can improve problem-solving skills.* |

## Introduction, body and conclusion

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | opens with a **hook** to grab the reader, gives brief background, and ends with the **thesis** |
| **Body paragraphs** | each makes **one point** that supports the thesis, with a topic sentence, evidence and explanation |
| **Conclusion** | restates the thesis **in new words**, sums up the main points, and ends with a final thought or call to action |

:::example Recognizing an introduction
> Have you ever wondered where your rubbish goes? Every year, millions of tonnes end up in landfill. Our town should introduce weekly food-waste collections.

@step *Have you ever wondered…?* is a hook.
@step *Every year, millions of tonnes…* gives background.
@step *Our town should…* is the thesis.
@answer This is an introduction.
:::

:::mistake No new arguments in the conclusion
A conclusion should remind the reader of your point and end with impact. If you think of a brand-new argument while writing the conclusion, it belongs in a body paragraph instead.
:::

:::summary
- The **thesis** states the main point of the essay; make it specific and, for an argument, arguable.
- The **introduction** has a hook, background and the thesis.
- Each **body paragraph** makes one point with evidence.
- The **conclusion** restates the thesis in new words and ends strongly, with no new arguments.
:::
