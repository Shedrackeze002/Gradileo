Stories do not always move in a straight line from beginning to end. A writer can drop a hint about the future, or jump back into the past. These two tools are called **foreshadowing** and **flashback**.

## Foreshadowing

:::definition Foreshadowing
**Foreshadowing** is a hint or clue about something that will happen later in a story.
:::

Foreshadowing does two jobs. It builds suspense, because the reader starts to wonder, and it makes later events feel believable, because the writer has prepared us for them. Common kinds of foreshadowing are a **warning** (“Stay off the pond”), an **odd detail** (a crack in a bridge), a **narrator’s hint** (“She didn’t know it yet, but…”) and a **change in the weather** (black clouds creeping in).

:::example Spotting foreshadowing
> As the cart rolled over the old bridge, Ravi heard it creak. He noticed a long crack running through the middle plank, but he said nothing.

@step The writer points out a creak and a crack. Why mention them unless they matter?
@step The detail hints that the bridge may fail.
@answer The crack foreshadows the bridge breaking later in the story.
:::

:::mistake A planted object is not just luck
If a character pockets a small key in chapter 1 and escapes a locked cellar in chapter 9, the key was foreshadowing. A good writer plants it early, so the escape does not feel like a lucky accident.
:::

## Flashback

:::definition Flashback
A **flashback** is a scene that interrupts the present story to show events from the past.
:::

Writers use flashbacks to explain a character’s fear, a secret or a relationship. Watch for signal words: *remembered*, *years before*, *back then*, *she was five again*. Watch, too, for the return to the present: *now*, *years later*, *someone touched his shoulder*.

:::example Why a flashback?
> The dog trotted towards them, wagging its tail. Kenji stepped back. He was five again, in the park, and the big brown dog was lunging, teeth flashing. He still had the scar on his arm.

@step *He was five again* takes us into the past.
@step The memory of the bite explains why Kenji steps back from a friendly dog.
@answer The flashback explains Kenji’s fear of dogs.
:::

| Foreshadowing | Flashback |
|---|---|
| hints at the **future** | shows the **past** |
| stays in the present scene | interrupts it with an earlier scene |
| builds suspense about what will happen | explains why things are as they are |

:::summary
- **Foreshadowing** hints at later events. It builds suspense and makes the ending feel planned.
- A **flashback** shows a scene from the past, often to explain a character.
- Signal words such as *remembered* and *years before* start a flashback; *now* and *years later* end it.
- Foreshadowing looks forward; flashback looks back.
:::
