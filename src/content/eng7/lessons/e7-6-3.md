Two of the most common sentence errors are opposites. A **fragment** is too little: it is missing part of a sentence. A **run-on** is too much: two sentences crammed together with nothing proper between them.

## Sentence fragments

:::definition Fragment
A **fragment** is a group of words punctuated like a sentence that is not a complete sentence. It is missing a subject, a verb or a complete thought.
:::

| Fragment | What is missing | One way to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| *Running down the hill.* | a subject and a full verb | *Leo was running down the hill.* |
| *Because the shop was closed.* | a main clause | *We left because the shop was closed.* |
| *The girl with the red umbrella.* | a verb | *The girl with the red umbrella waved.* |

A dependent clause on its own, such as *After the match ended*, is a fragment. Join it to an independent clause: *After the match ended, we went for pizza.*

:::mistake Commands are complete
*Stop!* is a full sentence. In a command, the subject *you* is understood: *(You) stop!*
:::

Writers of stories sometimes use fragments on purpose, for drama: *Footsteps. Closer. Closer.* In essays and formal writing, avoid them.

## Run-ons and comma splices

:::definition Run-on and comma splice
- A **run-on sentence** joins two independent clauses with no punctuation: *I love pizza I eat it every Friday.*
- A **comma splice** joins them with only a comma: *It was late, we went home.*
:::

A comma alone is not strong enough to hold two sentences together. There are four good fixes.

| Fix | Example |
|---|---|
| a full stop | *It was late. We went home.* |
| a comma and a coordinating conjunction | *It was late, so we went home.* |
| a semicolon | *It was late; we went home.* |
| a subordinating conjunction | *Because it was late, we went home.* |

:::example Fixing a comma splice
> Ravi was tired, he kept working.

@step There are two independent clauses: *Ravi was tired* and *he kept working*.
@step Only a comma joins them, so this is a comma splice.
@answer *Ravi was tired, but he kept working.* (Or: *Although Ravi was tired, he kept working.*)
:::

:::summary
- A **fragment** is missing a subject, a verb or a complete thought.
- A dependent clause alone is a fragment; join it to a main clause.
- A **run-on** has no punctuation between two sentences; a **comma splice** has only a comma.
- Fix them with a full stop, a semicolon, a comma and conjunction, or a subordinating conjunction.
:::
