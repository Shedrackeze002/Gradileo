The **setting** of a story is when and where it happens: the place, the time of day, the season, the weather and even the society the characters live in. A setting is never just a background. It can cause the conflict, shape what characters can do, and create the feeling of a scene.

## How setting shapes a story

:::definition Setting
The **setting** is the time and place of a story. It includes the physical place (a desert, a city, a ship), the time (night, winter, the year 1890) and the society (its rules and customs).
:::

Setting can shape a story in three main ways.

- **It can be the conflict.** A desert with no water, a flood or a blizzard puts characters in danger.
- **It decides what is possible.** In 1890 there were no phones, so a character stranded on a farm cannot just call for help.
- **It can echo a character’s feelings.** An empty grey beach can mirror a character’s loneliness.

:::example Setting as the problem
> Nia shook the bottle. Empty. In every direction the sand stretched to the horizon, shimmering in the heat. The nearest well was a day’s walk away.

@step The place is a desert, and the heat is fierce.
@step With no water and a long walk ahead, the setting itself is the danger.
@answer The setting causes the conflict: Nia must survive the desert.
:::

## Setting and mood

The **mood** of a passage is the feeling it gives the reader: peaceful, tense, gloomy, joyful, eerie. Writers create mood mainly through the details of the setting.

| Details | Mood they often create |
|---|---|
| warm light, gentle sounds, slow water | peaceful |
| silence, a flickering light, something moving | tense |
| fog, bare trees, cold rain | gloomy |
| music, laughter, bright colours | joyful |

:::example Naming the mood
> Every clock in the house had stopped at seven minutes past four. Dust lay thick on the wedding cake, and the curtains had not been opened in years.

@step Stopped clocks suggest that time froze at a terrible moment.
@step Dust and closed curtains suggest grief and neglect.
@answer The mood is gloomy and sad.
:::

:::mistake Mood is not the same as tone
**Mood** is how the passage makes the *reader* feel. **Tone** is the *writer’s or speaker’s* attitude. You will meet tone in the poetry unit.
:::

- A setting can cause the conflict, decide what is possible, and echo characters’ feelings.
- **Mood** is the feeling a passage gives the reader, and details of the setting create it.
- Look at light, sound, weather and colour to name the mood.
