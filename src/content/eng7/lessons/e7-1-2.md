We come to know people in real life by watching what they do and listening to what they say. We get to know characters in stories in the same way. The ways a writer reveals what a character is like are called **characterization**.

## Direct and indirect characterization

:::definition Two ways to reveal a character
- **Direct characterization**: the writer tells us a trait straight out. *Mia was the most generous person in the village.*
- **Indirect characterization**: the writer shows us, and we **infer** the trait. *Mia gave her only coat to the shivering stranger and walked home in the snow.*
:::

Indirect characterization asks more of the reader, which is why it often feels more real. We are trusted to work the character out for ourselves, as we do with people we meet.

:::example Direct or indirect?
> “Hurry up! I haven’t got all day,” Omar snapped, tapping his foot.

@step The writer never uses the word *impatient*.
@step We work it out from what Omar says (*Hurry up!*) and does (taps his foot).
@answer Indirect characterization.
:::

## Five clues to character

When a writer uses indirect characterization, look for five kinds of clue. Some readers remember them as **STEAL**.

| Clue | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **S**peech | What does the character say, and how? |
| **T**houghts | What does the character think or feel inside? |
| **E**ffect on others | How do other characters react to them? |
| **A**ctions | What does the character do? |
| **L**ooks | What does the character look like, and what do they wear? |

A character often has more than one trait, and the traits can pull against each other. Someone can be proud *and* kind, or brave *and* frightened. The best answer usually fits all the clues, not just one.

:::example Reading the clues
> “I don’t need anyone’s help,” Kofi said, lifting the heavy box alone. On the way out, he saw an old man struggling with his shopping, and without a word he carried it to his car.

@step Speech: *I don’t need anyone’s help* suggests pride.
@step Actions: helping a stranger without being asked shows kindness.
@answer Kofi is proud but kind.
:::

:::mistake Do not stop at the first clue
Outside, a character may smile; inside, their thoughts may be full of worry. If you only read the smile, you will miss the nerves. Check speech, thoughts and actions together.
:::

:::summary
- **Characterization** is how a writer reveals what a character is like.
- **Direct** characterization names a trait; **indirect** characterization shows it, and we infer the trait.
- Look for five clues: **speech, thoughts, effect on others, actions and looks** (STEAL).
- Characters often have more than one trait, so choose the answer that fits every clue.
:::
