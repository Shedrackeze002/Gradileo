A story can be about a girl who plants a tree, and also about something much bigger: growing up, hope, or family. Writers carry those bigger ideas through **symbols**, and the bigger message of the story is its **theme**.

## Symbols

:::definition Symbol
A **symbol** is something concrete (an object, a person, a place, an animal or even a colour) that stands for a bigger, abstract idea.
:::

Some symbols are shared by many cultures: a white dove for peace, a rising sun for a new beginning, a storm for trouble. Others belong to one story. If a girl plants a tree when her brother is born and it grows as he grows, the tree stands for his life.

How can you tell that an object is a symbol? Writers rarely say so. Look for something that **keeps appearing**, that is linked to a **character’s feelings**, or that **changes** when the character changes.

:::example Reading a symbol
> Every evening, Lucas stood at the locked gate of the big house and watched the children play on the lawn. He could hear their laughter, but the bars were cold under his hands.

@step The gate keeps Lucas outside, while the laughter is inside.
@step The cold bars stress how shut out he feels.
@answer The locked gate symbolizes Lucas being shut out of a life he wants.
:::

## How a theme develops

In Grade 6 you learned that a **theme** is a message about life, stated as a full sentence. A **topic** is just a subject.

| Topic | Theme |
|---|---|
| courage | Courage means acting even when you are afraid. |
| friendship | True friends stand by you when things go wrong. |
| greed | Greed leads to loss. |

A theme is not usually stated in one place. It **develops** across the story, mostly through how the main character changes. To trace a theme, compare the character at the start, in the middle and at the end, and ask what they have learned.

:::example Tracing a theme
> At the start, Zara refused every offer of help: “I can do it myself.” By the middle, her project was falling apart. At the end, she finally asked Ben to help, and together they finished it in an afternoon.

@step Start: Zara will not accept help.
@step Middle: doing it alone is not working.
@step End: she asks for help and succeeds.
@answer The theme is that asking for help is a strength, not a weakness.
:::

Symbols and themes often work together. Curtains kept closed since a mother’s death might stand for grief; when the son finally opens them at the end, the symbol shows the theme of healing.

:::summary
- A **symbol** is something concrete that stands for a bigger idea.
- Symbols are often repeated, linked to feelings, and change as the story changes.
- A **theme** is a full-sentence message about life; a **topic** is a single subject.
- Trace a theme by comparing the main character at the start and the end.
:::
