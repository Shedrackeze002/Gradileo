In a correct sentence, words that belong together must **agree**: a singular subject takes a singular verb, and a pronoun matches the noun it stands for. Agreement is easy in short sentences and tricky in long ones, so this lesson focuses on the tricky cases.

## Subject–verb agreement

:::definition Agreement
A verb must **agree** with its subject in number: a **singular** subject takes a singular verb (*my sister plays*), and a **plural** subject takes a plural verb (*the dogs play*).
:::

The hard part is finding the real subject.

- **Ignore phrases in between.** *The box **of chocolates** is on the table.* The subject is *box*, so the verb is *is*.
- **Each, every, either, neither and one are singular.** *Each of the players gets a medal.*
- **With *neither… nor* or *either… or*, match the nearer subject.** *Neither the teacher nor the students were ready.*
- **A group acting as one unit is usually singular.** *The team is celebrating its win.* (In British English, *the team are* is also common, but then it is *their win*.)

:::example Finding the subject
> The list of names on the board ___ long.

@step Cross out the phrase *of names on the board*.
@step The subject is *list*, which is singular.
@answer *The list of names on the board **is** long.*
:::

## Pronoun–antecedent agreement

:::definition Antecedent
The **antecedent** is the noun a pronoun stands for. In *The girls packed their bags*, *girls* is the antecedent of *their*.
:::

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number.

- *The tree lost **its** leaves.* (one tree)
- *The committee announced **its** decision.* (one committee)
- *Neither of the boys remembered **his** coat.* (*neither* is singular)

For a word like *everyone* or *someone*, when we do not know the person’s gender, **singular *they*** is widely accepted: *Everyone should bring their own lunch.* *His or her* is also correct.

:::mistake Unclear pronouns
*When Tom met Jack, he smiled.* Who smiled? If a pronoun could point to two people, name the person instead: *When Tom met Jack, Jack smiled.*
:::

:::summary
- A verb agrees with its subject: singular with singular, plural with plural.
- Ignore phrases between the subject and verb; *each* and *every* are singular.
- A pronoun agrees with its **antecedent**, the noun it replaces.
- Avoid pronouns that could point to more than one person.
:::
