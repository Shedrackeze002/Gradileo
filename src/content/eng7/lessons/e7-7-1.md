A paragraph is a group of sentences about one main idea. Strong paragraphs are the building blocks of every essay, report and story, and they share the same shape: a clear main idea, details that support it, and an ending that ties it together.

## The topic sentence

:::definition Parts of a paragraph
- The **topic sentence** states the main idea of the paragraph. It usually comes first.
- **Supporting details** (reasons, facts, examples and explanations) back up the main idea.
- The **concluding sentence** wraps up the point and often links to the next idea.
:::

A good topic sentence is **focused**: narrow enough for one paragraph, but broad enough to need support. *The history of the whole world is interesting* is far too broad; *Bicycles were invented a long time ago* is a single fact with nothing to argue. *Cycling to school has many benefits for students* is just right.

:::example Choosing a topic sentence
These are the supporting details of a paragraph:

> They carry pollen from flower to flower. Without them, crops such as apples and almonds would produce far less fruit. Farmers even rent hives to pollinate their fields.

@step Every detail is about bees helping crops grow.
@step The topic sentence must make that point.
@answer *Bees are vital to growing our food.*
:::

In an essay, a topic sentence should also make a clear point that supports the thesis. *This paragraph is going to be about school uniforms* only announces a topic; *Uniforms reduce pressure on students to wear expensive clothes* makes a point.

## Unity and coherence

:::definition Unity and coherence
- A paragraph has **unity** when every sentence supports the main idea.
- A paragraph has **coherence** when its sentences follow a logical order and connect smoothly, often with transitions such as *first*, *also*, *however* and *as a result*.
:::

:::example Finding the sentence that does not belong
> Parrots are a bigger commitment than most people realize. Many parrots can live for more than fifty years. Some parrots need hours of attention every day. My grandmother has a parrot called Rio. Parrots can be noisy, especially in the morning.

@step The main idea is that parrots are a big commitment.
@step Living fifty years, needing attention and being noisy all support it.
@answer *My grandmother has a parrot called Rio* adds nothing to the point, so it breaks the unity.
:::

Choose an order that suits the paragraph: **time order** for instructions and stories (*first, next, then, finally*), **order of importance** for arguments, and **space order** for describing a place.

:::summary
- A paragraph has a **topic sentence**, **supporting details** and a **concluding sentence**.
- A good topic sentence is focused and makes a clear point.
- **Unity**: every sentence supports the main idea.
- **Coherence**: sentences follow a logical order and are linked by transitions.
:::
