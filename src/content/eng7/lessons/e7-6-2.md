Good writers vary their sentences. Short simple sentences are punchy; longer sentences that join ideas show how those ideas connect. In this lesson you will join clauses with **subordinating conjunctions** and learn the four sentence structures.

## Subordinating conjunctions

A **subordinating conjunction** begins a dependent clause and shows how it connects to the main clause.

| Relationship | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| cause | because, since, as | *We stayed in **because** it was raining.* |
| contrast | although, though, even though | ***Although** she was tired, she finished the race.* |
| condition | if, unless | *You will miss the bus **unless** you hurry.* |
| time | when, while, after, before, until | ***While** the cake baked, we washed the dishes.* |

Choose the conjunction that shows the right relationship. *Although she was tired, she finished* shows a contrast; *because she was tired, she finished* would make no sense.

:::example Joining two sentences
> The museum was closed. We visited the park.

@step The first sentence is the reason for the second: a cause.
@step *Since* shows cause and can open the sentence.
@answer *Since the museum was closed, we visited the park.*
:::

## Four sentence structures

| Structure | Clauses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | one independent clause | *The cat slept on the warm windowsill.* |
| **Compound** | two or more independent clauses, joined by *and*, *but*, *or*, *so* (or a semicolon) | *The cat slept, and the dog played.* |
| **Complex** | one independent clause and at least one dependent clause | *The cat slept because the sun was warm.* |
| **Compound-complex** | two or more independent clauses and at least one dependent clause | *When the storm ended, the birds sang, and the children ran outside.* |

:::mistake Two subjects do not make two clauses
*Ana and Ben laughed and danced all night* has two subjects and two verbs, but they belong to one clause. It is still a simple sentence.
:::

:::example Naming a structure
> If it rains, we will stay in, but we will still have fun.

@step Dependent clause: *If it rains*.
@step Independent clauses: *we will stay in* and *we will still have fun*, joined by *but*.
@answer Compound-complex.
:::

:::summary
- **Subordinating conjunctions** show cause, contrast, condition or time.
- **Simple**: one independent clause. **Compound**: two or more independent clauses.
- **Complex**: an independent clause and a dependent clause.
- **Compound-complex**: two or more independent clauses and at least one dependent clause.
:::
