Sentences are built from smaller units, like a wall from bricks. The two most important units are **phrases** and **clauses**. Once you can tell them apart, you can build any kind of sentence and fix sentences that go wrong.

## Phrases and clauses

:::definition Phrase and clause
- A **clause** is a group of words with a **subject** and a **verb** working together. *the dog barked*
- A **phrase** is a group of words *without* a subject–verb pair. *under the old bridge*
:::

To test a group of words, ask: *Who or what is doing something here?* If you can find a subject doing a verb, it is a clause.

| Group of words | Subject + verb? | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| *the dog barked* | dog + barked | clause |
| *because the bus was late* | bus + was | clause |
| *after the long and tiring match* | none (*match* is a noun) | phrase |
| *running through the forest at night* | none: who is running? | phrase |

:::mistake An -ing word is not always a verb with a subject
*Running through the forest* looks busy, but nobody is named as doing the running. Without a subject, it is a phrase. *Mia was running through the forest* is a clause.
:::

## Independent and dependent clauses

:::definition Two kinds of clause
- An **independent clause** makes complete sense on its own and can stand as a sentence. *The bell rang.*
- A **dependent clause** has a subject and verb but cannot stand alone; it depends on another clause. *Although the bell rang…*
:::

Dependent clauses usually begin with a **subordinating word** such as *because*, *although*, *when*, *if*, *since*, *while*, *after* or *unless*. Adding one of these words to an independent clause makes it dependent: *we went inside* becomes *because we went inside*, which leaves us waiting for more.

:::example Finding the independent clause
> When the rain stopped, the children ran outside.

@step *When the rain stopped* has a subject (rain) and verb (stopped), but *when* makes it wait for more.
@step *the children ran outside* makes sense on its own.
@answer The independent clause is *the children ran outside*.
:::

:::summary
- A **clause** has a subject and a verb; a **phrase** does not.
- An **independent clause** can stand alone as a sentence.
- A **dependent clause** cannot stand alone; it often begins with *because*, *although*, *when* or *if*.
- Ask: *who or what is doing something?* to test for a clause.
:::
