Research is how we find out what we do not already know. A good research project starts with a focused question, uses reliable sources, and gives credit to every source it uses.

## The research question

:::definition Research question
A **research question** is the question your research sets out to answer. It focuses your search for information.
:::

A good research question is:

- **focused**: *How do bees find their way back to their hive?*, not *Bees*;
- **not too broad**: *What is the history of science?* would take a lifetime;
- **not too narrow**: *What year was the Eiffel Tower finished?* can be answered in seconds;
- **open**: it needs information from several sources to answer.

:::example Improving a research question
> Is pollution bad?

@step The answer is obviously yes, and *pollution* is huge.
@step Narrow it to one kind of pollution, one living thing and one place.
@answer *How does plastic pollution affect sea turtles in the Mediterranean?*
:::

## Paraphrasing, quoting and citing

:::definition Using sources
- To **quote** is to use a source’s exact words, inside quotation marks.
- To **paraphrase** is to put a source’s idea into your own words and sentence structure.
- To **cite** is to say where an idea or quotation came from.
- **Plagiarism** is using someone else’s words or ideas without saying where they came from.
:::

Here is a source and a paraphrase of it.

> Honeybees perform a “waggle dance” to tell other bees where flowers are. The angle of the dance shows the direction, and its length shows the distance.

*Paraphrase:* Bees share the location of food by dancing: the angle of the dance gives the direction, and how long it lasts gives the distance.

The paraphrase keeps the meaning but changes the words and the order. It still needs a citation, because **the idea belongs to the original author**.

Quote when the exact words are especially powerful or important; paraphrase the rest. A citation for a website usually gives the **author or organization**, the **title**, the **date** and the **address**, so that a reader can find it and judge it.

:::mistake Changing a few words is not paraphrasing
Swapping one or two words for synonyms while keeping the original sentence is still too close to the source. Read the passage, look away, and write the idea in your own way. Then check that you kept the meaning.
:::

:::summary
- A good **research question** is focused, neither too broad nor too narrow, and needs several sources.
- **Quote** exact words; **paraphrase** ideas in your own words.
- Always **cite** your sources, whether you quote or paraphrase.
- **Plagiarism** is presenting others’ words or ideas as your own.
:::
