A news report looks simple, but every one is carefully built. Reporters follow a pattern that gets the key facts to readers quickly, and the choices they make about words and order give each story its **angle**.

## How a news report is built

> Hundreds of volunteers cleared 2 tonnes of litter from Riverside Beach on Saturday morning. The clean-up, organized by the local sailing club, began at 8 a.m. and ended at noon. “We were amazed by how many people came,” said club captain Ana Silva. The council has promised new bins along the beach by the summer.

:::definition Features of a news report
- The **headline** sums up the story in a few words.
- The **lead** is the first sentence or paragraph, giving the most important facts.
- The **five Ws**: a report answers **who**, **what**, **when**, **where** and **why** (and often **how**).
- **Quotations** from people involved add a human voice.
- The **inverted pyramid**: the most important facts come first, and less important details later.
:::

| W | In the report |
|---|---|
| Who | hundreds of volunteers; the sailing club |
| What | cleared 2 tonnes of litter |
| When | Saturday morning, 8 a.m. to noon |
| Where | Riverside Beach |
| Why | to clean up the beach (implied) |

The inverted pyramid means a reader who stops after one sentence still knows what happened. The promise of new bins, the least important detail, comes last.

## The angle of a story

The **angle** is the focus or point of view a writer chooses. Two reporters can describe the same event very differently by choosing:

- **which facts go in the headline and lead**,
- **whom to quote first**,
- **which photograph to print**, and
- **which words to use**.

:::example Same event, two angles
*Shop owners cheer new car park* and *Park lost to concrete as car park opens* report the same opening.

@step The first stresses the joy of shop owners.
@step The second stresses the loss of a green space.
@answer The headlines give the same event two different angles.
:::

Single words matter too. *The mayor said the plan was late* is neutral; *the mayor admitted the plan was late* suggests owning up to a failure.

:::summary
- A news report has a **headline**, a **lead**, the **five Ws**, **quotations** and an **inverted pyramid** shape.
- The most important facts come first.
- A story’s **angle** is the focus the writer chooses.
- Headlines, quotations, photographs and single words such as *admitted* shape the angle.
:::
