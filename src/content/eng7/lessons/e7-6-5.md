Commas and quotation marks are small, but they tell a reader how to read a sentence: where to pause, which words are spoken, and who is speaking. This lesson covers commas with clauses and the rules for punctuating dialogue.

## Commas with clauses

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| After a dependent clause that **opens** a sentence | *When the bell rang, we ran outside.* |
| Before *and*, *but*, *or* or *so* joining **two independent clauses** | *It was cold, so we lit the fire.* |
| Around **extra information** in the middle of a sentence | *My uncle, who lives in Lagos, is visiting.* |

When the dependent clause comes *after* the main clause, a comma is usually not needed: *We lit the fire because it was cold.*

:::example Placing the commas
> If you are free come to the party and bring a friend.

@step *If you are free* is an opening dependent clause, so a comma follows it.
@step *come to the party* and *bring a friend* are joined by *and*; both are commands with *you* understood, so a comma can go before *and*.
@answer *If you are free, come to the party, and bring a friend.*
:::

:::mistake Extra information needs two commas
*My uncle, who lives in Lagos is visiting* is missing a comma. Information in the middle of a sentence needs a comma on **both** sides, like a pair of brackets.
:::

## Punctuating dialogue

:::definition The rules of dialogue
- Put the spoken words inside **quotation marks**.
- The punctuation that ends the speech goes **inside** the closing quotation mark.
- Use a **comma** between the speech and the **dialogue tag** (*said Ana*), unless the speech ends with ? or !.
- Begin a **new paragraph** each time a new person speaks.
:::

| Correct | Why |
|---|---|
| *“Wait for me,” called Leo.* | comma inside the quotation marks, before the tag |
| *Leo asked, “Where are you going?”* | comma after the tag; question mark inside |
| *“Run!” shouted Leo. “The bridge is falling!”* | ! inside; tag not capitalized; a new sentence of speech after the full stop |
| *“I can’t,” said Leo, “because I have to work.”* | one sentence split by the tag, so commas and no capital |

**Direct speech** gives a person’s exact words in quotation marks. **Reported speech** tells what they said without quoting it, and needs no quotation marks: *Leo said that he was hungry.*

:::summary
- Use a comma after an opening dependent clause, before a conjunction joining two sentences, and on both sides of extra information.
- In dialogue, end punctuation goes inside the quotation marks.
- Use a comma between speech and tag, and start a new paragraph for each new speaker.
- Reported speech needs no quotation marks.
:::
