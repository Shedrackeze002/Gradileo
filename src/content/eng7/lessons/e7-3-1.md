A play is written to be performed, not just read. Its text, the **script**, is a set of instructions for actors, directors and designers, and it looks quite different from a story on the page. In this lesson you will learn to read one.

## The parts of a script

:::definition Script
A **script** is the written text of a play. It is written by a **playwright** (*-wright* means maker, as in *shipwright*).
:::

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Cast list** | the list of characters at the start, often with a short description of each |
| **Acts and scenes** | the divisions of a play: acts are the large parts, and scenes are the smaller parts inside them; a new scene usually means a new place or time |
| **Dialogue** | the words the characters speak, each line after the speaker’s name |
| **Stage directions** | instructions about the setting, actions, sounds and how lines are spoken, usually in brackets or italics |

Here is the start of a scene written for this unit.

> Scene 2. An attic, dusty and dim. A single window. A large trunk stands in the corner.
>
> (Mira enters, holding a torch. Jonah follows, close behind.)
>
> MIRA: (whispering) Grandma said never to open the trunk.\
> JONAH: Then why are we here?\
> MIRA: (kneeling by the trunk) Because she never said why.\
> (A floorboard creaks downstairs. They both freeze.)

Notice what is missing: there are no quotation marks, no “she said”, and no narrator telling us what Mira is thinking. Everything we learn comes from the dialogue and the stage directions.

## What stage directions do

Stage directions do several jobs at once.

- **Set the scene**: *An attic, dusty and dim* tells the designer what to build and gives the reader the setting and mood.
- **Show actions**: *Jonah follows, close behind* shows that he is nervous about being left alone.
- **Show how to speak**: *(whispering)* tells the actor how to say the line, and tells us Mira is afraid of being heard.
- **Add sounds and effects**: *A floorboard creaks downstairs* builds suspense.

:::example Reading a stage direction
@step Mira says *Because she never said why* while *kneeling by the trunk*.
@step Kneeling by it as she speaks shows that she means to look inside.
@answer The direction shows Mira’s decision in action: she is going to open the trunk.
:::

:::mistake Do not skip the brackets
When reading a play, it is tempting to read only the lines. But a reader cannot see the stage. The stage directions supply the actions, sounds and tones of voice that complete the meaning.
:::

:::summary
- A **script** is the written text of a play, written by a **playwright**.
- A script has a **cast list**, **acts and scenes**, **dialogue** and **stage directions**.
- Each line of dialogue follows the speaker’s name; there are no quotation marks.
- Stage directions set the scene, show actions and sounds, and show how lines are spoken.
:::
