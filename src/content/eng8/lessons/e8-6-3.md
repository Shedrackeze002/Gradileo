Listen to the rhythm of “We will fight for our schools, for our parks and for our future.” Each part has the same shape, so the sentence is easy to follow and easy to remember. That is **parallel structure**. In this lesson you will recognise it and fix sentences where it breaks.

## What parallel structure is

:::definition Parallel structure
**Parallel structure** means using the same grammatical form for items in a list or a pair: all *-ing* words, all *to* + verb, all nouns, all adjectives, or all clauses of the same shape.
:::

| Parallel | Not parallel |
|---|---|
| Ada likes **reading**, **painting** and **dancing**. | Ada likes reading, to paint and dancing. |
| The coach told us **to stretch**, **to drink** water and **to rest**. | The coach told us to stretch, drinking water and rest. |
| The room was **clean**, **bright** and **quiet**. | The room was clean, bright and had quiet. |

Pairs joined by *not only … but also*, *either … or*, *neither … nor* and *both … and* need parallel structure too. The words after each half should be the same kind of word.

> The plan is not only **cheap** but also **quick**.

:::example Finding the odd one out
@step “On Saturday Ada had to wash the car, to feed the cat and she sang loudly.”
@step Look at the form of each item: *to wash the car*, *to feed the cat*, *she sang loudly*.
@step The first two are *to* + verb. The third is a whole clause.
@answer “She sang loudly” breaks the parallel structure.
:::

Speakers love parallel structure because the repeated pattern creates rhythm and makes ideas memorable. Famous speeches are full of it.

## Fixing faulty parallelism

To fix a sentence:

1. Find the items in the list or pair.
2. Decide which form most of them use.
3. Rewrite the odd one out in that form.

:::example Fixing a list
@step “The job requires patience, being careful and good skills with people.”
@step The items are *patience* (a noun), *being careful* (an -ing phrase) and *good skills with people* (a noun phrase).
@step Make them all nouns: *patience*, *care*, *people skills*.
@answer “The job requires patience, care and people skills.”
:::

Comparisons need balance too. “It is better to give than receiving” compares two different forms. Make them match: “It is better **to give** than **to receive**.”

:::mistake Watch the last item
Faulty parallelism usually appears at the end of a list, when the writer runs out of steam and adds a whole new clause: “The essay was clear, well organised and it persuaded readers.” Fix it with a matching word: “The essay was clear, well organised and persuasive.”
:::

:::summary
- **Parallel structure** uses the same form for every item in a list or pair.
- Pairs such as *not only … but also* and comparisons such as *better to … than to …* must be balanced.
- To fix a sentence, find the odd item and rewrite it in the same form as the others.
:::
