“The dog ate the cake” and “The cake was eaten by the dog” describe the same event, but they point the reader in different directions. The first is in the **active voice**; the second is in the **passive voice**. In this lesson you will tell them apart, change one into the other, and decide which voice suits a sentence.

## Active and passive

:::definition Active and passive voice
In the **active voice**, the subject *does* the action: *Amara kicked the ball.* In the **passive voice**, the subject *receives* the action: *The ball was kicked.* The passive uses a form of **be** (*is, was, were, has been, will be*) plus a past participle (*kicked, eaten, opened*).
:::

The doer in a passive sentence, called the **agent**, can come after *by*, or it can be left out altogether.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The dog ate the cake. | The cake was eaten by the dog. |
| The mayor will open the library. | The library will be opened by the mayor. |
| The storm damaged three houses. | Three houses were damaged by the storm. |
| Someone broke a window. | A window was broken. |

:::example Changing passive to active
@step Start with: “The match was won by our team in the last minute.”
@step Find the doer: *our team*. Make it the subject.
@step Change the verb from *was won* to *won*, and make *the match* the object.
@answer “Our team won the match in the last minute.” The active version is shorter and puts the doer first.
:::

:::mistake Past tense is not passive
“Amara was running” uses *was*, but Amara is still doing the running, so it is active. A sentence is passive only when the subject receives the action. Ask: is the subject doing it, or having it done to them?

## Choosing a voice

Writers usually prefer the **active voice**, because it is clearer and more direct about who did what. But the passive is the right choice in some situations.

| Use the passive when… | Example |
|---|---|
| the doer is **unknown** | A window was broken at the school last night. |
| the action matters more than the doer | The water was heated to 80 °C. (a science method) |
| the doer is **obvious** or unimportant | The rubbish is collected on Mondays. |

The passive can also be used to **dodge responsibility**, because it lets the writer leave out who did the action.

:::example Spotting a hidden doer
@step A company writes to customers: “Errors were made in your bill.”
@step The passive *were made* has no agent: it never says who made the errors.
@step The active version would be “We made errors in your bill.”
@answer The passive avoids saying that the company made the errors. When you see a passive with no agent, ask who did it.
:::

:::summary
- **Active**: the subject does the action. **Passive**: the subject receives it, with *be* + past participle.
- To make a passive active, make the doer the subject.
- Prefer the active for clarity. Use the passive when the doer is unknown or unimportant, or when the action matters most.
- A passive with no agent can hide who is responsible.
:::
