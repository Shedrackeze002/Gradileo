*A Midsummer Night’s Dream* is one of William Shakespeare’s best-loved comedies, first performed in the mid-1590s. Four groups of characters, from a royal court to a band of fairies, collide in a magical wood outside Athens over one confusing night. In this lesson you will meet the characters and follow how their stories tangle together and come apart.

## Four groups, four stories

| Group | Who | Their story |
|---|---|---|
| **The court** | Theseus, Duke of Athens, and Hippolyta, Queen of the Amazons | they are preparing for their wedding |
| **The lovers** | Hermia, Lysander, Helena and Demetrius, four young Athenians | a tangle of love that goes wrong in the woods |
| **The fairies** | Oberon and Titania, the fairy king and queen, and Puck, Oberon’s servant | a royal quarrel and a magic love potion |
| **The mechanicals** | Bottom, Quince and other workmen of Athens | rehearsing a play to perform at the Duke’s wedding |

## The plot

**Act 1.** The play begins in Athens, at the court of Duke Theseus. Egeus wants his daughter Hermia to marry Demetrius, but she loves Lysander. By Athenian law, she must obey her father or face death or life in a convent. Hermia and Lysander plan to run away through the woods. Hermia tells her friend Helena, who loves Demetrius, and Helena tells Demetrius, hoping to win his thanks and his love. Meanwhile, the mechanicals meet to cast their play, “Pyramus and Thisbe”. Bottom, a weaver, wants to play every part.

**Act 2.** In the woods, Oberon and Titania are quarrelling: both want a young changeling boy as their own. To punish Titania, Oberon sends Puck for a magic flower whose juice makes a sleeper fall in love with the first creature they see on waking. Seeing Demetrius scorn Helena, Oberon also tells Puck to put the juice on “the Athenian” so that Demetrius will love her. But Puck puts it on Lysander instead, who wakes, sees Helena and falls in love with her.

**Act 3.** Puck gives Bottom the head of an ass (a donkey). His friends run away in terror, and Titania, under the potion, wakes and falls in love with him. Puck and Oberon try to fix the lovers, but now *both* young men love Helena. Helena thinks they are mocking her, and Hermia is furious with her old friend. At last Puck leads the rivals apart in the dark until all four lovers fall asleep, and he puts a cure on Lysander’s eyes.

**Act 4.** Oberon gets the changeling boy and releases Titania, and Puck removes Bottom’s ass’s head. Theseus finds the lovers at dawn: Lysander loves Hermia again, and Demetrius stays in love with Helena. Theseus overrules Egeus, so both couples will marry.

**Act 5.** At the triple wedding, the mechanicals perform “Pyramus and Thisbe”, very badly. The fairies bless the house, and Puck speaks the epilogue.

:::mistake Two “Athenians”
Oberon asks Puck to charm the man in Athenian clothes. There are two Athenian men in the wood, so Puck charms the wrong one. Much of the comedy grows from this one mistake.
:::

## The characters

- **Hermia** is loyal and brave, and refuses to give up Lysander. She is small, and has a fiery temper when she believes Helena has stolen him.
- **Helena** loves Demetrius, who used to love her and now scorns her. She is insecure, so when both men suddenly love her she cannot believe they mean it.
- **Lysander** and **Demetrius** are rivals whose feelings swing wildly under the potion.
- **Oberon** is proud and controlling, but he does try to help Helena.
- **Puck** (also called Robin Goodfellow) is a mischievous fairy who serves Oberon and loves playing tricks.
- **Bottom** is confident and eager, wanting to play every part, and he stays cheerfully himself even with an ass’s head.

:::example Reading a motive
@step Helena knows Demetrius loves Hermia. Why does she tell him that Hermia is running away?

:::summary
- Four groups: the **court**, the **lovers**, the **fairies** and the **mechanicals**.
- Hermia and Lysander flee Athens; Puck’s potion mix-up makes both men love Helena.
- Oberon and Titania quarrel over a changeling boy; Titania falls in love with Bottom, who has an ass’s head.
- Order is restored at dawn, and the play ends with a triple wedding and the mechanicals’ play.
