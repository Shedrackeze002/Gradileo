*A Midsummer Night’s Dream* is a **comedy**, which in Shakespeare’s time meant more than “funny”. In this lesson you will learn the features of a Shakespearean comedy, see how the mechanicals’ **play within a play** mirrors the main story, and explain what some of the play’s most famous lines reveal.

## What makes a Shakespearean comedy

| Feature | In *A Midsummer Night’s Dream* |
|---|---|
| **Love and obstacles** | Hermia’s father and the law stand between her and Lysander |
| **Mistaken identity and confusion** | Puck charms the wrong Athenian, and both men love Helena |
| **An escape to a “green world”** | the magical wood outside Athens, where the rules of the city do not apply |
| **Order restored** | the potions are undone and Theseus overrules Egeus |
| **A happy ending with marriage** | three couples marry at the end |

A comedy, in short, moves from confusion to order and usually ends with one or more marriages. A tragedy moves the other way, towards death.

## The play within the play

:::definition Play within a play
A **play within a play** is a play that characters perform inside the main play. Here, the mechanicals perform “Pyramus and Thisbe” at the Duke’s wedding.
:::

“Pyramus and Thisbe” is meant to be a tragedy. Two young lovers, kept apart by their parents, talk through a crack in a wall and plan to meet at night. A lion frightens Thisbe away; Pyramus finds her bloody cloak, thinks she is dead and kills himself; Thisbe finds him and kills herself too.

But the mechanicals act it so badly that it becomes absurd. A man plays the Wall and holds up his fingers for the crack, another plays Moonshine with a lantern, and the Lion explains to the ladies that he is not a real lion. Bottom, as Pyramus, dies at great length.

:::example How the play mirrors the main plot
@step In “Pyramus and Thisbe”, young lovers are kept apart by their parents, run away at night, and die.
@step In the main plot, Hermia and Lysander are also kept apart by a parent and also run away at night.
@step Their story could have become a tragedy, but it ends happily instead.
@answer The mechanicals’ play shows the tragic version of the lovers’ story, and laughing at it reminds us how close the main plot came to the same fate.
:::

At the very end, Puck steps forward to speak the **epilogue**, a speech to the audience:

> If we shadows have offended,\
> Think but this, and all is mended,\
> That you have but slumber’d here\
> While these visions did appear.
>
> — William Shakespeare, *A Midsummer Night’s Dream*, Act 5, Scene 1

The actors (“shadows”) ask the audience to treat the play as a dream if they did not like it. The play ends by blurring the line between the dream on stage and the audience’s own night.

## Key lines

| Line | Speaker and scene | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| “The course of true love never did run smooth.” | Lysander, 1.1 | love always meets obstacles: the play’s first big idea |
| “Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind, / And therefore is wing’d Cupid painted blind.” | Helena, 1.1 | love is not based on what we see, so it can be blind and unfair |
| “Lord, what fools these mortals be!” | Puck, 3.2 | to the fairies, humans in love behave foolishly |
| “And though she be but little, she is fierce.” | Helena, 3.2 | Hermia is small but has a fiery temper |
| “The lunatic, the lover and the poet / Are of imagination all compact.” | Theseus, 5.1 | imagination shapes how people see the world, like the dream in the woods |

:::summary
- A Shakespearean **comedy** moves from confusion to order and ends with marriage.
- Its features include love and obstacles, mistaken identity, a “green world” escape and a happy ending.
- The mechanicals’ “Pyramus and Thisbe” is a **play within a play**: a tragic version of the lovers’ story, acted so badly it becomes funny.
- Puck’s **epilogue** asks the audience to treat the play as a dream.
:::
