A film director cannot write “she felt lonely”. Instead, they show it, through where the camera stands, how the scene is lit, what we hear and how the shots are cut together. In this lesson you will learn the language of film so that you can explain how a scene shapes what an audience feels.

## Camera shots and angles

| Shot or angle | What we see | Usual effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Close-up** | a face or object fills the screen | shows emotion or an important detail |
| **Long shot** | a whole figure in a wide setting | shows the setting, or how small a character is in it |
| **Establishing shot** | a wide shot at the start of a scene | shows where the scene takes place, such as a city skyline before a scene in an office |
| **Low angle** | the camera looks up at a subject | makes the subject seem powerful or threatening |
| **High angle** | the camera looks down at a subject | makes the subject seem small or vulnerable |

:::example Reading a shot
@step The camera is placed near a villain’s feet and looks up at him, so he towers over the frame.
@step Looking up at a character makes them dominate the screen, and makes us feel small beside them.
@answer The low angle makes the villain seem powerful and threatening.
:::

:::mistake Name it, then explain it
“This is a close-up” is only half an answer. Say what the shot shows and how it makes the audience feel: “The close-up of her trembling hands shows how frightened she is, even though she is smiling.”
:::

## Lighting, sound and editing

**Lighting** sets the mood. Bright, even lighting feels safe and cheerful. Dim lighting with deep shadows (called **low-key** lighting) creates mystery or danger, which is why it is used in thrillers and horror. Colour matters too: warm golden light suggests comfort, and cold blue light suggests sadness or distance.

**Sound** comes in two kinds.

:::definition Diegetic and non-diegetic sound
**Diegetic** sound comes from inside the story world, so the characters can hear it: a door slamming, a voice, a radio. **Non-diegetic** sound is added for the audience only, such as the soundtrack or a narrator’s voice.
:::

Tense music that grows louder as a character walks down a dark corridor is non-diegetic. It builds suspense, telling the audience that danger is near before anything happens.

**Editing** is how shots are cut together. Fast cuts between shots during a chase create speed, energy and tension. Long, slow shots feel calm or thoughtful. A **montage** is a series of short shots edited together to show time passing, such as weeks of training squeezed into a minute.

:::example Reading lighting
@step Early in a film, a family eats dinner in warm, golden light.
@step Later, the same house is shown empty, in cold blue light.
@answer The change of lighting suggests that the family’s happiness has been lost: the mood has changed for the worse.
:::

:::summary
- **Close-ups** show emotion; **long shots** and **establishing shots** show setting.
- **Low angles** make subjects powerful; **high angles** make them small or vulnerable.
- **Low-key lighting** creates mystery or danger; warm and cold colours suggest mood.
- **Diegetic** sound is heard by the characters; **non-diegetic** sound is added for the audience.
- Fast editing creates tension; a **montage** shows time passing.
:::
