The tide rises and falls twice a day, a Ferris wheel seat goes up and down with every turn, and the hours of daylight in a northern town swing from long summer days to short winter ones. Each of these repeats in regular cycles, and many of them can be modelled by a smooth wave called a sinusoidal function.

## Periodic behaviour

:::definition Periodic function
A function whose graph repeats a pattern at regular intervals. One repetition of the pattern is a **cycle**, and the horizontal length of one cycle is the **period**.
:::

A graph is periodic only if **every** cycle is exactly the same: the same length, the same highest points and the same lowest points. A swing that slowly stops is not periodic, because each swing is smaller than the one before.

:::definition Sinusoidal function
A periodic function whose graph is a smooth wave with the shape of the sine curve, such as $y = \sin x$ or $y = \cos x$ and their stretches and translations.
:::

:::example Is it periodic?
A Ferris wheel turns at a steady speed, making one rotation every 40 s. A child on a swing is pushed once and then left to slow down. Which height is periodic?
@step The Ferris wheel seat repeats exactly the same heights every 40 s, so its height is periodic with a period of 40 s.
@step The swing's highest point gets lower with each swing, so no cycle repeats exactly.
@answer The Ferris wheel height is periodic (period 40 s); the swing's height is not.
:::

:::definition Midline and amplitude
The **midline** is the horizontal line halfway between the maximum and minimum values: $y = \dfrac{\text{max} + \text{min}}{2}$. The **amplitude** is the vertical distance from the midline to a maximum: $\dfrac{\text{max} - \text{min}}{2}$.
:::

:::example Read the graph
For the graph above, find the maximum, the minimum, the midline, the amplitude and the period.
@step Maximum $7$ (at $x = 45^\circ$) and minimum $1$ (at $x = 135^\circ$).
@step Midline: $y = \dfrac{7 + 1}{2} = 4$. Amplitude: $\dfrac{7 - 1}{2} = 3$.
@step The maximums are at $45^\circ$ and $225^\circ$, so one cycle is $225^\circ - 45^\circ = 180^\circ$ long.
@answer Max $7$, min $1$, midline $y = 4$, amplitude $3$, period $180^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake Amplitude is half the height
The amplitude is **half** the distance from the minimum to the maximum. For the graph above, $7 - 1 = 6$ is the total height of the wave; the amplitude is $3$.
:::

## The equation y = a sin(bx + c) + d

Every sinusoidal function can be written as $y = a\sin(bx + c) + d$ or $y = a\cos(bx + c) + d$, with $b > 0$. You can read its features straight from the equation.

| Feature | From the equation |
|---|---|
| Amplitude | $\lvert a \rvert$ |
| Period | $\dfrac{360^\circ}{b}$ in degrees, or $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$ in radians |
| Midline | $y = d$ |
| Maximum and minimum | $d + \lvert a \rvert$ and $d - \lvert a \rvert$ |
| Range | $\{y \mid d - \lvert a \rvert \le y \le d + \lvert a \rvert,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$ |

The value of $c$ slides the graph left or right; it does not change any of these features. A negative $a$ flips the graph upside down, but the amplitude is still positive.

:::example Features from an equation
For $y = -4\cos(3x) + 1$, with $x$ in degrees, find the amplitude, period, midline, range and $y$-intercept.
@step Amplitude $|{-4}| = 4$; period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{3} = 120^\circ$; midline $y = 1$.
@step Maximum $1 + 4 = 5$ and minimum $1 - 4 = -3$, so the range is $\{y \mid -3 \le y \le 5,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
@step $y$-intercept: $y = -4\cos 0^\circ + 1 = -4(1) + 1 = -3$.
@answer Amplitude $4$, period $120^\circ$, midline $y = 1$, range $-3 \le y \le 5$, $y$-intercept $-3$.
:::

Angles can also be measured in **radians**, where $360^\circ = 2\pi$. So $180^\circ = \pi$, $90^\circ = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $270^\circ = \frac{3\pi}{2}$. With $x$ in radians, the period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$: for $y = 2\sin(4x) - 3$, the period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
The period of $y = \sin(3x)$ is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{3} = 120^\circ$, not $3 \times 360^\circ$. A larger $b$ squeezes more cycles into the same distance, so the period gets **shorter**.
:::

## Sinusoidal situations

In context, the maximum and minimum often come from the description, and the period is the time for one full cycle.

:::example High and low tide
At a dock in Saint John, high tide is at 4:00 a.m. with a depth of 7.2 m. The next low tide is 6.2 hours later, with a depth of 1.6 m. Find the amplitude, the midline, the period and the time of the next high tide.
@step Amplitude $= \dfrac{7.2 - 1.6}{2} = 2.8$ m and midline $y = \dfrac{7.2 + 1.6}{2} = 4.4$.
@step High tide to low tide is half a cycle, so the period is $2 \times 6.2 = 12.4$ hours.
@step 4:00 a.m. plus 12.4 h (12 h 24 min) is 4:24 p.m.
@answer Amplitude 2.8 m, midline $y = 4.4$, period 12.4 h; the next high tide is at 4:24 p.m.
:::

:::example A Ferris wheel
A Ferris wheel has a diameter of 50 m, riders board from a platform 2 m above the ground, and one rotation takes 60 s. Find the maximum height, the amplitude, the midline and the period.
@step The lowest height is 2 m, so the highest is $2 + 50 = 52$ m.
@step The amplitude is the radius, $25$ m, and the midline is the height of the centre: $y = 2 + 25 = 27$.
@answer Maximum 52 m, amplitude 25 m, midline $y = 27$, period 60 s.
:::

Draw the maximum, minimum and midline as horizontal lines, mark one period along the time axis, and then fit the wave inside that frame.
:::

:::summary
- A periodic function repeats identical cycles; the period is the length of one cycle.
- Midline $y = \dfrac{\text{max} + \text{min}}{2}$; amplitude $= \dfrac{\text{max} - \text{min}}{2}$.
- For $y = a\sin(bx + c) + d$: amplitude $|a|$, period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{b}$ (or $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$ in radians), midline $y = d$, range from $d - |a|$ to $d + |a|$.
- The value of $c$ shifts the graph sideways but does not change the amplitude, period or midline.
- In context, read the maximum and minimum from the description and the period from the time for one cycle.
:::
