At one high school, 42 students in a survey play hockey and 33 play soccer. That does not mean 75 of them play a sport, because some students play both. Venn diagrams and set operations let you count overlapping groups without counting anyone twice.

## Union and intersection

:::definition Union and intersection
- The **union** $A \cup B$ is the set of elements that are in $A$ **or** in $B$ (or in both).
- The **intersection** $A \cap B$ is the set of elements that are in **both** $A$ and $B$.
:::

## Counting with two sets

When you add $n(A)$ and $n(B)$, the elements in $A \cap B$ are counted twice, so subtract them once. This is the **principle of inclusion and exclusion**:

$$n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$$

:::example Hockey and soccer
In a survey of 68 students, 42 play hockey, 33 play soccer and 18 play both. How many play neither sport?
@step $n(H \cup S) = n(H) + n(S) - n(H \cap S) = 42 + 33 - 18 = 57$
@step Neither: $68 - 57 = 11$
@step Check with a Venn diagram: only hockey $42 - 18 = 24$, only soccer $33 - 18 = 15$, and $24 + 18 + 15 + 11 = 68$.
@answer 11 students play neither sport.
:::

## Three sets

With three sets, a Venn diagram has eight regions. Fill it in from the centre outward, because the count for "Biology and Chemistry" includes the students who take all three.

:::example Science courses
A survey of 100 Grade 12 students found that 37 take Biology, 30 take Chemistry and 30 take Physics. Of these, 12 take Biology and Chemistry, 9 take Biology and Physics, 10 take Chemistry and Physics, and 4 take all three. How many take none of the three courses?
@step Centre: 4 take all three.
@step Exactly two: Bio and Chem only $12 - 4 = 8$; Bio and Phys only $9 - 4 = 5$; Chem and Phys only $10 - 4 = 6$.
@step Only one: Bio $37 - 8 - 5 - 4 = 20$; Chem $30 - 8 - 6 - 4 = 12$; Phys $30 - 5 - 6 - 4 = 15$.
@step In at least one course: $20 + 12 + 15 + 8 + 5 + 6 + 4 = 70$
@answer None of the three: $100 - 70 = 30$ students.
