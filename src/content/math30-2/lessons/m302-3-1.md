Sports broadcasts quote the odds that a team will win, and weather forecasts give the chance of snow as a percent. Both describe how likely something is, but they compare different things. Knowing the difference helps you read claims carefully and make better decisions.

## Probability

:::definition Probability
When all outcomes are equally likely, the **probability** of an event $A$ is
$$P(A) = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}$$
A probability is between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain). It can be written as a fraction, a decimal or a percent.
:::

The **complement** of $A$, written $A'$, is "$A$ does not happen". Since one of the two must happen, $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$.

:::example Drawing a marble
A bag holds 4 red and 6 blue marbles. One marble is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is red, and the probability that it is not red.
@step Favourable outcomes: 4 red marbles. Total: $4 + 6 = 10$ marbles.
@step $P(\text{red}) = \frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}$
@answer $P(\text{red}) = \frac{2}{5}$ and $P(\text{not red}) = 1 - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$
:::

## Odds in favour and odds against

Odds compare favourable outcomes with **unfavourable** outcomes, not with all outcomes.

:::definition Odds
- The **odds in favour** of an event are favourable : unfavourable.
- The **odds against** an event are unfavourable : favourable.
Odds are written as a ratio such as $2 : 3$, usually in lowest terms.
:::

:::example Odds with marbles
For the bag of 4 red and 6 blue marbles, find the odds in favour of drawing red and the odds against it.
@step Favourable: 4 red. Unfavourable: 6 blue.
@step Odds in favour $= 4 : 6 = 2 : 3$ (divide both terms by 2).
@answer The odds in favour of red are $2 : 3$, and the odds against red are $3 : 2$.
:::

:::tip Odds against are odds in favour, reversed
If the odds in favour are $a : b$, the odds against are $b : a$.
:::

## Converting between odds and probability

If the odds in favour of an event are $a : b$, there are $a$ favourable outcomes for every $b$ unfavourable ones, so $a + b$ outcomes in all:
$$P(\text{event}) = \frac{a}{a + b}$$
Going the other way, a probability of $\frac{m}{n}$ means $m$ favourable and $n - m$ unfavourable outcomes, so the odds in favour are $m : (n - m)$.

:::example From odds to probability
The odds against the Riverton Hawks winning their next game are $5 : 3$. What is the probability that they win?
@step Odds against $5 : 3$: 5 unfavourable outcomes for every 3 favourable ones.
@step $P(\text{win}) = \frac{3}{5 + 3} = \frac{3}{8}$
@answer $P(\text{win}) = \frac{3}{8} = 0.375$, or 37.5%.
:::

:::example From probability to odds
The probability of snow tomorrow in Pinewood is 35%. What are the odds in favour of snow?
@step 35% means 35 favourable outcomes out of 100, so 65 are unfavourable.
@answer Odds in favour $= 35 : 65 = 7 : 13$
:::

:::mistake "1 in 5" is not $1 : 5$
"A 1 in 5 chance" is a probability: $\frac{1}{5}$. There is 1 favourable outcome and 4 unfavourable ones, so the odds in favour are $1 : 4$, not $1 : 5$.
:::

## Solving problems and assessing claims

Odds split a whole into parts. Use them to find how many items are favourable.

:::example Finding a count
A jar holds 60 marbles. The odds against drawing a green marble are $7 : 3$. How many green marbles are in the jar?
@step Odds against $7 : 3$ means $P(\text{green}) = \frac{3}{7 + 3} = \frac{3}{10}$.
@step Green marbles: $60 \times \frac{3}{10} = 18$
@answer There are 18 green marbles.
:::

Probabilities and odds can come from theory (counting equally likely outcomes), from experiments or past results, or from someone's judgment. A volleyball team that won 34 of its 56 games last season might estimate the odds in favour of winning its next game as $34 : 22 = 17 : 11$. That is an **experimental** estimate, not a guarantee.

:::mistake The gambler's fallacy
A fair coin that has landed heads five times in a row is not "due" for tails. Each toss is independent, so the odds in favour of tails are still $1 : 1$.
:::

:::summary
- $P(A) = \dfrac{\text{favourable}}{\text{total}}$ and $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$.
- Odds in favour = favourable : unfavourable. Odds against = unfavourable : favourable.
- Odds in favour of $a : b$ give $P = \dfrac{a}{a + b}$.
- "1 in $n$" is a probability; the odds in favour are $1 : (n - 1)$.
- Past results give experimental estimates, and earlier independent trials do not change the next one.
:::
