Ghana's gold, bauxite and manganese have been mined for generations, and limestone is quarried across West Africa to make cement. Turning rock into metal or into cement takes a chain of steps, each one chosen to separate what is valuable from what is not, and each one with costs for the land, the water and the people nearby.

In this lesson you will describe how ores are mined and processed, the environmental effects of mining, and how cement is made, how it sets and its effects.

## Ores and mining

:::definition Ore
An **ore** is a rock that contains enough of a metal or its compound to be worth extracting.
:::

Unreactive metals such as gold occur **native**, as the metal; reactive metals such as aluminium and manganese occur combined, as oxides. Ores near the surface are dug by **open-pit (surface) mining**, which is cheap but scars large areas; deeper ores are reached by **underground mining** through shafts and tunnels.

After mining, the ore is crushed and **concentrated**, by washing or froth flotation, to remove waste rock (gangue), so that less energy and reactant are needed later.

| Ore | Main mineral | Processing |
|---|---|---|
| Gold ore | native gold | crushed, then cyanide leaching |
| Bauxite | aluminium oxide | purified to alumina, then electrolysis |
| Haematite | iron(III) oxide | reduced in the blast furnace |
| Manganese ore | manganese(IV) oxide | used for steels and dry cells |

In **cyanide leaching**, cyanide solution dissolves gold from the crushed ore as the complex ion $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$, and zinc or activated carbon then recovers the gold. Manganese makes hard, tough steels for rails and rock crushers.

## Mining and the environment

Mining clears forest and farmland, and its waste and chemicals can pollute rivers. Illegal small-scale gold mining, known in Ghana as **galamsey**, often uses mercury to collect gold as an amalgam and then burns the mercury off, releasing poisonous vapour, while digging silts the rivers. **Land reclamation** fills in the pits, replaces the topsoil and replants the land.

## Making cement

1. **Limestone** and **clay** (or shale) are crushed and mixed.
2. The mixture is heated in a **rotary kiln** at about 1400 °C. Limestone decomposes, $\ce{CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2}$, and the calcium oxide reacts with the silica and alumina of the clay to form calcium silicates and aluminates, called **clinker**.
3. The clinker is ground with a little **gypsum**, which slows the setting.

Cement **sets** by hydration: it reacts with water to form interlocking crystals of hydrated calcium silicates, so it sets even under water. **Mortar** is cement, sand and water; **concrete** is cement, sand, gravel and water.

A cement works releases dust and a great deal of carbon dioxide, both from the limestone and from the fuel.

:::example Carbon dioxide from limestone
What mass of carbon dioxide is released when 10 tonnes of limestone is fully decomposed? ($\ce{CaCO3}$ = 100, $\ce{CO2}$ = 44.)
@step 100 tonnes of calcium carbonate gives 44 tonnes of carbon dioxide.
@answer 10 × 44 ÷ 100 = 4.40 tonnes
:::

:::summary
- An ore is worth extracting; gold occurs native, aluminium and manganese as oxides.
- Ores are mined in open pits or underground, crushed and concentrated.
- Gold is dissolved by cyanide as [Au(CN)₂]⁻; galamsey's mercury and silt harm rivers and land.
- Cement: limestone and clay heated in a rotary kiln to clinker, ground with gypsum; it sets by hydration.
:::
