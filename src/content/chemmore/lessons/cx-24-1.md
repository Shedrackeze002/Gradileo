The sign of $\Delta G^\circ$ tells you whether a reaction is thermodynamically favoured. The equilibrium constant tells you how far it goes. The two must be connected, because a reaction that is strongly favoured should end with mostly products.

In this lesson you will link $\Delta G^\circ$ to $K$, and look at reactions that are favoured but slow, reactions driven by coupling, and the free energy of dissolving.

## Free energy and the equilibrium constant

:::definition Free energy and K
$$\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K$$
where $R = 8.314$ J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and $T$ is in kelvin. Remember to change $\Delta G^\circ$ into joules.
:::

So a negative $\Delta G^\circ$ gives $\ln K > 0$ and $K > 1$, a positive $\Delta G^\circ$ gives $K < 1$, and $\Delta G^\circ = 0$ gives $K = 1$. Because $K$ depends exponentially on $\Delta G^\circ$, a change of only a few kJ/mol changes $K$ by a large factor.

:::example Finding K from ΔG°
A reaction has $\Delta G^\circ = -10.0$ kJ/mol at 298 K. What is $K$?
@step $\ln K = -\dfrac{\Delta G^\circ}{RT} = -\dfrac{-10\,000}{8.314 \times 298} = 4.04$.
@answer $K = e^{4.04} = 57$.
:::

## Kinetic control

A thermodynamically favoured reaction can still be too slow to notice. Diamond turning into graphite has a negative $\Delta G^\circ$, but its activation energy is so high that diamonds last for millions of years. Such a reaction is under **kinetic control**: it is favoured, but its rate is too slow to measure. A mixture that is not changing is not necessarily at equilibrium.

## Coupled reactions

A reaction that is not favoured can be driven by joining it to a favoured one that shares a substance with it. The free energy changes add up, so if the total is negative, the coupled process is favoured. Living cells use the breakdown of ATP in this way to build molecules such as proteins.

## The free energy of dissolution

Dissolving a solid has three steps. Separating the particles of the solid and making room among the solvent molecules both take in energy, while the attraction between the particles and the solvent gives energy out. Whether a substance dissolves depends on the balance of these enthalpy changes and on the change in entropy, so some salts dissolve even though dissolving them takes in heat.

:::summary
- $\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K$: negative $\Delta G^\circ$ means $K > 1$, positive means $K < 1$.
- A favoured reaction with a high activation energy can be too slow to see: it is under kinetic control.
- Coupled reactions add their free energy changes, so a favoured reaction can drive one that is not.
- Dissolving depends on the balance of three enthalpy changes and on the entropy change.
:::
