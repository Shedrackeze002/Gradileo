Yam, cassava, rice and bread are mostly starch, the sugar in a cup of tea is sucrose, and the sweetness of ripe fruit comes from glucose and fructose. All of these are carbohydrates. A few simple tests, done in any school laboratory, show which food molecules a sample contains.

In this lesson you will classify carbohydrates, describe and calculate their hydrolysis, and identify starch, reducing sugars, proteins and fats by their tests.

## Classifying carbohydrates

:::definition Carbohydrate
A **carbohydrate** is a compound of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen in which hydrogen and oxygen are in the ratio 2 : 1, as in water. Carbohydrates are classed by how many sugar units they contain.
:::

| Class | Formula | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharide | $\ce{C6H12O6}$ | glucose, fructose, galactose |
| Disaccharide | $\ce{C12H22O11}$ | sucrose, maltose, lactose |
| Polysaccharide | $\ce{(C6H10O5)_n}$ | starch, cellulose, glycogen |

Two monosaccharides join with the loss of water to form a disaccharide; many join to form a polysaccharide.

## Hydrolysis

**Hydrolysis**, by dilute acid or by enzymes, splits the links again:

- sucrose → glucose + fructose: $\ce{C12H22O11 + H2O -> C6H12O6 + C6H12O6}$
- maltose → two glucose
- lactose → glucose + galactose
- starch → maltose → glucose

:::example Glucose from sucrose
What mass of glucose (M = 180) forms when 68.4 g of sucrose (M = 342) is completely hydrolysed?
@step Sucrose = 68.4 ÷ 342 = 0.200 mol, which gives 0.200 mol of glucose.
@answer 0.200 × 180 = 36.0 g
:::

## Tests for food molecules

| Food molecule | Test | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| Starch | add iodine solution | blue-black |
| Reducing sugar | warm with Benedict's or Fehling's solution | brick-red precipitate |
| Protein | sodium hydroxide, then a few drops of copper(II) sulfate (Biuret test) | violet |
| Protein | heat with Millon's reagent | brick-red |
| Fat or oil | shake with ethanol, then add water | cloudy white emulsion |
| Fat or oil | rub on paper | translucent spot that does not dry |

All monosaccharides and most disaccharides are **reducing sugars**. **Sucrose** is not: it gives no change with Benedict's solution until it has been boiled with dilute hydrochloric acid, neutralised with sodium hydrogencarbonate, and then tested, because the hydrolysis gives glucose and fructose. The solution must be neutralised first, because Benedict's solution only works in alkaline conditions.

:::summary
- Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose) join into disaccharides (sucrose, maltose) and polysaccharides (starch).
- Hydrolysis: sucrose gives glucose and fructose; maltose gives two glucose; starch gives glucose.
- Iodine: starch. Benedict's: reducing sugar. Biuret: protein. Ethanol emulsion: fat.
- Sucrose is non-reducing; hydrolyse and neutralise it before the Benedict's test.
:::
