Your blood stays within a narrow band around pH 7.4, even as your muscles pour acid into it during exercise. It can do this because it is a buffer. The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation lets you work out a buffer's pH in one line, and see how much acid or base it can take.

In this lesson you will use the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation, and compare the capacities of buffers.

## The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

A buffer is a mixture of a weak acid HA and its conjugate base A⁻. Taking logs of the $K_a$ expression and rearranging gives an equation for its pH.

:::definition The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation
$$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$$
:::

So when the acid and its conjugate base are present in equal amounts, the ratio is 1, its log is 0, and the pH equals the pKa. More conjugate base raises the pH, and more acid lowers it.

:::example A buffer's pH
A buffer is 0.20 mol/L ethanoic acid and 0.30 mol/L sodium ethanoate. The pKa of ethanoic acid is 4.76.
@step $\text{pH} = 4.76 + \log\dfrac{0.30}{0.20}$.
@answer $\text{pH} = 4.76 + 0.18 = 4.94$.
:::

## Adding acid or base to a buffer

Added strong base reacts with HA, and added strong acid reacts with A⁻. Work in moles: change the amounts of HA and A⁻ by the moles added, then put the new amounts into the equation. Both are in the same volume, so the ratio of moles is the same as the ratio of concentrations.

:::check cx-ap-henderson 3
:::

## Buffer capacity

:::definition Buffer capacity
The amount of strong acid or base a buffer can neutralise before its pH changes a lot.
:::

A buffer with **more** of both components has a greater capacity, though its pH may be the same. A buffer with more A⁻ than HA can take more added acid than added base, and the reverse is true for a buffer with more HA.

:::summary
- $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\dfrac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$, and pH = pKa when the two are equal.
- Added base turns HA into A⁻ and added acid turns A⁻ into HA; work in moles, then use the ratio.
- More of both components means a greater capacity, and a buffer resists best the addition that uses up the component it has more of.
:::
