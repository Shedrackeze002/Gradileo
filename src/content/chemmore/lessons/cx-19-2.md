Petrol straight from the distillation of crude oil would make a car engine knock and lose power. Refineries change the shape of the molecules in petrol to make it burn smoothly, and they turn the leftovers into the raw materials for plastics, solvents and medicines.

In this lesson you will explain knocking and the octane number, and describe how reforming, isomerisation, cracking and additives improve petrol, and the petrochemicals made from crude oil.

## Knocking and the octane number

:::definition Octane number
The **octane number** of a petrol is the percentage of 2,2,4-trimethylpentane in a mixture with heptane that knocks in the same way in a test engine. 2,2,4-Trimethylpentane is given 100 and heptane 0.
:::

**Knocking** is the early, uneven explosion of the fuel–air mixture in an engine cylinder, before the spark. It makes a metallic noise, wastes energy and damages the engine. A petrol that knocks like a mixture of 95% 2,2,4-trimethylpentane and 5% heptane has an octane number of 95.

Highly branched, cyclic and aromatic hydrocarbons burn more smoothly than straight chains, so they have higher octane numbers.

## Improving petrol

- **Reforming** turns straight-chain alkanes into branched, cyclic and aromatic compounds over a catalyst; for example $\ce{C6H14 -> C6H6 + 4H2}$. The hydrogen is a useful by-product for making ammonia or removing sulfur from fuels.
- **Isomerisation** rearranges a straight chain into a branched one with the same formula, such as pentane into 2-methylbutane.
- **Cracking** breaks large alkanes into smaller ones suitable for petrol, and alkenes: $\ce{C10H22 -> C8H18 + C2H4}$.
- **Additives** raise the octane number. Tetraethyl lead was used in the past, but the lead it released is poisonous, so it has been phased out in most countries. Oxygen-containing compounds such as ethanol and MTBE are used instead.

## Petrochemicals

**Petrochemicals** are chemicals made from crude oil or natural gas. Ethene from cracking is made into poly(ethene), ethanol and ethane-1,2-diol; propene into poly(propene); and other fractions into detergents, solvents and drugs.

:::summary
- Knocking is early, uneven burning in the engine; the octane number measures resistance to it.
- Branched, cyclic and aromatic hydrocarbons have higher octane numbers than straight chains.
- Reforming, isomerisation and cracking improve petrol; lead additives were phased out because lead is poisonous.
- Petrochemicals such as ethene are the raw materials for plastics and many other products.
:::
