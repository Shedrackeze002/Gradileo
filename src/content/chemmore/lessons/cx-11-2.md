Copper sulfate solution is blue, add ammonia and it turns a deep royal blue, and add hydrochloric acid instead and it turns yellow-green. Each colour belongs to a different **complex ion**, and transition metals make far more of these than any other part of the periodic table.

In this lesson you will describe complex ions and their shapes, explain why they are coloured, and see why transition metals make good catalysts.

## Complex ions and ligands

:::definition Ligand
A molecule or ion that donates a lone pair of electrons to a central metal ion, forming a **coordinate** (dative) bond. A **complex** is a central metal ion surrounded by ligands.
:::

:::definition Coordination number
The number of coordinate bonds to the central metal ion.
:::

Ligands can be:

- **monodentate**, with one lone pair used: $\ce{H2O}$, $\ce{NH3}$, $\ce{Cl-}$;
- **bidentate**, with two: ethane-1,2-diamine $\ce{H2NCH2CH2NH2}$ (en), ethanedioate $\ce{C2O4^2-}$;
- **multidentate**, such as $\ce{EDTA^4-}$, which forms six coordinate bonds.

Haem is a multidentate ligand round an iron(II) ion. Oxygen binds to the iron as a ligand, so blood can carry it, but carbon monoxide binds more strongly and stops it.

## Shapes and oxidation states

| Coordination number | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | octahedral | $\ce{[Cu(H2O)6]^2+}$ |
| 4 | tetrahedral | $\ce{[CuCl4]^2-}$ (Cl⁻ is large) |
| 4 | square planar | cisplatin, $\ce{Pt(NH3)2Cl2}$ |
| 2 | linear | $\ce{[Ag(NH3)2]+}$ (Tollens’ reagent) |

To find the oxidation state of the metal, subtract the charges of the ligands from the overall charge.

:::example The oxidation state in a complex
What is the oxidation state of cobalt in $\ce{[CoCl4]^2-}$?
@step The four chloride ligands contribute $4 \times (-1) = -4$.
@step Overall charge $-2 = x + (-4)$, so $x = +2$.
@answer Cobalt is +2, and the complex is tetrahedral.
:::

## Why complexes are coloured

The ligands split the metal’s d orbitals into two energy levels. A d electron can absorb visible light and jump up the gap $\Delta E$:

$$\Delta E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

The colour we see is the light that is **not** absorbed. Changing the **ligand**, the **coordination number** or the **oxidation state** changes $\Delta E$, so it changes the colour. $\ce{Zn^2+}$ and $\ce{Sc^3+}$ complexes are colourless because they have a full or empty 3d sub-shell, so no electron can move up.

In **colorimetry**, absorbance is proportional to concentration, so a calibration graph made from known solutions gives the concentration of an unknown one.

## Variable oxidation states: vanadium

Zinc in acid reduces vanadium(V) one step at a time, through four colours:

| Ion | $\ce{VO2+}$ | $\ce{VO^2+}$ | $\ce{V^3+}$ | $\ce{V^2+}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxidation state | +5 | +4 | +3 | +2 |
| Colour | yellow | blue | green | violet |

## Transition metals as catalysts

- **Heterogeneous** catalysts are in a different phase from the reactants: iron in the Haber process, vanadium(V) oxide in the Contact process. Reactants adsorb onto the surface. They can be **poisoned** by impurities that coat the surface, such as lead in a catalytic converter.
- **Homogeneous** catalysts are in the same phase. $\ce{Fe^2+}$ speeds up $\ce{S2O8^2- + 2I- -> 2SO4^2- + I2}$ because it can be oxidised to $\ce{Fe^3+}$ and reduced back, so neither step involves two negative ions repelling each other.
- In **autocatalysis**, a product catalyses the reaction: $\ce{Mn^2+}$ formed when manganate(VII) oxidises ethanedioate ions.

:::mistake The colour you see is not the colour absorbed
A blue complex looks blue because it absorbs orange-red light and lets blue through. Saying it “absorbs blue light” has it backwards.
:::

:::summary
- Ligands donate lone pairs to a metal ion; they can be mono-, bi- or multidentate.
- Coordination number 6 is usually octahedral; 4 is tetrahedral (or square planar for Pt); 2 is linear.
- Colour comes from d electrons absorbing light, $\Delta E = h\nu$; it changes with ligand, coordination number and oxidation state.
- Vanadium: yellow (+5), blue (+4), green (+3), violet (+2).
- Transition metals catalyse because they have variable oxidation states and surfaces that adsorb reactants.
:::
