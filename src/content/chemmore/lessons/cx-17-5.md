Carbon is the element of coal and charcoal, of the coke that runs blast furnaces, and of the two oxides that every chemistry student learns to tell apart. Coal itself formed from plants buried for millions of years, and heating it without air turns it into a whole set of useful products.

In this lesson you will describe the types of coal and the products of destructive distillation, the fuel gases made from coke, and the properties of carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide and the carbonates.

## Coal and coke

:::definition Destructive distillation
**Destructive distillation** is heating a substance strongly without air, so that it breaks down into new products.
:::

The older and more compressed a coal is, the more carbon it contains:

| Type | Carbon content |
|---|---|
| Peat | about 50 to 60% |
| Lignite (brown coal) | about 60 to 75% |
| Bituminous coal | about 75 to 90% |
| Anthracite | about 92 to 98% |

Destructive distillation of coal gives **coke** (almost pure carbon, used in the blast furnace), **coal tar** (benzene, dyes, drugs, road tar), **ammoniacal liquor** (made into ammonium sulfate fertiliser) and **coal gas** (a fuel).

## Fuel gases from coke

**Water gas** is made by passing steam over white-hot coke: $\ce{C + H2O -> CO + H2}$. Both of its gases burn, so it is a good fuel, and as **synthesis gas** it is used to make methanol and to supply hydrogen. **Producer gas** is made by passing air over red-hot coke: $\ce{2C + O2 -> 2CO}$, with the nitrogen of the air left in it, so it is a poorer fuel.

## The oxides of carbon

| | Carbon dioxide | Carbon monoxide |
|---|---|---|
| Laboratory preparation | marble chips and dilute HCl | dehydrating methanoic acid with conc. H₂SO₄ |
| Burning | does not burn or support burning | burns with a blue flame |
| Acid or base | acidic | neutral |
| Effect on the body | not poisonous in small amounts | poisonous: binds to haemoglobin |
| Use | fire extinguishers, fizzy drinks | reducing agent in the blast furnace |

Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky; with more carbon dioxide, the milkiness clears as soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate forms. Carbon monoxide reduces metal oxides: $\ce{Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2}$.

## Carbonates

Most carbonates are insoluble; those of sodium, potassium and ammonium dissolve. All carbonates give carbon dioxide with dilute acid. On heating, sodium and potassium carbonates do not decompose, but most others give the oxide and carbon dioxide: green copper(II) carbonate turns black, and white zinc carbonate gives zinc oxide, which is yellow when hot.

:::example Carbon dioxide from heating a carbonate
What volume of carbon dioxide, at room temperature and pressure, forms when 10 g of calcium carbonate (M = 100) decomposes completely? One mole of gas occupies 24 dm³.
@step Amount = 10 ÷ 100 = 0.10 mol, which gives 0.10 mol of carbon dioxide.
@answer 0.10 × 24 = 2.4 dm³
:::

:::summary
- Coal ranges from peat to anthracite as its carbon content rises.
- Destructive distillation of coal gives coke, coal tar, ammoniacal liquor and coal gas.
- Water gas (CO + H₂) is made with steam; producer gas (CO + N₂) with air.
- CO₂ is acidic and turns limewater milky; CO is neutral, poisonous and a reducing agent.
- Most carbonates give the oxide and CO₂ on heating; Na and K carbonates do not.
:::
