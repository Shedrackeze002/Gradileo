A pharmaceutical chemist rarely finds the molecule they want ready-made. They plan a route: a short chain of reactions, each changing one functional group, from something cheap to something valuable.

In this lesson you will choose the reagent for each step and plan routes of two or three steps.

## The reactions you know

| From | To | Reagent and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| alkene | alcohol | steam, phosphoric acid catalyst |
| alkene | dibromoalkane | bromine |
| alkane | halogenoalkane | halogen, UV light |
| halogenoalkane | alcohol | NaOH(aq), warm |
| halogenoalkane | nitrile (one C longer) | KCN in ethanol, warm |
| halogenoalkane | amine | excess $\ce{NH3}$ in ethanol, heat |
| halogenoalkane | alkene | KOH in ethanol, hot |
| primary alcohol | aldehyde | acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, distil |
| primary alcohol | carboxylic acid | acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, reflux |
| secondary alcohol | ketone | acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, reflux |
| aldehyde or ketone | alcohol | $\ce{NaBH4}$ |
| aldehyde or ketone | hydroxynitrile | KCN, then dilute acid |
| nitrile | primary amine | $\ce{H2}$ with Ni, or $\ce{LiAlH4}$ |
| nitrile | carboxylic acid | reflux with dilute HCl |
| carboxylic acid + alcohol | ester | conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$ catalyst |
| acyl chloride | ester or amide | alcohol, or ammonia/amine |
| benzene | nitrobenzene | conc. $\ce{HNO3}$ + conc. $\ce{H2SO4}$, 50 °C |
| nitrobenzene | phenylamine | Sn + conc. HCl, then NaOH |

## Why fewer steps is better

Each step loses some product. Multiply the yields to find the overall yield: three steps of 80%, 75% and 90% give $0.80 \times 0.75 \times 0.90 = 0.54$, only 54%. Greener routes also use reactions with a **high atom economy** and avoid hazardous solvents.

## Planning a route

Work **backwards** from the target. Ask which functional group it has, which reaction makes that group, and what the starting compound for that reaction would be. Keep an eye on the number of carbons: only the cyanide reactions add one.

:::example Making propanoic acid from bromoethane
Plan a route from $\ce{CH3CH2Br}$ (2 carbons) to $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$ (3 carbons).
@step A carbon must be added, so use cyanide: KCN in ethanol gives propanenitrile, $\ce{CH3CH2CN}$.
@step Hydrolysing the nitrile, by heating it under reflux with dilute acid, turns the CN group into COOH.
@answer Step 1: KCN in ethanol. Step 2: reflux with dilute HCl, giving $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$.
:::

:::example Making propanone from propene
Plan a two-step route from $\ce{CH3CH=CH2}$ to $\ce{CH3COCH3}$.
@step Propanone is a ketone, made by oxidising a secondary alcohol: propan-2-ol.
@step Propan-2-ol is the major product when steam adds to propene.
@answer Step 1: steam with a phosphoric acid catalyst. Step 2: reflux with acidified potassium dichromate.
:::

:::mistake Distil or reflux?
Oxidising a primary alcohol and **distilling** removes the aldehyde as soon as it forms. **Refluxing** keeps it in the flask, so it is oxidised on to the carboxylic acid.
:::

:::summary
- Each step changes one functional group; learn the reagent and conditions for each.
- Overall yield = the product of the step yields, so short routes are better.
- Plan backwards from the target, and count carbons: cyanide adds one.
- Distil to stop at the aldehyde; reflux to reach the carboxylic acid.
:::
