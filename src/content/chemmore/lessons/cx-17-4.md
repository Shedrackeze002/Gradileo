Sulfur is a yellow solid found near volcanoes, and its most important compound, sulfuric acid, is made in larger amounts than any other chemical. On the way from sulfur to sulfuric acid comes sulfur dioxide, a choking gas that preserves food but also causes acid rain.

In this lesson you will describe the allotropes of sulfur, the preparation, properties and uses of sulfur dioxide, the Contact process, and the ways sulfuric acid acts.

## Allotropes of sulfur

:::definition Allotropes
**Allotropes** are different forms of the same element in the same physical state.
:::

**Rhombic (α) sulfur** is stable below the transition temperature of 96 °C, and **monoclinic (β) sulfur** above it. Pouring boiling sulfur into cold water gives **plastic sulfur**, a soft, rubbery solid made of long tangled chains, which slowly turns back to rhombic sulfur.

## Sulfur dioxide

Sulfur dioxide is made in the laboratory by warming sodium sulfite with dilute hydrochloric acid, or by heating copper with concentrated sulfuric acid:

$$\ce{Na2SO3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + H2O + SO2}$$

It is a colourless, choking gas, denser than air and soluble in water, giving sulfurous acid (trioxosulphate(IV) acid): $\ce{SO2 + H2O <=> H2SO3}$. It is a **reducing agent**: it turns orange acidified potassium dichromate(VI) green, which is its test. It bleaches coloured flowers by reduction, and the colour slowly returns as air re-oxidises the dye. It is used to preserve fruit juices, to bleach wood pulp and to make sulfuric acid. From burning fuels it forms acid rain.

## The Contact process

1. Sulfur is burned in air: $\ce{S + O2 -> SO2}$.
2. Sulfur dioxide and air pass over **vanadium(V) oxide** at about 450 °C and 1 to 2 atmospheres: $\ce{2SO2 + O2 <=> 2SO3}$.
3. Sulfur trioxide is absorbed in concentrated sulfuric acid to give oleum, $\ce{H2S2O7}$, which is diluted with water to give sulfuric acid (tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid).

Sulfur trioxide is not absorbed in water directly, because it forms a fine acid mist that is hard to collect. A low pressure is used because the yield is already very high, so expensive high-pressure plant is not worth it.

## How sulfuric acid acts

| Role | Example |
|---|---|
| As an acid (dilute) | $\ce{Zn + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2}$ |
| As an oxidising agent (hot, concentrated) | $\ce{Cu + 2H2SO4 -> CuSO4 + SO2 + 2H2O}$ |
| As a dehydrating agent (concentrated) | turns sugar into black carbon; turns blue $\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}$ white |
| As a drying agent (concentrated) | dries chlorine and hydrogen chloride |

A **dehydrating agent** removes the elements of water from a compound; a **drying agent** removes water that is only mixed with a substance.

:::example Sulfuric acid from sulfur
What mass of sulfuric acid can be made from 16 kg of sulfur, if each sulfur atom ends up in one molecule of $\ce{H2SO4}$? (S = 32, H₂SO₄ = 98.)
@step 32 kg of sulfur gives 98 kg of sulfuric acid.
@answer 16 × 98 ÷ 32 = 49.0 kg
:::

:::summary
- Rhombic sulfur is stable below 96 °C, monoclinic above; plastic sulfur forms in cold water.
- SO₂: sulfite and dilute acid; choking, acidic, a reducing agent that turns dichromate green.
- Contact process: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ over V₂O₅ at about 450 °C, absorbed in conc. H₂SO₄ as oleum.
- Sulfuric acid acts as an acid, an oxidising agent, a dehydrating agent and a drying agent.
:::
