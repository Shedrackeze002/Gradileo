Microwaves warm your food, infrared lamps warm your skin, and ultraviolet light can burn it. All three are the same kind of radiation. What makes them behave so differently is the energy carried by each photon.

In this lesson you will calculate the energy of photons, and link each region of the electromagnetic spectrum to the change it causes in a molecule.

## Photons and their energy

Light travels as a wave, and its speed $c$ is the product of its wavelength $\lambda$ and frequency $\nu$. It also comes in packets of energy called **photons**.

:::definition The energy of a photon
$$c = \lambda\nu \qquad E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
where $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}$ J s is Planck's constant and $c = 2.998 \times 10^{8}$ m/s is the speed of light.
:::

So a shorter wavelength means a higher frequency and more energy in each photon. Remember to change the wavelength into metres before you use the formula.

:::example A photon of green light
What is the energy of a photon with a wavelength of 520 nm?
@step $\lambda = 520 \times 10^{-9}$ m.
@step $E = \dfrac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34})(2.998 \times 10^{8})}{520 \times 10^{-9}}$.
@answer $E = 3.82 \times 10^{-19}$ J.
:::

## What each region of the spectrum does

A molecule absorbs a photon only when the photon's energy matches the gap between two of the molecule's energy levels. Different regions of the spectrum match different kinds of gaps.

| Region | Wavelength (roughly) | What the photon does |
|---|---|---|
| Microwave | 1 mm to 30 cm | makes the molecule rotate faster |
| Infrared | 700 nm to 1 mm | makes the bonds vibrate more strongly |
| Visible and ultraviolet | 10 nm to 700 nm | moves an electron to a higher energy level |

This is why infrared spectra tell chemists which kinds of bonds a molecule has, while ultraviolet and visible light give substances their colour and can break bonds.

- $c = \lambda\nu$ and $E = h\nu = hc/\lambda$: shorter wavelengths carry more energy per photon.
- Divide a molar energy by $N_A$ to get the energy for one bond or one molecule.
- Microwaves change rotation, infrared changes vibration, and visible and ultraviolet light move electrons between energy levels.
