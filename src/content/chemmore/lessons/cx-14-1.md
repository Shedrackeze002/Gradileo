Open a bottle of perfume at the front of a classroom and, a little later, people at the back can smell it. The perfume molecules have spread through the air by diffusion. Light gases spread faster than heavy ones, and Thomas Graham found exactly how much faster. When gases are mixed instead, John Dalton showed how each one shares the pressure.

In this lesson you will use Graham's law of diffusion and Dalton's law of partial pressures, including the correction for a gas collected over water.

## Graham's law of diffusion

:::definition Graham's law
**Graham's law** says that, at the same temperature and pressure, the rate of diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass (or its density).
:::

$$\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}}$$

where $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the rates of diffusion of the two gases and $M_1$ and $M_2$ their molar masses. The time taken for the same volume to diffuse is the other way round: it is proportional to $\sqrt{M}$, so a heavier gas takes longer.

:::example How much faster hydrogen diffuses
How many times faster does hydrogen ($M$ = 2) diffuse than oxygen ($M$ = 32)?
@step $\dfrac{r_{\text{H}_2}}{r_{\text{O}_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{32}{2}} = \sqrt{16}$
@answer 4 times faster
:::

When ammonia and hydrogen chloride are let into opposite ends of a long tube, the white ring of ammonium chloride forms nearer the hydrogen chloride end, because the lighter ammonia molecules travel further in the same time.

To find an unknown molar mass, compare the time it takes with the time for a known gas: $\dfrac{t_X}{t_{\text{O}_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{M_X}{32}}$, so $M_X = 32 \times \left(\dfrac{t_X}{t_{\text{O}_2}}\right)^2$.

## Dalton's law of partial pressures

**Dalton's law** says that the total pressure of a mixture of gases that do not react is the sum of the **partial pressures** of the gases. The partial pressure of each gas is the pressure it would exert if it filled the container alone, and it equals its **mole fraction** times the total pressure:

$$p_A = \frac{n_A}{n_{\text{total}}} \times P_{\text{total}}$$

## Gases collected over water

A gas collected over water is mixed with water vapour, so the pressure in the jar is the pressure of the gas plus the **saturated vapour pressure** of water at that temperature. To find the pressure of the dry gas, subtract:

$$p_{\text{dry gas}} = P_{\text{atmosphere}} - p_{\text{water vapour}}$$

The volume of dry gas at another pressure then follows from Boyle's law, $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$.

:::mistake Subtract the water vapour first
Use the pressure of the dry gas, not the atmospheric pressure, in any calculation about the gas itself.
:::

:::summary
- Rate of diffusion is proportional to $1/\sqrt{M}$; time to diffuse is proportional to $\sqrt{M}$.
- Lighter gases diffuse faster, so the ammonium chloride ring forms nearer the hydrogen chloride end.
- Total pressure is the sum of the partial pressures; each partial pressure is mole fraction × total pressure.
- For a gas collected over water, subtract the vapour pressure of water to find the pressure of the dry gas.
:::
