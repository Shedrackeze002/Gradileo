Without its blanket of gases the Earth would be a frozen ball, with an average temperature far below zero. A small amount of carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour in the air keeps the planet warm enough for life. The trouble starts when human activity makes the blanket thicker.

In this lesson you will explain how greenhouse gases warm the Earth and what a carbon footprint is, calculate one, and say how the pollutants from burning fuels form and the harm they do.

## The greenhouse effect

:::definition Greenhouse gas
A **greenhouse gas** is a gas in the atmosphere that absorbs radiation given out by the Earth, so that less heat escapes into space. The main greenhouse gases are carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour.
:::

Radiation from the Sun has a short wavelength, and most of it passes straight through the atmosphere and warms the ground. The warm Earth gives out radiation of a longer wavelength. Greenhouse gases absorb this longer-wavelength radiation, and the atmosphere is warmed.

Human activities are adding greenhouse gases to the air:

| Gas | Activities that add it |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | Burning fossil fuels; cutting down forests, which would have taken carbon dioxide in |
| Methane | Keeping cattle; growing rice in flooded fields; rubbish rotting in landfill |

## Climate change

Evidence that has been checked by many scientists shows that human activity is raising the temperature of the Earth, and that this will change the climate. The climate is so complicated that it is very hard to model, so exact predictions are uncertain. Expected effects include:

- sea levels rising, as ice melts and the oceans warm and expand;
- more frequent and more severe storms;
- changes in rainfall, bringing floods to some places and droughts to others;
- changes in where crops can be grown, and where wildlife can live.

## Carbon footprints

:::definition Carbon footprint
A **carbon footprint** is the total amount of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases given out over the full life of a product, a service or an event.
:::

:::example A journey by car
A car gives out $150\ \text{g}$ of carbon dioxide for each kilometre. How much does it give out on a journey of $40\ \text{km}$? Give the answer in kilograms.
@step Mass $= 150 \times 40 = 6000\ \text{g}$.
@step There are 1000 g in 1 kg: $6000 \div 1000$.
@answer $6\ \text{kg}$
:::

A footprint can be reduced by using less energy (insulating a home, travelling by train), by using energy that does not come from fossil fuels, and by capturing carbon dioxide at a power station and storing it underground. It is not easy: low-carbon choices can cost more or be less convenient, and people and governments do not always agree to make them.

## Pollutants from burning fuels

Most fuels are compounds of carbon and hydrogen, and some contain a little sulfur. In plenty of air a fuel burns completely to carbon dioxide and water. Other, more harmful, products form as well.

:::definition Incomplete combustion
**Incomplete combustion** is burning without enough oxygen. Some of the carbon in the fuel becomes carbon monoxide or soot, where complete combustion would have made carbon dioxide.
:::

| Pollutant | How it forms | The harm it does |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide, $\ce{CO}$ | Incomplete combustion | A toxic gas: it stops the blood carrying oxygen. It has no colour and no smell |
| Soot (particles of carbon) | Incomplete combustion | Breathing problems; **global dimming**, as the particles reflect sunlight |
| Sulfur dioxide, $\ce{SO2}$ | Sulfur impurities in the fuel burn | **Acid rain**; breathing problems |
| Oxides of nitrogen | Nitrogen and oxygen from the **air** react at the high temperature inside an engine | Acid rain; breathing problems |

:::mistake The nitrogen does not come from the fuel
Petrol and diesel contain almost no nitrogen. The oxides of nitrogen are made from the air drawn into the engine, which is why even an engine burning pure hydrogen produces them.
:::

:::example Which pollutant?
A gas fire in a closed room burns with a yellow flame and leaves black marks on the wall above it. Which pollutants is it making, and why is this dangerous?
@step A yellow flame and black marks show soot, so the gas is burning incompletely.
@step Incomplete combustion also makes carbon monoxide.
@answer Soot and carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide is toxic and cannot be seen or smelled.

:::summary
- **Greenhouse gases** (carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour) absorb radiation given out by the Earth and keep it warm.
- Burning fossil fuels adds carbon dioxide; cattle, rice fields and landfill add methane. The extra gases are causing **climate change**.
- A **carbon footprint** is the total greenhouse gases given out over the life of a product, service or event.
- **Incomplete combustion** makes toxic **carbon monoxide** and **soot**, which causes global dimming.
- **Sulfur dioxide**, from sulfur in fuels, and **oxides of nitrogen**, from the air in hot engines, cause acid rain and breathing problems.
:::
