A rate law tells you how fast a reaction goes at one moment. Often you want something more useful, such as how much reactant will be left after ten minutes, or how long it takes for half of a drug to leave the blood. The integrated rate laws answer those questions.

In this lesson you will use the integrated rate laws, find the order of a reaction from graphs, and use the half-life of a first-order reaction.

## The integrated rate laws

For a reaction with one reactant A and rate constant $k$, each order has its own equation linking the concentration at time $t$ to the starting concentration $[\text{A}]_0$.

:::definition Integrated rate laws
| Order | Integrated rate law | Straight-line graph | Slope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | $[\text{A}]_t = [\text{A}]_0 - kt$ | $[\text{A}]$ against $t$ | $-k$ |
| First | $\ln[\text{A}]_t = \ln[\text{A}]_0 - kt$ | $\ln[\text{A}]$ against $t$ | $-k$ |
| Second | $\dfrac{1}{[\text{A}]_t} = \dfrac{1}{[\text{A}]_0} + kt$ | $1/[\text{A}]$ against $t$ | $+k$ |
:::

:::example How much is left?
A first-order reaction has $k = 0.0040$ s⁻¹ and starts with $[\text{A}]_0 = 0.500$ mol/L. What is $[\text{A}]$ after 200 s?
@step $\ln[\text{A}]_t = \ln(0.500) - (0.0040)(200) = -0.693 - 0.800 = -1.493$.
@answer $[\text{A}]_t = e^{-1.493} = 0.225$ mol/L.
:::

## Finding the order from graphs

To find the order from data, plot $[\text{A}]$, $\ln[\text{A}]$ and $1/[\text{A}]$ against time. Only one of the three graphs is a straight line, and that graph tells you the order. Its slope then gives $k$.

## Half-life

:::definition Half-life
The time taken for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half its value. For a first-order reaction it is the same however much reactant there is:
$$t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k}$$
:::

A constant half-life is a sign of a first-order reaction. Radioactive decay is first order, which is why each isotope has a fixed half-life.

:::summary
- Zero order: $[\text{A}]$ against $t$ is linear. First order: $\ln[\text{A}]$ against $t$ is linear. Second order: $1/[\text{A}]$ against $t$ is linear.
- The slope of the straight-line graph gives $k$ ($-k$ for zero and first order, $+k$ for second order).
- A first-order reaction has a constant half-life, $t_{1/2} = 0.693/k$.
:::
