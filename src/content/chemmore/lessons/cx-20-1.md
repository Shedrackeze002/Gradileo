A chemist who is handed a white powder can burn a weighed sample, or dissolve it and precipitate one of its elements, and find how much of each element it holds. Those numbers tell them what the compound is, and whether the sample is pure.

In this lesson you will work out mass percents from formulas, use the law of definite proportions, and find the purity of a sample from an elemental analysis.

## Mass percent

:::definition Mass percent of an element
The share of a compound's mass that comes from one element, written as a percentage:
$$\text{mass percent} = \frac{\text{mass of the element in one mole}}{\text{molar mass of the compound}} \times 100$$
:::

Every sample of a pure compound has the same mass percent of each element, however big or small the sample is and wherever it came from. This is the **law of definite proportions**, and it is why a mass percent can identify a compound.

:::example Carbon in ethanol
What is the mass percent of carbon in ethanol, $\ce{C2H5OH}$? Relative atomic masses: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16.
@step Molar mass: $2 \times 12 + 6 \times 1 + 16 = 46$ g/mol.
@step Mass of carbon in one mole: $2 \times 12 = 24$ g.
@answer Mass percent of C $= \dfrac{24}{46} \times 100 = 52.2\%$.
:::

## Purity from an elemental analysis

A real sample is often a compound mixed with impurities. If the impurities contain none of the element being measured, all of that element came from the compound, so its mass tells you how much compound there is.

:::example An impure sample of copper(II) sulfate
A 5.00 g sample of impure copper(II) sulfate, $\ce{CuSO4}$, contains 1.82 g of copper. The impurities contain no copper. What is the percent purity? Relative atomic masses: Cu = 63.5, S = 32, O = 16.
@step $\ce{CuSO4}$ has a molar mass of 159.5 g/mol, so it is $\dfrac{63.5}{159.5} = 0.3981$ copper by mass.
@step Mass of $\ce{CuSO4}$ $= \dfrac{1.82}{0.3981} = 4.572$ g.
@answer Purity $= \dfrac{4.572}{5.00} \times 100 = 91.4\%$.
:::

The same idea works with percents. If a sample is 34.2% copper and pure $\ce{CuSO4}$ is 39.8% copper, the sample is $\dfrac{34.2}{39.8} \times 100 = 85.9\%$ copper sulfate.

## A mixture of two compounds

When both parts of a mixture contain the element, call the mass of one compound $x$ and the mass of the other (total $- x$). The element's mass from both must add up to what was measured, which gives one equation in $x$.

:::mistake Using the mass percent of the whole mixture
Each compound has its own mass fraction of the element. Write one term for each compound, each with its own fraction, and only then solve for $x$.
:::

:::check cx-ap-purity 3
:::

:::summary
- Mass percent of an element $= \dfrac{\text{its mass in one mole}}{\text{molar mass}} \times 100$.
- A pure compound always has the same mass percents (the law of definite proportions).
- If the impurities hold none of the element, the element's mass gives the mass of the compound, and so the purity.
- For a mixture of two compounds that both contain the element, write the element's total mass as one equation in the mass of one compound.
:::
