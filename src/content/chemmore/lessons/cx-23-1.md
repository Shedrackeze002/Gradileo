The equilibrium constant tells you the balance a reaction will settle at. It does not tell you, on its own, which way a mixture you have just made will go. For that you compare $K$ with the same expression worked out from the mixture as it is now.

In this lesson you will use the reaction quotient to predict the direction of a reaction, and work out $K$ for reactions that are reversed, multiplied or added.

## The reaction quotient

:::definition Reaction quotient, Q
The same expression as $K$, but worked out from the concentrations (or partial pressures) at any moment, whether or not the system is at equilibrium. For $\ce{aA + bB <=> cC + dD}$,
$$Q_c = \frac{[\text{C}]^c[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a[\text{B}]^b}$$
:::

Comparing $Q$ with $K$ tells you which way the reaction goes:

- $Q < K$: too few products, so the reaction goes **forward**.
- $Q > K$: too many products, so the reaction goes **backward**.
- $Q = K$: the system is at equilibrium.

:::check cx-ap-reaction-quotient 1
:::

:::example Which way will it go?
For $\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}$, $K_c = 50$. A mixture has $[\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = 0.10$ mol/L and $[\ce{HI}] = 1.0$ mol/L.
@step $Q_c = \dfrac{(1.0)^2}{(0.10)(0.10)} = 100$.
@answer $Q_c > K_c$, so the reaction goes backward, making more $\ce{H2}$ and $\ce{I2}$.
:::

:::check cx-ap-reaction-quotient 2
:::

Le Chatelier's principle can be explained the same way. A change in concentration or volume changes $Q$ while $K$ stays the same, and the system shifts to bring $Q$ back to $K$.

## The properties of K

When you change the equation of a reaction, its equilibrium constant changes in a fixed way.

:::definition Changing the equation
- **Reverse** the reaction: the new constant is $1/K$.
- **Multiply** every coefficient by $n$: the new constant is $K^n$.
- **Add** two reactions: the new constant is $K_1 \times K_2$.
:::

:::summary
- $Q$ has the same form as $K$ but uses the concentrations at any moment.
- $Q < K$: forward. $Q > K$: backward. $Q = K$: at equilibrium.
- A change of conditions moves $Q$ away from $K$, and the system shifts to bring $Q$ back.
- Reversing a reaction gives $1/K$, multiplying by $n$ gives $K^n$, and adding reactions multiplies their constants.
:::
