Some samples cannot be titrated directly. Limestone is a solid that reacts slowly with acid, so there is no sharp end point. A mixture of sodium carbonate and sodium hydrogencarbonate reacts with acid in two stages, so one indicator cannot tell you how much of each there is. Two clever techniques solve these problems: the back titration and the double indicator titration.

In this lesson you will work out the purity of a sample from a back titration, and the composition of a mixture from a double indicator titration.

## Back titrations

:::definition Back titration
In a **back titration**, a known excess of a reagent is added to the sample, and the amount left over is then found by titration. The amount that reacted with the sample is the amount added minus the excess.
:::

:::example The purity of limestone
1.50 g of limestone is added to 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ HCl. When the reaction is over, the excess acid needs 25.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Find the percentage of $\ce{CaCO3}$ (M = 100) in the limestone. $\ce{CaCO3 + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2}$
@step Acid added = 1.00 × 50.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0500 mol.
@step Excess acid = NaOH used = 1.00 × 25.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0250 mol.
@step Acid that reacted = 0.0500 − 0.0250 = 0.0250 mol, so $\ce{CaCO3}$ = 0.0250 ÷ 2 = 0.0125 mol = 1.25 g.
@answer 1.25 ÷ 1.50 × 100 = 83.3% calcium carbonate
:::

Back titrations are used for insoluble bases such as limestone, magnesium oxide and zinc oxide, and for ammonium salts, which are boiled with excess sodium hydroxide to drive off all the ammonia before the alkali left is titrated.

## Double indicator titrations

Hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium carbonate in two stages:

$$\ce{Na2CO3 + HCl -> NaHCO3 + NaCl} \quad \text{(complete at the phenolphthalein end point, pink to colourless)}$$

$$\ce{NaHCO3 + HCl -> NaCl + H2O + CO2} \quad \text{(complete at the methyl orange end point, yellow to orange)}$$

Phenolphthalein is added first and the first titre read; then methyl orange is added and the titration continued.

- For a **carbonate and hydrogencarbonate** mixture: the first titre gives the carbonate. The second titre neutralises the hydrogencarbonate made from the carbonate plus the hydrogencarbonate there at the start, so the original hydrogencarbonate is (second titre − first titre) worth of acid.
- For a **hydroxide and carbonate** mixture: the first titre neutralises the hydroxide and turns the carbonate into hydrogencarbonate. The second titre gives the carbonate, so the hydroxide is (first titre − second titre) worth of acid.

:::mistake Read which titre is which
In a hydroxide and carbonate mixture, the first titre is larger than the second; in a carbonate and hydrogencarbonate mixture, the second is larger.
:::

:::summary
- Back titration: amount reacted with the sample = amount added − excess found by titration.
- It suits insoluble or slow-reacting samples and ammonium salts.
- Phenolphthalein marks $\ce{CO3^2-}$ to $\ce{HCO3-}$; methyl orange marks $\ce{HCO3-}$ to $\ce{CO2}$.
- Use the two titres together to find each component of the mixture.
:::
