Carbon has two electrons in its 2s orbital and two in its 2p orbitals, so on paper it should form only two bonds, at right angles. Yet methane has four identical C–H bonds pointing to the corners of a tetrahedron. The idea that explains this, hybridisation, also explains why double bonds stop molecules from twisting.

In this lesson you will work out the hybridisation of a central atom, link it to the shape and bond angle, and count the sigma and pi bonds in a molecule.

## Hybrid orbitals

:::definition Hybridisation
**Hybridisation** is the mixing of atomic orbitals on one atom to form a set of new, identical **hybrid orbitals** that point in particular directions.
:::

To find the hybridisation, count the **regions of electron density** round the central atom: each bonded atom counts once (a double or triple bond still counts once), and each lone pair counts once.

| Regions | Hybridisation | Arrangement | Angle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | sp | linear | 180° | $\ce{BeCl2}$, $\ce{CO2}$, each C in ethyne |
| 3 | sp² | trigonal planar | 120° | $\ce{BF3}$, each C in ethene |
| 4 | sp³ | tetrahedral | 109.5° | $\ce{CH4}$, $\ce{NH3}$, $\ce{H2O}$ |
| 6 | sp³d² | octahedral | 90° | $\ce{SF6}$ |

Ammonia and water are sp³ too: their lone pairs fill some of the hybrid orbitals, so ammonia is pyramidal (107°) and water is bent (104.5°).

:::example Hybridisation in methanal
What is the hybridisation of carbon in methanal, $\ce{H2C=O}$?
@step Carbon is bonded to two hydrogens and one oxygen, and has no lone pairs.
@step The double bond counts as one region, so there are 3 regions.
@answer sp², trigonal planar, with bond angles of about 120°.
:::

## Sigma and pi bonds

A **sigma (σ) bond** forms by the end-on overlap of orbitals along the line between two nuclei. A **pi (π) bond** forms by the sideways overlap of two p orbitals, above and below that line.

- A single bond is one σ bond.
- A double bond is one σ bond and one π bond.
- A triple bond is one σ bond and two π bonds.

In ethene, each carbon is sp² hybridised; the unhybridised p orbital on each carbon overlaps sideways with its neighbour to form the π bond. A σ bond is **stronger** than a π bond, because end-on overlap is greater than sideways overlap. Twisting one end of a double bond would pull the p orbitals apart and break the π bond, so there is **no free rotation** about a C=C bond.

:::mistake Count every bond
When counting σ bonds, include every C–H bond. Ethene, $\ce{H2C=CH2}$, has 5 σ bonds (four C–H and one C–C) and 1 π bond.

:::summary
- Count regions of electron density round the central atom: 2 is sp, 3 is sp², 4 is sp³, 6 is sp³d².
- The arrangements are linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral and octahedral; lone pairs change the shape of the atoms.
- Single bond: 1 σ. Double: 1 σ + 1 π. Triple: 1 σ + 2 π.
- σ bonds are stronger than π bonds, and a π bond stops rotation about a double bond.
:::
