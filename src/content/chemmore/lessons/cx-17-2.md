In some towns, soap lathers at once; in others, it forms a grey scum and kettles fur up with white scale. The difference is hard water, which carries dissolved calcium and magnesium compounds from the rocks it has flowed through. Water for a town also has to be made clean and safe before it reaches the taps.

In this lesson you will explain temporary and permanent hardness and how each is removed, interpret a soap test, and describe how water is treated for a town supply.

## Hard water

:::definition Hard water
**Hard water** is water that does not easily form a lather with soap, because it contains dissolved calcium or magnesium ions.
:::

**Temporary hardness** is caused by dissolved calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates. Rain water containing carbon dioxide dissolves limestone:

$$\ce{CaCO3 + H2O + CO2 -> Ca(HCO3)2}$$

Boiling reverses this, so it removes temporary hardness and leaves the scale (fur) in kettles:

$$\ce{Ca(HCO3)2 -> CaCO3 + H2O + CO2}$$

**Permanent hardness** is caused by dissolved calcium and magnesium sulfates and chlorides, which boiling does not change.

| Method | Removes |
|---|---|
| Boiling | temporary hardness only |
| Adding calcium hydroxide (Clark's method) | temporary hardness only |
| Adding washing soda, $\ce{Na2CO3}$ | both |
| Ion-exchange resin | both |
| Distillation | both |

Washing soda precipitates the calcium ions: $\ce{CaSO4 + Na2CO3 -> CaCO3 + Na2SO4}$. An ion-exchange resin swaps calcium and magnesium ions for sodium ions.

## The soap test

Soap is sodium stearate; calcium ions turn it into insoluble calcium stearate, the scum, so soap only lathers once all the calcium ions are used up. The volume of soap solution needed for a lasting lather measures the hardness.

:::example Reading a soap test
Distilled water needs 1 cm³ of soap solution, water X needs 12 cm³, and water X after boiling needs 6 cm³. What hardness does X have?
@step Unboiled, X needs far more soap than distilled water, so it is hard.
@step Boiling lowers the volume to 6 cm³, so some hardness was temporary; 6 cm³ is still more than 1 cm³, so some is permanent.
@answer Both temporary and permanent hardness
:::

## Advantages and disadvantages of hard water

Hard water tastes better, supplies calcium for bones and teeth, and the scale it forms lines old lead pipes so less lead dissolves. It wastes soap, forms scale in kettles and boilers that wastes fuel, and can block pipes. Soapless detergents work well in hard water because their calcium salts are soluble.

## Treating water for a town

1. **Screening** removes large objects.
2. **Coagulation**: alum (aluminium sulfate) makes fine particles clump together.
3. **Sedimentation**: the clumps settle in tanks.
4. **Filtration** through sand beds removes the rest.
5. **Chlorination** kills bacteria, so the water stays safe in the pipes.

:::summary
- Temporary hardness: hydrogencarbonates, removed by boiling. Permanent hardness: sulfates and chlorides.
- Washing soda, ion exchange and distillation remove both kinds.
- In a soap test, compare unboiled and boiled water with distilled water.
- Town water is screened, coagulated with alum, settled, filtered and chlorinated.
:::
