For reactions between gases, it is easier to measure pressures than concentrations. So for gas equilibria such as the Haber process, chemists use an equilibrium constant written in pressures: **Kp**.

In this lesson you will calculate mole fractions, partial pressures and Kp, and explain what does and does not change Kp.

## Mole fractions and partial pressures

:::definition Partial pressure
The pressure that one gas in a mixture would exert if it filled the container on its own. The partial pressures of all the gases add up to the total pressure.
:::

$$\text{mole fraction of A} = \frac{\text{moles of A}}{\text{total moles of gas}} \qquad p(\ce{A}) = \text{mole fraction of A} \times \text{total pressure}$$

## Writing and calculating Kp

For $a\ce{A}(g) + b\ce{B}(g) <=> c\ce{C}(g) + d\ce{D}(g)$:

$$K_p = \frac{p(\ce{C})^c \, p(\ce{D})^d}{p(\ce{A})^a \, p(\ce{B})^b}$$

Only gases appear in Kp. Find its units by cancelling the pressure units, as you did for Kc.

:::example Kp for dinitrogen tetroxide
1.00 mol of $\ce{N2O4}$ is heated until 20% has dissociated: $\ce{N2O4(g) <=> 2NO2(g)}$. The total pressure is 100 kPa. Find Kp.
@step At equilibrium: $\ce{N2O4}$ $1.00 - 0.20 = 0.80$ mol; $\ce{NO2}$ $2 \times 0.20 = 0.40$ mol; total 1.20 mol.
@step $p(\ce{N2O4}) = \dfrac{0.80}{1.20} \times 100 = 66.7$ kPa; $p(\ce{NO2}) = \dfrac{0.40}{1.20} \times 100 = 33.3$ kPa.
@step $K_p = \dfrac{33.3^2}{66.7}$
@answer $K_p = 16.7$ kPa
:::

## What changes Kp?

Like Kc, Kp depends **only on temperature**.

- **Temperature**: if the forward reaction is exothermic, raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the left and Kp **decreases**. If it is endothermic, Kp increases.
- **Pressure**: the position of equilibrium may shift, but the partial pressures adjust so that Kp stays the same.
- **A catalyst**: speeds up the forward and backward reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner but Kp is unchanged.

:::summary
- Mole fraction = moles of the gas ÷ total moles; partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure.
- Kp uses partial pressures of gases only, each raised to its number of moles.
- Only temperature changes Kp: for an exothermic forward reaction, a higher temperature lowers Kp.
- Pressure and catalysts do not change Kp.
:::
