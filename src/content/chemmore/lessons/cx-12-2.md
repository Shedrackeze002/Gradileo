Aldehydes and ketones both contain the C=O group, and the same group gives them their reactions: it is polar, so nucleophiles attack its carbon. That lets you turn them into alcohols, or add an extra carbon to make hydroxynitriles.

In this lesson you will predict the products of reducing aldehydes and ketones, explain the mechanism, and work out which hydroxynitriles form.

## The polar C=O bond

Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, so the C=O carbon is $\delta+$. Nucleophiles attack it and the C=O π bond breaks: this is **nucleophilic addition**.

Aldehydes and ketones can be told apart because only **aldehydes** are oxidised further:

- **Tollens’ reagent**: an aldehyde gives a silver mirror;
- **Fehling’s solution**: an aldehyde turns it from blue to a brick-red precipitate.

Ketones give no change with either.

## Reduction with NaBH₄

Sodium tetrahydridoborate, $\ce{NaBH4}$, in water or methanol is a source of the **hydride ion**, $\ce{H-}$, which acts as the nucleophile. We write the reducing agent as [H]:

- an aldehyde is reduced to a **primary** alcohol: $\ce{CH3CHO + 2[H] -> CH3CH2OH}$;
- a ketone is reduced to a **secondary** alcohol: $\ce{CH3COCH3 + 2[H] -> CH3CH(OH)CH3}$.

:::example Reducing butanone
What forms when butanone, $\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}$, is reduced with $\ce{NaBH4}$?
@step Butanone is a ketone, so it gives a secondary alcohol.
@step The C=O on carbon 2 becomes CH(OH).
@answer Butan-2-ol, $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3}$
:::

### The mechanism

1. The hydride ion attacks the $\delta+$ carbon, and the C=O π electrons move onto the oxygen.
2. The negatively charged oxygen takes an $\ce{H+}$ from water, forming the OH group.

## Hydroxynitriles

With potassium cyanide followed by dilute acid, the cyanide ion adds to the C=O group, giving a **hydroxynitrile**. The chain gets one carbon longer, and the nitrile carbon is carbon 1 in the name.

$$\ce{CH3CHO + HCN -> CH3CH(OH)CN}$$

:::example Naming hydroxynitriles
Name the products from ethanal and from propanone.
@step Ethanal gives $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CN}$: three carbons, OH on carbon 2.
@step Propanone gives $\ce{(CH3)2C(OH)CN}$: three carbons in the chain, with OH and a methyl group on carbon 2.
@answer 2-hydroxypropanenitrile and 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile
:::

KCN is used rather than HCN because HCN is a very toxic gas. KCN is still toxic, so it must be handled with great care.

:::mistake Not every hydroxynitrile is chiral
2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile has two identical $\ce{CH3}$ groups on its central carbon, so it is not chiral. Ethanal’s product is chiral, and forms as a racemate.
:::

:::summary
- The C=O carbon is $\delta+$, so it undergoes nucleophilic addition.
- Tollens’ reagent and Fehling’s solution react with aldehydes but not ketones.
- $\ce{NaBH4}$ reduces aldehydes to primary alcohols and ketones to secondary alcohols; $\ce{H-}$ is the nucleophile.
- KCN then dilute acid gives hydroxynitriles, one carbon longer.
:::
