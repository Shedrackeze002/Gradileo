Double the concentration of one reactant and a reaction might go twice as fast, four times as fast, or not change at all. Which one happens tells you about the steps the molecules take as they react.

In this lesson you will find orders of reaction, write rate equations, calculate the rate constant, and use the Arrhenius equation.

## Rate equations and orders

:::definition Rate equation
An equation such as $\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^m[\ce{B}]^n$, found by experiment. $m$ and $n$ are the **orders** with respect to A and B, and $k$ is the **rate constant**. The overall order is $m + n$.
:::

Orders are usually 0, 1 or 2:

| Order with respect to A | If [A] is doubled, the rate… |
|---|---|
| 0 | stays the same |
| 1 | doubles |
| 2 | quadruples |

## The units of k

Rearrange the rate equation for $k$ and put in the units: rate in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ and each concentration in mol dm⁻³.

| Overall order | Units of k |
|---|---|
| 1 | s⁻¹ |
| 2 | mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹ |
| 3 | mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹ |

:::example Calculating k
$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^2[\ce{B}]$. When $[\ce{A}] = 0.20$ and $[\ce{B}] = 0.10$ mol dm⁻³, the rate is $4.0 \times 10^{-4}$ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. Find $k$.
@step $k = \dfrac{\text{rate}}{[\ce{A}]^2[\ce{B}]} = \dfrac{4.0 \times 10^{-4}}{0.20^2 \times 0.10} = \dfrac{4.0 \times 10^{-4}}{4.0 \times 10^{-3}}$
@step The overall order is 3, so the units are mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹.
@answer $k = 0.10$ mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹
:::

## The rate-determining step

Most reactions happen in several steps. The slowest is the **rate-determining step**, and the species in the rate equation are those taking part in it (or in steps before it). So if $\ce{A + 2B -> C}$ has $\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]$, B only reacts in a later, faster step.

## The Arrhenius equation

The rate constant rises with temperature, as the Arrhenius equation shows:

$$k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$$

where $E_a$ is the activation energy in J mol⁻¹, $R = 8.31$ J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, $T$ is in kelvin and $A$ is the Arrhenius constant. Taking natural logs:

$$\ln k = \ln A - \frac{E_a}{RT}$$

So a graph of $\ln k$ against $1/T$ is a straight line with gradient $-E_a/R$.

:::example Activation energy from a graph
A graph of $\ln k$ against $1/T$ has gradient $-9000$ K. Find $E_a$.
@step Gradient $= -\dfrac{E_a}{R}$, so $E_a = 9000 \times 8.31 = 74790$ J mol⁻¹.
@answer $E_a = 74.8$ kJ mol⁻¹
:::

:::mistake Kelvin and joules
Use $T$ in kelvin (add 273 to °C) and $E_a$ in J mol⁻¹, not kJ mol⁻¹. Then convert your final answer back to kJ if asked.
:::

:::summary
- $\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^m[\ce{B}]^n$; orders come from experiment, not from the equation.
- Order 0: no change; order 1: rate doubles; order 2: rate quadruples when the concentration doubles.
- Find the units of $k$ by rearranging the rate equation.
- The rate equation contains the species in the rate-determining step.
- $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$; the gradient of $\ln k$ against $1/T$ is $-E_a/R$.
:::
