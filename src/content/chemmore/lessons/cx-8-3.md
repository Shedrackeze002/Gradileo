Chlorine is a poisonous gas, yet it is added to the water we drink and is the active part of household bleach. Its relatives, bromine and iodine, follow the same patterns, but each a little more weakly than the one above.

In this lesson you will explain the trends down Group 7, predict displacement reactions, identify halide ions, and describe the uses of chlorine.

## Trends down Group 7

| Halogen | Appearance at room temperature | Colour in water |
|---|---|---|
| Fluorine, $\ce{F2}$ | pale yellow gas | (reacts with water) |
| Chlorine, $\ce{Cl2}$ | pale green gas | pale green |
| Bromine, $\ce{Br2}$ | red-brown liquid | orange |
| Iodine, $\ce{I2}$ | grey-black solid | brown |

- **Boiling points increase** down the group: the molecules get larger, with more electrons, so the van der Waals forces between them are stronger.
- **Electronegativity decreases**: the atoms get larger, so the nucleus attracts a bonding pair less strongly.
- **Oxidising ability decreases**: an oxidising agent gains electrons, and larger atoms attract an extra electron less strongly.

## Displacement reactions

A halogen displaces a halide ion that is **below** it in the group, because it is the stronger oxidising agent.

:::example Chlorine and iodide ions
Chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution. What happens?
@step Chlorine is above iodine, so it is the stronger oxidising agent.
@step It oxidises the iodide ions to iodine, and is reduced to chloride ions.
@answer $\ce{Cl2 + 2I- -> 2Cl- + I2}$: the solution turns brown.
:::

Bromine displaces iodide but not chloride, and iodine displaces neither.

:::

## Chlorine in water and in bleach

Chlorine reacts with water in a reversible reaction:

$$\ce{Cl2 + H2O <=> HCl + HClO}$$

Chlorine is both oxidised (0 to +1 in $\ce{HClO}$) and reduced (0 to −1 in $\ce{HCl}$). A reaction in which one element is both oxidised and reduced is called **disproportionation**. Chloric(I) acid, $\ce{HClO}$, kills bacteria, which is why chlorine is added to drinking water. Chlorine is toxic, but the amounts used are small, and the benefit of preventing diseases such as cholera outweighs the risks. In bright sunlight, chlorine water slowly gives off oxygen instead: $\ce{2Cl2 + 2H2O -> 4HCl + O2}$.

With cold, dilute sodium hydroxide, chlorine makes **bleach**:

$$\ce{Cl2 + 2NaOH -> NaClO + NaCl + H2O}$$

## Identifying halide ions

Add **dilute nitric acid**, then **silver nitrate** solution. The nitric acid removes carbonate and hydroxide ions, which would also give precipitates.

| Halide | Precipitate | Dilute ammonia | Concentrated ammonia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chloride | white AgCl | dissolves | dissolves |
| Bromide | cream AgBr | does not dissolve | dissolves |
| Iodide | yellow AgI | does not dissolve | does not dissolve |

Fluoride gives no precipitate, because silver fluoride is soluble.

## Halides as reducing agents

Down the group, the halide ions become **stronger reducing agents**: the outer electrons of a larger ion are further from the nucleus, so they are given away more easily. This shows with **concentrated sulfuric acid**:

| Solid | What forms | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| NaCl | misty fumes of HCl | chloride does not reduce the acid |
| NaBr | HBr, orange-brown fumes of $\ce{Br2}$, and $\ce{SO2}$ | bromide reduces S from +6 to +4 |
| NaI | HI, purple-black $\ce{I2}$, $\ce{SO2}$, yellow S, and $\ce{H2S}$ (bad eggs) | iodide reduces S as far as −2 |

:::

:::summary
- Down Group 7: boiling points rise; electronegativity and oxidising ability fall.
- A halogen displaces the halide ions below it: $\ce{Cl2 + 2Br- -> 2Cl- + Br2}$.
- $\ce{Cl2 + H2O <=> HCl + HClO}$ is disproportionation; chlorine kills bacteria in water. With cold NaOH it makes bleach.
- Nitric acid then silver nitrate: chloride white, bromide cream, iodide yellow; ammonia confirms which.
- Halide ions are stronger reducing agents down the group, so they reduce concentrated sulfuric acid further.
:::
