For many molecules you can draw more than one reasonable Lewis structure. Chemists need a way to choose the best one, and a way to describe molecules such as ozone, where no single structure gets the bonds right.

In this lesson you will calculate formal charges, use them to choose between Lewis structures, and describe molecules with resonance.

## Formal charge

:::definition Formal charge
The charge an atom would have in a Lewis structure if every bond's electrons were shared equally:
$$\text{formal charge} = \text{valence electrons} - \text{lone pair electrons} - \text{number of bonds}$$
:::

Count each single bond as one bond, a double bond as two and a triple bond as three. The formal charges in a structure add up to the overall charge of the molecule or ion.

:::example The formal charges in carbon monoxide
In $\ce{C#O}$, carbon has one lone pair and three bonds, and oxygen has one lone pair and three bonds.
@step Carbon: $4 - 2 - 3 = -1$.
@step Oxygen: $6 - 2 - 3 = +1$.
@answer The charges add up to 0, as they must for a neutral molecule.
:::

## Choosing the best Lewis structure

When several structures are possible, the best one usually:

- has formal charges as close to zero as possible;
- puts any negative formal charge on the most electronegative atom.

## Resonance

Ozone, $\ce{O3}$, has one double bond and one single bond in its Lewis structure, but the double bond could be drawn on either side. Experiments show that both bonds in ozone are the same length, between that of a single and a double bond.

:::definition Resonance
When a molecule can be drawn with two or more Lewis structures that differ only in where the electrons are, the real molecule is an average of them. The structures are **resonance structures**, and the electrons they share are **delocalised** over several bonds.
:::

The molecule does not flip between the structures. It always has the averaged structure, and the separate drawings are only a way of showing that with the Lewis model.

:::check cx-ap-resonance 1
:::

The **bond order** of each bond is the average number of bonds it has across the resonance structures.

:::example The nitrate ion
$\ce{NO3-}$ has three resonance structures, each with one N=O and two N–O bonds.
@step Across the three structures, each N–O position is a double bond once and a single bond twice.
@step Average bond order $= \dfrac{2 + 1 + 1}{3}$.
@answer Each N–O bond has a bond order of $\dfrac{4}{3} = 1.33$.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Formal charge $=$ valence electrons $-$ lone pair electrons $-$ bonds, and the charges add up to the overall charge.
- The best Lewis structure keeps formal charges near zero, with any negative charge on the most electronegative atom.
- Resonance structures differ only in where electrons are; the real molecule is their average, with delocalised electrons.
- Bond order is the average number of bonds across the resonance structures.
:::
