Many WASSCE practical questions start with a gas: how it was made, how it was dried and how it was collected. Each choice follows from the gas's properties, chiefly how soluble it is in water and whether it is lighter or denser than air.

In this lesson you will choose the reagents for preparing common gases, write the equations, and decide how each gas is dried and collected.

## Preparing gases in the laboratory

:::definition Laboratory preparation
A **laboratory preparation** of a gas is a reaction chosen to give the gas safely, at a steady rate and from cheap, available reagents.
:::

| Gas | Reagents | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen | zinc granules and dilute hydrochloric acid | $\ce{Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2}$ |
| Oxygen | hydrogen peroxide with manganese(IV) oxide catalyst | $\ce{2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2}$ |
| Carbon dioxide | marble chips and dilute hydrochloric acid | $\ce{CaCO3 + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2}$ |
| Ammonia | ammonium chloride heated with calcium hydroxide | $\ce{2NH4Cl + Ca(OH)2 -> CaCl2 + 2H2O + 2NH3}$ |
| Chlorine | concentrated hydrochloric acid heated with manganese(IV) oxide | $\ce{MnO2 + 4HCl -> MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2}$ |
| Hydrogen chloride | sodium chloride and concentrated sulfuric acid | $\ce{NaCl + H2SO4 -> NaHSO4 + HCl}$ |

Some reagents are avoided for good reasons. Dilute sulfuric acid is not used with marble chips, because insoluble calcium sulfate coats the chips and stops the reaction. Nitric acid is not used with zinc, because it is an oxidising agent and gives oxides of nitrogen instead of hydrogen.

## Collecting gases

| Property of the gas | Method |
|---|---|
| Only slightly soluble in water (hydrogen, oxygen) | over water |
| Soluble and lighter than air (ammonia) | upward delivery (downward displacement of air) |
| Soluble and denser than air (hydrogen chloride, chlorine, carbon dioxide) | downward delivery (upward displacement of air) |

Air has an average relative molecular mass of about 29, so a gas with $M_r$ below 29 is lighter than air.

## Drying gases

A drying agent must remove water without reacting with the gas. Concentrated sulfuric acid and fused calcium chloride dry most gases, but ammonia is a base, so it reacts with sulfuric acid and also with calcium chloride; ammonia is dried with **calcium oxide (quicklime)**. Acidic gases such as carbon dioxide, chlorine and hydrogen chloride cannot be dried with quicklime.

:::example Making and collecting ammonia
How is a dry sample of ammonia made and collected?
@step Heat ammonium chloride with calcium hydroxide in a tube tilted slightly downwards, so the water formed does not run back and crack the hot glass.
@step Pass the gas through a tower of quicklime to dry it.
@step Ammonia is very soluble and lighter than air, so collect it by upward delivery.
@answer Heat NH₄Cl with Ca(OH)₂, dry over CaO, and collect by upward delivery.
:::

:::summary
- Hydrogen: zinc and dilute HCl. Oxygen: H₂O₂ and MnO₂. Carbon dioxide: marble and dilute HCl.
- Ammonia: NH₄Cl and Ca(OH)₂. Chlorine: conc. HCl and MnO₂. Hydrogen chloride: NaCl and conc. H₂SO₄.
- Slightly soluble gases are collected over water; soluble gases by upward delivery if lighter than air and downward delivery if denser.
- Ammonia is dried with quicklime; most other gases with concentrated sulfuric acid.
:::
