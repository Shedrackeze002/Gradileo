But-2-ene has the formula $\ce{CH3CH=CHCH3}$, yet there are two different compounds with that structure. They boil at different temperatures and have different shapes, because the double bond holds their groups in place.

In this lesson you will explain E–Z isomerism, decide whether an alkene has E–Z isomers, and name them using priority rules.

## Why E–Z isomers exist

:::definition Stereoisomers
Compounds with the same structural formula but a different arrangement of their atoms in space.
:::

A single C–C bond lets the two carbons rotate freely. A C=C double bond does not: its π bond **restricts rotation**. So if two groups are on the same side of a double bond, they stay there.

For an alkene to have E–Z isomers, **each** carbon of the double bond must carry **two different groups**.

:::example Which alkenes have E–Z isomers?
Do but-1-ene and but-2-ene have E–Z isomers?
@step But-2-ene, $\ce{CH3CH=CHCH3}$: each carbon of the C=C has an H and a $\ce{CH3}$. Two different groups on each.
@step But-1-ene, $\ce{CH2=CHCH2CH3}$: carbon 1 has two hydrogens, so swapping them changes nothing.
@answer But-2-ene does; but-1-ene does not.
:::

## Naming E and Z: the priority rules

The **Cahn–Ingold–Prelog** (CIP) rules decide which group on each carbon has **priority**:

1. Look at the atom directly bonded to the carbon of the C=C. The higher its **atomic number**, the higher the priority. So Br (35) beats Cl (17), which beats O (8), which beats C (6), which beats H (1).
2. If the two atoms are the same, move one atom further along each group and compare again.

Then:

- **Z** (from the German *zusammen*, together): the two higher-priority groups are on the **same side**.
- **E** (from *entgegen*, opposite): they are on **opposite sides**.

:::check cx-ez-assign 1
:::

:::example Naming an isomer of 1-bromo-2-chloroprop-1-ene
Carbon 1 carries Br and H; carbon 2 carries Cl and $\ce{CH3}$. The Br and the Cl are on opposite sides. Is this E or Z?
@step Carbon 1: Br (35) beats H (1).
@step Carbon 2: Cl (17) beats C (6).
@step The two priority groups, Br and Cl, are on opposite sides.
@answer The E isomer
:::

:::mistake Priority is not size
The priority depends on atomic number, not on how big the group is. A single Cl atom outranks a long carbon chain, because chlorine’s atomic number (17) is higher than carbon’s (6).
:::

:::summary
- E–Z isomers are stereoisomers caused by **restricted rotation** about a C=C double bond.
- Each carbon of the C=C must carry two **different** groups.
- On each carbon, the group whose first atom has the higher **atomic number** has priority.
- **Z**: priority groups on the same side. **E**: on opposite sides.
:::
