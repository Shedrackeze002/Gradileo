The balanced equation for a reaction shows where it starts and ends, but most reactions get there through a series of simpler steps. That series, the mechanism, decides the rate law, and the rate law is how chemists test whether a proposed mechanism could be right.

In this lesson you will find intermediates and catalysts in a mechanism, and work out the rate law a mechanism predicts.

## Elementary steps and mechanisms

:::definition Reaction mechanism
A sequence of **elementary steps**, each a single collision or a single molecule breaking apart, that add up to the overall equation.
:::

For an elementary step, and only for an elementary step, the rate law comes straight from the equation. A step $\ce{A + B -> products}$ has rate $= k[\text{A}][\text{B}]$, and a step $\ce{2A -> products}$ has rate $= k[\text{A}]^2$.

:::definition Intermediates and catalysts
- An **intermediate** is made in one step and used up in a later step.
- A **catalyst** is used up in one step and made again in a later step.

Neither appears in the overall equation.
:::

## The rate-determining step

The slowest step limits how fast the whole reaction can go, so it is called the **rate-determining step**. If the first step is the slow one, the rate law of the reaction is the rate law of that step.

:::example A slow first step
$\ce{NO2 + NO2 -> NO3 + NO}$ (slow), then $\ce{NO3 + CO -> NO2 + CO2}$ (fast).
@step The slow first step is a collision of two $\ce{NO2}$ molecules.
@answer rate $= k[\ce{NO2}]^2$. Carbon monoxide does not appear in the rate law, though it is a reactant.
:::

## A fast equilibrium before the slow step

When a fast, reversible step comes first, its forward and reverse reactions keep it close to equilibrium. The slow step's rate law then contains an intermediate, which you replace using that equilibrium.

:::example The pre-equilibrium approximation
$\ce{2NO <=> N2O2}$ (fast), then $\ce{N2O2 + O2 -> 2NO2}$ (slow).
@step The slow step gives rate $= k_2[\ce{N2O2}][\ce{O2}]$, but $\ce{N2O2}$ is an intermediate.
@step The fast equilibrium gives $[\ce{N2O2}] = K[\ce{NO}]^2$.
@answer rate $= k_2K[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}] = k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]$.
:::

:::

## Energy profiles of mechanisms

Each elementary step has its own peak on an energy profile, and each trough between peaks is an intermediate. The step with the largest climb from its own starting level to its peak has the highest activation energy, so it is the slowest step.

:::summary
- A mechanism is a series of elementary steps that add up to the overall equation; each elementary step's rate law comes from its equation.
- Intermediates are made then used; catalysts are used then made again.
- The slowest step decides the rate. A slow first step gives the rate law directly.
- When a fast equilibrium comes first, replace the intermediate using that equilibrium.
- On an energy profile, troughs are intermediates and the largest climb is the slow step.
:::
