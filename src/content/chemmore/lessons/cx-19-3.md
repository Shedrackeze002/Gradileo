Palm oil is pressed from the fruit of the oil palm and used for cooking across West Africa. Boil the same oil with an alkali and it turns into soap, which is how black soap has been made for centuries. Fats, oils and soaps are all built from the same parts, and their structure explains how soap lifts grease from cloth.

In this lesson you will describe fats and oils as esters of glycerol, their hydrogenation and saponification, calculate the alkali needed to saponify a fat, and compare soaps with soapless detergents.

## Fats and oils

:::definition Triglyceride
A **triglyceride** is an ester formed from one molecule of propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol) and three molecules of long-chain fatty acids. Fats and oils are triglycerides.
:::

**Oils** are liquid at room temperature because their chains are **unsaturated**: their C=C bonds put kinks in the chains, so the molecules pack less closely. **Fats** such as beef fat have more saturated chains and are solid. An unsaturated oil decolourises bromine water.

**Hydrogenation** adds hydrogen to the C=C bonds using a nickel catalyst at about 150 °C, raising the melting point and hardening vegetable oil into margarine. Fats and oils go **rancid** when they are oxidised or hydrolysed, releasing smelly fatty acids.

## Saponification

**Saponification** is the hydrolysis of a fat or oil by an alkali, giving glycerol and the sodium (or potassium) salts of the fatty acids, which are soap. One mole of fat reacts with three moles of sodium hydroxide. Common salt is added afterwards to make the soap separate out (**salting out**). Potassium hydroxide gives softer, more soluble soaps.

:::example Alkali for saponification
What mass of sodium hydroxide (M = 40) is needed to saponify 178 g of glyceryl tristearate (M = 890)?
@step Fat = 178 ÷ 890 = 0.200 mol, so NaOH = 3 × 0.200 = 0.600 mol.
@answer 0.600 × 40 = 24.00 g
:::

## How soap cleans

A soap particle, such as sodium stearate $\ce{C17H35COO^-Na^+}$, has a long **hydrophobic** (water-hating) hydrocarbon tail and a **hydrophilic** (water-loving) ionic head. The tails dissolve in grease while the heads stay in the water, so the grease is lifted off in tiny droplets surrounded by charged heads, which repel each other and are rinsed away.

:::summary
- Fats and oils are triglycerides of glycerol; oils are more unsaturated than fats.
- Hydrogenation with nickel hardens oils into margarine.
- Saponification: fat + 3NaOH gives glycerol + soap.
- Soap has a hydrophobic tail and a hydrophilic head; soapless detergents do not form scum in hard water.
