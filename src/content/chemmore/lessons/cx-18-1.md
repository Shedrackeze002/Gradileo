Aluminium is the most common metal in the Earth's crust, yet it was once more precious than gold, because nobody could pull it out of its ore cheaply. Iron, by contrast, has been smelted with charcoal for thousands of years. The difference is reactivity: the more reactive a metal, the harder it is to extract.

In this lesson you will describe how aluminium, iron and tin are extracted, with their raw materials, products and by-products, and calculate the mass of metal obtained from an ore or from a charge passed in electrolysis.

## Choosing a method

:::definition Extraction
**Extraction** is obtaining a metal from its ore. Metals above carbon in the reactivity series are extracted by electrolysis of a molten compound; metals below carbon are extracted by reduction with carbon or carbon monoxide.
:::

| Metal | Ore | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminium | bauxite (impure $\ce{Al2O3}$) | electrolysis |
| Iron | haematite ($\ce{Fe2O3}$) | reduction in the blast furnace |
| Tin | cassiterite ($\ce{SnO2}$) | reduction with carbon |

## Aluminium

Bauxite is purified to **alumina**, $\ce{Al2O3}$, which is dissolved in molten **cryolite**. This lowers the working temperature from over 2000 °C to about 950 °C and saves a great deal of energy. In the cell, carbon is used for both electrodes.

$$\text{cathode: } \ce{Al^3+ + 3e- -> Al} \qquad \text{anode: } \ce{2O^2- -> O2 + 4e-}$$

The hot oxygen burns the carbon anodes away as carbon dioxide, so they are replaced regularly. Because the process uses so much electricity, recycling aluminium saves most of the energy.

:::example Aluminium from a charge
A current of 10 A flows for 2 hours. What mass of aluminium forms? (Al = 27; Faraday constant 96 500 C mol⁻¹.)
@step Charge = 10 × 2 × 3600 = 72 000 C, which is 72 000 ÷ 96 500 = 0.746 mol of electrons.
@step Each Al³⁺ needs 3 electrons, so aluminium = 0.746 ÷ 3 = 0.249 mol.
@answer Mass = 0.249 × 27 = 6.72 g
:::

## Iron in the blast furnace

Haematite, coke and limestone are fed in at the top, and hot air is blown in at the bottom.

1. Coke burns: $\ce{C + O2 -> CO2}$, then $\ce{CO2 + C -> 2CO}$.
2. Carbon monoxide reduces the ore: $\ce{Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2}$.
3. Limestone decomposes, $\ce{CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2}$, and the calcium oxide removes sand as **slag**: $\ce{CaO + SiO2 -> CaSiO3}$.

Molten iron and slag are tapped off separately; slag is used for roads and cement. The pig iron contains about 4% carbon and is brittle, so most is turned into steel by blowing oxygen through it.

## Tin

Cassiterite is concentrated and then reduced with coke in a furnace: $\ce{SnO2 + 2C -> Sn + 2CO}$.

## Masses in extraction

To find the mass of metal in an ore, first find the mass of the metal compound, then the fraction of that compound which is metal. In $\ce{Fe2O3}$ (M = 160), the iron is 2 × 56 = 112, so 160 kg of the oxide gives 112 kg of iron.

:::summary
- Aluminium: electrolysis of alumina in molten cryolite; carbon anodes burn away.
- Iron: haematite, coke and limestone in the blast furnace; CO reduces the ore; slag is CaSiO₃.
- Tin: cassiterite reduced with carbon.
- Mass of metal = mass of compound × (mass of metal in formula ÷ molar mass); in electrolysis use charge = current × time.
:::
