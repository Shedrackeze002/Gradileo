Which is better for the planet, a paper bag or a plastic one? The paper bag rots away and the plastic one does not, so the answer seems obvious. But the paper bag took more energy and more water to make, weighs more to deliver, and tears after one trip. To answer fairly you have to follow each bag through its whole life.

In this lesson you will name the four stages of a life cycle assessment and use one to compare products, tell reducing, reusing and recycling apart, and calculate the energy that recycling saves.

## Life cycle assessments

:::definition Life cycle assessment
A **life cycle assessment** (LCA) is a study of the effect a product has on the environment at every stage of its life.
:::

An LCA looks at four stages:

| Stage | For a plastic bottle |
|---|---|
| 1. Extracting and processing the raw materials | Drilling for crude oil and separating it |
| 2. Manufacturing and packaging | Making the plastic and moulding the bottle |
| 3. Use during its lifetime | Being filled, delivered and perhaps refilled |
| 4. Disposal at the end of its life | Landfill, burning or recycling |

At each stage the assessment records the energy and water used, the resources consumed and the waste and pollution produced, and it counts transport at every stage.

:::check cx-lca 1
:::

:::example Adding up a life cycle
The energy used over the life of a glass bottle is: raw materials 4 MJ, manufacture 9 MJ, use 1 MJ, disposal 2 MJ. A plastic bottle: raw materials 3 MJ, manufacture 4 MJ, use 1 MJ, disposal 1 MJ. How much more energy does the glass bottle account for?
@step Glass: $4 + 9 + 1 + 2 = 16\ \text{MJ}$.
@step Plastic: $3 + 4 + 1 + 1 = 9\ \text{MJ}$.
@answer $7\ \text{MJ}$ more
:::

## Comparing for each use

A product that takes more to make can still be the better choice if it is used many times. Divide the total by the number of uses.

:::example One use, or fifty?
A thin plastic bag accounts for 20 g of carbon dioxide and is used once. A cotton bag accounts for 500 g and is used 50 times. Which accounts for less carbon dioxide for each use?
@step Plastic: $20 \div 1 = 20\ \text{g}$ for each use.
@step Cotton: $500 \div 50 = 10\ \text{g}$ for each use.
@answer The cotton bag, as long as it really is used 50 times.
:::

:::note An LCA is not purely objective
Energy, water and waste can be measured. The harm done by a pollutant is much harder to put a number on, so it takes a judgement, and a different person may judge differently. An assessment that looks at only some of the effects can be used to support a conclusion chosen in advance, for example in an advertisement.
:::

:::

## Reduce, reuse, recycle

Metals, glass, building materials and most plastics are made from **finite** resources: ores and crude oil that are being used far faster than they form. Getting them out of the ground takes energy and damages the landscape. Three things cut the damage.

:::definition Recycling
**Recycling** is processing a used material so that it can be made into a new product. **Reusing** is using the same object again without remaking it, and **reducing** is using less in the first place.
:::

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **Reduce** | Drinks cans made with thinner walls |
| **Reuse** | A glass milk bottle washed and refilled |
| **Recycle** | Aluminium cans melted and made into new cans |

Reusing usually saves more energy than recycling, because recycling still needs the material to be collected, sorted and reprocessed: metals and glass are melted, and plastics are melted and remoulded.

:::example Energy saved by recycling
Making 1 tonne of a metal from its ore takes 200 GJ of energy. Making 1 tonne from recycled metal takes 10 GJ. What percentage of the energy does recycling save?
@step Energy saved $= 200 - 10 = 190\ \text{GJ}$.
@step Percentage saved $= \dfrac{190}{200} \times 100$.
@answer 95%
:::

:::check cx-recycling 3
:::

:::summary
- A **life cycle assessment** follows a product through four stages: raw materials, manufacture, use and disposal.
- To compare products, add up every stage, and divide by the number of uses when a product is used more than once.
- Some parts of an LCA are judgements, so an assessment can be selective or biased.
- **Reduce** (use less), **reuse** (use the object again) and **recycle** (remake the material) save finite resources and energy.
- Recycling still needs energy for collecting, sorting and reprocessing, but far less than starting from raw materials.
:::
