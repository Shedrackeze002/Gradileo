The flame of an oxy-acetylene torch is hot enough to cut through steel, and the fuel that makes it is ethyne, the simplest alkyne. Alkynes contain a carbon–carbon triple bond, which makes them even more unsaturated than the alkenes and gives them one reaction that alkenes cannot do.

In this lesson you will name alkynes, describe how ethyne is made and used, predict the addition reactions of alkynes, and tell alkanes, alkenes and terminal alkynes apart.

## Naming alkynes

:::definition Alkyne
An **alkyne** is a hydrocarbon containing a carbon–carbon triple bond. The alkynes form a homologous series with the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₋₂.
:::

## Making and using ethyne

Ethyne is made by adding water to calcium carbide:

$$\ce{CaC2 + 2H2O -> C2H2 + Ca(OH)2}$$

It burns in oxygen with a very hot flame, used for welding and cutting metals, and it is a starting material for plastics such as PVC. In air it burns with a smoky flame, because it has a high proportion of carbon.

:::example Ethyne from calcium carbide
What volume of ethyne, at room temperature and pressure, forms from 6.4 g of calcium carbide (M = 64)? One mole of gas occupies 24 dm³.
@step Amount = 6.4 ÷ 64 = 0.10 mol, which gives 0.10 mol of ethyne.
@answer 0.10 × 24 = 2.40 dm³
:::

## Reactions of alkynes

The triple bond is one σ bond and two π bonds, so an alkyne adds **two** molecules of a reagent, first giving an alkene-type product and then a saturated one.

$$\ce{HC#CH + H2 -> H2C=CH2} \qquad \ce{H2C=CH2 + H2 -> CH3-CH3} \quad \text{(nickel catalyst)}$$

$$\ce{HC#CH + 2Br2 -> CHBr2-CHBr2} \quad \text{(bromine water decolourised)}$$

With one mole of hydrogen chloride, ethyne gives chloroethene, $\ce{H2C=CHCl}$, the monomer of PVC.

## Telling them apart

Alkenes and alkynes both decolourise bromine water, so another test is needed. A **terminal alkyne**, with a hydrogen on a triple-bond carbon, gives a white precipitate with **ammoniacal silver nitrate** and a red precipitate with **ammoniacal copper(I) chloride**. Alkenes, and alkynes such as but-2-yne with no such hydrogen, do not.

| | Alkane | Alkene | Terminal alkyne |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bromine water | no change | decolourised | decolourised |
| Ammoniacal silver nitrate | no change | no change | white precipitate |

:::summary
- Alkynes have a C≡C triple bond and the formula CₙH₂ₙ₋₂; names end in -yne.
- Ethyne is made from calcium carbide and water; it is used in oxy-acetylene welding.
- Alkynes add two molecules of H₂, Br₂ or HBr across the triple bond.
- Only terminal alkynes give precipitates with ammoniacal silver nitrate or copper(I) chloride.
:::
