The smell of pear drops, the soap in your bathroom, the biodiesel in some buses and the aspirin in a first-aid kit all come from the same family of reactions: those of carboxylic acids and the compounds made from them.

In this lesson you will name esters, predict the products of ester hydrolysis, and predict the products of acylation with acyl chlorides and acid anhydrides.

## Esters

An ester forms when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol, with a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst. It is named **alkyl alkanoate**: the part from the alcohol first, then the part from the acid.

$$\ce{CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH <=> CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O}$$

So ethanoic acid and ethanol give **ethyl ethanoate**. Esters are sweet-smelling, so they are used in flavourings and perfumes, and as solvents and plasticisers.

## Hydrolysis of esters

| Conditions | Products | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|
| Heat with dilute acid | carboxylic acid + alcohol | yes |
| Heat with NaOH(aq) | carboxylate salt + alcohol | no, goes to completion |

:::example Hydrolysing methyl propanoate
What forms when methyl propanoate is heated under reflux with sodium hydroxide solution?
@step The “methyl” part came from methanol; the “propanoate” part came from propanoic acid.
@step In alkali, the acid is formed as its sodium salt.
@answer Sodium propanoate and methanol
:::

Fats and oils are esters of **propane-1,2,3-triol** (glycerol). Heating them with sodium hydroxide gives **soap** (sodium salts of long-chain acids) and glycerol. Reacting them with **methanol** and an alkali catalyst gives **biodiesel**, a mixture of methyl esters.

## Acylation

Acyl chlorides, such as ethanoyl chloride $\ce{CH3COCl}$, react with nucleophiles in a **nucleophilic addition–elimination** reaction: the nucleophile adds to the $\delta+$ carbon, then HCl is eliminated.

| Nucleophile | Product with $\ce{CH3COCl}$ |
|---|---|
| water | ethanoic acid + HCl |
| an alcohol, $\ce{ROH}$ | ester $\ce{CH3COOR}$ + HCl |
| ammonia | ethanamide $\ce{CH3CONH2}$ + $\ce{NH4Cl}$ |
| a primary amine, $\ce{RNH2}$ | N-substituted amide $\ce{CH3CONHR}$ |

With ammonia and amines, two moles are needed, because the second one reacts with the HCl.

**Acid anhydrides**, such as ethanoic anhydride $\ce{(CH3CO)2O}$, react in the same way but give a **carboxylic acid** as the by-product instead of HCl. Aspirin is made industrially with ethanoic anhydride because it is cheaper, less corrosive, reacts less violently, and makes no HCl gas.

:::mistake Two products, every time
Acylation always gives two products. With an acyl chloride the second is HCl (or $\ce{NH4Cl}$ with ammonia); with an anhydride it is the carboxylic acid.
:::

:::summary
- Esters are named alkyl alkanoate, the alcohol part first.
- Acid hydrolysis gives acid + alcohol (reversible); alkaline hydrolysis gives the carboxylate salt + alcohol.
- Fats + NaOH give soap and glycerol; fats + methanol give biodiesel.
- Acyl chlorides react by nucleophilic addition–elimination, giving HCl; anhydrides give a carboxylic acid instead.
:::
