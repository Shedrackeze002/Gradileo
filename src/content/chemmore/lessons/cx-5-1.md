Nobody has ever looked at an atom the way you look at a chair. Everything we know about the inside of an atom was worked out from experiments, and each time an experiment gave a result the current picture could not explain, the picture was changed. The same thing happened to the periodic table.

In this lesson you will describe how the model of the atom changed from Dalton to Chadwick, explain the evidence from alpha particle scattering, and explain how Mendeleev arranged the elements and why the modern table follows atomic number.

## Models of the atom

:::definition Scientific model
A **scientific model** is a simplified picture that explains the evidence we have. When new evidence does not fit it, the model is changed or replaced.
:::

| Date | Scientist | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Early 1800s | John Dalton | Atoms are tiny solid spheres that cannot be divided. |
| 1897 | J. J. Thomson | He discovered the **electron**, so atoms can be divided. The **plum pudding model**: a ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it. |
| 1911 | Ernest Rutherford | The **nuclear model**: a tiny, positively charged nucleus holds almost all the mass, with electrons around it and mostly empty space. |
| 1913 | Niels Bohr | Electrons orbit the nucleus at fixed distances, in **shells**. |
| 1932 | James Chadwick | He discovered the **neutron**, which has no charge, in the nucleus. |

## The alpha particle scattering experiment

Rutherford's team fired positively charged alpha particles at a very thin sheet of gold foil and recorded where they went.

| What they saw | What it showed |
|---|---|
| Most alpha particles went straight through. | Most of the atom is empty space. |
| A few were deflected through small angles. | The centre of the atom is positively charged, so it repels the alpha particles. |
| A very few bounced back. | The mass and positive charge are concentrated in a tiny nucleus. |

If the plum pudding model were right, the positive charge would be spread thinly and nothing could turn an alpha particle back. So the plum pudding model was replaced.

:::example Reading the evidence
An alpha particle passes straight through the gold foil without changing direction. What does this tell us about the atom?
@step The particle met nothing that pushed it or stopped it.
@step Most alpha particles did the same.
@answer Most of the atom is empty space.
:::

## The development of the periodic table

:::definition Atomic weight
Before protons were known, elements were ordered by their **atomic weight**, the mass of an atom compared with a hydrogen atom.
:::

Early tables listed the elements strictly in order of atomic weight. This put some elements in groups with elements of very different properties, so the pattern broke down.

In 1869 Dmitri **Mendeleev** fixed this in two ways.

- He **swapped the order** of some pairs of elements, so that each element sat in a group with elements of similar properties. Iodine, for example, went after tellurium although it is lighter.
- He **left gaps** for elements that he thought had not been discovered yet, and predicted their properties. When elements such as gallium and germanium were found, their properties matched his predictions.

## Why atomic number, not atomic weight

Once protons and isotopes were discovered, Mendeleev's swaps made sense.

:::definition Isotopes
**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element, with the same number of protons, that have different numbers of neutrons and so different masses.
:::

An element's atomic weight depends on the mix of its isotopes, so it does not always rise in step with the number of protons. The modern periodic table is in order of **atomic number**, the number of protons, and in that order iodine comes after tellurium naturally.

:::summary
- The model of the atom went from solid spheres (Dalton) to the plum pudding (Thomson), the nuclear model (Rutherford), electron shells (Bohr) and the neutron (Chadwick).
- In alpha particle scattering, most particles passed straight through (empty space) and a very few bounced back (a tiny, dense, positive nucleus).
- A model is changed when new evidence does not fit it.
- Mendeleev swapped some elements to keep similar elements together and left gaps for undiscovered elements, whose properties he predicted.
- Isotopes explain why atomic weight is not a reliable order. The modern table is in order of atomic number.
:::
