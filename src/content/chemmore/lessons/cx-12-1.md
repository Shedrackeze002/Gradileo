Your left and right hands are mirror images, but you cannot lay one exactly on top of the other. Some molecules are “handed” in the same way, and the two hands can behave very differently: one form of a drug may heal while its mirror image does nothing, or does harm.

In this lesson you will find chiral centres, explain what enantiomers are, and explain why some reactions give a racemic mixture.

## Chiral centres and enantiomers

:::definition Chiral centre
A carbon atom bonded to **four different** atoms or groups.
:::

:::definition Enantiomers
Two forms of a molecule that are non-superimposable mirror images. They are optical isomers, a type of stereoisomer.
:::

Butan-2-ol, $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3}$, has a chiral centre: carbon 2 carries H, OH, $\ce{CH3}$ and $\ce{C2H5}$. Propan-2-ol, $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH3}$, does not, because its central carbon has two identical $\ce{CH3}$ groups.

:::example Spotting a chiral centre
Does 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid), $\ce{CH3CH(OH)COOH}$, have a chiral centre?
@step Look at carbon 2. It is bonded to H, OH, $\ce{CH3}$ and COOH.
@step All four groups are different.
@answer Yes: carbon 2 is a chiral centre, so lactic acid has two enantiomers.
:::

:::mistake Look at whole groups, not just the first atom
$\ce{CH3}$ and $\ce{CH2CH3}$ both start with carbon, but they are different groups. A carbon is chiral if the four whole groups are different, even when two of them start with the same atom.

## Optical activity

Enantiomers have the same physical and chemical properties except one: they **rotate plane-polarised light** by the same amount in **opposite directions**. So they are called optically active.

:::definition Racemic mixture (racemate)
An equal mixture of the two enantiomers. Their rotations cancel, so it has no effect on plane-polarised light.
:::

## Why some reactions give a racemate

The C=O group of an aldehyde or ketone is **planar**. When a nucleophile such as the cyanide ion attacks it, it is equally likely to attack from **above or below** the plane. If the product has a chiral centre, the two enantiomers form in equal amounts.

$$\ce{CH3CHO + HCN -> CH3CH(OH)CN}$$

So ethanal gives a racemic mixture of 2-hydroxypropanenitrile. Drugs are often made as a single enantiomer instead, because the other one may be inactive or harmful, and separating a racemate is expensive.

:::summary
- A chiral centre is a carbon with four different groups.
- Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images; they rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions.
- A racemic mixture has equal amounts of each and does not rotate the light.
- Nucleophilic attack on a planar C=O from either side gives a racemate.
:::
