Which phone plan is the better deal? Which taxi should you call? How old is your cousin? Linear equations answer real questions like these. The hard part is usually not solving the equation, but writing it. In this lesson you will follow a plan that turns words into an equation.

## A plan for word problems

:::definition Problem-solving plan
1. **Define** a variable: say exactly what it stands for, with units.
2. **Write** an expression for each quantity in the problem.
3. **Write** an equation that says what is equal.
4. **Solve** the equation.
5. **Verify** the answer in the words of the problem, not only in your equation.
6. **Answer** the question in a sentence, with units.
:::

:::example Two phone plans
Plan A costs \$20 per month plus \$4 per gigabyte (GB) of data. Plan B costs \$35 per month plus \$1.50 per GB. For how many gigabytes do the two plans cost the same?
@step Let $g$ be the number of gigabytes used in a month.
@step Plan A costs $20 + 4g$ dollars. Plan B costs $35 + 1.5g$ dollars.
@step The costs are equal: $20 + 4g = 35 + 1.5g$
@step Subtract $1.5g$ from both sides: $20 + 2.5g = 35$. Subtract $20$: $2.5g = 15$. Divide by $2.5$: $g = 6$.
@step Verify: Plan A costs $20 + 4(6) = 44$ dollars and Plan B costs $35 + 1.5(6) = 44$ dollars. They match.
@answer Both plans cost \$44 when 6 GB of data are used.
:::

## Comparing two options

When two options both have a starting fee and a rate, setting them equal tells you where one option stops being cheaper and the other starts.

:::example Two taxi companies
In one town, a taxi company charges \$3.50 plus \$1.80 per kilometre. Another charges \$5.00 plus \$1.60 per kilometre. For what distance are the fares the same?
@step Let $k$ be the distance in kilometres.
@step The equation is $3.5 + 1.8k = 5 + 1.6k$.
@step Subtract $1.6k$ from both sides: $3.5 + 0.2k = 5$. Subtract $3.5$: $0.2k = 1.5$. Divide by $0.2$: $k = 7.5$.
@step Verify: the first fare is $3.5 + 1.8(7.5) = 17$ dollars and the second is $5 + 1.6(7.5) = 17$ dollars.
@answer The fares are the same, \$17.00, for a trip of $7.5$ km.
:::

:::tip Which option is better?
For trips longer than $7.5$ km, the company with the lower rate per kilometre (\$1.60) is cheaper. For shorter trips, the lower starting fee wins. Try $k = 10$ to see it: \$21.50 compared with \$21.00.
:::

## Ages and perimeters

:::example Ages
Maria is 3 times as old as her brother Kenji. In 6 years, Maria will be twice as old as Kenji. How old is each of them now?
@step Let $k$ be Kenji's age now. Then Maria is $3k$.
@step In 6 years, Kenji will be $k + 6$ and Maria will be $3k + 6$.
@step "Twice as old": $3k + 6 = 2(k + 6)$
@step Expand: $3k + 6 = 2k + 12$. Subtract $2k$ and $6$ from both sides: $k = 6$.
@step Verify: now Kenji is 6 and Maria is 18. In 6 years they will be 12 and 24, and $24 = 2 \times 12$.
@answer Kenji is 6 years old and Maria is 18 years old.
:::

:::example Equal perimeters
A square has sides of $(x + 3)$ cm. An equilateral triangle has sides of $(2x - 1)$ cm. The two shapes have the same perimeter. Find the perimeter.
@step The square has 4 sides and the triangle has 3 equal sides: $4(x + 3) = 3(2x - 1)$
@step Expand: $4x + 12 = 6x - 3$
@step Subtract $4x$ from both sides: $12 = 2x - 3$. Add $3$: $15 = 2x$. Divide by $2$: $x = 7.5$.
@step The square's side is $7.5 + 3 = 10.5$ cm, so its perimeter is $4 \times 10.5 = 42$ cm. The triangle's side is $2(7.5) - 1 = 14$ cm, so its perimeter is $3 \times 14 = 42$ cm.
@answer Each perimeter is $42$ cm.
:::

:::mistake Stopping at x
In the perimeter example, $x = 7.5$ is not the answer to the question. The question asks for the perimeter. Always reread the question after you solve, and answer what it asks, with units.
:::

:::summary
- Define the variable, write expressions, write an equation, solve, verify and answer.
- When two options each have a starting fee and a rate, set the two costs equal to find when they are the same.
- For age problems, add the same number of years to every age.
- Verify in the words of the problem, and answer the question that was asked, with units.
:::
