*Frankenstein* is Mary Shelley’s novel, first published in 1818 and revised in 1831. Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist, discovers how to give life to a body he has built, then runs from it in horror. The creature, abandoned and rejected by everyone it meets, turns to revenge. The novel is Gothic, and it asks who is truly to blame: the creature or the man who made him.

In this lesson you will learn who the characters are and the order of the story, read its key quotations closely, and connect the novel to the science and ideas of Shelley’s time.

## A story within a story

The novel is a **frame narrative**. The explorer Robert Walton writes letters to his sister; inside them, Victor tells Walton his story; and inside Victor’s story, the creature tells his own.

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| Walton’s letters | Walton, sailing to the North Pole, rescues Victor from the ice. |
| Victor’s story | Victor builds the creature and abandons it. His brother William is murdered, and Justine is hanged for it. |
| The creature’s story | The creature learns to speak by watching the De Lacey family, but they drive him away. He demands a female companion. |
| Victor’s story again | Victor destroys the half-made female. The creature kills Clerval, then Elizabeth on her wedding night. |
| Walton’s letters again | Victor dies on Walton’s ship, and the creature says he will end his own life. |

## Key quotations

Victor warns Walton: “Learn from me, if not by my precepts, at least by my example, how dangerous is the acquirement of knowledge”. He had dreamed that “A new species would bless me as its creator and source”. The moment the creature opens its eyes, “the beauty of the dream vanished, and breathless horror and disgust filled my heart.” Victor judges his creation by its looks before it has done anything.

:::example Reading a quotation
The creature tells Victor: *“I ought to be thy Adam, but I am rather the fallen angel”*. What does he mean?
@step In the Bible, Adam is the first man, made and loved by God.
@step The fallen angel is Satan, cast out of heaven.
@answer Like Adam, the creature should have been loved by his maker; instead Victor cast him out, though he had done nothing wrong.
:::

The creature explains: “I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend.” and “I am malicious because I am miserable.” Refused a companion, he threatens: “if I cannot inspire love, I will cause fear”.

## Context and Shelley’s methods

Scientists of Shelley’s time made the muscles of dead animals twitch with electricity, an idea called **galvanism**. The subtitle, *The Modern Prometheus*, compares Victor to the Titan who stole fire from the gods and was punished. Shelley’s mountains, glaciers and Arctic ice are **sublime**, awe-inspiring and terrifying, and their emptiness matches the isolation of creator and creature.

:::mistake The creature is not called Frankenstein
Frankenstein is Victor, the creator. The creature is never given a name, which is part of how he is rejected.
:::

:::summary
- A **frame narrative**: Walton, then Victor, then the creature, at the centre.
- **Dangerous knowledge**: Victor oversteps human limits, like Prometheus.
- **Creator and creation**: Victor abandons what he made; the creature was born good.
- **Isolation, prejudice and revenge**: rejected for his looks, the creature turns to violence.
:::
