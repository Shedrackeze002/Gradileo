*Julius Caesar* is a Shakespeare tragedy first performed in 1599. Caesar has become so powerful that some senators fear he wants to be crowned king. Cassius draws the honourable Brutus into a plot, and on the Ides of March they stab Caesar to death in the Senate. At the funeral, Mark Antony’s speech turns the people against them, and civil war follows, ending in the conspirators’ deaths at Philippi.

In this lesson you will learn who the characters are and the order of the play, read its key speeches closely, and connect it to the politics of Shakespeare’s England.

## The play in five acts

| Act | What happens |
|---|---|
| Act 1 | A soothsayer warns Caesar to beware the Ides of March; Cassius begins to win Brutus over; a storm full of omens. |
| Act 2 | Brutus decides Caesar must die; Calphurnia dreams of his murder, but Caesar goes to the Senate. |
| Act 3 | The conspirators kill Caesar; Brutus and Antony speak at the funeral; the mob riots and kills Cinna the poet. |
| Act 4 | Brutus and Cassius quarrel; Caesar’s ghost appears to Brutus. |
| Act 5 | At Philippi, Cassius and then Brutus take their own lives; Antony calls Brutus the noblest Roman of them all. |

## Key speeches

Cassius stirs Brutus’s resentment: Caesar “doth bestride the narrow world / Like a Colossus”, and “The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, / But in ourselves, that we are underlings.” Caesar sees the danger: “Yond Cassius has a lean and hungry look; / He thinks too much: such men are dangerous.”

Brutus decides to kill Caesar for what he might become, and to “think him as a serpent’s egg / Which hatch’d, would, as his kind grow mischievous; / And kill him in the shell.” Moments before he dies, Caesar declares, “I am constant as the northern star”.

:::example Reading a speech
At the funeral Antony repeats: *“But Brutus says he was ambitious, / And Brutus is an honourable man.”* How does he persuade the crowd?
@step Each time, Antony first lists something good that Caesar did for Rome.
@step Then he repeats that Brutus called Caesar ambitious, and that Brutus is honourable.
@answer The repetition makes “honourable” sound more and more false, so the crowd turns against Brutus without Antony ever attacking him directly.
:::

Brutus had told the crowd: “Not that I loved Caesar less, but that I loved Rome more.” Antony shows them the wound Brutus made: “This was the most unkindest cut of all”. When the crowd rushes off to riot, he says: “Mischief, thou art afoot, / Take thou what course thou wilt!”

## Context and Shakespeare’s methods

In 1599 Elizabeth I was old and had no heir, and people feared civil war when she died, as Rome fell into war after Caesar. Shakespeare took the story from Plutarch’s *Lives*, translated by Thomas North. The play is a **tragedy**: Brutus is a noble man brought down by his idealism and trust. The storm and omens suggest that nature itself is disturbed by the plot. Brutus speaks to the crowd in calm prose; Antony speaks in verse and moves them with emotion, irony, Caesar’s wounds and his will.

:::mistake Caesar is not the play’s main tragic hero
Caesar dies in Act 3. The play follows Brutus, the man who killed his friend for Rome, to his own death at Philippi.
:::

:::summary
- Before and after the murder: the **conspiracy** (Acts 1 and 2), the **killing and funeral** (Act 3), **civil war** (Acts 4 and 5).
- **Power and ambition**: fear of what Caesar might become.
- **Persuasion and rhetoric**: Cassius persuades Brutus; Antony’s irony turns Rome.
- **Honour, loyalty and betrayal**: Brutus, “the noblest Roman of them all”, betrays his friend for his country.
:::
