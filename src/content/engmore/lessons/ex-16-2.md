American literature grew alongside the nation. Its early writers argued for independence, its Romantics looked inward and to nature, and after the Civil War its Realists set out to show American life as it was. Knowing the periods helps us read each work in its time, and comparing works shows how ideas such as independence and freedom were taken up, questioned and renewed.

In this lesson you will place foundational American works in their periods and compare how texts of one or several periods treat similar themes.

## The periods

| Period | Roughly | Writers | Concerns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puritan and Colonial | 1600s to mid-1700s | Anne Bradstreet, Jonathan Edwards | faith, duty, a new start |
| Revolutionary | about 1750 to 1800 | Benjamin Franklin, Thomas Paine, Thomas Jefferson | reason, rights, independence |
| Romantic, including Transcendentalism | about 1800 to 1865 | Poe, Hawthorne, Melville; Emerson, Thoreau | imagination, the individual, nature |
| Realism and Naturalism | about 1865 to 1910 | Mark Twain, Henry James; Stephen Crane | everyday life, speech, forces beyond control |
| Modernism and the Harlem Renaissance | about 1910 to 1945 | Fitzgerald, Hemingway; Langston Hughes, Zora Neale Hurston | new forms, a changed world, Black art and voice |

## Reading the periods in their words

Thomas Paine wrote the first of his *Crisis* papers in December 1776, when the Revolution was going badly [1]:

> THESE are the times that try men’s souls. The summer soldier and the sunshine patriot will, in this crisis, shrink from the service of their country.

Sixty-five years later, Ralph Waldo Emerson urged a different independence in “Self-Reliance” [2]:

> Trust thyself: every heart vibrates to that iron string.

And Henry David Thoreau explained why he went to live alone by Walden Pond [3]:

> I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach, and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived.

:::example Comparing two periods
What theme do Paine and Emerson share, and how does each treat it?
@step Paine writes in wartime, calling on citizens to stand firm for their country.
@step Emerson writes in peacetime, calling on each person to trust their own mind.
@answer Both prize independence: Paine the independence of a nation, Emerson the independence of the individual.
:::

Texts of the same period can also disagree or see a theme from very different positions. Frederick Douglass published his *Narrative* in 1845, nine years before *Walden*: both writers prize freedom, but Douglass had to escape slavery to win the freedom Thoreau could choose how to use.

:::summary
- The main periods run **Puritan**, **Revolutionary**, **Romantic** (with **Transcendentalism**), **Realist** and **Naturalist**, then **Modernist**.
- Place a work by its date and its concerns.
- Themes such as **independence**, **freedom** and a **new start** recur across periods.
- Compare how each writer’s time and situation shape a shared theme.
:::
