A spelling mistake rarely stops a reader understanding you, but it does make them trust you a little less. In an exam, accurate spelling earns marks of its own, and most mistakes come from a small number of words and rules that can be learned.

In this lesson you will choose the right homophone for a sentence, spell commonly misspelt words, and apply the rules for adding suffixes and making plurals.

## Homophones

:::definition Homophones
**Homophones** are words that sound the same but are spelt differently and mean different things, such as *there*, *their* and *they’re*.
:::

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *there* | a place | Put it over there. |
| *their* | belonging to them | They lost their keys. |
| *they’re* | they are | They’re late. |
| *its* | belonging to it | The dog wagged its tail. |
| *it’s* | it is, or it has | It’s raining. |
| *whose* | belonging to whom | Whose coat is this? |
| *who’s* | who is | Who’s coming? |

:::note Test the short form
If you are unsure whether to write *it’s*, *they’re*, *you’re* or *who’s*, say the full form: *it is*, *they are*, *you are*, *who is*. If it makes sense, use the apostrophe.
:::

## Noun or verb?

Some pairs differ by whether the word is a noun or a verb.

| Noun | Verb |
|---|---|
| *effect* (a result) | *affect* (to influence) |
| *practice* | *practise* |
| *advice* | *advise* |
| *licence* | *license* |

There are also pairs that are simply different words: *principal* (a head) and *principle* (a rule); *stationary* (not moving) and *stationery* (paper and pens); *compliment* (praise) and *complement* (something that completes).

:::example Practice or practise?
Which word fills the gap? *“You need to ___ your lines before the play.”*
@step The gap needs a verb: something you do.
@step In British English the verb is spelt with an s.
@answer practise
:::

## Commonly misspelt words

Some words catch out almost everyone. A memory trick helps.

| Word | Trick |
|---|---|
| *definitely* | It contains *finite*. |
| *separate* | There is *a rat* in sep-a-rat-e. |
| *necessary* | One collar (c) and two sleeves (ss). |
| *environment* | There is *iron* in env-iron-ment. |
| *receive* | *i* before *e*, except after *c*. |
| *accommodation* | Big enough for two cs and two ms. |

## Suffixes and plurals

:::definition Suffix
A **suffix** is a group of letters added to the end of a word to make a new word: *-ing*, *-ed*, *-ful*, *-ness*.
:::

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Short vowel + one consonant: double the consonant | *hop*, *hopping*; *begin*, *beginning* |
| Silent *e*: drop it before a vowel suffix | *hope*, *hoping*; *make*, *making* |
| Consonant + *y*: change *y* to *i* | *carry*, *carried*; *happy*, *happiness*; *baby*, *babies* |
| Vowel + *y*: keep the *y* | *day*, *days*; *play*, *played* |
| Many words in *-f* or *-fe*: change to *-ves* | *knife*, *knives*; *half*, *halves* |
| Some words in *-o*: add *-es* | *tomato*, *tomatoes*; *potato*, *potatoes* |

:::summary
- **Homophones** sound alike but differ in spelling and meaning; test a short form by saying it in full.
- Some pairs split into noun and verb: *effect* and *affect*, *practice* and *practise*, *advice* and *advise*.
- Learn the commonly misspelt words with memory tricks.
- Apply the suffix rules: double the consonant, drop the silent *e*, change *y* to *i*, and *-f* to *-ves*.
:::
