Words are connected to each other in patterns. A petal is part of a flower, a drought can cause a famine, and an oak is a kind of tree. Seeing these patterns helps you understand new words and remember old ones, and it is the key to analogies, a kind of puzzle that tests how well you see relationships.

In this lesson you will name the relationship between two words and complete analogies.

## Word relationships

| Relationship | Example | A sentence that shows it |
|---|---|---|
| **Part and whole** | petal, flower | A petal is part of a flower. |
| **Cause and effect** | drought, famine | A drought can cause a famine. |
| **Item and category** | oak, tree | An oak is a kind of tree. |
| **Synonyms** | brave, courageous | Brave means nearly the same as courageous. |
| **Antonyms** | ancient, modern | Ancient is the opposite of modern. |
| **Degree** | warm, hot | Hot is a stronger degree of warm. |
| **Tool and use** | scissors, cut | Scissors are used to cut. |
| **Maker and thing made** | author, novel | An author creates a novel. |

## Analogies

:::definition Analogy
An **analogy** compares two pairs of words that share the same relationship. It is often written *petal : flower :: page : book* and read “petal is to flower as page is to book”.
:::

:::example Completing an analogy
Complete the analogy: *rain is to puddle as fire is to ___*
@step Make a sentence for the first pair: rain leaves a puddle behind, so rain causes a puddle.
@step Use the same sentence for the second pair: fire leaves ___ behind.
@step Fire leaves ash, so the relationship is cause and effect.
@answer ash
:::

:::mistake Match the relationship, not the topic
In *author : novel*, the answer is not *reader : library*, even though both are about books. The relationship is maker and thing made, so the answer is *composer : symphony*.

:::summary
- Words are related as **part and whole**, **cause and effect**, **item and category**, **synonyms**, **antonyms**, **degree**, **tool and use**, and **maker and thing made**.
- To solve an **analogy**, name the relationship of the first pair in a sentence, then test each choice with the same sentence.
:::
