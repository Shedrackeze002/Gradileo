We often understand a character best when we see them beside someone different. Comparing two characters, two settings or two events shows what is special about each, and it often shows how a character changes and what the story is really about.

In this lesson you will compare two characters in a story, compare settings and events, and compare how stories in different settings treat a similar theme.

## Comparing two characters

:::definition Compare and contrast
To **compare** is to find how two things are alike; to **contrast** is to find how they differ. A good comparison does both, and backs each point with a detail from the text.
:::

Read this story, written for this lesson.

> Priya had planned every minute of the camping trip. Her list said *swim at ten, hike at two*, and she checked it twice before breakfast. Her cousin Leo had not packed a list, or a toothbrush. He spent the morning lying on the dock, watching dragonflies.
>
> At two o’clock the sky turned the colour of a bruise. Priya wanted to start the hike anyway; the list said so. Leo pointed at the clouds. “The lake will still be here tomorrow,” he said. They spent the afternoon in the tent, and Priya, to her own surprise, enjoyed the rain drumming on the roof.

:::example Comparing Priya and Leo
How are Priya and Leo different at the start, and how is Priya different at the end?
@step At the start, Priya’s list and her checking it twice show a planner; Leo’s missing list and toothbrush show someone who takes things as they come.
@step At the end, Priya enjoys an afternoon that broke her plan, *to her own surprise*.
@answer Priya begins as a careful planner and Leo as a relaxed boy, and by the end Priya has learned to enjoy what she did not plan.
:::

Writers often place contrasting characters side by side because each makes the other clearer, and their meeting can set up the theme, here that plans sometimes have to bend.

## Comparing settings and events

A change of setting, such as the storm in the story above, can drive the main event. When two events in a story are alike but turn out differently, such as two races a girl loses, the difference shows how a character has changed.

:::tip Signal words
| To show a likeness | To show a difference |
|---|---|
| both, also, likewise, in the same way | whereas, however, but, on the other hand |
:::

We can also compare whole stories. Two folk tales from different countries may both tell of a small, clever animal tricking a big one: the settings differ, but the theme, that wit can defeat strength, is the same.

:::summary
- A comparison names **likenesses** and **differences**, each backed by a detail from the text.
- Contrasting characters make each other clearer and often reveal the theme.
- A change in **setting** can drive the plot; a change between two similar **events** shows how a character has changed.
- Stories in different settings and cultures can share a **theme**.
:::
