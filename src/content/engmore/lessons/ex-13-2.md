Reading aloud well lets listeners hear a story the way the writer meant it, and speaking clearly lets an audience follow what you have to say. Both depend on the same habits: reading the punctuation, matching your voice to the mood, and keeping your audience in mind.

In this lesson you will read a text aloud with accuracy, pace and expression, and give a spoken account that is clear, well ordered and suited to its audience.

## Reading aloud with expression

:::definition Fluency
A **fluent** reader reads accurately, at a pace that suits the text, and with **expression**: the changes of voice that bring out the meaning.
:::

Punctuation tells you how to phrase a sentence:

| Mark | What your voice does |
|---|---|
| comma | a short pause, then on with the same sentence |
| full stop | a longer pause, with the voice falling |
| question mark | the voice often rises |
| exclamation mark | more force or urgency |

Words such as *shouted*, *whispered* and *sighed* tell you how a line of dialogue should sound, and the mood of a scene tells you the pace: slower for a tense, quiet moment, livelier for an exciting one.

:::example Reading a line aloud
How should you read this line? *“Stop!” shouted Ana. “The bridge is out!”*
@step The word *shouted* and the two exclamation marks call for force.
@step Ana is warning someone of danger, so the tone is urgent.
@answer Loudly and urgently, as if warning someone of danger.
:::

## Speaking clearly to an audience

When you give a spoken report or tell about an experience, put your ideas in a logical order and choose details that help listeners picture what you mean. Look up at your audience, speak loudly enough for the back row and pronounce your words clearly.

:::definition Adapting speech
We **adapt** our speech to the context. Formal occasions, such as presenting to judges or parents, call for formal English: a polite greeting, complete sentences and no fillers such as *um*, *like* and *stuff*.

:::summary
- Read aloud **accurately**, at a suitable **pace** and with **expression**, guided by punctuation and the mood of the text.
- Reread when a sentence stops making sense.
- Speak with eye contact, a clear voice and relevant, well-ordered details.
- **Adapt** your speech to the audience, using formal English for formal occasions.
:::
