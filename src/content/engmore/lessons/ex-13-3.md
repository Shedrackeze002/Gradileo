A presentation has two parts that must work together: what you say, and what your audience sees and hears while you say it. A clear plan gives the talk its shape, and well-chosen visuals and media make its key points easier to understand and remember.

In this lesson you will order a presentation logically, keep the details that support your point, and choose visuals and multimedia that clarify it.

## Planning the talk

Most reports follow a simple shape: an **introduction** that tells the audience what the talk is about, the **main points** in a logical order, and a **conclusion** that sums up.

:::definition Relevant detail
A **relevant** detail supports the main idea of the talk. A detail can be true and interesting and still be irrelevant, if it does not help the audience understand your point.
:::

:::example Choosing a relevant detail
Your talk is about why bees matter to farmers. Which detail belongs in it: *bees have six legs*, or *bees pollinate many of the crops that farmers grow*?
@step The main idea is the link between bees and farming.
@step Only the second detail supports that link.
@answer Bees pollinate many of the crops that farmers grow.
:::

Support every claim with evidence, such as survey results or a fact from a reliable source, and if time runs short, give your most important point and sum up the rest briefly.

## Visuals and multimedia

:::definition Multimedia
**Multimedia** means combining more than one medium: images, graphs, audio and video alongside your spoken words.
:::

Choose each visual for what it shows best:

| To show | Use |
|---|---|
| change over time | a line graph |
| how parts make up a whole | a pie chart or a labelled diagram |
| what something looks or sounds like | a photo, an audio clip or a short video |
| the steps of a process | a numbered diagram or a short clip of the step |

:::mistake The slide that does the talking
A slide crowded with paragraphs makes the audience read instead of listening. Keep slides to key words and visuals, and do the explaining yourself.
:::

Keep clips short and tie each one to your point, and credit the source of every image, chart or clip you borrow, just as you would cite a source in writing.

:::summary
- Plan an **introduction**, **main points** in a logical order and a **conclusion**.
- Keep only **relevant** details, and support claims with evidence.
- Choose **visuals and media** that clarify a point: graphs for change, clips for sound and motion.
- Keep slides short, explain them yourself, and credit your sources.
:::
