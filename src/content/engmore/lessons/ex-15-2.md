Writing published online is never quite finished: readers respond, facts change, and the writer can return to improve it. Presentations, too, now rely on digital media that can make evidence clearer or, used carelessly, bury it.

In this lesson you will publish and update writing online, link it to other information, write with others in a shared document, and choose digital media that strengthens a presentation.

## Publishing and updating writing

:::definition Digital publishing
**Digital publishing** means producing writing in a form readers reach online, where it can link to sources, be shared and be updated after it is released.
:::

Good online writing links its key claims to reliable sources, such as a court’s own opinion or an official report, so readers can check them. When readers send corrections, the writer checks them and **updates** the piece, adding a *last updated* date or a corrections note so readers know what changed.

:::example Responding to a reader’s correction
Your class guide to local history says the town hall was built in 1910. A reader writes that the town’s records give 1912. What should you do?
@step Check the reader’s source against your own.
@step If the records are reliable, correct the date in the published guide.
@step Add a note so that readers know the guide has changed.
@answer Verify the date, update the guide to 1912 if the records confirm it, and add a dated corrections note.
:::

## Digital media in presentations

Choose each digital element for what it adds to the audience’s understanding of your findings, reasoning and evidence.

| To show | A strategic choice |
|---|---|
| a trend over many years | an interactive chart that viewers can explore |
| a process with stages | an animated flowchart, one stage at a time |
| a voice or a moment | a short clip that illustrates the main claim |

:::mistake A chart that misleads
A chart whose vertical axis starts at 95 instead of 0 makes a small change look enormous. Present evidence honestly, so that the media supports your reasoning instead of distorting it.
:::

Make media accessible, with captions on video and colours that are easy to read, and adapt it to how the audience will use it: slides that a panel will read later need brief labels that make sense without your talk.

:::summary
- Online writing can **link** to sources and be **updated**; mark what changed.
- Coordinate shared writing with suggestions, comments and version history, and credit what you borrow.
- Use digital media **strategically**, to clarify findings and evidence.
- Keep media **accessible** and **honest**, and adapt it to the audience.
:::
