Pronouns save us from repeating nouns, but only if the reader always knows who or what each one means. This lesson looks at three ways pronouns go wrong, and at the pronouns that end in *-self*.

In this lesson you will use intensive pronouns, tell them from reflexive pronouns, keep pronouns consistent in number and person, and fix pronouns that are vague.

## Intensive and reflexive pronouns

The pronouns that end in *-self* or *-selves* are *myself*, *yourself*, *himself*, *herself*, *itself*, *ourselves*, *yourselves* and *themselves*. They have two jobs.

:::definition Intensive pronoun
An **intensive pronoun** adds emphasis to a noun or pronoun just before it. The sentence still makes sense without it: *The mayor **herself** opened the park.*
:::

:::definition Reflexive pronoun
A **reflexive pronoun** shows that the subject does something to itself, and the sentence needs it: *Hana taught **herself** to knit.*
:::

:::

:::mistake Don’t use -self instead of I or me
A *-self* pronoun must point back to someone already named in the sentence. Write *Send the photos to Esi and **me***, not *to Esi and myself*, and *Omar and **I** will bring the drinks*, not *Myself and Omar*. *Hisself* and *theirselves* are not standard: use *himself* and *themselves*.
:::

## Keeping pronouns consistent

A pronoun should match its noun in **number** (one or more than one) and stay in the same **person** (*I* and *we*; *you*; *he*, *she*, *it* and *they*) through a sentence.

| Shift | Fixed |
|---|---|
| *When **we** reached the top, **you** could see the town.* | *When we reached the top, **we** could see the town.* |
| *If **students** study hard, **you** will do well.* | *If students study hard, **they** will do well.* |
| ***Tomatoes** grow best in sun, so plant **it** in a bright spot.* | *Tomatoes grow best in sun, so plant **them** in a bright spot.* |

## Vague pronouns

:::definition Vague pronoun
A **vague pronoun** could point to more than one noun, or to none at all, so the reader cannot be sure what it means.
:::

- *Priya told Ines that **she** had won.* Who won?
- *I put the vase on the shelf, and then **it** broke.* What broke?
- ***They** say the river will flood.* Who says so?

Fix a vague pronoun by naming the noun, or by rewriting the sentence: *Priya told Ines, “You have won.”* *Forecasters say the river will flood.*

:::example Fixing a vague pronoun
Rewrite the sentence so it is clear: *“When Arjun met his brother at the station, he was tired.”*
@step Ask who *he* means. It could be Arjun or his brother.
@step Decide who was tired (say, Arjun) and put his name where the reader needs it.
@answer Arjun was tired when he met his brother at the station.
:::

:::summary
- An **intensive pronoun** adds emphasis and can be removed; a **reflexive pronoun** is needed for the sentence to make sense.
- A *-self* pronoun never replaces *I* or *me*.
- Keep pronouns consistent in **number** and **person**.
- Rewrite a **vague pronoun** so the reader knows exactly who or what it means.
:::
