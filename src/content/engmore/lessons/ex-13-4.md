Most writing today is typed, shared and published on screens. Technology can help a writer draft, revise and work with others, and it can put finished writing in front of real readers, but it also asks the writer to check its work and to publish responsibly.

In this lesson you will use writing tools wisely, work with a partner in a shared document, and publish writing online with its sources linked and private details kept safe.

## Writing tools

:::definition Spell checker
A **spell checker** marks words that are not in its dictionary. It cannot tell whether a real word is the right one, so it passes *their* for *they’re* and *for* for *four*.
:::

:::example Proofreading after the checker
A spell checker finds no errors in *“Their going to the park at for o’clock.”* Correct the sentence.
@step Every word is a real word, so the checker passes them all.
@step *Their* should be *They’re* (they are), and *for* should be *four*.
@answer They’re going to the park at four o’clock.
:::

### Working in a shared document

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| suggesting, or track changes | proposes an edit that your partner can accept or reject |
| comment | leaves a note or question about part of the text |
| version history | keeps earlier drafts, so you can go back |

Use suggestions and comments rather than deleting a partner’s words, and save a version before a big revision. Typing fluently matters too: the standards expect a Grade 5 writer to type at least two pages in one sitting, and a Grade 6 writer three.

## Publishing online

When you publish on a class website or blog, link each fact to the page it came from and list your sources, so readers can check them. Headings and links can show how your ideas connect, and because published writing can be updated, you can revise it when readers point out a problem.

:::mistake Sharing too much
Never publish private details such as your home address or phone number. A first name or a nickname is enough.
:::

Real readers see what you publish, so keep your writing and your replies to comments respectful and on topic.

:::summary
- A **spell checker** misses real words used wrongly, so always proofread.
- In a shared document, **suggest** and **comment** rather than delete, and keep **versions**.
- When you publish, **link and cite** your sources and use headings and links to show how ideas connect.
- Keep private details offline, stay respectful, and **update** your work after feedback.
:::
