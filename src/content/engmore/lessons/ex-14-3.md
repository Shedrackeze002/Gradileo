A story can be told in words alone, in words and pictures, on a stage or on a screen. Each way of telling adds something of its own, so comparing them shows what the pictures, the actors or the camera bring to the meaning.

In this lesson you will explain how pictures add to a story, and compare reading a text with hearing, watching or seeing it performed.

## Stories in pictures

In a picture book or a **graphic novel**, the images do more than decorate the words. They can show a feeling a character hides, create a mood, and control how fast we read.

| Choice | Its effect |
|---|---|
| a picture that contradicts the words | reveals what a character really feels |
| a low angle, looking up | makes a figure look huge or threatening |
| a wide, empty space | makes a figure feel small and alone |
| small panels close together | speeds up the reading, for action |
| a large panel with no words | slows the reader down, for a key moment |

:::example Reading a panel
In a graphic novel, a character says “I’m fine,” but the picture shows her clenching her fists. What does the picture add?
@step The words say one thing and the picture shows another.
@step Clenched fists suggest anger or tension that she is hiding.
@answer It shows she is not really fine, which the words alone hide.
:::

## On stage and on screen

When a play is performed, the actors decide how each line sounds. The same words, *“I suppose you’re right,”* can sound defeated with a sigh or teasing with a smile. Hearing a poem read aloud brings out its rhythm and pauses, and watching a speech being given shows the speaker’s tone, gestures and the audience’s reaction, which the printed text cannot.

A **film** tells a story with images, lighting, sound and camera movement in place of a narrator’s words. Directors also adapt: they cut scenes and characters to fit the running time, and the camera can show a character’s face that the book’s narrator never describes, which can change our point of view.

:::tip Questions for comparing versions
- What does the performance or film show that the text only tells, or leaves out?
- What did the director or actor change, and why?
- How does the change affect the mood, the point of view or the meaning?

:::summary
- Pictures add **meaning**, **mood** and **pace** through angle, space, colour and panel size.
- A performance adds **voice** and **gesture**; the same line can mean different things.
- A film uses **images and sound** in place of a narrator, and directors **adapt** the text for the screen.
- Compare versions by asking what each shows, what was changed, and why.
:::
