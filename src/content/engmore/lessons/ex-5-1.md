*A Christmas Carol* is a short novel, a novella, that Charles Dickens published in December 1843. It tells how Ebenezer Scrooge, a rich and miserly old man, is visited on Christmas Eve by the ghost of his dead partner and then by three spirits, and how he wakes on Christmas morning a changed man. It is a ghost story, but it is also an argument: Dickens wanted his comfortable readers to look at the poor and feel responsible for them.

In this lesson you will learn who the characters are and the order of the story, read its key quotations closely, and connect the novella to the England of the 1840s.

## The story in five staves

Dickens called his chapters **staves**, the verses of a song, because the book is a “carol”.

| Stave | What happens |
|---|---|
| Stave 1 | Scrooge refuses his nephew’s invitation and two gentlemen collecting for the poor. Marley’s ghost appears in chains and warns him that three spirits will come. |
| Stave 2 | The Ghost of Christmas Past shows him his lonely childhood, Fezziwig’s party, and Belle ending their engagement. |
| Stave 3 | The Ghost of Christmas Present shows him the Cratchits’ Christmas dinner, Tiny Tim, Fred’s party, and two children called Ignorance and Want. |
| Stave 4 | The Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come shows him a death nobody mourns, and his own neglected grave. |
| Stave 5 | Scrooge wakes, sends a turkey to the Cratchits, goes to Fred’s dinner and raises Bob’s salary. |

## The characters

**Scrooge** is at the centre of every stave. Around him, Dickens sets characters who show him what he has lost or ignored: **Bob Cratchit**, his underpaid clerk; **Tiny Tim**, Bob’s sick son; **Fred**, his cheerful nephew; **Belle**, the woman he gave up for money; and **Fezziwig**, the generous employer of his youth. **Marley** is a warning of what Scrooge will become.

:::example Putting events in order
Which comes first: Scrooge watching Belle end their engagement, or Scrooge watching the Cratchits’ dinner?
@step Belle belongs to Scrooge’s past, so she appears with the Ghost of Christmas Past in Stave 2.
@step The Cratchits’ dinner is happening this Christmas, so it is shown by the Ghost of Christmas Present in Stave 3.
@answer Belle comes first: each spirit moves Scrooge forward in time.
:::

## Key quotations

The narrator introduces Scrooge with a list of grasping verbs:

> “a squeezing, wrenching, grasping, scraping, clutching, covetous, old sinner!”

The list piles up to show that his whole life is spent getting and holding on to money. A moment later he is “solitary as an oyster”, shut inside a hard shell.

When the gentlemen ask him to give to the poor, Scrooge says that if the poor would rather die, “they had better do it, and decrease the surplus population.” He talks of people as numbers. In Stave 3 the Ghost of Christmas Present throws the same words back at him about Tiny Tim, and Scrooge “hung his head”.

:::example Reading a quotation
Marley’s ghost says: *“Mankind was my business.”* What does he mean?
@step Scrooge has just praised Marley as “a good man of business”.
@step Marley replies that his real business, his duty, was other people.
@answer Marley says, too late, that caring for others mattered more than trade; he turns Scrooge’s idea of business upside down.
:::

By Stave 4 Scrooge promises: “I will honour Christmas in my heart, and try to keep it all the year.” In Stave 5 he is “as light as a feather”, and the narrator tells us he became “as good a friend, as good a master, and as good a man, as the good old city knew”.

## Context and Dickens’s methods

In the 1840s many working people went hungry. Under the Poor Law of 1834, the destitute were sent to **workhouses**, where families were split up and life was made deliberately harsh. When Scrooge asks “Are there no prisons?” and mentions the workhouses, he repeats what many comfortable people thought. Dickens wanted readers to see the poor as people, and the spirits of Ignorance and Want are his warning to society.

:::mistake Scrooge is not simply a villain
The novella is about change. Stave 2 shows how a lonely boy became a cold man, so Scrooge’s redemption is believable, and the reader is meant to see that anyone can change.
:::

:::summary
- Five **staves**: Marley, then the Ghosts of Christmas Past, Present and Yet to Come, then Christmas morning.
- Scrooge’s greed and **isolation** are shown through lists, similes and cold imagery; his **redemption** through joyful, childlike language.
- **Poverty and social responsibility**: Scrooge’s words about the “surplus population” are turned back on him.
- **Context**: the hunger of the 1840s and the workhouses; Dickens wrote to make readers act.
:::
