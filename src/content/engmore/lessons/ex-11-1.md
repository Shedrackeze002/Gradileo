Some of the smallest words in English do some of the most important work. Conjunctions join ideas and show how they relate, prepositions show where and when things happen, and interjections let a speaker burst out with a feeling.

In this lesson you will find and choose conjunctions, use the pairs called correlative conjunctions, and tell prepositions and interjections from the other parts of speech.

## Conjunctions

:::definition Conjunction
A **conjunction** is a word that joins words, phrases or clauses and shows how the ideas relate: *and*, *but*, *or*, *so*, *because*, *although*, *if*, *when*.
:::

**Coordinating conjunctions** join two ideas of equal weight. There are seven, and the word FANBOYS helps you remember them: *for*, *and*, *nor*, *but*, *or*, *yet*, *so*.

**Subordinating conjunctions** join a less important idea (a dependent clause) to a main one, and show how the two connect.

| The ideas show | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast | *but*, *yet*, *although*, *while* | *Although it was late, Mei kept drawing.* |
| Result | *so* | *The bread was warm, so we ate it.* |
| Reason | *because*, *since* | *We stayed in because it was raining.* |
| Condition | *if*, *unless* | *Bring a coat if you go out.* |
| Time | *when*, *until*, *after*, *before* | *Wait until everyone arrives.* |

## Correlative conjunctions

:::definition Correlative conjunctions
**Correlative conjunctions** are pairs that work together: *either … or*, *neither … nor*, *both … and*, *not only … but also*, *whether … or*.
:::

- *You can have **either** soup **or** salad.*
- ***Neither** the bus **nor** the train goes to the beach.*
- *The film was **both** funny **and** moving.*
- *Ravi **not only** won the race **but also** broke the record.*

:::mistake Keep the pairs together
*Either* goes with *or*, and *neither* goes with *nor*. Do not write *neither … or* or *either … nor*.
:::

## Prepositions and interjections

:::definition Preposition
A **preposition** links a noun or pronoun to the rest of the sentence, often to show where or when: *in*, *on*, *under*, *over*, *between*, *during*, *after*, *before*. It begins a **prepositional phrase**: *under the bed*, *during the holiday*.
:::

:::definition Interjection
An **interjection** is a word or short phrase that shows a sudden feeling: *Wow!*, *Ouch!*, *Oh no*, *Hey*. A strong one takes an exclamation mark; a mild one takes a comma.
:::

## Preposition or conjunction?

Some words, such as *after*, *before*, *since* and *until*, can be either. Look at what follows them.

- A **preposition** is followed by a noun phrase: *We went for a walk **after** lunch.*
- A **conjunction** is followed by a clause with its own verb: *We went for a walk **after** the rain stopped.*

:::check ex-prepositions-interjections 3
:::

:::summary
- **Conjunctions** join ideas: coordinating ones (FANBOYS) join equal ideas, and subordinating ones show reason, condition, time or contrast.
- **Correlative conjunctions** come in fixed pairs: *either … or*, *neither … nor*, *both … and*, *not only … but also*.
- **Prepositions** begin phrases that show where or when; **interjections** show a sudden feeling.
- A word such as *after* is a preposition before a noun phrase and a conjunction before a clause.
:::
