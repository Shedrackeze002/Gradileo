A good reader does not need a dictionary for every unfamiliar word, because the context often gives the meaning. But when the context is not enough, or when you need to be sure, a dictionary, a glossary or a thesaurus will tell you more than any guess can.

In this lesson you will read a dictionary entry for a word’s pronunciation, part of speech, meanings and history, choose the meaning that fits a sentence, use guide words and glossaries, choose a synonym from a thesaurus, and check a guess about a word.

## Reading a dictionary entry

> **current** (kûr′ənt) *adjective* **1.** happening now; of the present time. *noun* **2.** a flow of water or air in one direction. **3.** a flow of electricity. [Latin *currere*, to run]

| Part of the entry | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **current** | the headword, spelt correctly and divided into syllables in some dictionaries |
| (kûr′ənt) | the **pronunciation**; the mark ′ follows the stressed syllable |
| *adjective*, *noun* | the **part of speech** of each group of meanings |
| **1.**, **2.**, **3.** | the **senses**, or meanings, numbered |
| [Latin *currere*, to run] | the **etymology**: where the word came from |

## Choosing the right sense

Many words have several meanings. Try each sense in the sentence and keep the one that makes sense.

:::example Which meaning?
*“The swimmers were pulled out to sea by a strong current.”*
@step Sense 1, “happening now”, is an adjective, but here *current* is a noun after *a strong*.
@step Sense 3, a flow of electricity, does not pull swimmers.
@step Sense 2, a flow of water in one direction, fits the sea.
@answer a flow of water or air in one direction
:::

## Guide words and glossaries

The two **guide words** at the top of a dictionary page are the first and last words on that page. A word is on the page if it comes between them in alphabetical order: *land* is on the page headed *lamp* and *lane*.

A **glossary** is a short dictionary at the back of a textbook that defines the book’s key terms as that book uses them.

## Using a thesaurus

:::definition Thesaurus
A **thesaurus** lists synonyms: words with similar meanings. Some also list antonyms.
:::

Synonyms are rarely exact. A thesaurus may give *slim*, *slender*, *skinny* and *scrawny* for *thin*, but *slender* is admiring and *scrawny* is not. Choose the word whose connotation fits, and check it in a dictionary if you are unsure.

## Checking a guess

When you work out a word from its context, **verify** the guess: look it up, then reread the sentence with the dictionary meaning to make sure it fits.

:::summary
- A dictionary entry gives the **pronunciation**, **part of speech**, numbered **senses** and **etymology** of a word.
- Choose the sense that fits by trying each one in the sentence.
- **Guide words** show the first and last words on a page; a **glossary** defines a book’s key terms.
- A **thesaurus** suggests synonyms, which differ in connotation; verify a meaning in a dictionary.
:::
