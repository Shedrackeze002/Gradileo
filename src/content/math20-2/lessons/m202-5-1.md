Two bus routes from a city suburb to downtown both take $30$ minutes on average. On Route A the trip is almost always close to $30$ minutes; on Route B it swings from about $20$ to almost $40$. The mean cannot tell these routes apart. In this lesson you will measure how **spread out** data is, using the standard deviation.

## Measuring spread

The mean tells you where the centre of a data set is. **Dispersion** tells you how spread out the data is around that centre.

:::definition Dispersion
**Dispersion** (or spread) describes how far the values in a data set are from each other and from the mean. The **range**, the greatest value minus the least value, is a quick measure of dispersion, but it uses only the two most extreme values.
:::

:::definition Standard deviation
The **standard deviation** measures how far the data values typically are from the mean. For a population of $N$ values with mean $\mu$ (the Greek letter mu), the **population standard deviation** is
$$\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma(x - \mu)^2}{N}}$$
The symbol $\sigma$ is the Greek letter sigma, and $\Sigma$ (capital sigma) means “add them all up”.
:::

To find $\sigma$ by hand:

1. Find the mean, $\mu$.
2. Subtract the mean from each value to find its **deviation**, $x - \mu$.
3. Square each deviation.
4. Add the squares and divide by $N$, the number of values.
5. Take the square root.

:::example Route A
Route A’s travel times, in minutes, on five days were $28,\ 30,\ 31,\ 29,\ 32$. Find the mean and the population standard deviation.
@step Mean: $\mu = \dfrac{28 + 30 + 31 + 29 + 32}{5} = \dfrac{150}{5} = 30$
@step Deviations, $x - \mu$: $-2,\ 0,\ 1,\ -1,\ 2$
@step Squared deviations: $4,\ 0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 4$. Their sum is $10$.
@step $\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{5}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$
@answer The mean is $30$ min and the standard deviation is about $1.4$ min.
:::

:::mistake Two standard deviations on your calculator
One-variable statistics on a calculator shows two standard deviations. $\sigma x$ divides by $N$: it is the **population** standard deviation used in this course. $Sx$ divides by $N - 1$ and is used for samples. For Route A, $\sigma x \approx 1.41$ but $Sx \approx 1.58$.
:::

## Standard deviation from a frequency table

When values repeat, the data is often shown in a bar graph or a frequency table. Multiply by each value’s frequency, $f$, so that each value is counted the right number of times.
$$\mu = \dfrac{\Sigma fx}{N} \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma f(x - \mu)^2}{N}}$$

:::example Goals per game
A ringette team recorded the number of goals it scored in each game of its season. Find the mean and the standard deviation.
@step Number of games: $N = 2 + 5 + 6 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 20$
@step $\Sigma fx = 2(0) + 5(1) + 6(2) + 4(3) + 2(4) + 1(5) = 42$, so $\mu = \dfrac{42}{20} = 2.1$
@step $\Sigma f(x - \mu)^2 = 2(-2.1)^2 + 5(-1.1)^2 + 6(-0.1)^2 + 4(0.9)^2 + 2(1.9)^2 + 1(2.9)^2 = 33.8$
@step $\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{33.8}{20}} = \sqrt{1.69} = 1.3$
@answer The mean is $2.1$ goals per game, and the standard deviation is $1.3$ goals.
:::

Put the values in one list and the frequencies in a second list, then run one-variable statistics with the frequency list. Check that $n$ matches the total frequency before you read $\sigma x$.

## Comparing data sets

Standard deviation lets you compare **consistency**. A smaller standard deviation means the data is closer to the mean, so the results are more consistent.

:::example Two bus routes
Route B’s times on five days were $22,\ 35,\ 30,\ 38,\ 25$ minutes. Compare Route B with Route A.
@step Route B: $\mu = \dfrac{150}{5} = 30$. The deviations are $-8,\ 5,\ 0,\ 8,\ -5$, and their squares add to $178$.
@step $\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{178}{5}} = \sqrt{35.6} \approx 5.97$
@step Both routes have a mean of $30$ min, but Route A has $\sigma \approx 1.4$ min and Route B has $\sigma \approx 6.0$ min.
@answer Route A is much more consistent. To be on time for an exam, Route A is the safer choice.
:::

| Route | Mean | Standard deviation | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $30$ min | $1.4$ min | trips are close to $30$ min |
| B | $30$ min | $6.0$ min | trips vary a lot |

:::mistake Mean versus spread
The mean tells you the typical value; the standard deviation tells you the spread. A basketball player with a higher mean scores more points per game, but the player with the **smaller** standard deviation is more consistent. Do not rely on the range alone either: two data sets can have the same mean and the same range but different standard deviations.
:::

:::summary
- Dispersion describes how spread out data is. The range uses only the two extreme values.
- Population standard deviation: $\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma(x - \mu)^2}{N}}$. Find the mean and the deviations, square them, add, divide by $N$, then take the square root.
- From a frequency table: $\mu = \dfrac{\Sigma fx}{N}$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma f(x - \mu)^2}{N}}$.
- On a calculator, use $\sigma x$ (divides by $N$), not $Sx$.
- A smaller standard deviation means more consistent data. Compare means for the typical value and standard deviations for the spread.
:::
