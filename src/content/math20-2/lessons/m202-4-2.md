A landscaper edging a triangular flower bed in a city park measures the sides as $\sqrt{8}$ m, $\sqrt{18}$ m and $\sqrt{32}$ m. How much edging is needed, exactly? Adding radicals works a lot like collecting like terms in algebra — once the radicals are simplified, the answer is a tidy $9\sqrt{2}$ m.

## Like radicals

:::definition Like radicals
**Like radicals** have the same radicand (and the same index), such as $5\sqrt{3}$ and $-2\sqrt{3}$. Unlike radicals, such as $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$, cannot be combined into one term.
:::

Add or subtract like radicals by combining their coefficients; the radical stays the same. Compare with like terms:

$$5x + 2x - x = 6x \qquad\qquad 5\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3}$$

:::example Two kinds of radicals
Simplify $4\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{5}$.
@step Group like radicals: $(4 - 1)\sqrt{2} + (3 + 2)\sqrt{5}$
@answer $3\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{5}$
:::

:::mistake Adding the radicands
$\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is **not** $\sqrt{5}$. Check with a calculator: $1.414 + 1.732 = 3.146$, but $\sqrt{5} \approx 2.236$. The sum $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is already in simplest form.
:::

## Simplify first, then combine

Radicals that look different may become like radicals once they are simplified.

:::example Simplifying first
Simplify (a) $\sqrt{12} + \sqrt{75}$ (b) $3\sqrt{20} - \sqrt{45}$.
@step (a) $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{75} = 5\sqrt{3}$, so the sum is $2\sqrt{3} + 5\sqrt{3}$.
@step (b) $3\sqrt{20} = 3 \times 2\sqrt{5} = 6\sqrt{5}$ and $\sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$, so the difference is $6\sqrt{5} - 3\sqrt{5}$.
@answer (a) $7\sqrt{3}$ (b) $3\sqrt{5}$
:::

:::example Edging a flower bed
The sides of a triangular flower bed are $\sqrt{8}$ m, $\sqrt{18}$ m and $\sqrt{32}$ m. Find the exact perimeter.
@step $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}$
@step $P = 2\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{2}$
@answer $P = 9\sqrt{2}$ m, about $12.7$ m of edging.
:::

## Multiplying radicals

For $a, b \ge 0$, $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$. With numbers in front, multiply the numbers in front together and the radicands together:

$$\left(a\sqrt{m}\right)\left(b\sqrt{n}\right) = ab\sqrt{mn}$$

:::example Multiplying and simplifying
Simplify (a) $\sqrt{6} \times \sqrt{15}$ (b) $\left(2\sqrt{3}\right)\left(5\sqrt{2}\right)$.
@step (a) $\sqrt{6 \times 15} = \sqrt{90} = \sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{10} = 3\sqrt{10}$
@step (b) $(2 \times 5)\sqrt{3 \times 2} = 10\sqrt{6}$
@answer (a) $3\sqrt{10}$ (b) $10\sqrt{6}$
:::

:::tip Squaring a radical
$\sqrt{7} \times \sqrt{7} = \sqrt{49} = 7$. In general $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = a$ — the square root and the square undo each other.
:::

## The distributive property

Multiply each term inside the brackets by the term outside, then simplify each product.

:::example Distributing a radical
Expand and simplify $\sqrt{2}\left(3 + \sqrt{6}\right)$.
@step $\sqrt{2} \times 3 + \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{6} = 3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{12}$
@step $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$
@answer $3\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{3}$
:::

For two binomials, multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. An area model keeps track of the four products:

:::example The area of a garden
A rectangular garden measures $\left(4 + \sqrt{3}\right)$ m by $\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right)$ m. Find its exact area.
@step $A = \left(4 + \sqrt{3}\right)\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right) = 8 + 4\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} + 3$
@step Collect like terms: $8 + 3 = 11$ and $4\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3}$.
@answer $A = 11 + 6\sqrt{3}$ m², about $21.4$ m².
:::

:::mistake Multiplying only the first terms
$\left(4 + \sqrt{3}\right)\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right)$ is not $8 + 3$. There are **four** products, one in each cell of the area model.
:::

:::summary
- Like radicals have the same radicand; combine them by adding or subtracting the coefficients.
- Simplify every radical first — unlike-looking radicals may turn out to be like radicals.
- $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b} \ne \sqrt{a + b}$.
- $\left(a\sqrt{m}\right)\left(b\sqrt{n}\right) = ab\sqrt{mn}$, and $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = a$.
- Use the distributive property (or an area model) to multiply expressions with brackets.
:::
