Rates let you predict. How long will the drive to the mountains take? How much will the gas cost? How long until the pool is full? Each answer comes from multiplying or dividing by a rate, and the units tell you which to do.

## Using a unit rate

A rate connects two quantities, so it works in both directions:

- **Multiply** by the rate to go from the second quantity to the first: litres × dollars per litre = dollars.
- **Divide** by the rate to go the other way: kilometres ÷ kilometres per hour = hours.

:::example Buying gas
Gas costs \$1.539 per litre. How much do $46.5\ \text{L}$ cost, to the nearest cent?
@step The rate is dollars per litre, and you know litres, so multiply.
@step $46.5 \times 1.539 = 71.5635$
@answer The gas costs \$71.56.
:::

:::example Travel time
A family drives $342\ \text{km}$ at an average speed of $90$ km/h. How long does the drive take?
@step You know kilometres, and the rate is kilometres per hour, so divide: $\dfrac{342\ \text{km}}{90\ \text{km/h}} = 3.8\ \text{h}$
@step $0.8\ \text{h} = 0.8 \times 60 = 48\ \text{min}$
@answer The drive takes $3.8\ \text{h}$, or $3\ \text{h}\ 48\ \text{min}$.
:::

:::tip Let the units guide you
Write the units in every step. If they cancel to leave the unit you want, the setup is right: $\text{km} \div \dfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} = \text{h}$.
:::

## Changing units along the way

Many problems need more than one step, often with a change of units.

:::example Gas for a road trip
A car uses $8.2$ L/100 km. Gas costs \$1.489 per litre. How much will the gas cost for a $640\ \text{km}$ trip, to the nearest cent?
@step $640\ \text{km}$ is $6.4$ hundreds of kilometres, so the car needs $6.4 \times 8.2 = 52.48\ \text{L}$.
@step Cost: $52.48 \times 1.489 \approx 78.14$
@answer The gas will cost about \$78.14.
:::

:::example Filling a pool
A pool is a rectangular prism $8\ \text{m}$ long, $4\ \text{m}$ wide and $1.5\ \text{m}$ deep. A hose fills it at $60$ L/min. How many hours will it take, to the nearest tenth? (Use $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}$.)
@step Volume: $8 \times 4 \times 1.5 = 48\ \text{m}^3 = 48\,000\ \text{L}$
@step Minutes: $48\,000 \div 60 = 800\ \text{min}$
@step Hours: $800 \div 60 \approx 13.33\ \text{h}$
@answer It will take about $13.3$ hours.
:::

:::mistake Decimal hours are not minutes
$13.3\ \text{h}$ is **not** $13\ \text{h}\ 30\ \text{min}$. The decimal part is a fraction of an hour: $0.3\ \text{h} = 0.3 \times 60 = 18\ \text{min}$. Multiply by $60$ to change hours to minutes.
:::

## Combining rates

When two things work together, their rates add. When one works against the other, subtract.

:::example Two hoses
Two hoses fill a $6000\ \text{L}$ above-ground pool together. One delivers $30$ L/min and the other $20$ L/min. How long will it take?
@step Combined rate: $30 + 20 = 50$ L/min
@step Time: $6000 \div 50 = 120\ \text{min}$
@answer It takes $120$ minutes, or $2$ hours. (If a leak lost $5$ L/min, the net rate would be $45$ L/min instead.)
:::

:::example Average speed for a whole trip
Gurpreet drives $120\ \text{km}$ on a highway at $100$ km/h, then $60\ \text{km}$ on a gravel road at $60$ km/h. What is the average speed for the whole trip, to the nearest tenth?
@step Highway time: $120 \div 100 = 1.2\ \text{h}$. Gravel time: $60 \div 60 = 1\ \text{h}$.
@step Total: $180\ \text{km}$ in $2.2\ \text{h}$
@step Average speed: $180 \div 2.2 \approx 81.8$ km/h
@answer The average speed is about $81.8$ km/h.
:::

:::mistake Averaging the speeds
The mean of $100$ and $60$ is $80$, but that is not the average speed. Gurpreet spent more time at the slower speed, so the average is pulled toward it. Average speed is always **total distance ÷ total time**.
:::

:::check m202-rate-apply 3
:::

:::summary
- Multiply by a rate or divide by it; check that the units cancel to the unit you want.
- Change units as you go: hours ↔ minutes (×60 or ÷60), $\text{m}^3$ → L (×1000), km → hundreds of km (÷100).
- A decimal part of an hour is not minutes: multiply it by $60$.
- Rates that work together add; rates that work against each other subtract.
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time, not the mean of the speeds.
:::
