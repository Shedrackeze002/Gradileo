The drama club at a high school needs to raise at least \$1500 from ticket sales. Many combinations of adult and student tickets work — far too many to list. An inequality in two variables describes all of them at once, and its graph is a whole region of the plane.

## Solutions of a linear inequality

:::definition Linear inequality in two variables
An inequality such as $3x - 2y \le 6$ or $y > 2x + 1$ that uses one of $<$, $>$, $\le$ or $\ge$ with a linear expression in $x$ and $y$.
:::

:::definition Solution region
A **solution** of an inequality in two variables is an ordered pair $(x, y)$ that makes it true. All the solutions together form the **solution region**.
:::

To decide whether a point is a solution, substitute its coordinates and check whether the statement is true.

:::example Testing points
Are $(2, -1)$ and $(0, 0)$ solutions of $3x - 2y \le 6$?
@step $(2, -1)$: $3(2) - 2(-1) = 6 + 2 = 8$. Is $8 \le 6$? No.
@step $(0, 0)$: $3(0) - 2(0) = 0$. Is $0 \le 6$? Yes.
@answer $(0, 0)$ is a solution; $(2, -1)$ is not.
:::

## Graphing: the boundary and the shading

The line $3x - 2y = 6$ splits the plane into two half-planes. Every point on one side makes $3x - 2y < 6$ true, and every point on the other side makes $3x - 2y > 6$ true.

:::definition Boundary and test point
The **boundary** is the line you get by replacing the inequality sign with $=$. A **test point** is any point not on the boundary that you substitute to decide which side to shade.
:::

1. Graph the boundary. Draw it **solid** for $\le$ or $\ge$ (points on the line are solutions) and **dashed** for $<$ or $>$ (they are not).
2. Substitute a test point not on the line, such as $(0, 0)$.
3. If the test point makes the inequality true, shade its side. If not, shade the other side.

:::example Graphing an inequality
Graph $2x - 3y > 6$.
@step Boundary: $2x - 3y = 6$ has intercepts $(3, 0)$ and $(0, -2)$. The sign is $>$, so the line is dashed.
@step Test $(0, 0)$: $2(0) - 3(0) = 0$. Is $0 > 6$? No.
@step Shade the side that does **not** contain $(0, 0)$: below the line.
@answer Check by solving for $y$: $-3y > -2x + 6$, so $y < \frac{2}{3}x - 2$ — the points below the line.
:::

:::mistake Dividing by a negative
When you solve $2x - 3y > 6$ for $y$, you divide by $-3$. Dividing or multiplying an inequality by a negative number **reverses** the sign: $y < \frac{2}{3}x - 2$, not $y > \frac{2}{3}x - 2$.
:::

:::tip When y is alone
For $y > mx + b$ or $y \ge mx + b$, shade above the line. For $y < mx + b$ or $y \le mx + b$, shade below it. For a vertical boundary such as $x \ge 2$, shade to the right.
:::

## Writing an inequality for a situation

Translate the words carefully: “at least” is $\ge$, “at most” or “no more than” is $\le$, “more than” is $>$ and “less than” is $<$.

:::example Ticket sales
Adult tickets cost \$15 and student tickets cost \$10. The drama club needs at least \$1500. Write an inequality, and decide whether selling 60 adult and 70 student tickets is enough.
@step Let $a$ be the number of adult tickets and $s$ the number of student tickets. The money raised is $15a + 10s$.
@step “At least \$1500”: $15a + 10s \ge 1500$
@step Test $(60, 70)$: $15(60) + 10(70) = 900 + 700 = 1600$, and $1600 \ge 1500$ is true.
@answer $15a + 10s \ge 1500$; yes, 60 adult and 70 student tickets is enough.
:::

In a situation like this, only points with whole-number coordinates where $a \ge 0$ and $s \ge 0$ make sense, even though the graph shades a whole region.

:::check m201-linear-inequality 3
:::

:::summary
- A solution of an inequality in two variables is a point that makes it true; the solutions form a region.
- The boundary is solid for $\le$ and $\ge$, dashed for $<$ and $>$.
- Use a test point to decide which side to shade; reverse the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative.
- “At least” means $\ge$ and “at most” means $\le$; check that the answers make sense in the situation.
:::
