Tell three friends to draw a triangle with $\angle A = 35^\circ$, $b = 14$ cm and $a = 9$ cm. They might not all draw the same triangle — and none of them would be wrong. Knowing two sides and an angle that is **not** between them (SSA) is the **ambiguous case**.

## Why SSA can give two triangles

Start with $\angle A$ and side $b$ fixed. Side $a$ hangs from $C$ and must reach the base line. Picture $a$ as a gate swinging from $C$: it might miss the base, touch it once, or cross it in two places.

Here side $a$ crosses the base at $B_1$ and at $B_2$, so $\triangle AB_1C$ and $\triangle AB_2C$ both fit the information. The angles at $B_1$ and $B_2$ are supplementary: $\triangle B_1B_2C$ is isosceles ($CB_1 = CB_2 = a$), so its base angles are equal, and $\angle AB_2C$ is the supplement of one of them.

:::definition Ambiguous case
When the given information is two sides and an angle opposite one of them (SSA), there may be no triangle, one triangle or two triangles.
:::

## Counting the triangles

The height from $C$ to the base is $h = b\sin A$. Compare side $a$ (opposite the given angle) with $h$ and with $b$.

| $\angle A$ acute | Number of triangles |
|---|---|
| $a < h$ | none: $a$ is too short to reach the base |
| $a = h$ | one right triangle |
| $h < a < b$ | **two** |
| $a \ge b$ | one |

When $\angle A$ is obtuse (or right), it is the largest angle, so $a$ must be the longest side: **one** triangle if $a > b$, **none** if $a \le b$.

:::example Using the height
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 30^\circ$ and $b = 12$ cm. How many triangles are there when $a = 4$ cm, $6$ cm, $9$ cm or $15$ cm?
@step $h = 12\sin 30^\circ = 6$ cm
@step $a = 4 < h$: none. $a = 6 = h$: one right triangle.
@step $a = 9$: $6 < 9 < 12$, so two. $a = 15 \ge 12$: one.
@answer None, one, two and one triangle
:::

:::example An obtuse angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 110^\circ$ and $b = 15$ m. How many triangles are there when $a = 12$ m? When $a = 18$ m?
@step $\angle A$ is obtuse, so $a$ must be longer than $b$.
@answer $a = 12$: no triangle. $a = 18$: exactly one triangle.
:::

:::mistake Skipping the check
The sine law alone will not warn you. Always check SSA information first: if you only take the calculator's acute angle, you may miss a second triangle; if $a < h$, $\sin B$ comes out greater than 1 and there is no triangle at all.
:::

## Solving both triangles

When there are two triangles, find the acute angle with $\sin^{-1}$, then its supplement. Then finish each triangle separately.

:::example Two solutions
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 35^\circ$, $a = 9$ cm and $b = 14$ cm. Solve both triangles to the nearest tenth.
@step $h = 14\sin 35^\circ \approx 8.03$, and $8.03 < 9 < 14$: two triangles.
@step $\sin B = \dfrac{14\sin 35^\circ}{9} \approx 0.8922$, so $\angle B_1 \approx 63.2^\circ$ and $\angle B_2 \approx 180^\circ - 63.15^\circ \approx 116.8^\circ$.
@step $\angle C_1 \approx 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 63.15^\circ \approx 81.8^\circ$ and $\angle C_2 \approx 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 116.85^\circ \approx 28.2^\circ$
@step $c_1 = \dfrac{9\sin 81.85^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ} \approx 15.5$ cm and $c_2 = \dfrac{9\sin 28.15^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ} \approx 7.4$ cm
@answer Triangle 1: $\angle B \approx 63.2^\circ$, $\angle C \approx 81.8^\circ$, $c \approx 15.5$ cm. Triangle 2: $\angle B \approx 116.8^\circ$, $\angle C \approx 28.2^\circ$, $c \approx 7.4$ cm.
:::

:::tip Check the obtuse angle
The supplement only works if the angle sum stays under $180^\circ$: here $35^\circ + 116.8^\circ < 180^\circ$. If the given angle plus the supplement reached $180^\circ$, there would be only one triangle.
:::

## The ambiguous case in context

:::example Cell tower range
A straight highway leaves Riverton. A cell tower stands 30 km from Riverton, and the line to it makes a $25^\circ$ angle with the highway. The tower's signal reaches 16 km in every direction. At what two distances along the highway is a car exactly 16 km from the tower?
@step In the triangle town–car–tower, the $25^\circ$ angle is at the town, the 16 km side is opposite it, and the 30 km side is adjacent. $h = 30\sin 25^\circ \approx 12.68$, and $12.68 < 16 < 30$: two positions.
@step At the car: $\sin X = \dfrac{30\sin 25^\circ}{16} \approx 0.7924$, so $\angle X \approx 52.41^\circ$ or $127.59^\circ$.
@step At the tower: $\angle T \approx 102.59^\circ$ or $27.41^\circ$.
@step Distance from town $= \dfrac{16\sin T}{\sin 25^\circ}$
@answer About 17.4 km and 36.9 km from Riverton
:::

Between those two points the car is inside the tower's range.

:::summary
- SSA (two sides and a non-included angle) can give zero, one or two triangles.
- For an acute angle $A$: compare $a$ with $h = b\sin A$ and with $b$. Two triangles when $h < a < b$.
- For an obtuse angle $A$: one triangle if $a > b$, none otherwise.
- In the two-triangle case, the two possible angles are $\sin^{-1}$ of the value and its supplement; solve each triangle separately.
:::
