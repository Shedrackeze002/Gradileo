Factoring turns a sum into a product, and products are what let you solve equations, simplify rational expressions and find the zeros of a function. In Grade 10 you factored $x^2 + bx + c$. Now you will factor any trinomial $ax^2 + bx + c$, differences of squares in two variables, and expressions that only look complicated.

## Factoring ax² + bx + c by decomposition

Multiply two binomials and watch where each term comes from:

$$(mx + p)(nx + q) = mnx^2 + (mq + np)x + pq$$

The two middle products, $mq$ and $np$, **add** to $b$ and **multiply** to $(mn)(pq) = ac$. So to factor $ax^2 + bx + c$, look for two integers with product $ac$ and sum $b$. They tell you how to split the middle term.

:::definition Decomposition
To factor $ax^2 + bx + c$: find integers $m$ and $n$ with $mn = ac$ and $m + n = b$, rewrite $bx$ as $mx + nx$, then factor the four terms by grouping.
:::

:::example A negative constant
Factor $6x^2 - 7x - 3$.
@step $ac = 6(-3) = -18$. Two integers with product $-18$ and sum $-7$: $-9$ and $2$.
@step Split the middle term: $6x^2 - 9x + 2x - 3$
@step Group: $3x(2x - 3) + 1(2x - 3)$
@answer $(3x + 1)(2x - 3)$
:::

:::example Two variables
Factor $4x^2 + 4xy - 15y^2$.
@step $ac = 4(-15) = -60$. Two integers with product $-60$ and sum $4$: $10$ and $-6$.
@step $4x^2 + 10xy - 6xy - 15y^2 = 2x(2x + 5y) - 3y(2x + 5y)$
@answer $(2x - 3y)(2x + 5y)$
:::

:::mistake Signs in the second group
In $6x^2 - 9x + 2x - 3$, the second pair is $+2x - 3 = 1(2x - 3)$. In $4x^2 + 10xy - 6xy - 15y^2$ it is $-6xy - 15y^2 = -3y(2x + 5y)$. Factor out the negative so the binomials match.
:::

## Common factors first, including fractions

**Factor completely** means that no factor can be factored further. Always remove the greatest common factor first — it makes the numbers smaller. If the leading coefficient is negative, factor out a negative.

:::example A negative common factor
Factor $-12x^2 + 2x + 4$ completely.
@step Factor out $-2$: $-2(6x^2 - x - 2)$
@step $ac = -12$, sum $-1$: $-4$ and $3$, so $6x^2 - 4x + 3x - 2 = 2x(3x - 2) + 1(3x - 2)$
@answer $-2(3x - 2)(2x + 1)$
:::

The coefficients $a$, $b$ and $c$ can be any rational numbers. Factor out a rational common factor so that the brackets have integer coefficients.

:::example Rational coefficients
Factor (a) $\frac{1}{2}x^2 - x - 4$ (b) $\frac{2}{3}x^2 + \frac{2}{3}x - 4$.
@step (a) Factor out $\frac{1}{2}$: $\frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 2x - 8) = \frac{1}{2}(x - 4)(x + 2)$
@step (b) Factor out $\frac{2}{3}$ (divide each coefficient by $\frac{2}{3}$): $\frac{2}{3}(x^2 + x - 6) = \frac{2}{3}(x + 3)(x - 2)$
@step Check (b): $\frac{2}{3} \times (-6) = -4$ ✓
:::

:::tip Typing a fraction in front
Type $\frac{1}{2}(x - 4)(x + 2)$ as `1/2(x - 4)(x + 2)`.
:::

## Difference of squares

Multiply $(A - B)(A + B)$: the middle terms cancel.

$$(A - B)(A + B) = A^2 + AB - AB - B^2 = A^2 - B^2$$

:::definition Difference of squares
$$A^2 - B^2 = (A - B)(A + B)$$
Here $A$ and $B$ can be any expressions, such as $7x$, $4y$ or $x^2$.
:::

:::example Differences of squares
Factor completely: (a) $49x^2 - 16y^2$ (b) $3x^3 - 12xy^2$ (c) $16x^4 - 81$ (d) $\frac{1}{4}x^2 - 9$.
@step (a) $(7x)^2 - (4y)^2 = (7x - 4y)(7x + 4y)$
@step (b) GCF $3x$: $3x(x^2 - 4y^2) = 3x(x - 2y)(x + 2y)$
@step (c) $(4x^2)^2 - 9^2 = (4x^2 - 9)(4x^2 + 9) = (2x - 3)(2x + 3)(4x^2 + 9)$
@step (d) $\frac{1}{4}(x^2 - 36) = \frac{1}{4}(x - 6)(x + 6)$
:::

:::mistake A sum of squares does not factor
$4x^2 + 9$ cannot be factored over the integers: no two integers have product $36$ and sum $0$. Only a **difference** of squares factors.
:::

## Factoring by substitution

When the same expression appears more than once, replace it with a single letter. The expression then looks like a trinomial or a difference of squares you already know how to factor.

:::example A repeated binomial
Factor $2(x + 1)^2 - 5(x + 1) - 3$.
@step Let $u = x + 1$: $2u^2 - 5u - 3$
@step $ac = -6$, sum $-5$: $-6$ and $1$, so $2u^2 - 5u - 3 = (2u + 1)(u - 3)$
@step Substitute back: $(2(x + 1) + 1)((x + 1) - 3)$
@answer $(2x + 3)(x - 2)$
:::

:::example Two substitutions
Factor (a) $x^4 - 13x^2 + 36$ (b) $9(x - 2)^2 - 4y^2$.
@step (a) Let $u = x^2$: $u^2 - 13u + 36 = (u - 4)(u - 9) = (x^2 - 4)(x^2 - 9)$
@step Both factors are differences of squares: $(x - 2)(x + 2)(x - 3)(x + 3)$
@step (b) Let $A = x - 2$: $9A^2 - 4y^2 = (3A - 2y)(3A + 2y)$
@step Substitute back and simplify: $(3x - 6 - 2y)(3x - 6 + 2y) = (3x - 2y - 6)(3x + 2y - 6)$
:::

:::tip After you substitute back
Simplify each bracket, then check two things: does a bracket have a common factor, and does a bracket factor again?

:::summary
- To factor $ax^2 + bx + c$, find two integers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term and group.
- Remove common factors first, including a negative or a fraction; factor completely.
- $A^2 - B^2 = (A - B)(A + B)$; a sum of squares does not factor over the integers.
- Substitute a single letter for a repeated expression, factor, substitute back and simplify.
:::
