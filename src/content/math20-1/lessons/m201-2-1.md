A wind turbine blade on a windy ridge, a car's steering wheel, the arm of a crane: all of them turn through angles far bigger than $90^\circ$. To use trigonometry for these turns, we draw every angle the same way — on a coordinate grid, starting from the positive $x$-axis.

## Angles in standard position

:::definition Standard position
An angle is in **standard position** when its vertex is at the origin and its **initial arm** lies along the positive $x$-axis. The angle opens counterclockwise to its **terminal arm**.
:::

The axes split the plane into four **quadrants**, numbered counterclockwise:

| Quadrant | I | II | III | IV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Terminal arm for | $0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$ | $90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$ | $180^\circ < \theta < 270^\circ$ | $270^\circ < \theta < 360^\circ$ |

Angles of $0^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ have a terminal arm on an axis. They are called **quadrantal angles** and are not in any quadrant.

:::note Coterminal angles
Angles in standard position that share a terminal arm are **coterminal**. For example, $40^\circ$ and $400^\circ$ are coterminal, because $400^\circ = 360^\circ + 40^\circ$. In this course we work with angles from $0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$.
:::

## Reference angles

:::definition Reference angle
The **reference angle** $\theta_R$ of an angle $\theta$ in standard position is the acute angle between the terminal arm and the $x$-axis. It is always between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.
:::

The terminal arm of $210^\circ$ is $30^\circ$ past the negative $x$-axis, so its reference angle is $30^\circ$. Measure toward the nearest part of the $x$-axis ($0^\circ$, $180^\circ$ or $360^\circ$):

| Quadrant | I | II | III | IV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reference angle | $\theta_R = \theta$ | $\theta_R = 180^\circ - \theta$ | $\theta_R = \theta - 180^\circ$ | $\theta_R = 360^\circ - \theta$ |

:::example Finding a reference angle
Determine the quadrant and the reference angle of $\theta = 300^\circ$.
@step $270^\circ < 300^\circ < 360^\circ$, so the terminal arm is in quadrant IV.
@step $\theta_R = 360^\circ - 300^\circ$
@answer Quadrant IV, $\theta_R = 60^\circ$
:::

:::example Working backward
An angle in quadrant III has a reference angle of $25^\circ$. Determine the angle.
@step In quadrant III the terminal arm is $\theta_R$ past $180^\circ$.
@answer $\theta = 180^\circ + 25^\circ = 205^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Measuring to the y-axis
For $300^\circ$, the calculation $300^\circ - 270^\circ = 30^\circ$ measures to the $y$-axis. A reference angle is always measured to the **$x$-axis**: $\theta_R = 60^\circ$.
:::

## One reference angle, four angles

Each reference angle appears once in every quadrant. For $\theta_R = 40^\circ$:

$$40^\circ \text{ (I)},\quad 180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ \text{ (II)},\quad 180^\circ + 40^\circ = 220^\circ \text{ (III)},\quad 360^\circ - 40^\circ = 320^\circ \text{ (IV)}$$

These four angles are reflections of each other in the axes, so they have the same "shape" of triangle when you drop a perpendicular from the terminal arm to the $x$-axis. That is the key to the next section.

:::check m201-reference-angle 3
:::

## Special triangles

Two right triangles give exact values for $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$.

- **$45^\circ$–$45^\circ$–$90^\circ$:** an isosceles right triangle with legs $1$. By the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$.
- **$30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$:** cut an equilateral triangle with sides $2$ in half. The half-base is $1$, the hypotenuse is $2$, and the height is $\sqrt{2^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt{3}$.

| $\theta$ | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin\theta$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ |
| $\cos\theta$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ |
| $\tan\theta$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt{3}$ |

:::example Exact values
Determine the exact values of $\cos 30^\circ$ and $\tan 60^\circ$.
@step In the $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle, the side adjacent to $30^\circ$ is $\sqrt{3}$ and the hypotenuse is $2$: $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
@step Opposite $60^\circ$ is $\sqrt{3}$ and adjacent is $1$: $\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{1}$.
@answer $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}$
:::

:::tip Rationalized form
$\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, which we usually write as $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$. Both are exact and both are correct.
:::

## Exact values in every quadrant

Place the special triangle against the $x$-axis in the angle's quadrant. A point $(x, y)$ on the terminal arm, at distance $r$ from the origin, gives
$$\sin\theta = \frac{y}{r} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{x}{r} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}$$
For an acute angle these match SOH CAH TOA. In other quadrants, $x$ or $y$ can be **negative**, and that sets the sign.

For $150^\circ$, the $30^\circ$ triangle puts the point $(-\sqrt{3}, 1)$ on the terminal arm with $r = 2$. So $\sin 150^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $\cos 150^\circ = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\tan 150^\circ = \dfrac{1}{-\sqrt{3}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

:::example Using the reference angle
Determine the exact value of $\cos 225^\circ$.
@step Quadrant III: $\theta_R = 225^\circ - 180^\circ = 45^\circ$.
@step In quadrant III, $x < 0$, so $\cos 225^\circ = \dfrac{x}{r}$ is negative.
@step $\cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
@answer $\cos 225^\circ = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
:::

For quadrantal angles, use the points $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(-1, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$, each with $r = 1$. For example, $\sin 270^\circ = \dfrac{-1}{1} = -1$, and $\tan 90^\circ = \dfrac{1}{0}$ is **undefined**.

:::mistake Dropping the sign
The reference angle gives the size of the ratio, not its sign. $\cos 225^\circ$ is not $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$: the point on the terminal arm has a negative $x$-coordinate.
:::

:::summary
- An angle in standard position starts on the positive $x$-axis and opens counterclockwise.
- The reference angle is the acute angle between the terminal arm and the $x$-axis: $\theta$, $180^\circ - \theta$, $\theta - 180^\circ$ or $360^\circ - \theta$.
- One reference angle gives one angle in each quadrant.
- The special triangles have sides $1, 1, \sqrt{2}$ and $1, \sqrt{3}, 2$.
- $\sin\theta = \dfrac{y}{r}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{x}{r}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{y}{x}$: the reference angle gives the size, the signs of $x$ and $y$ give the sign.
:::
