:::note Spoiler
This lesson follows the whole plot of *Hamlet*, including how each of the main characters dies at the end of the play.
:::

Alone with Hamlet on the battlements, the Ghost reveals how the old King really died:

> **Ghost.** The serpent that did sting thy father’s life\
> Now wears his crown.
>
> **Hamlet.** O my prophetic soul!\
> Mine uncle!
>
> — *Hamlet*, Act 1, Scene 5

Hamlet’s “prophetic soul” had already suspected his uncle. From this moment he has a secret, a duty and a terrible doubt, and every other character in the play is caught up in what he does with them. In this lesson you will follow who the characters are and what happens in each act, and then explain why the characters act as they do, with evidence.

## Who’s who

| Character | Who they are |
|---|---|
| Hamlet | the Prince of Denmark, a student at Wittenberg, whose father’s ghost asks him for revenge |
| Claudius | the new King, Hamlet’s uncle, who murdered his brother and married his brother’s widow |
| Gertrude | the Queen, Hamlet’s mother, who married her dead husband’s brother within a month of his funeral |
| The Ghost | the spirit of the dead King, Hamlet’s father, who demands revenge for his murder |
| Polonius | the King’s chief counsellor, a talkative and meddling father |
| Ophelia | Polonius’s daughter, whom Hamlet has courted |
| Laertes | Polonius’s son, a student in France |
| Horatio | Hamlet’s loyal friend from Wittenberg |
| Rosencrantz and Guildenstern | Hamlet’s old school friends, summoned by the King to spy on him |
| Fortinbras | the Prince of Norway, whose father was killed in single combat by Hamlet’s father |

:::tip Families mirror each other
Two families stand at the centre of the play. Hamlet’s father is murdered, and Hamlet is asked to avenge him. Then Hamlet kills Polonius, and Polonius’s son, Laertes, sets out to avenge *his* father. Fortinbras, whose father was also killed, makes a third son with a father to avenge.
:::

## The play, act by act

**Act 1.** The Ghost appears to the guards at midnight. At court, the new King and Queen urge Hamlet to stop mourning. Laertes leaves for France, and Polonius forbids Ophelia to see Hamlet. The Ghost tells Hamlet that Claudius poisoned him in his orchard, and Hamlet swears his friends to secrecy, warning them that he may pretend to be mad.

**Act 2.** Hamlet visits Ophelia looking wild and distracted, and Polonius decides that love has driven him mad. Rosencrantz and Guildenstern arrive to spy on Hamlet. A company of actors comes to the castle, and Hamlet plans to have them perform a play like his father’s murder, to test the King.

**Act 3.** Hamlet speaks the “To be, or not to be” soliloquy and turns cruelly on Ophelia while the King and Polonius secretly watch. At the play, Claudius rises and calls for light. Hamlet finds the King alone at prayer and spares him. In his mother’s room he stabs through a tapestry and kills Polonius, who was hiding there, and the Ghost appears once more.

**Act 4.** Claudius sends Hamlet to England with sealed letters ordering his death. Ophelia goes mad with grief, and Laertes returns, demanding revenge. A letter reveals that pirates have brought Hamlet back to Denmark, so Claudius and Laertes plot to kill him in a fencing match with a poisoned sword and a poisoned cup. Gertrude brings the news that Ophelia has drowned.

**Act 5.** In a churchyard Hamlet holds the skull of Yorick, the old King’s jester, and then sees Ophelia’s funeral and fights with Laertes in her grave. He tells Horatio how he sent Rosencrantz and Guildenstern to their deaths in his place. In the fencing match Gertrude drinks the poisoned cup, Laertes and Hamlet are both wounded by the poisoned sword, and Hamlet kills Claudius before he dies himself. Fortinbras arrives to take the throne.

## Motives

:::definition Motive
A **motive** is the reason behind a character’s action: what they want, fear or believe. To explain a motive, find what the character says or does, and then the evidence for *why*.
:::

| Character | What drives them | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hamlet | revenge, but only once he is sure the Ghost told the truth | “I’ll have grounds / More relative than this” (Act 2, Scene 2) |
| Claudius | keeping the crown and the queen he gained by murder | “My crown, mine own ambition, and my queen” (Act 3, Scene 3) |
| Gertrude | peace in her family and her new marriage | “Good Hamlet, cast thy nighted colour off” (Act 1, Scene 2) |
| Polonius | serving the King, and proving that he is right about Hamlet | “This is the very ecstasy of love” (Act 2, Scene 1) |
| Ophelia | obedience to her father | “I shall obey, my lord.” (Act 1, Scene 3) |
| Laertes | revenge for his father, whatever it costs | “I dare damnation” (Act 4, Scene 5) |

The richest evidence of a motive often comes when a character is alone. In public, Claudius is the calm, wise King. Alone, he tries to pray and finds that he cannot:

> But O, what form of prayer\
> Can serve my turn? Forgive me my foul murder!\
> That cannot be; since I am still possess’d\
> Of those effects for which I did the murder,—\
> My crown, mine own ambition, and my queen.\
> May one be pardon’d and retain th’offence?
>
> — Claudius, *Hamlet*, Act 3, Scene 3

His guilt is real, but so is his motive: he will not give up what the murder won him.

:::mistake Taking a public speech at face value
When Claudius tells Hamlet that endless mourning is “unmanly grief”, he speaks as a king in front of his court, and he has every reason to want Hamlet to stop brooding over his father’s death. Check a character’s public words against what they say alone, or in an aside, before you decide what they want.
:::

:::example Explaining a motive with evidence
Why does Claudius send Hamlet to England after the death of Polonius?
@step What he says in public: he tells Hamlet that the voyage is “for thine especial safety”, to protect him after the killing.
@step What he says alone: the letters he has sent with Hamlet order “The present death of Hamlet”, and he calls the prince a fever raging in his blood that only England can cure.
@step Why he does it: the play has shown him that Hamlet knows his secret, and the death of Polonius shows that Hamlet will kill. As Claudius says, “It had been so with us, had we been there.”
@answer Claudius sends Hamlet to England to have him killed there, out of sight. He pretends it is for Hamlet’s safety, but he fears that Hamlet knows of the murder and is dangerous, and he wants him dead without the people of Denmark blaming their King.
:::

:::summary
- Hamlet, Laertes and Fortinbras are all sons whose fathers have been killed, so the play sets their responses side by side.
- Act 1: the Ghost’s revelation. Act 2: the court watches Hamlet, and the actors arrive. Act 3: the play, the prayer scene and the death of Polonius. Act 4: England, Ophelia’s madness and the plot against Hamlet. Act 5: the graveyard and the fatal fencing match.
- A motive is the reason behind an action. Support it with evidence, and trust what characters say alone more than what they say in public.
:::
