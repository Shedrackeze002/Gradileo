A train on a long, straight stretch of track covers the same distance in the same direction every second. This is the simplest kind of motion there is, and it is the place to start, because every other motion is described by how it departs from it.

## Uniform motion

:::definition Uniform motion and velocity
**Uniform motion** is motion at a constant velocity: the same speed, in the same direction.

**Velocity** is the displacement each second. It is a vector, measured in m/s.

$$v = \dfrac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}$$

where $\Delta d$ is the displacement and $\Delta t$ is the time taken. The sign of a velocity gives its direction, exactly as the sign of a displacement does.
:::

:::example A velocity from two positions
East is the positive direction. A drone moves at a steady velocity from the position 30 m to the position 65 m in 5 s. What is its velocity?
@step Displacement: $\Delta d = 65 - 30 = 35$ m.
@step Velocity: $v = \dfrac{\Delta d}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{35}{5}$.
@answer $v = 7$ m/s, which is 7 m/s [E].
:::

:::example A negative velocity
North is the positive direction. A cyclist moves at a steady velocity from the position 50 m to the position 30 m in 4 s. What is the velocity?
@step Displacement: $\Delta d = 30 - 50 = -20$ m.
@step Velocity: $v = \dfrac{-20}{4} = -5$ m/s.
@answer $v = -5$ m/s, which is 5 m/s [S].
:::

The equation can be rearranged to find a displacement or a time: $\Delta d = v\,\Delta t$ and $\Delta t = \dfrac{\Delta d}{v}$.

:::example How long to get there
North is the positive direction. A train is at the position $-30$ m and moves north at a steady 20 m/s. How long does it take to reach the position 270 m?
@step Displacement needed: $\Delta d = 270 - (-30) = 300$ m. Subtracting a negative position adds.
@step Time: $\Delta t = \dfrac{\Delta d}{v} = \dfrac{300}{20}$.
@answer 15 s.
:::

When two objects move along the same line, write the position of each after a time $t$. They meet when the two positions are equal.

:::example Two cyclists riding towards each other
Cyclist A starts at the position 0 m and rides at 4 m/s in the positive direction. Cyclist B starts at the position 225 m at the same moment and rides at 5 m/s in the negative direction. When and where do they meet?
@step After $t$ seconds, A is at $4t$ and B is at $225 - 5t$.
@step They meet when the positions are equal: $4t = 225 - 5t$, so $9t = 225$ and $t = 25$ s.
@step Position: $4 \times 25 = 100$ m. Check with B: $225 - 5 \times 25 = 100$ m.
@answer They meet after 25 s, at the position 100 m.
:::

:::mistake Using the positions in place of the displacement
An object that moves from 30 m to 65 m has not moved 65 m. The velocity uses the **change** in position, $65 - 30 = 35$ m, not the final position.
:::

## Position-time graphs

A position-time graph shows where an object is at each moment. The time goes along the horizontal axis and the position goes up the vertical axis.

:::definition Slope of a position-time graph
The **slope** of a position-time graph is the velocity:

$$\text{slope} = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \dfrac{\Delta d}{\Delta t} = v$$

A straight line means a constant velocity. A line that slopes upwards is a positive velocity, a horizontal line is an object at rest, and a line that slopes downwards is a negative velocity. A steeper line is a faster motion.
:::

:::example Reading velocities from the graph
Use the graph above. Find the velocity of the robot in each of the three parts of its trip.
@step From 0 s to 2 s the position rises from 0 m to 20 m: $v = \dfrac{20 - 0}{2} = 10$ m/s.
@step From 2 s to 4 s the line is horizontal. The position does not change, so $v = 0$: the robot is at rest.
@step From 4 s to 8 s the position falls from 20 m to $-20$ m: $v = \dfrac{-20 - 20}{4} = \dfrac{-40}{4} = -10$ m/s.
@answer 10 m/s, then 0 m/s, then $-10$ m/s. The robot moves forward, waits, and comes back past the origin.
:::

:::example Distance and average velocity from the graph
For the whole 8 s of the same trip, find the distance travelled and the average velocity.
@step Distance: the robot moves 20 m forward and then 40 m back, so the distance is $20 + 40 = 60$ m.
@step Displacement: it starts at 0 m and ends at $-20$ m, so $\Delta d = -20 - 0 = -20$ m.
@step Average velocity: $v_{\text{avg}} = \dfrac{\Delta d}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{-20}{8} = -2.5$ m/s.
@answer The robot travels 60 m, and its average velocity is $-2.5$ m/s.
:::

Where the line crosses the time axis, the position is zero. That means the object is passing through the origin. It does not mean that the object has stopped.

:::mistake Reading the height of the graph as the velocity
At 3 s the robot is at the position 20 m, but its velocity is zero. The **height** of a position-time graph is the position. The velocity is the **slope**.
:::

:::summary
- Uniform motion is motion at a constant velocity.
- $v = \dfrac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}$, and the sign of the velocity gives its direction.
- Two objects on one line meet when their positions are equal.
- The slope of a position-time graph is the velocity: upwards is positive, horizontal is at rest, downwards is negative.
- Average velocity is the total displacement divided by the total time.
:::
