You pull a sled with a rope that slants up to your hand, not with a rope that runs along the snow. Part of your pull drags the sled forward and part of it lifts. To deal with forces that point in different directions, we do what we did for velocities: resolve them into components and treat each direction on its own.

## A force at an angle

A force $F$ at an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal has two components:

$$F_x = F\cos\theta \qquad F_y = F\sin\theta$$

Newton's second law holds separately for each direction:

$$F_{\text{net},\,x} = ma_x \qquad F_{\text{net},\,y} = ma_y$$

For an object sliding along level ground there is no vertical acceleration, so the vertical forces balance. The horizontal forces give the acceleration.

:::example Components of a pull
A rope pulls on a sled with a force of 80 N at 30° above the horizontal. Find the horizontal and vertical components of the pull.
@step Horizontal: $F_x = 80 \times \cos 30° = 69.3$ N.
@step Vertical: $F_y = 80 \times \sin 30° = 40.0$ N.
@answer 69.3 N forwards and 40.0 N upwards.
:::

:::example A sled on frictionless ice
The sled has a mass of 20 kg and is on level, frictionless ice. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. Find its acceleration and the normal force of the ice on it.
@step Horizontal: only $F_x$ acts. $a = \dfrac{F\cos\theta}{m} = \dfrac{69.28}{20} = 3.46\ \text{m/s}^2$.
@step Vertical: the normal force and the upward component of the pull together balance the weight. $F_N + F\sin\theta = mg$.
@step $F_N = 20 \times 9.81 - 40.0 = 196.2 - 40.0$.
@answer $a = 3.46\ \text{m/s}^2$ and $F_N = 156.2$ N.
:::

The normal force is less than the weight, because the rope carries part of it. That matters as soon as there is friction, because friction depends on the normal force.

:::example The same sled on snow
The same 20 kg sled is pulled with the same 80 N at 30°, on level snow where the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.1. Find its acceleration.
@step The normal force is still 156.2 N, from the vertical balance.
@step Friction: $F_f = \mu_k F_N = 0.1 \times 156.2 = 15.62$ N, backwards.
@step Horizontal: $a = \dfrac{F\cos\theta - F_f}{m} = \dfrac{69.28 - 15.62}{20}$.
@answer $a = 2.68\ \text{m/s}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using mg for the normal force when the pull is at an angle
With a pull at an angle above the horizontal, $F_N = mg - F\sin\theta$. Using $F_N = mg$ makes the friction too large. A push that slants **downwards** does the opposite: it presses the object into the ground, and $F_N = mg + F\sin\theta$.
:::

## Net force and equilibrium in two dimensions

Two forces at right angles add like displacements at right angles. The size of the net force comes from the Pythagorean theorem and its direction from the tangent. The acceleration points along the net force.

:::example Two forces at right angles
Two horizontal forces act on a 10 kg puck on frictionless ice: 30 N east and 40 N north. Find the size and the direction of the puck's acceleration.
@step Net force: $F_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{2500} = 50$ N.
@step $a = \dfrac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} = \dfrac{50}{10} = 5\ \text{m/s}^2$.
@step Direction: $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{40}{30}\right) = 53.1°$ north of east.
@answer $5\ \text{m/s}^2$ at 53.1° north of east.
:::

:::definition Equilibrium
An object is in **equilibrium** when the net force on it is zero. In two dimensions this means that the forces balance in each direction separately:

$$F_{\text{net},\,x} = 0 \qquad F_{\text{net},\,y} = 0$$

The single force that would balance a set of forces is called their **equilibrant**. It is equal in size to their resultant and opposite in direction.
:::

For the puck above, a third force of 50 N at 53.1° south of west would hold it in equilibrium.

Objects hung from cables are in equilibrium. Resolve each tension into components, then balance the horizontal and vertical forces.

:::example A sign on two cables
A 12 kg sign hangs at rest from two cables. Each cable makes an angle of 40° with the horizontal. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. What is the tension in each cable?
@step Horizontal: the two cables pull sideways with equal and opposite components, $F_T\cos 40°$ each, so they cancel. This is why the two tensions are equal.
@step Vertical: the upward components of the two tensions balance the weight. $2F_T\sin 40° = mg$.
@step $F_T = \dfrac{mg}{2\sin 40°} = \dfrac{12 \times 9.81}{2 \times 0.6428}$.
@answer $F_T = 91.6$ N in each cable.
:::

The weight of the sign is 117.7 N, so each cable carries more than half of it. Only the vertical part of each tension helps to hold the sign up. The flatter the cables, the larger the tension has to be.

:::example A lamp pulled to one side
A 7 kg lamp hangs from a cable. A horizontal rope pulls the lamp to one side and holds it at rest, with the cable at 30° to the vertical. Find the tension in the cable and the force in the rope.
@step The angle is measured from the vertical, so the vertical component of the tension is $F_T\cos 30°$ and the horizontal component is $F_T\sin 30°$.
@step Vertical: $F_T\cos 30° = mg$, so $F_T = \dfrac{7 \times 9.81}{\cos 30°} = 79.3$ N.
@step Horizontal: the rope balances the horizontal component. $F_{\text{rope}} = F_T\sin 30° = 79.29 \times 0.5$.
@answer The tension in the cable is 79.3 N, and the rope pulls with 39.6 N.
:::

:::mistake Sharing the weight equally between the cables without resolving
Two cables that hold a 117.7 N sign do not each have a tension of 58.9 N unless they are vertical. Each tension must be large enough for its **vertical component** to be half the weight.
:::

:::summary
- Resolve a force at an angle: $F_x = F\cos\theta$ and $F_y = F\sin\theta$, with $\theta$ measured from the horizontal.
- Apply Newton's second law to each direction separately.
- A pull that slants upwards reduces the normal force: $F_N = mg - F\sin\theta$. Friction is then $\mu_k F_N$.
- Perpendicular forces: $F_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2}$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{F_y}{F_x}$.
- In equilibrium the forces balance in each direction. For a load on two equal cables at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, $2F_T\sin\theta = mg$.
:::
