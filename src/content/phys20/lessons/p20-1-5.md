Drop a hammer and a feather on Earth and the hammer lands first. An astronaut tried the same thing on the Moon, where there is no air, and the two landed together. Take away air resistance and every falling object, heavy or light, speeds up at exactly the same rate.

## The acceleration due to gravity

:::definition Free fall
An object is in **free fall** when gravity is the only force acting on it. Near the surface of the Earth, every object in free fall has the same acceleration, the **acceleration due to gravity**:

$$g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2 \text{, downwards}$$

This holds whatever the mass of the object, and whether it is moving up, moving down or stopped for an instant at the top of its path.
:::

Free fall is uniformly accelerated motion, so the four equations of motion all apply, with $a = g$. In this lesson we ignore air resistance, which is a good approximation for small, dense objects that fall a short way.

## Dropped from rest

For an object dropped from rest, take **down as positive**. Then $u = 0$ and $a = +9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$, and the equations become short:

$$v = gt \qquad d = \tfrac12gt^2 \qquad v^2 = 2gd$$

:::example Speed and distance after a known time
A steel ball is dropped from rest. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. How fast is it moving after 3 s, and how far has it fallen?
@step Speed: $v = gt = 9.81 \times 3 = 29.4$ m/s.
@step Distance: $d = \tfrac12gt^2 = \tfrac12 \times 9.81 \times 3^2 = 44.1$ m.
@answer After 3 s the ball is moving at 29.4 m/s and has fallen 44.1 m.
:::

:::example The depth of a well
A pebble is dropped from rest down a well and hits the water 2.4 s later. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. How far did it fall?
@step $d = \tfrac12gt^2 = \tfrac12 \times 9.81 \times 2.4^2$.
@step $2.4^2 = 5.76$, so $d = \tfrac12 \times 9.81 \times 5.76 = 28.3$ m.
@answer The water is 28.3 m below the top of the well.
:::

:::example Time and landing speed from a height
A key is dropped from rest from a height of 20 m. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. Find the time it takes to reach the ground and its speed just before it lands.
@step Time: $d = \tfrac12gt^2$ gives $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2d}{g}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 20}{9.81}} = \sqrt{4.077} = 2.02$ s.
@step Speed: $v^2 = 2gd = 2 \times 9.81 \times 20 = 392.4$, so $v = \sqrt{392.4} = 19.8$ m/s.
@answer The key falls for 2.02 s and lands at 19.8 m/s.
:::

:::mistake Thinking a heavier object falls faster
In free fall the mass does not appear in any of the equations. A heavy ball and a light ball dropped together from the same height land together. In everyday life it is air resistance, not mass, that makes a feather fall slowly.
:::

## Thrown straight up

For an object thrown upwards, take **up as positive**. The initial velocity $u$ is then positive, and the acceleration is negative, because gravity pulls downwards:

$$a = -9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The object slows down by 9.81 m/s every second, stops for an instant at its highest point, and then falls. At the highest point, $v = 0$.

:::example Time to the top and greatest height
A ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. How long does it take to reach its highest point, and how high does it rise?
@step At the top, $v = 0$. Use $v = u + at$: $0 = 15 - 9.81t$, so $t = \dfrac{15}{9.81} = 1.53$ s.
@step For the height, use $v^2 = u^2 + 2ad$: $0 = 15^2 - 2 \times 9.81 \times d$, so $d = \dfrac{225}{19.62} = 11.5$ m.
@answer The ball takes 1.53 s to reach its highest point, 11.5 m above the point of release.
:::

The motion is symmetrical. The ball takes as long to come down as it took to go up, so it returns to the thrower's hand after $2 \times 1.53 = 3.06$ s, moving downwards at the same 15 m/s it was thrown with.

One equation covers the whole flight, up and down. A negative velocity simply means that the ball is on its way down.

:::example Velocity and height part way through
A ball is thrown straight up at 18 m/s. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$ and take up as positive. Find its velocity 2.4 s after it is thrown, and its height above the point of release at that moment.
@step Velocity: $v = u + at = 18 - 9.81 \times 2.4 = 18 - 23.5 = -5.5$ m/s.
@step Height: $d = ut + \tfrac12at^2 = 18 \times 2.4 - \tfrac12 \times 9.81 \times 2.4^2 = 43.2 - 28.3 = 14.9$ m.
@answer The velocity is $-5.5$ m/s, so the ball is already falling, and it is 14.9 m above the point of release.
:::

:::mistake Making the acceleration zero at the top
At its highest point the ball's velocity is zero, but its acceleration is still $9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$ downwards. If the acceleration were zero there, the ball would stay at the top and never come down.
:::

:::mistake Giving g the wrong sign
Choose the positive direction first and keep to it. With up as positive, an upward throw has a positive $u$ and $a = -9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. Using $+9.81$ makes the ball speed up as it rises.
:::

:::summary
- In free fall, gravity is the only force, and the acceleration is $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$ downwards for every object.
- Dropped from rest, with down as positive: $v = gt$, $d = \tfrac12gt^2$ and $v^2 = 2gd$.
- Thrown upwards, with up as positive: $a = -9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$, and $v = 0$ at the highest point.
- The trip up and the trip down take the same time, and the object returns at the speed it left with.
- The acceleration is never zero during the flight, not even at the top.
:::
