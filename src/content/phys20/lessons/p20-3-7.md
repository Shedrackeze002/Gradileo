Two cranes lift the same load to the same height. One takes ten seconds and the other takes a minute. They do the same work, but nobody would call them equally good. What separates them is how **fast** they do the work, and how much of the energy they are fed ends up where it is wanted.

## Power

:::definition Power
**Power**, $P$, is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred:

$$P = \dfrac{W}{t}$$

Power is measured in **watts** (W): 1 W = 1 J/s. A kilowatt (kW) is 1000 W.
:::

:::example Power from work and time
A machine does 6000 J of work in 20 s. What is its power?
@step $P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{6000}{20}$.
@answer $P = 300$ W.
:::

To lift something at a steady speed you do work against gravity, and that work is the gain in gravitational potential energy, $mgh$.

:::example Climbing stairs
A student of mass 60 kg climbs a staircase 4 m high in 5 s at a steady speed. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. What power does the student develop against gravity?
@step Work: $W = mgh = 60 \times 9.81 \times 4 = 2354.4$ J.
@step $P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{2354.4}{5}$.
@answer $P = 470.9$ W.
:::

For something that moves at a steady speed there is a shortcut. The work is $W = Fd$, so

$$P = \dfrac{Fd}{t} = Fv$$

because $\dfrac{d}{t}$ is the speed. Power is force × speed.

:::example The power of an engine
A car travels along a level road at a steady 25 m/s. Air resistance and friction together push back on it with 800 N. What power must the engine deliver to the wheels?
@step The speed is steady, so the forces balance: the driving force is 800 N.
@step $P = Fv = 800 \times 25 = 20\,000$ W.
@answer $P = 20$ kW.
:::

:::example Top speed
The engine of a boat delivers 60 kW to the propeller. At its top speed the water pushes back on the boat with 2500 N. What is the top speed of the boat?
@step Change the units: $P = 60\ \text{kW} = 60\,000$ W.
@step Rearrange $P = Fv$: $v = \dfrac{P}{F} = \dfrac{60\,000}{2500}$.
@answer $v = 24$ m/s.
:::

:::example Lifting at a steady speed
A motor delivers 1500 W to a cable that lifts a 100 kg load at a steady speed. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. At what speed does the load rise?
@step At a steady speed the tension equals the weight: $F = mg = 100 \times 9.81 = 981$ N.
@step $v = \dfrac{P}{F} = \dfrac{1500}{981}$.
@answer $v = 1.53$ m/s.
:::

Electricity bills use the **kilowatt-hour** (kW·h). In spite of its name it is a unit of energy, not of power: the energy transferred by 1 kW in one hour, which is $1000 \times 3600 = 3.6 \times 10^{6}$ J.

:::mistake Confusing power with energy
A powerful machine does not necessarily do more work. It does its work **faster**. A 100 W motor and a 1000 W motor can lift the same load to the same height and do the same work, and the second simply takes one tenth of the time.
:::

## Efficiency

No machine turns all the energy it takes in into useful work. Friction, air resistance and electrical resistance change part of it into thermal energy, and some becomes sound.

:::definition Efficiency
The **efficiency** of a machine is the fraction of the energy it takes in that comes out in the useful form:

$$\text{efficiency} = \dfrac{\text{useful energy out}}{\text{total energy in}} \times 100\%$$

The same ratio can be written with powers: useful power out ÷ total power in.
:::

:::example Efficiency as a percent
An electric motor takes in 1000 J of energy and gives out 800 J as useful work. Find its efficiency and the energy it wastes.
@step Efficiency: $\dfrac{800}{1000} \times 100\%$.
@step Wasted: $1000 - 800 = 200$ J, mostly as thermal energy.
@answer The motor is 80% efficient and wastes 200 J.
:::

:::example A hoist
An electric hoist uses 650 J of electrical energy to lift a 10 kg load through 5 m. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. What is the efficiency of the hoist?
@step Useful energy out: $mgh = 10 \times 9.81 \times 5 = 490.5$ J.
@step Efficiency: $\dfrac{490.5}{650} \times 100\%$.
@answer 75.5%.
:::

When the efficiency is known, use it as a decimal. Multiply the input by it to find the useful output, and divide the useful output by it to find the input.

:::example How long a lift takes
A motor takes in electrical energy at 800 W and is 75% efficient. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. How long does it take to lift a 150 kg load through 8 m?
@step Useful power: $0.75 \times 800 = 600$ W.
@step Useful work: $mgh = 150 \times 9.81 \times 8 = 11\,772$ J.
@step $t = \dfrac{W}{P} = \dfrac{11\,772}{600}$.
@answer $t = 19.6$ s.
:::

:::example The energy a fuel must supply
The engine of a 1200 kg car is 25% efficient. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. Counting only the work done against gravity, how much energy must the fuel supply for the car to climb a hill 50 m high at a steady speed?
@step Useful energy out: $mgh = 1200 \times 9.81 \times 50 = 588\,600$ J.
@step Energy in: $\dfrac{588\,600}{0.25} = 2\,354\,400$ J.
@answer About 2354 kJ. Three quarters of it leaves the engine as thermal energy.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
The useful output is always less than the input, so an efficiency is always less than 100%. If your answer is more than 100%, you have divided the input by the output. To find an **input** from an output, divide by the efficiency: the input must come out larger.
:::

:::summary
- Power is the rate of doing work: $P = \dfrac{W}{t}$, in watts. 1 W = 1 J/s.
- Lifting at a steady speed: $P = \dfrac{mgh}{t}$.
- At a steady speed, $P = Fv$.
- Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ total energy in × 100%.
- Useful output = efficiency × input, and input = useful output ÷ efficiency, with the efficiency as a decimal.
:::
