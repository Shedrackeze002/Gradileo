A ferry does not have to sail due east or due north. Most real motion points somewhere in between, and so do most forces. To work with a vector that points at an angle, physics uses one idea again and again: replace it with two vectors at right angles that together do the same job.

## Components of a vector

:::definition Components
The **components** of a vector are two perpendicular vectors that add up to it, usually one horizontal and one vertical, or one east-west and one north-south. Finding them is called **resolving** the vector.

For a vector of size $R$ at an angle $\theta$ measured from the horizontal:

$$R_x = R\cos\theta \qquad R_y = R\sin\theta$$
:::

The vector and its two components form a right triangle, with the vector as the hypotenuse. The component next to the angle uses the cosine, and the component opposite the angle uses the sine.

Set your calculator to **degrees** before you start.

:::example Horizontal and vertical components
A ball is kicked at 15 m/s, at 30° above the horizontal. Find the horizontal and vertical components of its velocity.
@step Horizontal: $R_x = R\cos\theta = 15 \times \cos 30° = 13.0$ m/s.
@step Vertical: $R_y = R\sin\theta = 15 \times \sin 30° = 7.5$ m/s.
@answer 13.0 m/s horizontally and 7.5 m/s vertically.
:::

Compass directions are given as an angle from one of the four main directions. "20° north of east" means: face east, then turn 20° towards the north.

:::example East and north components
A force of 40 N points at 20° north of east. Find its east component and its north component.
@step The angle is measured from east, so the east component is next to the angle: $40 \times \cos 20° = 37.6$ N.
@step The north component is opposite the angle: $40 \times \sin 20° = 13.7$ N.
@answer 37.6 N east and 13.7 N north.
:::

A component that points west or south is negative when east and north are the positive directions.

:::example A negative component
East and north are the positive directions. A displacement of 28 km points at 25° south of east. Find its east and north components.
@step East-west: $28 \times \cos 25° = 25.4$ km, pointing east, so the east component is $+25.4$ km.
@step North-south: $28 \times \sin 25° = 11.8$ km, pointing south, so the north component is $-11.8$ km.
@answer East component 25.4 km, north component $-11.8$ km.
:::

:::mistake Swapping sine and cosine
The cosine always goes with the component **next to** the angle you were given. If an angle is measured from the vertical, or from north, the component along that direction uses the cosine. Sketch the right triangle and mark the angle before you choose.
:::

## Adding vectors in two dimensions

Two vectors at right angles form the two shorter sides of a right triangle, and their resultant is the hypotenuse. Its size comes from the Pythagorean theorem, and its direction from the tangent.

$$R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2} \qquad \tan\theta = \dfrac{R_y}{R_x}$$

:::example Two displacements at right angles
A drone flies 6 m east and then 8 m north. Find the size and the direction of the resultant displacement.
@step Size: $R = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$ m.
@step Direction: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{8}{6}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{8}{6}\right) = 53.1°$.
@step The angle is measured from east, turning towards the north.
@answer 10 m at 53.1° north of east.
:::

:::example Two forces at right angles
Two forces act on a crate: 8 N east and 26 N north. Find the size of the resultant and its direction.
@step Size: $R = \sqrt{8^2 + 26^2} = \sqrt{740} = 27.2$ N.
@step Direction: $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{26}{8}\right) = 72.9°$ north of east.
@answer 27.2 N at 72.9° north of east.
:::

When the vectors are not at right angles, resolve each one into components first. Then add the east components, add the north components, and build the resultant from the two totals.

:::example Adding a vector at an angle
A boat sails 12 km east, and then 17 km at 30° north of east. Find the size and the direction of the resultant displacement.
@step Resolve the second displacement: east $17\cos 30° = 14.72$ km, north $17\sin 30° = 8.5$ km. The first displacement is 12 km east and 0 km north.
@step Add the components: east $12 + 14.72 = 26.72$ km, north $0 + 8.5 = 8.5$ km.
@step Size: $R = \sqrt{26.72^2 + 8.5^2} = 28.0$ km.
@step Direction: $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{8.5}{26.72}\right) = 17.6°$ north of east.
@answer 28.0 km at 17.6° north of east.
:::

:::mistake Adding the sizes of vectors that point in different directions
6 m east and 8 m north do not make 14 m. The sizes add only when the vectors point the same way. At right angles, use the Pythagorean theorem. At any other angle, add the components.
:::

:::mistake Giving a resultant without its direction
A resultant is a vector, so "10 m" is only half the answer. State the angle and what it is measured from: "53.1° north of east".

:::summary
- A vector at an angle can be resolved into two perpendicular components: $R_x = R\cos\theta$ and $R_y = R\sin\theta$, with $\theta$ measured from the horizontal.
- The cosine goes with the component next to the angle.
- Components that point west or south are negative when east and north are positive.
- For two vectors at right angles: $R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2}$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{R_y}{R_x}$.
- For any other vectors, resolve each one, add the components, and then find the size and the direction of the resultant.
:::
