A child on a roundabout moves at a steady speed, and a speedometer fixed to the seat would never change its reading. Yet the child is accelerating the whole time. This lesson explains how that can be, and gives the acceleration a size and a direction.

## Period, frequency and speed

:::definition Uniform circular motion
An object is in **uniform circular motion** when it moves round a circle at a steady speed.

- The **period**, $T$, is the time for one complete turn, in seconds.
- The **frequency**, $f$, is the number of turns each second, in hertz (Hz).

$$f = \dfrac{1}{T} \qquad T = \dfrac{1}{f}$$
:::

In one period the object travels once round the circle, a distance of one circumference, $2\pi r$. Its speed is therefore

$$v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$$

:::example Period and frequency
A roundabout makes 10 complete turns in 25 s at a steady rate. Find its period and its frequency.
@step Period: $T = \dfrac{25}{10} = 2.5$ s for each turn.
@step Frequency: $f = \dfrac{10}{25} = 0.4$ turns each second.
@answer $T = 2.5$ s and $f = 0.4$ Hz.
:::

:::example The speed of a rider
A child sits 1.5 m from the centre of that roundabout. How fast is the child moving?
@step $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 1.5}{2.5}$.
@answer $v = 3.77$ m/s.
:::

A child nearer the centre goes round in the same 2.5 s but travels round a smaller circle, so that child is slower. Every point on a turning object has the same period, but not the same speed.

:::example Revolutions per minute
A wheel of radius 0.25 m spins at 150 revolutions per minute. Find its period and the speed of a point on its rim.
@step One minute is 60 s, so $T = \dfrac{60}{150} = 0.4$ s.
@step $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 0.25}{0.4}$.
@answer $T = 0.4$ s and $v = 3.93$ m/s.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up period and frequency
Ten turns in 25 s is a period of $25 \div 10 = 2.5$ s, not $10 \div 25$. Ask yourself which you want: the **time for one turn** (time ÷ turns) or the **turns in one second** (turns ÷ time).
:::

## Centripetal acceleration

Velocity is a vector: it has a size and a direction. In uniform circular motion the size stays the same, but the direction turns all the time. A changing velocity is an acceleration, so the object is accelerating even though it never gets faster or slower.

The velocity points along the **tangent** to the circle, the direction in which the object is moving at that instant. The acceleration points to the centre of the circle, at right angles to the velocity. An acceleration at right angles to the motion turns the object without changing its speed.

:::definition Centripetal acceleration
The acceleration of an object in uniform circular motion is called **centripetal acceleration**, $a_c$. It points to the centre of the circle, and its size is

$$a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$$

"Centripetal" means centre-seeking. Putting $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$ into the formula gives a second form:

$$a_c = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2}$$
:::

:::example From the speed
A car travels at a steady 20 m/s round a bend of radius 80 m. What is its centripetal acceleration?
@step $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r} = \dfrac{20^2}{80} = \dfrac{400}{80}$.
@answer $a_c = 5\ \text{m/s}^2$, towards the centre of the bend.
:::

:::example From the period
A seat on a fairground ride moves round a circle of radius 4 m with a period of 5 s. What is its centripetal acceleration?
@step $a_c = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 \times 4}{5^2} = \dfrac{157.91}{25}$.
@answer $a_c = 6.32\ \text{m/s}^2$.
:::

:::example The greatest safe speed
For the comfort of its passengers, a train must keep its centripetal acceleration below 0.4 times $g$. Use $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. What is the greatest speed at which it can take a curve of radius 60 m?
@step Greatest acceleration: $a_c = 0.4 \times 9.81 = 3.924\ \text{m/s}^2$.
@step Rearrange $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$: $v = \sqrt{a_c r} = \sqrt{3.924 \times 60} = \sqrt{235.44}$.
@answer $v = 15.3$ m/s.
:::

The speed is squared in the formula, so twice the speed round the same bend needs four times the acceleration. A tighter bend, with a smaller radius, also needs more.

:::mistake Thinking that a steady speed means no acceleration
Acceleration is the rate of change of **velocity**, and velocity includes direction. An object that moves round a circle at a steady speed is always changing direction, so it always has an acceleration, $\dfrac{v^2}{r}$, towards the centre.
:::

:::summary
- Period $T$ is the time for one turn, and frequency $f$ is the number of turns each second: $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$.
- Speed round a circle: $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$.
- The velocity points along the tangent, and its direction keeps changing.
- Centripetal acceleration points to the centre: $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2}$.
- Twice the speed needs four times the acceleration.
:::
