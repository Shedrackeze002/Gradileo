When a bus pulls away from a stop you feel pushed back into your seat, and when it brakes you tip forward. At a steady speed on a straight road you feel nothing at all. What your body notices is not velocity. It is the change in velocity, and that is what this lesson measures.

## Acceleration

:::definition Acceleration
**Acceleration** is the change in velocity each second. It is a vector, measured in metres per second squared, m/s².

$$a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$$

where $u$ is the initial velocity, $v$ is the final velocity and $t$ is the time taken. Motion with a constant acceleration is called **uniformly accelerated motion**: the velocity changes by the same amount every second.
:::

An acceleration of $4\ \text{m/s}^2$ means that the velocity grows by 4 m/s in each second.

:::example Speeding up
A car moving in the positive direction speeds up steadily from 9 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s. What is its acceleration?
@step Write what you know: $u = 9$ m/s, $v = 25$ m/s, $t = 4$ s.
@step $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} = \dfrac{25 - 9}{4} = \dfrac{16}{4}$.
@answer $a = 4\ \text{m/s}^2$.
:::

Velocity is a vector, so a velocity in the negative direction must go into the equation with a minus sign.

:::example Turning round
East is the positive direction. A ball is moving east at 3 m/s. A steady force acts on it, and 4 s later it is moving west at 2 m/s. What is its acceleration?
@step East is positive, so $u = +3$ m/s and $v = -2$ m/s.
@step $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} = \dfrac{-2 - 3}{4} = \dfrac{-5}{4}$.
@answer $a = -1.25\ \text{m/s}^2$, which is $1.25\ \text{m/s}^2$ [W]. The ball slowed down, stopped for an instant and then sped up towards the west, all with the same acceleration.
:::

:::example Changing the units first
A car travelling at 90 km/h in the positive direction brakes steadily and stops in 4 s. What is its acceleration in m/s²?
@step Change km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6: $90 \div 3.6 = 25$ m/s.
@step The car stops, so $v = 0$: $a = \dfrac{0 - 25}{4}$.
@answer $a = -6.25\ \text{m/s}^2$.
:::

A negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down. What matters is whether the acceleration points the same way as the velocity.

| Velocity | Acceleration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| positive | positive | speeds up |
| positive | negative | slows down |
| negative | negative | speeds up, in the negative direction |
| negative | positive | slows down |

:::mistake Saying the acceleration is zero when the velocity is zero
A ball thrown straight up stops for an instant at its highest point. Its velocity there is zero, but its velocity is still changing, from upwards to downwards, so its acceleration is not zero. Velocity and acceleration are different quantities.
:::

## Velocity-time graphs

A velocity-time graph shows how fast an object is moving, and in which direction, at each moment. Two things can be read from it.

:::definition Slope and area of a velocity-time graph
The **slope** of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration.

The **area** between the graph and the time axis is the displacement. Area above the time axis is displacement in the positive direction, and area below it is displacement in the negative direction.
:::

:::example Accelerations from the slope
Use the graph above. Find the acceleration of the lift from 0 s to 4 s, and from 8 s to 14 s.
@step From 0 s to 4 s the velocity rises from 0 to 8 m/s: $a = \dfrac{8 - 0}{4} = 2\ \text{m/s}^2$.
@step From 8 s to 14 s the velocity changes from 8 m/s to $-4$ m/s: $a = \dfrac{-4 - 8}{6} = \dfrac{-12}{6} = -2\ \text{m/s}^2$.
@answer $2\ \text{m/s}^2$, then $-2\ \text{m/s}^2$. Between 4 s and 8 s the line is horizontal, so the acceleration is zero and the lift rises at a steady 8 m/s.
:::

:::example A displacement from the area
Use the same graph. What is the displacement of the lift from 0 s to 8 s?
@step Split the area under the graph into a triangle (0 s to 4 s) and a rectangle (4 s to 8 s).
@step Triangle: $\tfrac12 \times 4 \times 8 = 16$ m. Rectangle: $4 \times 8 = 32$ m.
@answer The displacement is $16 + 32 = 48$ m upwards.
:::

When the line crosses the time axis, the velocity changes sign: the object has turned round.

:::example Displacement and distance when the object turns round
Use the same graph. For the whole 14 s, find the displacement of the lift and the distance it travels.
@step The line falls 2 m/s every second after 8 s, so it reaches zero at 12 s. The lift rises until 12 s and then moves down.
@step Area above the axis, from 0 s to 12 s: $16 + 32 + \tfrac12 \times 4 \times 8 = 64$ m.
@step Area below the axis, from 12 s to 14 s: $\tfrac12 \times 2 \times 4 = 4$ m.
@step Displacement: $64 - 4 = 60$ m. Distance: $64 + 4 = 68$ m.
@answer The displacement is 60 m upwards, and the lift travels 68 m.
:::

:::mistake Reading a downward slope as moving backwards
From 8 s to 12 s the line slopes downwards, but the velocity is still positive: the lift is still rising, only more slowly. The object moves backwards only where the graph is **below** the time axis.
:::

:::summary
- Acceleration is the change in velocity each second: $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$, in m/s².
- Put velocities in with their signs. An object speeds up when its acceleration points the same way as its velocity, and slows down when it points the opposite way.
- The slope of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration.
- The area between a velocity-time graph and the time axis is the displacement. Area below the axis counts as negative.
- For the distance travelled, add the areas without their signs.
:::
