Read the ingredients on a bottle of shampoo or a tube of sun cream, and almost every one is a compound of carbon. The gas in a camping stove, the plastic of a water bottle, the sugar in fruit and the DNA in your cells are all built on frameworks of carbon atoms. Chemists know of many millions of carbon compounds, far more than the compounds of all the other elements put together.

In this lesson you will tell organic compounds from inorganic ones, sort hydrocarbons into their families, and move between the different ways of writing and drawing an organic molecule.

## Organic compounds

:::definition Organic compound
An **organic compound** is a compound of carbon in which carbon atoms are nearly always bonded to hydrogen atoms, and often to oxygen, nitrogen or a halogen as well. A few simple carbon compounds are classed as **inorganic**: the oxides of carbon ($\ce{CO}$ and $\ce{CO2}$), carbonates and hydrogen carbonates (such as $\ce{CaCO3}$ and $\ce{NaHCO3}$), cyanides (such as $\ce{KCN}$) and carbides (such as $\ce{CaC2}$).
:::

Why does carbon form so many compounds? Three facts about the carbon atom explain it:

- It has four valence electrons, so it forms **four covalent bonds**.
- Carbon atoms bond strongly to **each other**, so they can make chains of any length, branched chains and rings.
- Two carbon atoms can share one, two or three pairs of electrons, making **single, double and triple bonds**.

:::note Made in a laboratory
Chemists once thought that organic compounds could only be made by living things. In 1828 the chemist Friedrich Wöhler made urea, $\ce{CO(NH2)2}$, a compound found in urine, by heating ammonium cyanate, an inorganic salt. Today most of the organic compounds in medicines, plastics and dyes are made in factories, and *organic* simply means "a compound of carbon".
:::

:::example Organic or inorganic?
Which of these compounds are organic: methane, $\ce{CH4}$; carbon dioxide, $\ce{CO2}$; ethanol, $\ce{C2H5OH}$; and sodium carbonate, $\ce{Na2CO3}$?
@step Methane and ethanol contain carbon bonded to hydrogen, so they are organic.
@step Carbon dioxide is an oxide of carbon and sodium carbonate is a carbonate. Both contain carbon, but they are classed as inorganic.
@answer Methane and ethanol.
:::

## Families of hydrocarbons

:::definition Hydrocarbon
A **hydrocarbon** is a compound of carbon and hydrogen only. Crude oil and natural gas are mixtures of hydrocarbons.
:::

Hydrocarbons are sorted into families by the bonds between their carbon atoms, and by whether the carbons form a chain or a ring.

| Family | Carbon–carbon bonds | Example | General formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| alkanes | single bonds only, in a chain | propane, $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH3}$ | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$ |
| alkenes | a double bond | propene, $\ce{CH2=CH-CH3}$ | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ |
| alkynes | a triple bond | propyne, $\ce{CH#C-CH3}$ | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$ |
| cycloalkanes | single bonds, in a ring | cyclohexane, $\ce{C6H12}$ | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ |
| aromatic hydrocarbons | a benzene ring | benzene, $\ce{C6H6}$ | |

The general formulas for alkenes and alkynes are for molecules with one multiple bond.

:::definition Saturated and unsaturated
A **saturated** hydrocarbon has only single bonds between its carbon atoms, so it holds as many hydrogen atoms as its carbons can: alkanes and cycloalkanes are saturated. An **unsaturated** hydrocarbon has at least one carbon–carbon double or triple bond: alkenes, alkynes and cycloalkenes are unsaturated.
:::

Carbon atoms can also join into a ring. A ring with only single bonds is a **cycloalkane**; one with a double bond is a **cycloalkene**. Benzene, $\ce{C6H6}$, is a special ring of six carbon atoms. Instead of forming three separate double bonds, its carbons share six electrons evenly around the whole ring. These shared, or *delocalised*, electrons are drawn as a circle inside the hexagon. Compounds with a benzene ring are called **aromatic**, because many of the first ones found had strong smells. The benzene ring is very stable, and it does not react the way an alkene does.

:::example Classifying hydrocarbons
Classify $\ce{CH3-C#C-CH3}$ and $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH3}$, and say whether each is saturated or unsaturated.
@step $\ce{CH3-C#C-CH3}$ has a triple bond between its two middle carbons, in a chain: it is an alkyne, and unsaturated.
@step $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH3}$ has only single bonds, in a chain: it is an alkane, and saturated.
@answer The first is an unsaturated alkyne; the second is a saturated alkane.
:::

:::mistake One formula, two families
The molecular formula $\ce{C6H12}$ fits both hex-1-ene, an unsaturated alkene, and cyclohexane, a saturated ring. A molecular formula does not always tell you the family, so look at the structure. In the laboratory the two can be told apart with bromine water: an alkene quickly turns orange bromine water colourless, while an alkane or a cycloalkane leaves it orange.
:::

## Four ways to show a molecule

Butane, the fuel in a gas lighter, can be written or drawn in four ways. Each shows a little more, or a little less, than the others.

:::definition Molecular formula
A **molecular formula** gives the number of atoms of each element in one molecule: butane is $\ce{C4H10}$. It says nothing about how the atoms are joined.
:::

:::definition Structural formula
A **structural formula** shows every atom and every bond, each bond as a line. Here is butane:
:::

$$\begin{array}{ccccccccccc} & & \text{H} & & \text{H} & & \text{H} & & \text{H} & & \\ & & | & & | & & | & & | & & \\ \text{H} & - & \text{C} & - & \text{C} & - & \text{C} & - & \text{C} & - & \text{H} \\ & & | & & | & & | & & | & & \\ & & \text{H} & & \text{H} & & \text{H} & & \text{H} & & \end{array}$$

A structural formula is drawn flat, but the molecule is not: the four bonds of each carbon point to the corners of a tetrahedron, and the chain is really a zigzag.

:::definition Condensed formula
A **condensed formula** writes each carbon with the hydrogens bonded to it, and shows only the bonds between carbons: butane is $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH3}$. A branch goes in brackets after the carbon it is joined to, as in $\ce{CH3-CH(CH3)-CH3}$, and a double or triple bond is shown as $=$ or $\equiv$, as in $\ce{CH2=CH-CH3}$ and $\ce{CH#C-CH3}$. The bonds between carbons are sometimes left out too: $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH3}$.
:::

:::definition Line formula
A **line formula** (also called a skeletal formula) shows only the carbon skeleton, as a zigzag line. Each end and each corner of the line is a carbon atom. The hydrogen atoms on carbon are not drawn: each carbon has as many as it needs to make four bonds. A double line is a double bond.
:::

## General formulas

In an unbranched alkane, every carbon carries two hydrogen atoms, and the two end carbons carry one more each. So an alkane with $n$ carbons has $2n + 2$ hydrogens: $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$. Branching does not change the count: pentane and 2-methylbutane are both $\ce{C5H12}$.

Each double bond or ring takes away two hydrogen atoms, and a triple bond takes away four. That gives $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ for alkenes and cycloalkanes, and $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$ for alkynes.

:::example How many hydrogens?
What is the molecular formula of an alkyne with seven carbon atoms and one triple bond?
@step Alkynes are $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$. With $n = 7$: $2(7) - 2 = 12$ hydrogens.
@answer $\ce{C7H12}$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the hydrogens of a branch
For $\ce{CH3-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH3}$ it is easy to count only the chain: $3 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 9$ hydrogens. The methyl branch carries three more, so the molecule has 12: it is $\ce{C5H12}$. Check with $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$: five carbons need $2(5) + 2 = 12$ hydrogens.
:::

:::check c30-hydrocarbon-formula 2
:::

A general formula also works backwards, from a molar mass to the formula.

:::example A formula from a molar mass
An alkene with one double bond has a molar mass of $70.15\ \text{g/mol}$. What is its molecular formula? Use the molar masses C $12.01\ \text{g/mol}$ and H $1.01\ \text{g/mol}$.
@step An alkene is $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$, so $M = 12.01n + 1.01(2n) = 14.03n$.
@step $n = \dfrac{70.15}{14.03} = 5$
@answer $\ce{C5H10}$, a pentene.
:::

:::summary
- An **organic compound** is a compound of carbon, nearly always bonded to hydrogen. Oxides of carbon, carbonates, hydrogen carbonates, cyanides and carbides are inorganic.
- Carbon forms four bonds and bonds to itself in chains, branches and rings, with single, double or triple bonds.
- **Hydrocarbons** contain only carbon and hydrogen: alkanes (single bonds), alkenes (a double bond), alkynes (a triple bond), cycloalkanes and cycloalkenes (rings), and aromatic hydrocarbons (a benzene ring).
- **Saturated**: single bonds only. **Unsaturated**: at least one double or triple bond.
- A molecule can be shown by its **molecular**, **structural**, **condensed** or **line formula**. In a line formula each end and corner is a carbon, with enough hydrogens to make four bonds.
- General formulas: alkanes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$; alkenes and cycloalkanes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$; alkynes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$.
:::
