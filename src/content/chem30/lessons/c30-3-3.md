The fuel in a gas lighter and the gas in some aerosol cans can both have the formula $\ce{C4H10}$, yet they are not the same substance. One is butane, which boils at about $-1^\circ\text{C}$; the other is 2-methylpropane, which boils at about $-12^\circ\text{C}$. The same atoms, joined in a different order, make a different compound with different properties.

In this lesson you will recognise structural isomers, find all the isomers of a small alkane, and tell real isomers from one compound drawn two ways.

## Structural isomers

:::definition Isomers
**Isomers** are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structures. Because their structures differ, they are different compounds, with different names and different properties.
:::

:::definition Structural isomers
**Structural isomers** have their atoms joined in a different order: a straight chain or a branched one, a branch on a different carbon, a multiple bond or a functional group in a different place, or even a different functional group.
:::

| Formula | Isomers | Names | Boiling points |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\ce{C4H10}$ | $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH3}$ and $\ce{CH3-CH(CH3)-CH3}$ | butane and 2-methylpropane | about $-1^\circ\text{C}$ and $-12^\circ\text{C}$ |
| $\ce{C2H6O}$ | $\ce{CH3-CH2-OH}$ and $\ce{CH3-O-CH3}$ | ethanol and methoxymethane | about $78^\circ\text{C}$ and $-24^\circ\text{C}$ |

The more carbon atoms, the more ways there are to arrange them. Alkanes have:

| Formula | $\ce{C4H10}$ | $\ce{C5H12}$ | $\ce{C6H14}$ | $\ce{C7H16}$ | $\ce{C8H18}$ | $\ce{C10H22}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Structural isomers | 2 | 3 | 5 | 9 | 18 | 75 |

## Finding the isomers of an alkane

Work systematically, from the longest chain to the shortest, and name each structure as you go. Two drawings with the same name are the same compound, so a repeated name means you can cross one out.

1. Draw the **straight chain**.
2. Make the chain **one carbon shorter** and put the extra carbon on as a methyl group, on each inner carbon in turn. Never on an end carbon: that just makes the chain longer again.
3. Make the chain **two carbons shorter** and place the two extra carbons as two methyl groups (on the same carbon or on different ones), or as one ethyl group (never on carbon 2, which lengthens the chain).
4. Keep going until the chain is too short to hold the branches.

The three isomers of $\ce{C5H12}$ also show how a structure changes a property. The more branched the molecule, the more compact it is and the less of its surface touches its neighbours, so the London dispersion forces between molecules are weaker: pentane boils at about $36^\circ\text{C}$, 2-methylbutane at about $28^\circ\text{C}$ and 2,2-dimethylpropane at about $10^\circ\text{C}$.

:::example The isomers of hexane
Find all the structural isomers of $\ce{C6H14}$.
@step Six carbons in a chain: hexane, $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH3}$.
@step Five in the chain and a methyl group: on carbon 2 gives 2-methylpentane, $\ce{CH3-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH2-CH3}$; on carbon 3 gives 3-methylpentane, $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH3}$. On carbon 4 it would be 2-methylpentane again, numbered from the other end.
@step Four in the chain and two methyl groups: both on carbon 2 gives 2,2-dimethylbutane, $\ce{CH3-C(CH3)2-CH2-CH3}$; on carbons 2 and 3 gives 2,3-dimethylbutane, $\ce{CH3-CH(CH3)-CH(CH3)-CH3}$. An ethyl group on a four-carbon chain could only go on carbon 2, which makes a five-carbon chain again.
@step Three carbons cannot hold three branches without one of them lengthening the chain.
@answer Five isomers: hexane, 2-methylpentane, 3-methylpentane, 2,2-dimethylbutane and 2,3-dimethylbutane.
:::

## The same compound, drawn two ways

A molecule can be written from either end, and its chain can be drawn bent. Atoms also turn freely about single bonds, so a molecule has no fixed "shape on paper". None of this makes a new compound.

:::example Same compound or isomers?
How are $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH3}$ and $\ce{CH3-CH(CH2CH3)-CH3}$ related?
@step The first has a four-carbon chain with a methyl group on carbon 2 (numbering from the right): 2-methylbutane.
@step In the second, the longest chain runs from a $\ce{CH3}$ through the middle carbon into the ethyl group: four carbons, with a methyl group on carbon 2. It is also 2-methylbutane.
@answer The same compound, drawn two ways. They are not isomers of each other.
:::

:::mistake Drawn differently is not the same as isomers
Before you call two structures isomers, name them both. If the names match, they are one compound drawn differently. If the names differ but the molecular formulas match, they are isomers. If the molecular formulas differ, they are not isomers at all, however alike they look.
:::

## Isomers in different families

Isomers do not have to belong to the same family. Ethanol and methoxymethane are both $\ce{C2H6O}$: one is an alcohol and the other an ether. Moving a double bond, or swapping a functional group for another with the same atoms, also gives isomers.

:::example An alcohol and an ether
Is methoxyethane, $\ce{CH3-O-CH2-CH3}$, an isomer of propan-1-ol, $\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-OH}$?
@step Methoxyethane has 3 carbons, $3 + 2 + 3 = 8$ hydrogens and 1 oxygen: $\ce{C3H8O}$.
@step Propan-1-ol has 3 carbons, $3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 8$ hydrogens and 1 oxygen: $\ce{C3H8O}$.
@answer Yes: same molecular formula, different structures, so they are isomers, even though one is an ether and the other an alcohol.
:::

:::note Geometric isomers
A double bond cannot turn. In but-2-ene, $\ce{CH3-CH=CH-CH3}$, the two $\ce{CH3}$ groups can be on the same side of the double bond (*cis*) or on opposite sides (*trans*), and these are two different compounds. This kind of isomer is studied in later courses; in this course, but-2-ene is treated as one compound.
:::

:::summary
- **Isomers** have the same molecular formula but different structures, so they are different compounds with different properties.
- **Structural isomers** differ in the order their atoms are joined: chain or branches, the position of a branch, bond or group, or the functional group itself.
- To find the isomers of an alkane, shorten the chain one carbon at a time and place the extra carbons as branches on inner carbons, naming each structure to catch repeats. $\ce{C4H10}$ has 2 isomers, $\ce{C5H12}$ 3 and $\ce{C6H14}$ 5.
- More branching means weaker London dispersion forces and a lower boiling point.
- A molecule written from the other end, or with its chain bent, is the same compound. Name both structures: the same name means the same compound.
:::
