The smell of a ripe banana, a pineapple or a pear sweet comes from esters, small molecules that fruits make from acids and alcohols. Joining molecules together is also how plastics are made: a single plastic bag is made of chains of thousands of ethene molecules linked end to end, and the polyester in a shirt is a chain of acid and alcohol molecules joined by ester links.

In this lesson you will name the ester formed from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol, find the acid and alcohol that make an ester, and tell addition polymers from condensation polymers and find their monomers.

## Esterification

:::definition Esterification
**Esterification** is the reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol to form an ester and water. It is a **condensation reaction**: two molecules join, and a small molecule, water, is released.
:::

The $\ce{-OH}$ of the acid's carboxyl group and the hydrogen of the alcohol's $\ce{-OH}$ group leave as water, and the acid and alcohol join through an oxygen atom:

$$\ce{CH3-COOH + CH3-CH2-OH <=>[H2SO4] CH3-COO-CH2-CH3 + H2O}$$

The reaction is slow and reversible, so a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid are added as a catalyst. In the laboratory, the acid, the alcohol and the catalyst are warmed together in a water bath. The mixture is then poured into sodium hydrogen carbonate solution, which neutralises the leftover acid, and the fruity smell of the ester can be noticed by gently wafting the air above it towards the nose.

:::definition Naming an ester
An ester's name has two words. The first comes from the **alcohol**, named as an alkyl group: methanol gives methyl, ethanol ethyl, propan-1-ol propyl. The second comes from the **acid**, with -oic acid changed to **-oate**: ethanoic acid gives ethanoate. Ethanoic acid and ethanol make ethyl ethanoate.
:::

:::example Naming an ester
Propanoic acid reacts with methanol. Name the ester, and write the equation.
@step The alcohol, methanol, gives the first word: methyl.
@step The acid, propanoic acid, gives the second word: propanoate.
@step $\ce{CH3-CH2-COOH + CH3-OH <=>[H2SO4] CH3-CH2-COO-CH3 + H2O}$
@answer Methyl propanoate
:::

:::mistake The order of the words
In the condensed formula $\ce{CH3-CH2-COO-CH3}$ the acid part is written first, but in the name the alcohol part comes first: methyl propanoate, not "propyl methanoate". Find the carbon with the $\ce{C=O}$: it and the carbons on its side came from the acid. The chain joined through the other oxygen came from the alcohol. Some books write the alcohol part first, as $\ce{CH3-OOC-CH2-CH3}$: it is the same ester.
:::

To find the acid and alcohol that made an ester, split its name: the -oate word gives the acid (-oic acid) and the alkyl word gives the alcohol (-ol).

:::example From an ester back to its acid and alcohol
Which acid and which alcohol make ethyl methanoate, $\ce{HCOO-CH2-CH3}$?
@step Methanoate comes from methanoic acid, $\ce{HCOOH}$.
@step Ethyl comes from ethanol, $\ce{CH3-CH2-OH}$.
@answer Methanoic acid and ethanol.
:::

Esters are found in fruits and flowers, and many are made for flavourings and perfumes: pentyl ethanoate smells of bananas, ethyl butanoate of pineapples and methyl butanoate of apples. Fats and vegetable oils are esters too, of long-chain carboxylic acids and the alcohol glycerol.

## Polymers

:::definition Polymer and monomer
A **polymer** is a very large molecule made of many small molecules, called **monomers**, joined in a long chain. The part of the chain that repeats is the **repeating unit**. Making a polymer is **polymerisation**.
:::

### Addition polymers

:::definition Addition polymer
An **addition polymer** forms when monomers with a carbon–carbon double bond join: each double bond opens, and the carbons link to the neighbouring monomers. No other product forms, so the polymer has the same atoms as all the monomers together.
:::

$$\ce{n CH2=CH2 -> -[CH2-CH2]_{n}-{}}$$

The repeating unit has the same atoms as the monomer, with a single bond between the two carbons and a bond out at each end. The polymer is named poly(monomer):

| Monomer | Polymer | Repeating unit | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| ethene, $\ce{CH2=CH2}$ | poly(ethene), polyethylene | $\ce{-[CH2-CH2]-{}}$ | bags, bottles |
| propene, $\ce{CH2=CH-CH3}$ | poly(propene) | $\ce{-[CH2-CH(CH3)]-{}}$ | ropes, crates, food tubs |
| chloroethene, $\ce{CH2=CHCl}$ | poly(chloroethene), PVC | $\ce{-[CH2-CHCl]-{}}$ | water pipes, window frames |
| tetrafluoroethene, $\ce{CF2=CF2}$ | poly(tetrafluoroethene), PTFE | $\ce{-[CF2-CF2]-{}}$ | non-stick coatings |
| phenylethene, $\ce{CH2=CH(C6H5)}$ | poly(phenylethene), polystyrene | $\ce{-[CH2-CH(C6H5)]-{}}$ | foam packaging, cups |

:::example The repeating unit of poly(propene)
Draw the repeating unit of poly(propene), and a segment of three units.
@step Propene is $\ce{CH2=CH-CH3}$. The double bond opens, and the $\ce{CH3}$ becomes a branch.
@step Repeating unit: $\ce{-[CH2-CH(CH3)]_{n}-{}}$
@answer A segment of three units: $\ce{-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH(CH3)-{}}$
:::

To find the monomer of an addition polymer, find the two-carbon repeating unit and put the double bond back between its carbons.

:::mistake The double bond does not stay
The repeating unit of poly(chloroethene) is $\ce{-[CH2-CHCl]-{}}$, with a single bond. Writing $\ce{-[CH2=CHCl]-{}}$ is wrong: with a double bond as well as a bond to each neighbour, each carbon would have five bonds. The double bond is used up in linking the monomers.
:::

### Condensation polymers

:::definition Condensation polymer
A **condensation polymer** forms when monomers with two reacting groups each join by a condensation reaction, releasing a small molecule, usually water, at every link.
:::

- A **polyester** forms from a molecule with two $\ce{-OH}$ groups (a diol) and one with two $\ce{-COOH}$ groups (a dicarboxylic acid). Each link is an ester link, $\ce{-COO-{}}$. Ethane-1,2-diol, $\ce{HO-CH2-CH2-OH}$, and benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid, $\ce{HOOC-C6H4-COOH}$, make the polyester of drink bottles and clothing fibres.
- A **polyamide** forms from a molecule with two $\ce{-NH2}$ groups (a diamine) and a dicarboxylic acid. Each link is an amide link, $\ce{-CO-NH-{}}$. Hexane-1,6-diamine, $\ce{H2N-(CH2)6-NH2}$, and hexanedioic acid, $\ce{HOOC-(CH2)4-COOH}$, make nylon.
- Living things make condensation polymers too: **proteins** from amino acids, and **starch** and **cellulose** from glucose.

:::example The monomers of a condensation polymer
A segment of a polymer is $\ce{-O-CH2-CH2-O-CO-(CH2)4-CO-O-CH2-CH2-O-CO-(CH2)4-CO-{}}$. Which monomers made it?
@step The links $\ce{-O-CO-{}}$ are ester links, so it is a polyester, a condensation polymer.
@step Break each link and add back water: an $\ce{-OH}$ goes on each $\ce{C=O}$ carbon and an $\ce{H}$ on each oxygen.
@step The pieces are $\ce{HO-CH2-CH2-OH}$ and $\ce{HOOC-(CH2)4-COOH}$.
@answer Ethane-1,2-diol and hexanedioic acid.
:::

:::tip Addition or condensation?
Look at the monomers. A carbon–carbon double bond means an addition polymer, with no other product. Two reacting groups on each monomer ($\ce{-OH}$, $\ce{-COOH}$ or $\ce{-NH2}$) mean a condensation polymer, with water released at each link.
:::

:::note Plastics and the environment
Most addition polymers are not broken down by living things, so plastic waste can last for hundreds of years. Recycling melts and remoulds some plastics; burning them recovers energy but releases carbon dioxide. Some condensation polymers, such as poly(lactic acid) made from plant sugars, are designed to be biodegradable.
:::

Because an addition polymer loses no atoms, each repeating unit has the molar mass of the monomer. A poly(ethene) chain of 2000 units has a molar mass of $2000 \times 28.06 = 56\,120\ \text{g/mol}$, and dividing the molar mass of a chain by that of its monomer gives the number of units.

:::summary
- **Esterification**: carboxylic acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, with concentrated sulfuric acid as a catalyst. It is a **condensation reaction**.
- An ester's name: the alcohol's alkyl group first, then the acid with -oic acid changed to -oate: ethanol and ethanoic acid make ethyl ethanoate.
- A **polymer** is a long chain of **monomers**; the part that repeats is the repeating unit.
- **Addition polymers** form from monomers with a $\ce{C=C}$ double bond, with no other product: $\ce{n CH2=CH2 -> -[CH2-CH2]_{n}-{}}$. Put the double bond back to find the monomer.
- **Condensation polymers** form from monomers with two reacting groups, releasing water at each link: polyesters (diol + dicarboxylic acid), polyamides such as nylon (diamine + dicarboxylic acid), proteins, starch and cellulose.
:::
